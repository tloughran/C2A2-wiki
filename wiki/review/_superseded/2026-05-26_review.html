BARBARA FREDRICKSON · PAPER · 2025-11
High-quality listening behaviors linked to social connection between strangers
https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00342-2
SUMMARY
West, Huston, Chandler, Zhou & Fredrickson (Communications Psychology, Nov 2025) — an empirical study in which pairs of strangers were observed in either a 10-minute "deep talk" or a brief "small talk" exchange, with behavioral coding of listening quality (follow-up questions, attentive nonverbal behavior). High-quality listening behaviors predicted both observer-rated behavioral markers and self-reported markers of social connection between strangers, and appeared to mediate the effectiveness of a social connectedness intervention. The authors propose that high-quality listening fosters the conducive conditions of perceived safety and real-time sensory connection — and thereby sets the stage for positivity resonance ("togetherness") to emerge.
WHY IT MATTERS
This is the experimental confirmation of the mechanism hypothesized in PRS-08 (high-quality listening as resonance enabler). PRS-08 was based on the 2025 UBC colloquium identifying listening as a candidate mechanism; this paper provides the controlled behavioral-coding evidence that listening behaviors are causally upstream of positivity resonance markers in stranger interactions. It also operationalizes "high-quality listening" precisely enough to support replicable training and intervention — a key step from theory toward population-scale deployment.

CANDIDATE PRS 1 — High
P PRS-08 identified high-quality listening as a probable mechanism for positivity resonance with strangers, but the causal direction and the specific behavioral substrates of "high-quality listening" had not been established in a controlled behavioral-coding paradigm
R West et al. (2025) controlled study in Communications Psychology in which observed listening behaviors (follow-up questions, attentive nonverbal cues) were behaviorally coded and tested as predictors and mediators of both observer-rated and self-reported markers of social connection between strangers
S A confirmed behavioral pathway — high-quality listening behaviors → perceived safety + real-time sensory connection → positivity resonance — providing the specific behavioral observables that listening-quality interventions need to target for measurable population effects on stranger-connection
Evidence: Quote from the abstract: "high-quality listening behaviors (e.g., follow-up questions) are linked to behavioral and self-reported markers of social connection in conversations between strangers and may account for the effectiveness of a social connectedness intervention"
CANDIDATE PRS 2 — High
P Positivity resonance theory has been criticized for being descriptively rich but operationally underspecified — what exactly are the observable behaviors that produce it, beyond the post-hoc reports of participants?
R A behavioral coding scheme for "high-quality listening" — follow-up questions and attentive nonverbal cues — operationalized at a granularity that supports both observer rating and intervention training
S Positivity resonance has at least one well-specified behavioral antecedent that can be coded, trained, and measured — addressing the operationalization gap and providing the first concrete behavioral target for the kinds of population-scale interventions the C2A2 framework requires
Evidence: The paper introduces and validates a behavioral coding scheme for high-quality listening keyed to discrete behaviors (follow-up questions in particular) — a substantive methodological contribution
CROSS-TRADITION SIGNALS
- **McGilchrist connection (strong)**: Follow-up questions are the operational signature of right-hemispheric attention — sustained, contextual, oriented to the whole person being heard rather than to one's own next contribution. This study is empirically operationalizing what McGilchrist calls right-hemispheric attentional mode and showing it is the precondition for positivity resonance. - **Friston connection**: High-quality listening = generative-model updating in real time on the speaker's signal. Listening quality may map onto a measurable parameter in active inference: the relative weighting of incoming sensory evidence vs. one's own priors. - **Hawkins / Hoffman connection**: Real-time sensory connection (one of the two identified mediators) is a measurable interface phenomenon — relevant for Hoffman's interface theory and for Hawkins's hierarchical temporal memory account of attention. - **C2A2 core signal — flag strongly**: This paper gives C2A2 a *trainable behavioral protocol* (follow-up questions + attentive nonverbal behavior) that produces positivity resonance with strangers. Social Media Posts to Encourage In-Person Connections: Online Experiment With Young Adults
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1054139X25003982
SUMMARY
Lazard, Queen, Avendano-Galdamez, Varma, Zhou, Berman, West, Firestine, Dasgupta & Fredrickson (Journal of Adolescent Health, Sept 2025) — a between-persons online experiment with young adults (18–29, lower subjective social status, low-to-moderate psychosocial health) testing video social-media interventions designed to push viewers toward in-person social connection. The team systematically varied visual framing (socially connected peers vs. lonely peers vs. safe spaces vs. control) and message framing, optimizing the intervention messages using Lazard's data-driven attention-capture approach. This is the first behavioral test of using social media itself — typically implicated as a driver of isolation — as a vehicle to push young adults toward the in-person weak-tie contact that PRS-09/10/15 identify as the loneliness-relevant variable.
WHY IT MATTERS
PRS-10 and PRS-15 establish that weak-tie stranger encounters are the empirically critical variable for loneliness; PRS-16 (PROP-2026-04-27-002) gives a brief stranger-interaction micro-intervention. This new paper takes the next step: a *delivery channel* for that intervention at population scale, using the same medium that is otherwise drawing young adults away from in-person contact. It also brings systematic attention-design (Lazard's optimization for "stopping the scroll, engaging attention, inspiring action") into the positivity-resonance toolkit — a methodological enrichment Fredrickson's program previously lacked.
CANDIDATE PRS TRIPLETS
CANDIDATE PRS 1 — High
P Population-scale interventions to cultivate stranger-connection require a delivery channel that reaches young adults where they actually are — and social media, while often implicated as a driver of isolation, is also where young adults spend the largest share of their discretionary attention
R Lazard et al. (2025) experimental optimization of video social-media intervention messages — systematically varying visual framing (connected peers / lonely peers / safe spaces / control) and message framing in a between-persons online experiment with young adults
S A demonstrated experimental design and message-optimization methodology for using social media itself as a delivery vehicle for in-person connection cues — turning the medium implicated in loneliness into the means of its mitigation. The Lazard attention-capture framework becomes a transferable design tool for any C2A2 weak-tie-cultivation campaign
Evidence: The paper systematically tests four visual framings against an attention-capture-optimized control in a randomized online experiment targeting a high-priority loneliness demographic (young adults with low subjective social status), with intervention messages designed using Lazard's data-driven attention methodology

R A direct collaboration between Lazard's health-communication design lab and Fredrickson's positivity-resonance lab, embedding theory-informed message content within attention-optimized delivery designs
S A reproducible cross-disciplinary template — positivity-resonance theory × health-communication design — for translating positive-emotion mechanisms into scalable behavioral interventions, with measurable engagement and behavioral-intent outcomes

SUMMARY
Sean Carroll's Mindscape #353 (May 11, 2026) brings Nobel laureate Alvin Roth (Stanford, 2012 Nobel Prize in Economics for "the theory of stable allocations and the practice of market design") into long-form conversation about his new book *Moral Economics: From Prostitution to Organ Sales, What Controversial Transactions Reveal About How Markets Work*. The episode examines markets in things society is reluctant to subject to market forces — sex work, drugs, organ sales, adopting children — and the design choices that determine whether such markets exploit or benefit participants. Roth's approach is descriptive and design-theoretic rather than prescriptive: rather than declaring a market moral or immoral, he asks what is actually happening in the transaction, who is benefiting, and how the mechanism could be redesigned to mitigate harm.
WHY IT MATTERS
Carroll's poetic naturalism has its most worked-out apparatus on the descriptive side of physics; its normative wing remains the harder lift (see PRS-04 on compatibilism, PRS-05 on science-religion). Roth gives Carroll a working example of what a *naturalist normative engineering discipline* looks like: an empirical science of moral-laden transactions in which value, harm, and dignity are emergent higher-level descriptions, and the design question (which mechanisms preserve which goods?) is tractable without invoking dualism or theistic grounding.
