## Notes

- Q.19 a.1: two operations (divine and human) concur in one work — Aquinas's axe-and-lumberjack image: the human nature is the instrument the one divine person works through ('to touch the leper' human, 'to heal' divine).
- Q.19 a.4: Christ merits for others as *Head of the whole Church* — 'one mystical person'; merit reaches members as the head's action reaches the body; incorporation by baptism (Gal 3:27).
- Q.20: the Son is equal to the Father in divine nature, subject in human nature ('the Father is greater than I,' John 14:28; the *form of a servant*, Phil 2:7).
- Flag-worthy: merit-as-Head and incorporation-into-one-mystical-person is the loving-unity telos in Aquinas's own terms; Phil 2 (kenosis / form of a servant) is the project's banner text.
