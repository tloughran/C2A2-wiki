# Day 190 — Beauty & Disgrace

*Aquinas treats the two integral parts of temperance: shamefacedness (Q.144), the fear of disgrace, and onestum (Q.145) — honorableness, decency, the spiritual beauty of a life ordered to reason. Shame is the recoil from the disgraceful; onestum is the love of the beautiful that draws the soul forward.*

## Notes

- Shamefacedness = fear of a base action (Q.144 a.1). Not a virtue in the perfect man (who is above such fear) but praiseworthy in the ordinary man as a regulatory passion that keeps him from disgrace. A social-feedback signal.
- We are more shamed before those closely connected to us and 'people of the better sort' (a.3) — those whose judgment is most accurate AND those whose judgment most affects us. An explicitly relational/social-attestation account of self-regulation (Fredrickson positivity resonance; McGilchrist intersubjective attention).
- Onestum (Q.145) — Aquinas keeps the Latin; honor/decency/nobility. Synonymous with virtue itself (a.1), and identical to spiritual BEAUTY (a.2): beauty = clarity + due proportion (Dionysius); spiritual beauty = conduct well-proportioned to reason, reason to God.
- Onestum, the useful, and the pleasant converge in the virtuous man but differ in aspect (a.3). Onestum belongs especially to temperance (a.4) as both its motive and its effect — repelling animal lust, 'the most disgraceful and unbecoming to man.'
- Central-theme hook: beauty as clarity (the idea of man shining through) = a perspective made transparent to the whole; disgrace = the local model dimmed and opaque. Strong bridge to Hoffman/Kastrup on clarity-of-interface and to Fredrickson on beauty/positivity.
