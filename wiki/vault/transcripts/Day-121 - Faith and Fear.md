*Questions 6 and 7 of the Secunda Secundae: the cause of faith and the effects of faith. Aquinas argues that saving faith is infused by God — external inducements (a miracle seen, a sermon heard) are not sufficient causes, since of those who see one miracle some believe and some do not; the decisive cause is an interior supernatural principle, God moving the will to assent. Then the effects: faith produces fear (servile and filial), and faith is the first beginning of the heart's purification.*

## Notes

- Q.6 a.1 — the cause-of-faith argument is explicitly *underdetermination*: the same external evidence (one miracle, one sermon) yields belief in some and unbelief in others, so the external cause is insufficient and an internal mover (God, by grace) is posited. This is a clean bridge to Friston/active-inference: priors, not stimulus, fix the posterior. Mark for synthesis.
- Q.6 a.2 — 'lifeless faith' (faith without charity, retained after mortal sin) is still a gift of God; the habit persists even when the orienting love is gone. Useful for the form/animation distinction the synthesis can map onto Levin (pattern persists; the animating telos can be lost).
- Q.7 a.1 — two fears: servile (fear of punishment, from lifeless faith) and filial (dread of separation from the beloved God, from living faith quickened by charity). The phenomenology is relational, not merely deterrent — strong seam for Fredrickson (love-as-attunement) and Wright (covenant belonging).
- Q.7 a.2 — purification of the heart: the silver/lead image. The rational creature becomes impure by loving transient things below it and is purified by 'a contrary movement' tending to what is above it; faith is the first beginning of that movement (Heb 11:6). Reads as a gradient-ascent / re-orientation-of-the-objective-function picture.
- Very short episode (~1140 words ASR). Austin misstates the day number at the open ('is day 120') though it is Day 121 — content and Q-loci are correct.
