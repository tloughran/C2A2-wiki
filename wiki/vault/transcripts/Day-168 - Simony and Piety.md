## Notes
- Q.100 (Simony): buying or selling spiritual things — sacraments, ecclesiastical office, the gift of God for a price. From Simon Magus (Acts 8) trying to buy the Spirit's power. Aquinas: spiritual gifts are *not* in the order of exchangeable goods; pricing them treats grace as commodity and the church as marketplace.
- Three species: gift of the hand (money), gift of the tongue (flattery, lobbying), gift of obsequiousness (servile favor). All three are forms of corrupting the office of grace by exchanging it for created goods.
- Penalties for simony in Aquinas's era were severe — restitution of revenues, deposition. Aquinas notes the office once received may still confer grace (the sacrament is valid), but the simonist incurs personal guilt and obligation of restitution.
- Q.101 (Piety): not the modern 'piety' of churchgoing but the virtue of rendering duty to those from whom we receive being — parents and country. A part of justice, distinct from latria (worship of God) and observance (honor to superiors as such).
- Synthesis hook: simony is the original anti-pattern of mistaking the symbolic order for the market order. Piety is the original positive pattern of recognizing relational debt prior to and not reducible to contract. Both bear on how a community-of-inquiry transmits the goods of a tradition without commodifying them — a live problem for AI-mediated knowledge institutions.
