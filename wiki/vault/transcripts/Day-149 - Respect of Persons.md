# Day 149 — Respect of Persons

*Questions 62 and 63 of the Secunda Secundae — restitution and respect of persons. Q.62: restitution is an act of commutative (‘arithmetical’) justice (a.1); it is necessary for salvation, since to withhold what is another’s obstructs forgiveness (a.2); the exact amount taken suffices for a loan, but theft additionally incurs penalty once condemned (a.3); for goods one prevented another from gaining (the destroyed seed), one owes a ‘virtual,’ partial compensation, not the full actual value (a.4); restitution is made to the owner or his heir (a.5); those who took are always bound (a.6); and so are those who cooperated — by command, counsel, consent, flattery, receiving, participation, silence, or failure to prevent or denounce (a.7) — and it must be made without delay (a.8). Q.63: respect of persons is a sin against distributive justice, bestowing goods on the basis of irrelevant features of the person rather than warranted reasons of need, dignity, or office (a.1); it can corrupt the dispensation of spiritual goods (a.2), the showing of honor (a.3), and judicial sentences (a.4).*

## Notes

- Restitution restores arithmetical equality (a.1): the disturbed balance must be made level again — a conservation principle for the moral order. Strong hook for “equality as the real mean” carried from Day 147.
- The “virtual vs actual” possession argument (a.4): a thwarted future good (the harvest) is possessed only “virtually / potentially,” so its loss is compensated partially, not at full actual value. A graded ontology of potential vs actual goods.
- Cooperation in injustice (a.7): the long list (command, counsel, consent, silence, failure to prevent / denounce) distributes culpability across a whole network of agents, not just the principal — justice as a property of the relational web, not the isolated actor.
- Respect of persons (Q.63 a.1) is precisely distributive injustice: bestowing goods for features “that do not amount to a reason why this man be worthy of this gift.” The wrong is using an irrelevant variable as the basis for proportional distribution.
- a.3 distinguishes honoring a person *for* a warranted reason (virtue, office, representing God or community) from honoring “merely for wealth” — the same external act is just or unjust depending on the relation it tracks.
