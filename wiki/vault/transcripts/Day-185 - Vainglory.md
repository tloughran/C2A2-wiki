## Notes

- Vainglory (Q.132) is the inordinate desire for glory: glory that is 'vain' because sought for unworthy things, from those whose judgment is worthless, or not referred to God.
- Aquinas lists the daughters of vainglory (boasting, love of novelty, hypocrisy, contention, disobedience, etc.) — flag for the self-display economy.
- Pusillanimity (Q.133) is the defect opposed to magnanimity: shrinking from great goods within one's reach — an under-estimated self-model.
- Vainglory over-invests in the self's image; pusillanimity under-invests in the self's real capacity — mirror mis-perceptions of self-in-the-whole.
