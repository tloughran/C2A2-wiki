## Notes

- Q.17: Christ is *one* on account of the unity of person across two natures (the hypostatic union); 'one and being are convertible,' so there is one being (esse) belonging to the one hypostasis, even amid many accidental forms.
- Q.18: Christ has *two wills* — divine and human — plus a 'will of sensuality' (sensitive appetite, rational by participation); the rational human will is the proper human will.
- Q.18 a.5-6: Christ's human will was wholly conformed to the divine ('not as I will, but as thou wilt,' Matt 26); the sensitive appetite shrinks from pain but never rose against reason — no *contrariety* of wills.
- Flag-worthy: the conformity of a genuinely limited human will to the divine will, without coercion and without conflict, is the central-theme structure — a limited perspective freely aligning to the One who sees the whole.
