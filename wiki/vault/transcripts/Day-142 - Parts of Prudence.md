# Day 142 — Parts of Prudence

*Questions 48 and 49 of the Secunda Secundae — on the parts of prudence and on each quasi-integral part. Q.48 distinguishes three senses of “parts” of a virtue: integral parts (like ingredients, all required for the perfect act), subjective parts (species — individual, domestic, political prudence), and potential parts (allied virtues subordinate to the principal act). Q.49 treats the eight integral parts: memory of many things (a.1), since prudence works on contingent matters known only through experience of “what happens in most cases”; understanding — here the cogitative sense or “particular reason” that grasps the singular minor premise (a.2); docility, teachableness, especially needed because no one can survey the infinite variety of particulars alone and must be taught “especially by old folk” (a.3); shrewdness (eustochia), the quick discovery of the middle term (a.4); reason, since counsel proceeds discursively from one thing to another (a.5); foresight, the ordering of means to a distant end (a.6); circumspection, attention to whether the means suit the present circumstances (a.7); and caution, the grasp of good that avoids the evil mingled with it (a.8).*

## Notes

- The eight integral parts read as the component competencies of a bounded-rational agent: memory/priors, particular-binding (cogitative sense), teachableness, abductive search (shrewdness), inference, forecasting (foresight), context-fit (circumspection), risk-avoidance (caution).
- Memory + “what happens in most cases” + experience (a.1) = learned base rates / Bayesian priors built from data.
- The cogitative sense / “particular reason” (a.2): the binding of universal to singular — the interface where the general model meets the concrete state.
- Docility (a.3): “must be taught, especially by old folk who have a sane understanding of the ends” — guidance-from-those-who-perceive-more, operationalized as prior-acquisition / tradition.
