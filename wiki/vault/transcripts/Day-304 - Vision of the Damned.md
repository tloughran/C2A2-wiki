## Notes
- **Greater happiness — extensively.** After resurrection beatitude grows not in intensity but in *extent*, overflowing onto the body. Austin flags that this is early Aquinas (on Lombard's Sentences) corrected by the mature ST — read with care.
- **Mansions = degrees of glory.** Many mansions (Jn 14) = degrees of beatitude tracking degrees of charity/merit; the "light of glory" admits of degrees, so all see God but one more perfectly than another. Differentiation persists in glory.
- **The hard articles (94).** The blessed *see* the sufferings of the damned (contraries more conspicuous → more thanks to God); they do *not* pity (no sorrow in heaven; pity would oppose divine justice); they rejoice in the punishment only *indirectly* — in the order of divine justice and their own deliverance, never directly in the suffering.
- **Flag for synthesis:** Q.94 is the sharpest place to mark divergence from Aquinas — the eternal-conscious-torment frame and "rejoicing" sit in tension with Wright's new-creation and Rohr's universal-restoration readings. Theological/speculative, not empirical.
