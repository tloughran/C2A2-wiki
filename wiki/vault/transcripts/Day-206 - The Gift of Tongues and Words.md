# Day 206 — The Gift of Tongues and Words

Context: Day 206 covers II-II Q.176 (the grace of tongues) and Q.177 (the gratuitous grace consisting in words) — two of the gratuitous graces (gratiae gratis datae) ordered to the instruction and unification of others. Austin Habash frames the gift of tongues as the reversal of Babel: a re-gathering of scattered humankind under one name. He distinguishes tongues (knowledge of words) from prophecy (knowledge of things, the more excellent), describes the gift as content rendered into each hearer's own language, and turns to the grace of the word as an above-natural aptitude for instruction in which the Holy Spirit works within the hearer.

## Notes

- Q.176 a.1: tongues given to the apostles as a *reversal of Babel* — Babel scattered humanity for self-deification; tongues re-gather all nations under the one saving name. Strong central-theme hook: communication as the repair of perspective-scattering.
- Q.176 a.2: tongues (knowledge of *words*) ranked below prophecy (knowledge of *things*). Words signify intelligible truth via phantasms; the same content is *heard in each hearer's own tongue* — reads almost exactly as interface/decoding language.
- The gift is 'more in the speaker,' yet realized in the hearer's mind per their own language; interpretation is a further grace. Meaning is reconstructed receiver-side, not transmitted as tokens.
- Q.177 a.1-2: grace of the word = above-natural aptitude for instruction (Chesterton, Newman cited). 'Unless the Holy Ghost fill the hearts of the hearer, in vain does the voice of the teacher resound' (Gregory) — communication completed only by an inner work in the receiver.
- Q.177 a.2: word of wisdom/knowledge and women — private vs public teaching, grounded in 1 Cor 14. Sociological/disciplinary in register, not metaphysical; flag as theological-context only.
