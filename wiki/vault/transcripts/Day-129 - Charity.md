# Day 129 — Charity

*Questions 22 and 23 of the Secunda Secundae. Q.22 gives the precepts relating to hope and fear, completing the treatise on hope. Q.23 opens the great treatise on charity, treating charity considered in itself: whether charity is friendship, whether it is something created in the soul, whether it is a virtue, whether it is a special virtue, whether it is one virtue, and — the keystone — whether charity is the form of the virtues. Following Aristotle's account of friendship and John 15 ('I have called you friends'), Aquinas defines charity as the friendship of man with God, founded on God's communication of his own beatitude to us, and names it the form, mother, and root of all the virtues, since it directs every other virtuous act to the last end.*

## Notes

- Q.23 a.1 — charity is friendship: a mutual, benevolent love founded on a shared communication — here, God's sharing of his own beatitude with us. Not love of a good merely wanted but a reciprocal union of persons. Stump's signature reframe (charity as second-person, mutual indwelling); Friston PRS-06 (mutual modeling makes a coherent collective agent); Fredrickson PRS-02 (love as measurable coupling).
- Q.23 a.2 — charity is something created and infused in the soul, not the Holy Spirit moving us without any act of our own; we are genuine secondary causes who really love. Stump PRS-09 — grace perfects the natural power so the loving act is authentically the agent's own.
- Q.23 a.5 — charity is one virtue, one habit, though it loves both God and neighbor, because the neighbor is loved for God's sake: one formal motive unifies the many objects.
- Q.23 a.8 (the keystone) — charity is the form of the virtues: it directs the acts of all the other virtues to the last end, and so gives them their character as virtues. Reframe with the central theme: charity is the ordering principle that binds many limited powers into one teleologically-unified agent directed at loving union. Stump PRS-14/PRS-15 (hylomorphic corporate form — the form that makes the many one).
- Q.22 — the precepts of hope and fear are given not to command an impossible interior act on demand but to forbid what destroys hope (despair) and presumption, and to require the reverence that keeps the orientation alive. Law here protects the goal-prior rather than manufacturing it.
