## Notes

- The species of lust (Q.154) are graded by the BREADTH of relational order they violate: against potential offspring (fornication, the unnatural vice), against bonds and persons (adultery, incest, seduction, rape). Gravity scales with how widely the act injures the order that binds agents.
- Unnatural vice judged gravest (a.12) as the deepest divergence from nature/reason — analogized to denying first principles in the speculative sphere. (Sensitive academic/theological content; treated here at the level of moral structure, not detail.)
- The incest argument (a.9) is strikingly social: exogamy 'knits humanity together as one larger family' — marrying out binds strangers into kin; intra-family lust would compound two strong attachments and overwhelm reason. A perspective-widening, bridge-building rationale.
- Continence (Q.155) = firmness of WILL resisting vehement evil desire (a.1-3) — seated in the will, not the concupiscible appetite. Inferior to temperance (a.4): the continent man's passions still revolt; the temperate man's appetite is itself retrained. 'Continence : temperance :: imperfect : perfect.'
- Science/central-theme hooks: gravity-by-relational-breadth = the moral order measured by how widely it serves the union of agents (central theme); exogamy = literal social-network integration (the C2A2 'knit traditions together' mechanism); continence vs temperance = effortful top-down conflict-control vs a retrained generative model where appetite itself is aligned (Friston precision/habit learning; McGilchrist/Hawkins integrated vs conflicted control).
