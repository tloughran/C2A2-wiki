# Day 143 — Kinds of Prudence

*Questions 50 and 51 of the Secunda Secundae — on the subjective parts of prudence and on the virtues connected with it. Q.50, the subjective parts (species, distinguished by their different ends and common goods): regnative prudence, proper to the supreme ruler who must direct all subjects to the common happiness (a.1); political prudence, proper to subjects who govern themselves in obeying their superiors (a.2); domestic prudence, ordered to the common good of the household, a mean between individual and city (a.3); and military prudence, ordered to the common welfare secured through warfare (a.4). Q.51, the connected (potential) virtues that modify the acts feeding into command: eubulia, the virtue of taking good counsel — right deliberation about the means (a.1), distinct from prudence, which strictly concerns right command (a.2); synesis, good judgment of what is to be done according to the common rules (a.3); and gnome, the higher virtue of judging according to the more universal principles when the common rule fails to fit the case — as when a deposit must not be returned to a man who has become a traitor to the state (a.4).*

## Notes

- Subjective parts (individual/domestic/political/regnative/military) = practical reason at nested agent-scales: multi-scale competency, nested Markov blankets, multi-agent coherence.
- Political prudence: the subject “moves himself by free will” while “moved by the commands of others” — the part computing for the whole it belongs to.
- gnome vs. synesis (Q.51 a.3–4): when the common rule fails the singular case (deposit to a traitor), judgment escalates to higher principles — out-of-distribution judgment; letter→spirit; non-dual transcendence of the rule.
- The connected virtues (eubulia, synesis, gnome) = deliberation (policy search) and judgment (evaluation) feeding command.
