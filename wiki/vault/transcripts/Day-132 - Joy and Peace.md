# Day 132 — Joy and Peace

*Questions 28 and 29 of the Secunda Secundae — on joy and on peace, the first two interior effects of charity. Q.28: joy is caused in us by charity (a.1), both from the indwelling possession of God by grace and from the love of benevolence by which we rejoice in a friend's good as in our own; this spiritual joy is compatible with an admixture of sorrow (a.2), since here we possess God imperfectly, as wayfarers and not yet comprehensors — "I sought him whom my soul loves, but found him not"; the joy cannot be filled or complete in this life (a.3), only in heaven where the object is perfectly seen; and joy is not itself a virtue but a fruit of charity (a.4). Q.29: peace is more than concord (a.1) — concord is a union of wills among persons, but peace, in Augustine's phrase, is the tranquility of order, requiring union within the one man as well, so that a gang of thieves may have concord but never peace; all things desire peace (a.2), even by natural appetite, as everything moves toward its end and rests in its attainment; peace is the proper effect of charity (a.3), which integrates a man's many desires under one love and unites his will to his neighbor's; and peace, like joy, is a fruit of charity rather than a distinct virtue (a.4).*
