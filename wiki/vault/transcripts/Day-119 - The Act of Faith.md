## Notes

- Augustine's definition: "to believe is to think with assent." Aquinas develops this: belief sits between scientia (which has clear sight) and opinion (which lacks firmness) — distinctive epistemic state, neither pure inference nor pure trust.
- Three aspects of one act: *credere Deum* (believe God exists), *credere Deo* (believe what God says), *credere in Deum* (believe-into / set heart on God). Aquinas insists these are not three acts but one act under three relations.
- Material/formal structure of faith maps onto hylomorphism: believing-that-God-is is the matter, believing-God is the form. Direct seam for Hoffman interface-theory: the formal layer of trust requires the material layer of categorial reference.
- Q.2 a.3 — faith is necessary above natural reason because man's actual end (beatific vision) exceeds natural cognitive capacity; *Humani Generis* footnote (gratuity of supernatural order) acknowledged.
- "To know simple things defectively is not to know them at all" — Aquinas's epistemic strictness. The synthesis should mark how Hoffman's interface-theory reframes this: any finite agent's grasp of the simple is always interface-mediated, hence "defective" in a structural sense distinct from what Aquinas had in mind.
