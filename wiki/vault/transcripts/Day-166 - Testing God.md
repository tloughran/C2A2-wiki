## Notes
- Q.96 (Superstitions in Observances): wearing amulets, sacred relics around the neck, divinatory characters. Lawful insofar as text and intent are wholly directed to God; unlawful when the *means* presumes occult efficacy beyond what the words or matter naturally carry.
- Aquinas's test: is the practice grounded in (a) divine institution, (b) the nature of the thing, or (c) tacit pact with demons? Only (a) and (b) are licit.
- Q.97 (Temptation of God): tempting God = a deed by which one experimentally tests whether God's power, knowledge, or goodness will deliver. Explicit (Gideon-style demand for a sign without need) vs. implicit (refusing ordinary means while expecting miraculous rescue).
- Distinction Aquinas makes: not every petition for a sign is tempting God. When God commands it, when faith is being established (Moses, Gideon at start), or when the need is real and no ordinary means is available, asking is permitted.
- Synthesis hook: this is the question of how an agent under resource-limitation should *test* its world-model. Aquinas's prohibition on tempting God maps neatly onto Friston-style active inference: a healthy agent runs cheap, in-distribution tests; the agent who demands miraculous confirmations is one who refuses to update on the evidence it already has.
