## Notes

- Patience is defined as the virtue of *suffering well* — it modifies the irascible passion of sorrow so reason is not overcome (Q.136 a.1). Maps cleanly onto an affect/self-regulation frame (Fredrickson resilience; Friston precision-weighting).
- Patience is explicitly NOT the greatest virtue (a.2): it falls short of the theological virtues, of prudence/justice (which directly establish the good), and of fortitude/temperance (which guard against greater obstacles). Aquinas's ranking is a load-bearing claim for the synthesis.
- True patience requires grace and charity (a.3) — Paul's 'I endure everything for the sake of the elect.' The natural/supernatural distinction recurs.
- Perseverance answers a UNIQUE difficulty: holding the good across the *expanse of time* (Q.137 a.1). The gift of perseverance (a.4) is God protecting us in grace — habit alone does not guarantee right action (David, Solomon as counter-examples).
- Central-theme hook: perseverance-as-grace = guidance from one who sees more of the whole than we do; computing the good under temporal resource-scarcity.
