# Day 128 — Presumption

*Questions 20 and 21 of the Secunda Secundae — the two vices opposed to hope. Q.20 treats despair: whether it is a sin, whether there can be despair without unbelief, whether it is the greatest of sins, and whether it arises from sloth. Q.21 treats presumption: whether presumption trusts in God or in one's own power, whether it is a sin, whether it is more opposed to fear than to hope, and from what it arises. Despair abandons the good as unattainable; presumption expects the reward without the means — glory without merit, pardon without repentance. Both are disorders of the one rightly-ordered movement of hope.*

## Notes

- Q.20 a.1 — despair is a sin: a movement away from God, refusing the divine good as impossible-for-me, severing the appetite from its proper object. Reframe: despair is the collapse of the goal-prior — the system ceases to treat the preferred state as reachable and so stops seeking it (Friston PRS-03 loss of the target-prior; PRS-14 collapse into a rigid, low-hope attractor that resists update).
- Q.20 a.3 — despair, in its genus, is more dangerous than unbelief or hatred of God, because it removes the very hope that restrains a man from evil and draws him toward the good; once hope is gone he plunges into vice with no holds barred. Directly echoes Day 125 (despair removes hope as a restraint).
- Q.20 a.4 — despair arises chiefly from sloth (acedia) and from lust: the sluggish or carnally-absorbed appetite stops valuing the spiritual good as attainable. Embodied/affective gating of cognition again (Fredrickson, McGilchrist).
- Q.21 a.1-2 — presumption: an inordinate trust, either in one's own power or (worse) in the divine mercy or power — hoping for glory without merit or pardon without repentance. Reframe: presumption is a pathological down-weighting of the error signal, a false certainty that the target is already secured, muting the very correction that fear would supply (Friston PRS-16, mis-set precision).
- Q.21 a.4 — presumption arises from pride and vainglory: a man thinks so much of himself that he supposes God will not punish him. The self-model overweights its own estimate against the evidence — the structural mirror of despair (over-confidence vs. under-confidence in the reachable good).
