## Notes

- Q.21: excommunication defined as exclusion from the communion of the Church (chiefly from the sacraments and the suffrages of the faithful); its congruity defended as medicinal, not merely punitive.
- Aquinas frames the major excommunication as separation from the body — the offending member is cut off so that shame may lead to amendment (cf. 1 Cor 5).
- Q.22: who can excommunicate — those with jurisdiction; the power follows from the power of the keys treated in the prior days.
- Medicinal intent stressed: the end is the sinner's reintegration, not destruction; an unjust excommunication does not harm the soul of the one wrongly bound.
- Flag-worthy: excommunication = a collective agent redrawing its boundary around a defecting member; the medicinal aim is reintegration into the whole.
