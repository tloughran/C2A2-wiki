## Notes
- Idolatry: paying to a creature the *latria* due only to creator. Aquinas distinguishes veneration of saints/images (signification — they point to God) from idolatry (the creature as terminus). Christ's image takes latria *because of his divinity*.
- Idolatry is one of the gravest sins because it perverts the ultimate end — analogous to crowning a usurper.
- Causes of idolatry (Book of Wisdom 14): grief-driven ancestor memorialization; awe of absent rulers; demonic exploitation. Adam's fall removed the infused supernatural knowledge that would have made the error harder.
- Divination has three sweeping kinds: necromancy (open invocation of demons), augury (reading dispositions in external things), sortilege (acting to provoke an occult sign). All three usurp God's prerogative — knowing the contingent future of voluntary agents — or ally with the enemy who can guess it better than we can.
- Knowable futures: by natural causes (the stone falls), by conjecture (the weatherman), by revelation (the prophet). Astrology is licit for physical effects (weather, harvest) but illicit for predicting voluntary human action.
- Lots can be licit when no other means is available and one *trusts God in the outcome* (the choice of Matthias).
- Synthesis hook: this is the question of *epistemic humility about the future* and *which sources of information are even legitimate to consult*. Pre-modern "demons" map onto contemporary disinformation channels — sources whose intent is destruction will give you true bits in a frame that injures you.
