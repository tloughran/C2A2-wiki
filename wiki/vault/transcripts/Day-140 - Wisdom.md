# Day 140 — Wisdom

*Questions 44 and 45 of the Secunda Secundae — on the precepts of charity and on the gift of wisdom. Q.44: precepts of charity should be given (a.1), for God, being goodness itself, is due love as our ultimate end, enabled by faith, a good conscience, and purity of heart; there are two precepts, love of God and love of neighbor (a.2) — one who truly understood the love of God would see that it entails love of neighbor (as one who grasps the principle of sufficient reason sees that it entails an uncaused, perfect being), but the second precept is spelled out for the less intelligent; two precepts suffice (a.3, since love of self and of one's body need no command); we are to love God with the whole heart (a.4), nothing else loved as an end save God; the additions "whole soul, mind, strength" (a.5) spell out the will's intention, the intellect's submission, the appetite's regulation, and external obedience; the precept is fulfilled in this life in habit, not act (a.6, the soldier-and-commander analogy); love of neighbor is fittingly expressed (a.7), the neighbor loved as imago Dei called to the same beatitude; and the order of charity is included in the precept (a.8). Q.45: wisdom is reckoned among the gifts of the Holy Spirit (a.1), the knowledge of the highest cause, God, differing from faith in that faith assents to divine truth while wisdom judges according to it; it is in the intellect as its subject (a.2) but presupposes charity, judging divine things by *connaturality* — by being made like God through grace; it is both speculative and practical (a.3); it cannot be had as a gift without grace and with mortal sin (a.4); it is in all who have charity (a.5), though some receive a higher, gratuitous degree; and it answers to the seventh beatitude, "blessed are the peacemakers" (a.6) — for peace is the tranquility of order, and "it belongs to charity to be at peace, but to wisdom to make peace by setting things in order."*

## Notes

- Q.44 a.2 sufficient-reason analogy: grasping the principle entails grasping an uncaused, perfect being (God); and the love of God entails love of neighbor because "love unites affections." A metaphysical move — engage under the Levin/Hoffman/Kastrup frame (guardrail).
- The gift of wisdom judges divine things by *connaturality* — by being made like God through grace; knowing-by-being, not by representation. The clearest bridge to the central theme: the limit where perspective-limitation is removed and the simple whole is seen.
- Wisdom is both speculative and practical: from knowledge of the highest cause it orders lower things (the architect/philosopher analogy) — wisdom as the ordering of a whole.
- Peace = "tranquility of order"; "it belongs to charity to be at peace, but to wisdom to make peace by setting things in order" (a.6) — the computational telos of loving unity, named in Aquinas's own terms.
- The order of charity (one's own soul > neighbor's soul > one's body > nearer/holier neighbors) is the resource-bounded structure of love under a finite perspective.
