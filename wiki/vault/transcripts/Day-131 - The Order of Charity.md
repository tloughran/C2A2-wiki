# Day 131 — The Order of Charity

*Questions 26 and 27 of the Secunda Secundae — twenty articles, taken at a sprint. Q.26 treats the order of charity: that there is an order in the things we love (a.1); that God is loved above neighbor (a.2) and even above oneself (a.3), because nature itself bids the part to love the common good — the hand spends itself for the head, the soldier for his country; that one loves one's own soul above the neighbor's good but may lay down one's body for the neighbor (a.4-5); and that among neighbors the intensity of love is proportioned to nearness, the governing principle being that the affection of love increases in proportion to nearness to the two principles, God and oneself (a.6) — so that we love the better more by charity and the nearer more by natural intensity (a.7), love blood-kin more (a.8-9), and the order persists in heaven, transposed into the order of sanctity (a.13). Q.27 treats charity's principal act, to love: that loving is more proper to charity than being loved (a.1); that charity is more than goodwill, adding a real union of affection (a.2); that God is loved for himself (a.3), immediately even in this life (a.4), wholly (a.5), and without measure — Bernard's word, that the measure of loving God is to love him without measure (a.6); and that love of an enemy, though its object is lesser, is the greater proof of charity, as a furnace shows its power by throwing heat to the most distant things (a.7).*
