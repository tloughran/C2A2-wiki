## Notes

- Q.122 treats the precepts of justice (the Decalogue read as commands of justice toward God and neighbor).
- Q.123 treats fortitude: firmness of mind in facing difficulty and danger, chiefly the fear of death; its principal act is to endure (sustinere), not to attack.
- Aquinas: fortitude's endurance is harder and more virtuous than aggression because it stands firm against a present, stronger evil over time.
- Bridges: resilience and the undo-effect of positive emotion (Fredrickson); courage as standing-in-the-world (McGilchrist); holding a long-horizon expected-value attractor against immediate threat (Friston).
