## Notes
- Q.102 (Observance): the virtue of rendering honor to those who excel in dignity — superiors, dignitaries, elders. Subspecies: *dulia* (honor to persons of excellence) and *observantia* properly so called (deference paid by inferiors).
- Aquinas's careful gradation: latria to God alone; hyperdulia to Mary; dulia to saints and to excellent persons generally; observance to those who hold offices. The taxonomy preserves a hierarchy of warranted honor that maps to actual rank of being.
- Q.103 (Dulia): how dulia differs from latria. Latria is owed to God alone because God alone is the source of being; dulia is owed to created excellence as participated in the divine excellence. So dulia at its limit always refers up.
- Practical implications: bowing, kneeling, kissing of relics, address by title — Aquinas defends these as legitimate when the inner referral is right. They become idolatry only when the creature is taken as terminus rather than as window onto God.
- Synthesis hook: the latria-dulia distinction is a precision instrument for *what gets the response of ultimate concern*. Modern misreadings collapse the gradient and end up either iconoclastic (no created thing may receive honor) or idolatrous (all honor is collapsed into worship). The graded ontology Hoffman, Levin, and Kastrup defend, on which agency scales up and down, is the contemporary version of the same gradient.
