## Notes

- Q.88 (venial as compared with mortal): the standard distinction -- mortal sin destroys charity; venial sin disorders within charity. Aquinas does NOT treat them as differing only in degree; the distinction is in kind because of the relation to the final end.
- Habash emphasizes: mortal sin is a turning *away from* the final end (averting the ordo of charity); venial is a disordered means choice within the still-operative orientation toward the end.
- Q.89 (venial sin in itself): venial sin can coexist with charity precisely because it does not involve the will's deliberate aversion from God. It still disorders the soul's order toward the end, but at a lower level (means / disposition / habit), not at the level of the final end.
- Conditions of mortal sin (the classical trio Habash invokes): grave matter, full knowledge, deliberate consent. All three required; absence of any reduces to venial.
- Flag for synthesis: this is the binary-vs-continuum question. The dynamical reframe (residual bias, attractor capture) treats the disorder continuously; the medieval account insists on a categorical break at the loss of charity. The synthesis must take a position on whether the binary survives the reframe or is recast as a practically-important threshold.
