# Day 130 — What Charity Loves

*Questions 24 and 25 of the Secunda Secundae — two long questions, twelve articles each, on the subject and the object of charity. Q.24 treats charity in its subject: that the will is its seat, that it is infused rather than acquired, that it can increase — and increases by the subject's ever-deeper participation rather than by the addition of parts — that there is no fixed limit to its increase in this life, and that it admits of degrees: the beginners, the proficient, and the perfect. Q.25 treats the object of charity, what charity loves: God first of all, then oneself, one's neighbor, and one's own body; whether we ought to love sinners, our enemies, the angels, and even (the question is raised and answered) the demons; closing with the order of charity — God, then one's own soul, then the neighbor's soul, then the body, all loved for God's sake.*

## Notes

- Q.24 a.4-7 — charity increases, and in this life increases without any fixed limit, by the subject's ever-deeper rooting and participation, not by accretion of parts. Reframe: an open-ended deepening of coupling, an upward spiral with no ceiling in via (Fredrickson PRS-01 upward spirals; Friston: tightening of mutual model-alignment without a fixed bound).
- Q.24 a.9 — three degrees of charity: beginning (chiefly avoiding sin and resisting concupiscence), progress (intent on growing in good), and perfect (cleaving to and enjoying God, longing to depart and be with him). Maps onto Rohr's order-disorder-reorder developmental arc (PRS-02) and Stump on moral maturation.
- Q.25 a.4-5 — a man ought to love himself, and even his body, out of charity: proper self-love is part of the order of charity, not its enemy; the self is included, rightly ordered to God. The order of charity is concentric inclusion, not self-erasure.
- Q.25 a.6, a.8-9 — we love sinners out of charity (loving the nature and the possible good, not the sin); and charity requires love of enemies — at minimum in the common love of all and in readiness, and in its perfection by showing them the signs of love. The Jesus-distinctive (Wright on enemy-love); radical inclusivity with an empirical echo in Fredrickson PRS-06/PRS-15 (love extends to strangers and out-groups).
- Q.25 a.11-12 — we are not bound to love the demons out of charity, since their will is fixed in evil; we love only the good of nature that remains. And the closing order: God, then one's own soul, then the neighbor's soul, then the body, all loved by charity for God's sake. Reframe: the order of charity is the structured form of the loving whole — every limited perspective contained in the simple whole, but in an ordo proportioned to nearness to God; the one exclusion is the perspective finally sealed against the whole (echoing Day 125's final impenitence). Stump PRS-14/PRS-16; Levin on nested agency; Rohr's Universal Christ (everything belongs, in order).
