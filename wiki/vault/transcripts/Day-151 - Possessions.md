## Notes

- Q.66 opens the post-physical-injury arc of the justice treatise: external possessions, theft, and robbery as injustices of *taking*.
- The argument: possession is natural for man (steward, *imago Dei*); private property is lawful but the *use* remains common; necessity overrides positive property law.
- Aquinas: in dire necessity all things are common — taking another's goods to survive is *not theft*. Direct anchor for the central theme: justice presupposes the prior good of mutual flourishing.
- Q.67 turns to the judge: judicial injustice (judging one outside one's jurisdiction, judging on suspicion, against the law, judging the absent) — the asymmetry of perspective in the judgment seat is the crux.
- Robbery vs theft: violence vs stealth — robbery aggravated because it adds wrong to wrong, breaking the *peace* the political community is for.
