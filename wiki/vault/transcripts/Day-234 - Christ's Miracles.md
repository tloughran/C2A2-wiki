## Notes

- Q.43 a.1: the sealed-letters analogy — "when a man does works that God alone can do, we may believe that what he says is from God... as when a man is the bearer of letters sealed with the king's ring"; miracles are epistemic instruments for claims that "surpass human reason."
- Q.43 a.2: divine power working "through his humanity as through a human instrument" — a dual-nature operation, not borrowed power ("power came forth from him").
- Q.43 a.4: sufficiency rests on three legs — above nature's power (Lazarus, the man born blind), by his own power (the woman and the cloak), and in confirmation of his explicit self-claims ("Before Abraham was, I am").
- Q.44: miracles on every kind of creature — demons, heavenly bodies (the darkened sun; Habash flags that the Aristotelian cosmology here "is not likely to be accepted today"), men, irrational creatures — "it behooved him to work miracles on every kind of creature" since all are subject to divine power.
- Q.44 a.3: the means proportionate to the end — invisible forgiveness certified by visible healing ("which is easier...?"); the visible interface used to warrant the invisible reality.
- Flag-worthy: what *is* a miracle if nature is interface (Hoffman) rather than fundamental? Wright's reframe (new creation breaking in, not law-violation) and Levin's bioelectric override of default morphogenetic outcomes are the bridges — with careful grading: Levin's overrides are within nature and remodel the *concept*, not the evidence.
