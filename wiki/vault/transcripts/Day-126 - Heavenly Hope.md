# Day 126 — Heavenly Hope

*Questions 16 and 17 of the Secunda Secundae. Q.16 closes the treatment of faith with the precepts governing faith, knowledge, and understanding — the commandments of the Old and New Law that pertain to these gifts. Q.17 then opens the treatise on hope, treating hope considered in itself: whether hope is a virtue, whether eternal happiness (the beatific vision) is its proper object, whether one may hope for another's salvation, whether one may lawfully hope in man, whether hope is a theological virtue, and whether it is distinct from the other theological virtues. Aquinas defines hope as the movement of the rational appetite toward a future good that is arduous but possible to attain, leaning on the divine help by which it is reached.*

## Notes

- Q.17 a.1 — hope is a true (and theological) virtue: its rule and measure is God himself, the divine help on which it leans; it attains God and so is more than a passion. Contemporary reframe: hope is the goal-prior — the appetite's confident expectation that the arduous-but-possible good is genuinely reachable, the prior that orients all action toward the preferred state (Friston PRS-03/PRS-04; Wright PRS-03, inaugurated eschatology's already/not-yet).
- Q.17 a.2 — eternal happiness, the beatific vision of God himself, is the proper object of hope; everything else is hoped for in relation to it. This is the central theme almost verbatim: the object of hope is the simple whole, seen, in which every limited perspective is contained.
- Q.17 a.3 — we may hope for another's eternal happiness insofar as we are united to them by love; the union of charity extends hope's object to include the other. Bridge to Friston PRS-06 / Fredrickson PRS-03 — coupling widens the agent's preferred-states to take in the other's good.
- Q.17 a.4 — one may not place the principal hope of salvation in man, only in God, though we may hope in man secondarily as a helper or means. The central-theme thread of dependence on those who perceive more of the whole, human or divine — rightly ordered, never idolatrous.
- Q.17 a.8 (the closer) — hope and charity are reciprocal: hope leads to charity (hoping to be rewarded by God, a man is encouraged to love and obey him), yet in the order of perfection charity precedes and perfects hope, 'for we hope chiefly in our friends.' Sets up the charity questions ahead.
