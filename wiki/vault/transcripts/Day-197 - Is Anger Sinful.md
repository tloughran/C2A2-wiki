## Notes

- **Anger = the desire for revenge**, lawful when ordered to justice (correcting a wrong, not hating the person), sinful when it exceeds the deserved measure, targets the undeserving, or aims at something other than justice (Q.158 a.1-2).
- **Anger is a capital vice** (a.6): it leads to other vices both by the desirability of its end (vengeance under the attractive aspect of justice) and by its impetuosity. Six 'daughters': indignation, swelling of the mind, clamor, contumely, blasphemy, quarreling.
- **Defect of anger is also a vice** (a.8): the man who feels nothing at injustice is not virtuous; 'unreasonable patience is the hotbed of many vices.' Both excess AND deficiency miss the mark — anger is a calibrated, not merely suppressed, response.
- **Cruelty** (Q.159) is opposed to clemency as hardness of heart ready to *increase* punishment; it still has (disordered) justice in view, distinguishing it from savagery/brutality, which inflicts pain for pleasure and is 'bestial.'
- Synthesis hook: anger as the irascible appetite's precision-weighting on perceived injustice; virtue is right calibration, not zero gain.
