# Day 215 — The Order of Assumption

> Episode: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vIGOdhW99RA>
> Summa reference: *Tertia Pars (III), Q.5-6*

Context: Day 215 covers III Q.5 (the parts of human nature which were assumed — whether the Son assumed a true body, a carnal/earthly body, a soul, and a human mind/intellect) and III Q.6 (the order of assumption — whether the flesh was assumed through the medium of the soul, the soul through the medium of the spirit/intellect, whether soul was assumed before flesh, flesh before soul, the whole through the medium of the parts, and human nature through the medium of grace). Austin Habash stresses that the 'order' in question is one of dignity and causality, not of time — every part is assumed at the one instant of conception.

## Notes
- Q.5: a complete human nature is assumed — true earthly body, rational soul, and human intellect ('if Christ had had a soul without a mind, he would have had irrational flesh'). Completeness of the assumed perspective: nothing human is left out.
- Q.6 a.1-2: 'order' = dignity + causality, NOT time. Flesh assumed through the medium of the soul (the soul is nearer God in dignity and the body exists through the soul); the soul through the medium of the intellect/spirit ('the purest part of the soul,' Damascene). A hierarchy-of-form reading rather than a temporal sequence.
- Q.6 a.3-4: the soul is not assumed before the flesh, nor flesh before soul — 'created at the same time they are infused into bodies'; Christ's ensoulment tracks the ordinary human course (conception), so His nature is genuinely of the same kind as ours.
- Q.6 a.5: the whole is assumed through the medium of the parts only in the sense that the AGENT's intention of the whole comes first in the order of nature — 'the Word assumed the parts through the medium of the whole.' Whole-priority over parts: good for the morphological-whole bridge (Levin).
- Q.6 a.6: 'the grace of union is the personal being given gratis... and is the term of the assumption,' while habitual grace (the man's holiness) follows the union. The shortest episode of the batch; closes the opening movement of the Incarnation treatise.
