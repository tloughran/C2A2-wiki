## Notes
- Aquinas distinguishes oblations (offerings that remain entire and pass to God's worship or to ministers) from sacrifices (consumed in the offering). Material support of the church is a precept of natural law refined by ecclesial determination.
- First-fruits and tithes: natural law commands *that* we render some return to God; the *quantity* and *recipient* are determined by positive law (old law literal, new law ecclesial custom).
- Article 3 introduces a subtle ordering rule: even a religious gift becomes wrong if it sabotages a prior natural-law duty (parents, dependents). Habash flags Christ's "corban" reproach (Mark 7) as the paradigm case.
- Tithes flow downhill in the order of giving: the Levite tithes to the High Priest, the clergy tithe to Rome if asked, because authority over the common estate requires resources to discharge it.
- Synthesis hook: this whole question is about *resource flow under perspective-limitation* — the agent who sees only his household cannot also feed the polity; the polity needs structured offering to function.
