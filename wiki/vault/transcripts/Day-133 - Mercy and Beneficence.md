# Day 133 — Mercy and Beneficence

*Questions 30 and 31 of the Secunda Secundae — on mercy and on beneficence, charity turning outward toward the neighbor's misery. Q.30: the proper motive of mercy is another's evil or misery (a.1) — misericordia, misery-of-heart, by which a friend's suffering becomes our own sorrow; pity requires being passible and changeable (a.2), so that strictly God in his nature does not pity but acts "by love alone, in as much as he loves us as belonging to him," while Christ in his humanity truly suffered-with us, and for us creatures compassion is a perfection, an effect of love; mercy is a virtue (a.3); yet mercy is not the greatest virtue (a.4) — "the sum total of the Christian religion consists in mercy as regards external works," but charity, by which we are united to God, presides over it. Q.31: beneficence, doing good to others, is an act of charity (a.1), since charity is friendship and doing good is an act of friendship; we ought to do good to all (a.2), but to those more closely united to us in preference (a.3) — grace builds on nature, and as heat radiates most to what is nearest the flame, so love is proportioned to nearness, though extreme need may justly reorder the priority; and beneficence is not a distinct virtue but an act of charity (a.4).*

- Q.31 a.3 — the order of beneficence: "it is not possible as a finite temporal thing to spend the exact same amount of time and effort loving each and every single human being on the planet," so good is conferred by degrees of connection, with extreme need as an overriding exception. FLAG: this is the central theme almost verbatim — loving unity as a resource-bounded computation. A finite agent allocates finite love by coupling-strength and urgency; the stranger-in-extreme-need exception is a high-precision signal overriding the default gradient (Friston, expected-free-energy allocation; bounded rationality). Strongest central-theme locus in the batch.
- Q.30 a.2 — pity requires passibility; the impassible God pities only through the Incarnation. Reframe (and a gentle reframe of Aquinas): to couple, an agent must be affectable — an open Markov blanket susceptible to the other's states (Friston). The "defect" of being changeable is precisely the condition of compassion/coupling; the Incarnation is the whole entering passibility to couple with the limited (Rohr, Universal Christ PRS-03/PRS-04).
- Q.30 a.1 — mercy is "taking what happens to our friends to happen to us," considering their sorrows as our own. Reframe: the thinning of the perspective-boundary; the other's prediction-error registered as mine (Friston PRS-06 mutual modeling; Fredrickson resonance PRS-02). Mercy is co-suffering — coupling under the sign of the other's misery.
- Q.30 a.4 / Q.31 a.1, a.4 — "the sum total of the Christian religion consists in mercy as regards external works"; beneficence is charity-in-act, not a distinct virtue. Reframe: charity (the coupling) must externalize as enacted help — love is co-active, not sentiment (Fredrickson PRS-02; echoes Day 131 Q.27 a.1, that to love is more proper than to be loved). The acts are the coupling made visible.
- Q.31 a.3 — the heat/flame radiation analogy for proportioned love. Reframe: a field/gradient picture — love strongest at the source, diminishing with distance; this drops Aquinas's great-chain emanation grounding but keeps the finitude logic. Honest: the static hierarchy is dated; the resource-allocation insight survives and is better explained by precision-weighting.
