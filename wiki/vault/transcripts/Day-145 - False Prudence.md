# Day 145 — False Prudence

*Questions 54 and 55 of the Secunda Secundae — on negligence and on the vices opposed to prudence by way of resemblance. Q.54: negligence is a special sin, “a lack of due solicitude” (a.1); it is opposed to prudence, regarding the act of command, where the negligent man “fails through lack of a prompt will” (a.2); and it can be a mortal sin when it omits something necessary for salvation (a.3). Q.55 treats the counterfeits of prudence — practical skill rightly structured but wrongly directed: “prudence of the flesh,” which makes carnal goods the last end of life (a.1), mortal when the whole life is so ordered (a.2); craftiness (astutia), the pursuit of an end by “false and counterfeit” means (a.3); guile, the execution of craftiness chiefly in words (a.4); fraud, its execution in deeds (a.5); undue solicitude about temporal things (a.6) and about the future, against which our Lord warns “be not solicitous for tomorrow” (a.7); all of which arise chiefly from covetousness, the disordered desire that lures the mind from its true end (a.8).*

## Notes

- “Prudence of the flesh” (Q.55 a.1) = fully competent practical optimization toward a worldly last end; competence is end-neutral, so what makes prudence virtuous is the END (charity) — closing the arc opened at Day 141 a.13.
- Craftiness/guile/fraud (a.3–5): the right end (or a good-seeming end) pursued by counterfeit means — corrupt optimization, the instrumentalizing mode untethered from the real good.
- Undue solicitude about tomorrow (a.6–7, Matt 6) = misweighted precision on temporal variables; “every time has its own solicitude” — don't forestall future uncertainty into the present.
- All from covetousness (a.8): the disordered prior that absolutizes a partial good — the negative image of detachment and loving unity.
