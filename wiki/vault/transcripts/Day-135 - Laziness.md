# Day 135 — Laziness

*Questions 34 and 35 of the Secunda Secundae — on hatred and on sloth, the disorders opposed to charity's love and charity's joy. Q.34: it is possible to hate God (a.1), not in his nature, which is goodness itself and cannot be hated, but under some mistaken notion of him, or as the authority who forbids one's sins and inflicts punishment — Augustine: "they love the light of truth when it shines on them; they hate it when it reproves them"; hatred of God is the greatest of sins (a.2), since even unbelief derives its sinfulness from it; hatred of one's neighbor is always a sin (a.3), for we owe love to what he holds from God, nature and grace, hating only the sin that is not from God; interiorly, hatred is the most grievous sin against a neighbor (a.4), being a disorder of the will, the root of sin, though outward injuries do more external harm; hatred is not a capital vice (a.5) but something last, "the completion of vice," a proof of an already-corrupted nature; and hatred arises from envy (a.6), sorrow at a neighbor's good. Q.35: sloth is a sin (a.1) — "a sluggishness of the mind which neglects to begin good," a sorrow for spiritual good; it is a special vice (a.2), the sorrow at the very divine good about which charity rejoices; it can be a mortal sin (a.3) when reason consents in detestation of the divine good; and it is a capital vice (a.4), with six daughters: despair, faint-heartedness, sluggishness about the commandments, spite, malice, and wandering after unlawful things.*

## Notes

- Q.34 a.1 — one cannot hate goodness-itself; God is hated only "under whatever notion he mistakes God to be," or as the authority who reproves. FLAG: hatred is rooted in perspective-limitation — the limited view directed against a misperceived whole (Hoffman, the distorted icon rather than the reality; Kastrup, the dissociated alter mistaking mind-at-large for a threat). The central theme inverted: hatred is what the limited perspective produces when it mistakes its partial view for the whole and defends itself against the real whole.
- Q.34 a.5 — hatred is NOT a cause of other vices but the last disorder, "the completion of vice," "a proof of an already-corrupted nature." Reframe: the final-impenitence limit again (Day 125; Day 130 demon-exclusion) — hatred-of-God is the perspective finally sealed against the whole, the terminus, not a stage. The same boundary the whole charity arc has circled.
- Q.34 a.6 — hatred arises from envy, sorrow at another's good. Reframe: envy is the zero-sum misperception of a non-zero-sum reality. In the loving whole the other's good is my good (Day 132 love of benevolence; Fredrickson PRS-06 non-zero-sum love); envy reads it as my loss — a generative model with the wrong sign on the coupling term (Friston). Perspective-limitation again: when you cannot see the whole, another's good looks like subtraction.
- Q.35 a.1-2 — sloth/acedia is "sorrow at the divine good about which charity rejoices" — the exact inverse of joy (Day 132 Q.28). Reframe: a collapse of the goal-prior; the agent that no longer finds the global attractor attractive and stops computing toward it because the toil registers as evil (a.1). Active-inference reading: a locally-optimal-but-globally-suboptimal Bayesian attractor that resists update (Friston PRS-14), metastability collapse (PRS-13) — the will stuck in a low-energy local minimum.
- Q.35 a.4 — sloth's daughters include despair (Day 128) and faint-heartedness: the giving-up cluster. FLAG: sloth is the meta-failure of the central theme's computation — abandoning the working-out-of-salvation because the perceived resource-cost is too high. Where Day 133 named the resource-bounded computation, sloth names its abandonment.
