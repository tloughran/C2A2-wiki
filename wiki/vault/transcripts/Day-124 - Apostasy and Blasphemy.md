# Day 124 — Apostasy & Blasphemy

*Questions 12 and 13 of the Secunda Secundae: apostasy and the sin of blasphemy in general. Apostasy (Greek apo-stasis, 'standing away from') is a complete turning from the faith and so pertains to unbelief; Aquinas also treats the medieval question of whether an apostate prince forfeits dominion. Blasphemy is the disparagement of the surpassing goodness of God, opposed to the confession of faith, a mortal sin by its genus; the question closes with whether the damned blaspheme.*

## Notes

- Q.12 a.1 — apostasy as the etymology suggests is *standing-away*: a withdrawal of the whole person (intellect and will) from the faith, illustrated by John 6:66 ('many of his disciples drew back'). The complete-turning vs partial-turning distinction matters for the synthesis's account of how a perspective can lose its orientation entirely vs locally.
- Q.12 a.2 — the apostate-prince/forfeiture-of-allegiance article is irreducibly medieval (excommunication dissolving political bonds). Like Q.11 a.3, flag as a locus where the contemporary parallel departs: authority and conscience are decoupled from ecclesial sanction.
- Q.13 a.1 — blasphemy is defined informationally: 'denying anything befitting God or affirming anything unbefitting him disparages the divine goodness'; from the Greek, literally 'to harm the reputation of.' It is a *lie about the supreme good* — a corruption of the model of the whole. Bridge to Hoffman/Kastrup: blasphemy as a maximally-wrong representation of what is fundamental.
- Q.13 a.4 — the damned blaspheme: even absent spoken words, the habitual will set on something-other-than-God is 'interior blasphemy of the heart,' a standing testimony that God is not the greatest good. This is the will-as-orientation thesis in its sharpest form — the object of the will *is* a proposition about the whole.
- Short episode (~1494 words). Austin notes it and the prior episode give 'a glimpse into that long-lost Christendom' — useful framing for why several articles here need explicit reframing rather than endorsement in the synthesis.
