# Day 134 — Required Almsgiving

*Questions 32 and 33 of the Secunda Secundae — a long double question on almsgiving and on fraternal correction, charity made concrete and sometimes corrective. Q.32: almsgiving is an act of charity when done formally for God's sake (a.1), distinguished from the same material act done to impress or out of hatred; the kinds of almsdeeds are the seven corporal and seven spiritual works of mercy (a.2), since man is a body-soul composite; spiritual alms are greater absolutely, though urgent bodily need comes first (a.3); corporeal alms have spiritual effects (a.4); almsgiving is a matter of precept, not mere counsel (a.5) — Matthew 25's sheep and goats, with St. Basil's warning that "it is the hungry man's bread that you withhold"; one need not give from true necessity except in extreme cases (a.6), nor from ill-gotten goods that must instead be restored (a.7), nor against a master's will (a.8); alms follow the order of charity, closest first but reordered by holiness, usefulness, and extreme need (a.9); and it is better to give sufficiently to many than abundantly to one (a.10). Q.33: fraternal correction is an act of charity (a.1), flowing from forbearance and aimed at a brother's amendment; it is a matter of precept when needed for that end (a.2); it belongs to all, not only prelates (a.3), and may even oblige a subject to correct a prelate, with gentleness and respect (a.4); a sinner may reprove a wrongdoer if he does so with humility (a.5) — Augustine: "groan with him and invite him to repent with us"; one should forbear when correction would only make the man worse (a.6); and private admonition must precede witnesses, and witnesses precede public denunciation (a.7-8), following Christ's escalation in Matthew 18.*
