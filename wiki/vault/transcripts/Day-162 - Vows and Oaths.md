## Notes
- A vow is a deliberated promise to God; an oath calls God to witness a present, past, or future fact. Both are acts of *latria* (religion).
- Vows must be about a *better good* — neither necessary nor impossible — and "fix the will immovably." Aquinas argues that vowed virtue is more meritorious than ad-hoc virtue because the vow consecrates the *power to act*, not just the act.
- Solemn vows (holy orders, religious profession) alter status irreversibly and (per Aquinas) cannot be dispensed even by the pope for perfect continence — though Habash notes contemporary canon law has loosened this for solemnly professed religious while keeping celibacy for the laicized cleric a separate question.
- Oaths are remedial — they exist *because* humans don't trust one another's bare word. Three conditions: justice, judgment, truth. A rash oath lacks judgment, a false oath lacks truth, an unlawful oath lacks justice.
- Declaratory oaths (past/present facts) cannot be dispensed because the matter is fixed; promissory oaths can be voided by change of circumstance.
- Synthesis hook: this is the *commitment-device* question. Why bind yourself in advance? Because a future self under different precision-weighting may not honor what the present self can see. Vows are the agent's tool against its own perspectival drift.
