# Day 136 — Envy

*Questions 36 and 37 of the Secunda Secundae — on envy and on discord, two disorders opposed to the peace and joy of charity. Q.36: envy is a kind of sorrow (a.1) — John Damascene's "sorrow for another's good," where another's good is apprehended as one's own evil insofar as it lessens one's own excellence or repute; Aquinas notes we envy not those far above us but those near us, our rivals; envy is a sin (a.2), a disordered sorrow at a neighbor's temporal good that proves a disordered love of temporal things — though sorrow at another's greater spiritual good, which spurs self-reform (zeal), is praiseworthy; it is mortal in its genus (a.3), the contrary movement to charity, which rejoices in the neighbor's good, though venial as a sudden passion; and it is a capital vice (a.4) with five daughters per Gregory the Great: hatred, tale-bearing, detraction, joy at others' misfortunes, and grief at their prosperity. Q.37: discord is a sin (a.1) when one knowingly departs from the divine and the neighbor's good (then mortal) — but the "sharp contention" of Paul and Barnabas (Acts 15) was accidental discord, each intending good, the divergence owing to human deficiency; and discord is a daughter of vainglory (a.2), the refusal to give up one's own will.*

## Notes

- Envy defined (John Damascene) as sorrow at another's good apprehended as one's own evil — it diminishes one's own repute/excellence. Reads cleanly as a zero-sum misperception of a non-zero-sum reality.
- Aquinas's nuance: we envy the *near* (rivals), not the far-above — envy is indexed to a comparison-class / reference frame, not to absolute goods.
- Zeal vs. envy: sorrow at another's *spiritual* superiority that drives self-reform is praiseworthy — same affect, reordered object. The disorder is in the ordering, not the comparison.
- Paul-Barnabas "accidental discord" (Acts 15): each intends good, the divergence is "owing to human deficiency," and there is no sin — a clean medieval statement of perspective-limitation producing non-culpable disagreement (central theme).
- Both envy and discord trace to defending the partial self (vainglory) against a good shared in common.
