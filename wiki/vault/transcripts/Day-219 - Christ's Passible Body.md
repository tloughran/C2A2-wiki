# Day 219 — Christ's Passible Body

> Episode: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RErVApXorzg>
> Summa reference: *Tertia Pars (III), Q.13-14*

Context: Day 219 covers III Q.13 (the power of Christ's soul — whether His created soul had omnipotence, omnipotence over the transmutation of creatures, and over His own body) and III Q.14 (the defects of body assumed by the Son of God — whether He ought to have assumed a body subject to suffering and death, whether He contracted these defects of necessity or assumed them freely, and whether He was subject to all bodily defects). Austin Habash insists omnipotence belongs only to the divine nature: Christ's created finite human soul, 'similar to our own,' is not omnipotent — and the bodily defects were assumed freely, for our salvation, not contracted by necessity.

## Notes

- Q.13 a.1: omnipotence — 'an active power over everything that can have the nature of being' — belongs to God alone; Christ's created human soul, finite and like ours, is not omnipotent. Finite power is constitutive of the assumed humanity.
- Q.13: whatever the soul of Christ did beyond created power, it did 'through the grace of His divinity,' not by the soul's own finite power — a clear two-natures division of labor.
- Q.14 a.1: it was *fitting* for the Son of God to assume a body 'capable of destruction and suffering' (Isaiah 53: 'with his stripes we are healed') — passibility freely taken on for our healing.
- Flag-worthy: freely assumed finite power and passible body = kenosis (Phil 2): the one who comprehends voluntarily takes on the limited, sufferable perspective — agency-under-resource-scarcity (Friston) chosen rather than merely suffered.
