## Notes
- **Who judges with Christ.** The apostles / those who "left all" (voluntary poverty) judge in a derived sense; strict judgment belongs to God alone. Poverty is named as the *disposition* fitting one to judge (clears bribe, opens the heart to God's law).
- **Conformity of the judge.** Judges-assessors must share human nature so as to be visible to all; hence angels do not judge in the strict sense, and demons merely *execute* the sentence — order preserved through the angelic mean.
- **The "right habitual end."** Article 7 (key): unbelievers' works are not all sinful (Trent), but lacking faith they lack the correct ultimate end (the triune God), so their works are not meritorious. Agency is evaluated by the *end it is ordered to*.
- **Discussed vs not discussed.** The perfected are not "discussed" (no inquiry of merits); those with an admixture of good and evil are. Two registers of judgment.
- **Christ judges as man; Godhead not seen without joy.** The wicked see the divine essence only veiled in Christ's humanity (else they would be beatified). "It is impossible to see the Godhead without joy" — truth-seeing is intrinsically joyful (Aristotle: "all naturally desire to know").
