## Notes

- **Incontinence pertains to the soul, not the body** (Q.156 a.1): it is reason's failure to resist a passion. Two modes — *impetuosity* (sudden yielding with no deliberation) and *weakness* (one deliberates, judges rightly, then is overcome when the moment comes). A clean two-failure-mode taxonomy for a control architecture.
- **Incontinence is less grave than intemperance** (a.3): intemperance is a settled disposition of the *will*, acquired by repeated choice (habit); incontinence is the will swayed by a passing passion. Gravity tracks how deeply the disorder is seated.
- **Incontinence in desire is worse than in anger** (a.4): anger 'works openly' and involves a quasi-judgment; concupiscence 'creeps in by stealth' and works with pleasure, so it is less rational and more shameful. Austin explicitly flags that Aquinas's body-heat argument 'may not hold up with contemporary biology.'
- **Clemency vs. meekness** (Q.157): meekness moderates the *passion* of anger in the irascible appetite; clemency moderates the *external act* — the severity of punishment. Both are annexed parts of temperance, both genuine virtues, neither the greatest.
- Synthesis hook: meekness as alignment of the irascible appetite (vs. mere override) extends the continence-to-temperance distinction from Day 195 into the domain of anger and vengeance.
