# Day 144 — Gifted Counsel

*Questions 52 and 53 of the Secunda Secundae — on the gift of counsel and on imprudence. Q.52: counsel is reckoned among the gifts of the Holy Spirit (a.1), for “human reason is unable to grasp the singular and contingent things which may occur,” so that “the thoughts of mortal men are fearful and our counsels uncertain” (Wisdom 9); hence man needs to be directed by God, “who comprehends all things,” and is “counseled by God” through this gift; it corresponds to the virtue of prudence (a.2), perfecting deliberation, which prudence consummates in command; it remains even in heaven, in the blessed and the angels who “take counsel of God about things beneath them” (a.3); and it answers to the beatitude of mercy (a.4). Q.53, imprudence: a sin in so far as it infringes the rules of right reason — mortal when through contempt of the divine law, venial otherwise (a.1); a special sin opposed to prudence (a.2); with species answering to the acts prudence governs — precipitation/rashness, opposed to good counsel (a.3); thoughtlessness, opposed to right judgment (a.4); and inconstancy, opposed to command (a.5) — all of which arise chiefly from lust, which “corrupts the estimation of prudence” (a.6).*

## Notes

- FLAGSHIP for the central theme: “human reason is unable to grasp the singular and contingent things… the thoughts of mortal men are fearful and our counsels uncertain” (Wisdom 9) → man “directed as though counseled by God who comprehends all things.” The central claim almost verbatim: compute under radical perspective-limitation; be guided by one who perceives the whole.
- Gift of counsel perfects deliberation; prudence consummates in command (a.2): grace cooperating with, not replacing, the natural act.
- Imprudence's species map onto the three acts: precipitation (no policy search), thoughtlessness (mis-evaluation), inconstancy/negligence (failure to execute) — the failure points of bounded-rational action.
- John of the Cross / detachment (a.6): lowering precision on sensory reward to receive “the Spirit of God in pure transformation” — reorientation toward the whole.
