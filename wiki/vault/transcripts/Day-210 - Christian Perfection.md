# Day 210 — Christian Perfection

Context: Day 210 covers II-II Q.184 (the state of perfection in general) and Q.185 (things pertaining to the episcopal state). Austin Habash lays out Aquinas's account of Christian perfection as consisting chiefly in charity ('a thing is perfect insofar as it attains its proper end,' and man's end is the love of God); the sense in which perfection is possible in this life (the entirety of one's affections given to God, though imperfectly because 'distracted'); the relation of precepts to the evangelical counsels (poverty, celibacy, obedience as means to protect and grow charity); and the crucial distinction between the ESSENCE of perfection (perfect charity) and the STATE of perfection (religious or episcopal). Q.185 then treats the episcopal office in eight articles.

## Notes

- Q.184 a.1: Christian perfection consists chiefly in CHARITY — 'a thing is perfect insofar as it attains its proper end,' and man's end is to love God. Perfection IS loving union; this is the project's telos stated almost verbatim by Aquinas.
- Q.184 a.2: perfection of charity from lover and beloved. We cannot love God as much as He deserves (infinite/whole), but can give the entirety of our affections; in this life only imperfectly because we are 'distracted' and love becomes 'unfocused, variable' — perfected only in heaven. Available now: remove from the affections whatever is contrary to charity AND whatever hinders the mind 'from tending wholly to God' — 'seeing God in all things and loving God in all things.' Resource-limitation / bounded-attention hook + strong Rohr (panentheist seeing).
- Q.184 a.3: precepts (love God + neighbor, knit together — 'if anyone says I love God and hates his brother, he is a liar,' 1 John) vs evangelical counsels (poverty, celibacy, obedience from the rich young ruler) as means to protect and grow charity.
- Q.184 a.4: ESSENCE of perfection (perfect charity) vs STATE of perfection (binding oneself in perpetuity, solemnly, to the means). 'Nothing hinders some from being perfect without being in the state of perfection, and some in the state of perfection without being perfect' (two sons, Matt 21). Central correction restated: the loving union, not the role, is what perfection is.
- Q.184 a.5-8: bishops as 'perfectors,' religious as 'perfected' (Dionysius); both in the state of perfection in different senses; religious life a 'safer means'; the law of the Holy Spirit (a 'private law') leads only to something more perfect.
- Q.185: the episcopal office — lawful to desire (1 Tim 3), not to refuse absolutely once validly called, ought to be 'best for governing,' must not abandon the flock while useful, and 'the religious state tends to perfection as a way; the episcopal state pertains to perfection as a professorship of perfection.' Office-of-perfection vs state-of-perfection nuance.
