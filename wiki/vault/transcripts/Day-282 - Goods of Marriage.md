[00:05] Hello, my name is Austin Habash, the founder of Think Catholic, and you're listening to the Summa in a Year podcast, where we study St. Thomas Aquinas's Summa

[00:39] translation that always used in these podcasts is the one generously provided to me by a Maria Press. The five volume set translated by the fathers of the

English Dominican province which you can now get at a discounted price from a Maria Press's site by typing in the code Summa 10. And so here we go. Article one,

whether certain blessings are necessary in order to excuse marriage. What Aquinas is getting at here as he says is for a thing that has a loss attached to

[01:08] it to be eligible or choosable, it needs to have some good connected with it. So when a man gets married, he will lose according to scripture at the very least

an ease of focus on God. As St. Paul says, I want you to be free from anxieties. The unmarried man is anxious about the affairs of the Lord. how to

please the Lord. But the married man is anxious about worldly affairs, how to please his wife, and his interests are divided. And therefore, one would not

choose such a life. St. Thomas is saying, unless there were other goods which come from marriage that would excuse, as the article puts it, the

[01:41] choice to get married. Therefore, in the following articles, we are going to enumerate those goods which are attendant upon marriage and make such a

state of life not contrary to reason. Article two, whether the goods of marriage are sufficiently enumerated. The goods which come through marriage

are the following. The first, the good of offspring, to which marriage is directed both as an office of nature and as a sacrament. Second, the good of

fidelity between the spouses, that for justice's sake, each limit the marital act to one another. Third, there is also the goodness of the sacrament. Not only

[02:12] is grace given to the couple through the sacrament, but it is also a visible symbol of that invisible union of Christ with his body, the church. Article

three, whether the sacrament is the chief of the marriage goods. Looking at the goods of offspring, fidelity, and sacrament. Since the first two,

offspring and fidelity, concern marriage, both as merely an office of nature as well as found in the new law as a sign, these goods are more

essential to marriage, that marriage be directed towards offspring, and that spouses are not unfaithful to one another. However, if what we mean by

[02:45] chief in our question is greatest, then the sacramental good which has been instituted by God, this indissolvable bond due to it being a sign of Christ in

the church along with the sanctifying grace imparted through the sacrament. This is chief considered in this way, the sacrament is the chief of the

marriage goods. Article four, whether the marriage act is excused by the afor goods. Do the goods of marriage make the marriage act excusable? And this is

[03:13] exactly what the goods of the sacrament fidelity as ordered to offspring do. To quote St. Thomas, now a human act is said to be good in two ways. In one way,

by goodness of virtue, and thus an act derives its goodness from those things which place it in the mean. This is what faith and offspring do in the marriage

act. And another way by goodness of the sacrament. In which way an act is said to be not only good but also holy. And the marriage act derives this goodness

from the indissolvability of the union in respect of which it signifies the union of Christ with the church. Article five. Whether the marriage act can be

[03:49] excused without the marriage goods. the marital embrace. Sexual intercourse is virtuous in so far as it is ordered to the honest good which as we have noted

in previous episodes is either the good of offspring or in rendering the marriage debt to the other. Since in marriage the spouses become one. They

give over privileges of their body to the other. Thus, if the marital embrace is ordered towards children for the good of the species, the family, the state,

[04:19] the kingdom of God, or ordered towards justice, rendering the marriage debt, which pertains to fatality, then the marital act is excused. Using again

aquinus's word here, not my word. However, lust is not a sufficient reason for the marital act as St. Thomas puts it. Consequently, there are only two

ways in which married persons can come together without any sin at all. namely in order to have offspring and in order to pay the debt. Otherwise, it is always

at least a venial sin. Article six, whether it is a mortal sin for a man to have knowledge, that's sexual intercourse, of his wife with the

[04:56] intention not of a marriage good, but merely of pleasure. Does a man cast himself into hell by having sexual intercourse with his wife just for the

pleasure of it, not for the honest good of offspring or rendering the marriage debt? St. Thomas says such would be only a venial sin normally as we've already

mentioned because although it is an act of moral weakness like eating food just for the pleasure of it yet it is not an act directly contrary to the goods of

marriage like abortion or adultery would be. However, the marital embrace for the sake of the pleasure of it even with one's wife can be a mortal sin if a man

[05:34] do so simply because his wife is an available woman. because then in his heart there would be no difference between fornication and the marital

embrace as far as his intention is concerned. Now I think that's what Aquinas is saying here. But to allow the listener to judge for themselves here

are Aquinas's exact words. The right answer to this question is that if pleasure be sought in such a way as to exclude the honesty of marriage, so that

[06:02] to it it is not as a wife, but as a woman that a man treats his wife, and that he is ready to use her in the same way if she were not his wife, it is a

mortal sin. Wherefore such a man is said to be too ardent a lover of his wife, because his artor carries him away from the goods of marriage. If however he

seek pleasure within the bonds of marriage so that it would not be sought in another than in his wife, it is a venial sin. We move then on to question

[06:32] 50 of the impediments of marriage in general. Article one, whether it is fitting that impediment should be assigned to marriage. We've already

spoken about how having certain defects of consent would be an impediment to marriage. Here we get a more extensive list. There are first of all certain

impediments which make a marriage illicit but not invalid. In other words, that it would be wrong to do, but if done, the marriage is still validly

conferred. That would be what's called here veto of the church or holy tide, which I imagine would mean if the parish priest decided not to conduct the

[07:04] marriage for some reason, ruled against it, and then the couple simply went to another priest in another territory to have that marriage celebrated instead.

Holy tide would be if there is a lurggical season in which marriages are not to occur. In Aquinas's day, that might have been Lent or Advent. Next, we

have things which would simply invalidate the current marriage. First is error, thinking you are marrying Jane, and it's actually Suzie. Force,

being forced to marry Jane, or else be killed by her family. Vow, trying to marry Jane while already being a solemn professed Benedict and monk. Holy

[07:39] orders, trying to marry Jane but already an ordained priest. Impotence. Trying to marry Jane but being a unic physically. Bond of marriage. Trying to marry Jane

but already being married to Susie. Difference of worship. Trying to marry Jane the Baptist without dispensation from your parish priest. And his canon

law states explicitly on that point which I think is worth quoting. This is canon 11:24. Marriage between two baptized persons,

one of whom was baptized in the Catholic Church or received into it after baptism, and the other a member of a church or ecclesiastical community not

[08:13] in full communion with the Catholic Church, cannot be celebrated without the express permission of the competent authority. Back to our list, kinship.

So, it would be invalid to try and marry Jane, your sister or mother. affinity trying to marry Jane who was the daughter of your ex-wife though not your
