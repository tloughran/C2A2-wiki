## Notes

- Q.45 a.1 (Bede): "by his loving foresight he allowed them to taste for a short time the contemplation of eternal glory, so that they might bear persecution bravely" — the transfiguration is readiness-formation for the passion, given to the few who will steady the many.
- Q.45 a.4: baptism and transfiguration are paired Trinity-manifestations — first and second regeneration; the Father's voice marks both the beginning of adopted sonship and its promised glory.
- Q.46 a.1-2: necessity on supposition only — God could have delivered mankind otherwise; given his foreknowledge and ordinance, the passion is necessary. The modality is conditional, not absolute.
- Q.46 a.3: why MOST suitable — five concurring goods, the first being the hinge: "man knows thereby how much God loves him, and is thereby stirred to love him in return; and herein lies the perfection of human salvation." Deliverance works through freely returned love, not over it.
- Q.46 a.5-6: generic completeness (all kinds of people, all faculties, all senses) and supreme magnitude — pain "accepted voluntarily... proportionate to the magnitude of the fruit."
- Q.46 a.7-8: the layered soul — the lower faculties suffered while "the higher part of his soul enjoyed fruition perfectly while Christ was suffering"; vision and agony simultaneous in one person.
- Flag-worthy: a.3's stirred-to-love-in-return is the strongest hook yet for love-as-union (Stump, CROSS-005) and resonance-requiring-free-response (Fredrickson); necessity-on-supposition reads as optimality-under-constraint; the fruition/suffering simultaneity is a layered-perspectives structure begging the dissociation/nested-agents treatment.
