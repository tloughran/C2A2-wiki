## Notes
- Q.98 (Perjury): false oath. The form of an oath calls God as witness; falsehood under oath turns God into a witness of one's lie. Three integrity conditions for any oath: truth (the matter is true), justice (one swears to lawful things), judgment (deliberation).
- Promissory perjury: swearing to do something one does not intend, or failing to keep what one truly swore. The breach is not only the broken promise but the contempt of God-as-witness.
- Q.99 (Sacrilege): violation of any sacred thing — sacred person, sacred place, sacred object. Aquinas treats sacrilege as one virtue's worth of corruption (irreligion) with many species depending on what is violated.
- Aquinas distinguishes sacrilege against persons (clergy, consecrated), places (church desecration, asylum violation), and things (Eucharist, vessels, sacraments). Each carries different gravity scaled to the rank of the sacred thing.
- Synthesis hook: an oath is a costly signal of commitment in a community where deception is normally inexpensive; sacrilege is a violation of the symbolic structures by which a tradition encodes its highest commitments. Both can be reread under Wright's covenantal-faithfulness frame: the violation is not first of a rule, but of a community-constituting relation.
