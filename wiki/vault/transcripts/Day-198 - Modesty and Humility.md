## Notes

- **Modesty** (Q.160) is an annexed part of temperance moderating concupiscence in 'mundane general' outward matters — dress, bearing, actions — as liberality is to magnificence so modesty is to temperance.
- **Humility restrains the impulse toward excellence/honor** beyond what reason permits (Q.161 a.1-2). Its subject is the irascible appetite, especially the passion of *hope* (desire for the arduous good). It is paired with magnanimity, which *strengthens* against timidity — two virtues guarding the same good from opposite failures.
- **Subjection to all** (a.3): per Phil 2:3 and Augustine, esteem others as better — because another may possess a good *hidden from me* whereby he is better, even if my own apparent good is not hidden. An explicitly epistemic humility about the unseen goods in others.
- **Humility ranks just below the theological and intellectual virtues and justice** (a.5): it makes a man 'a good subject to rule... in all matters,' whereas other virtues operate in some special matter. Christ's self-emptying (Phil 2:6-8) is the exemplar; Benedict's 12 degrees give the practical ladder.
- Synthesis hook: humility = right-sizing the self under acknowledged perspective-limitation; 'another may have a good hidden to me' is nearly the project's central-theme epistemics in Aquinas's own voice.
