# Day 123 — Unbelievers

*Questions 10 and 11 of the Secunda Secundae: unbelief in general and heresy. Aquinas treats unbelief as a sin seated in the intellect (with the will as first mover), the greatest of sins in its genus, yet not so total as to make every act of an unbeliever sinful — natural reason survives and unbelievers can do generically good deeds. He then treats compulsion (faith cannot be coerced), toleration, dominion, and the baptism of children, and finally heresy as a species of unbelief.*

## Notes

- Q.10 a.1 — unbelief-as-sin is the *refusal to hear or accept* the gospel, not merely the privative state of lacking faith. Justice is invoked: if God reveals, the creature owes assent. Frames belief as a debt within an asymmetric epistemic relation (one who sees more / one who sees less) — central-theme seam.
- Q.10 a.4 + Council of Trent — unbelievers retain natural reason and can do 'generically good' deeds; Trent anathematizes the claim that all pre-justification works are sins. Important guardrail: the synthesis must NOT read Aquinas as denying natural goodness to those outside the faith. Pairs with Levin/Friston (agency and its goods exist at every scale, graced or not).
- Q.10 a.5 — the Dionysius/Chesterton principle: 'good happens in one way but evil in many ways… there are an infinity of angles at which one falls, only one at which one stands.' This is an error-geometry / single-target picture — strong bridge to Levin's morphogenetic target-state and Friston's free-energy minimum (one attractor, many ways to diverge).
- Q.10 a.8 — faith cannot be coerced: intellect and will are immaterial and cannot be determined from outside except by their proper object (truth/goodness itself). Coercion can only prevent hindrance, not produce belief. This non-coercibility of assent is the perspectival-freedom core the synthesis should foreground.
- Q.11 a.3 — the hard passage: Aquinas holds heretics, after due admonition, may be handed to the secular arm 'to be exterminated… by death.' Austin contextualizes it to a Christendom that no longer exists. The synthesis must name this honestly as a place where the contemporary parallel decisively departs from Aquinas (non-coercion, dignity of conscience), not soften it.
