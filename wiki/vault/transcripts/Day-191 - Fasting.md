## Notes

- Abstinence (Q.146) = moderation of food and drink under the command of reason; a special virtue because its object (the pleasure of taste) is particular, and ranked below temperance proper, which governs the greater pleasures of touch.
- Fasting (Q.147 a.1) is virtuous because reason directs it to three goods: bridling concupiscence (it 'bestows chastity'), freeing the mind to consider the things of God, and atoning/satisfying for past sin.
- 'Fasts are made for man's sake, not man for the fasts' (a.4): right reason can suspend the precept for health (Austin's chemotherapy example). The rule is reason-governed, not rote — dispensation logic is built in.
- Concrete calibration: one meal a day (a.6), at the ninth hour / 3pm (a.7), abstaining from the most delectable and costly foods — meat, eggs, milk (a.8). The discipline is aimed precisely where appetite pulls hardest.
- Science/central-theme hooks: self-regulation as resource-allocation under appetite — Friston active inference (interoception, allostasis, deliberate prediction-error); 'freeing the mind' = lowering metabolic/attentional load so higher-order modeling of the whole can run. Strong Friston+Fredrickson (vagal self-regulation) bridge.
