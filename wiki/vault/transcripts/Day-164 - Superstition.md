## Notes
- Superstition is a vice against religion *by excess* — not in *amount* of worship, but in *direction* (worshipping what shouldn't be worshipped) or *manner* (worshipping rightly worshipped objects wrongly).
- Three species: (1) idolatry (false god), (2) divination (wrong source of supernatural information), (3) "observances" (charms, amulets, knock-on-wood — false causal model dressed in religious clothing).
- Q.93: even worship of the *true* God can be perniciously wrong — Aquinas's example is post-Christ circumcision, which exteriorly says the Messiah has not come. The example dates Aquinas, but the structural point is sharp: ritual *signifies*, and signifying a falsehood is a real sin against faith.
- Aquinas distinguishes excess in *love* of God (impossible) from excess in *proportion* — proportion is the measurable thing.
- Synthesis hook: the agent's *model* of the cosmos shows in its ritual. Superstition is bad ontology made visible. The fix isn't piety but reality-tracking.
