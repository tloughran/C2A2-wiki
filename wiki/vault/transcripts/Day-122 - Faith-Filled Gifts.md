# Day 122 — Faith-Filled Gifts

*Questions 8 and 9 of the Secunda Secundae: the gift of understanding and the gift of knowledge, the two intellectual gifts of the Holy Spirit that correspond to the virtue of faith. Aquinas distinguishes natural understanding (grasp of self-evident first principles) from the supernatural gift; clarifies that the gift grasps not God's essence but that the articles of faith are not self-contradictory; and distinguishes understanding (perception), wisdom (judgment by divine ideas), and knowledge (judgment by human/created ideas).*

## Notes

- Q.8 a.2 — the gift of understanding does NOT comprehend God's essence; it grasps that the articles 'do not contradict the truth of things,' i.e. that the appearances don't defeat the faith. This is an interface/coherence move, not a face-to-face seeing — direct bridge to Hoffman (the finite agent never sees the thing-in-itself, only that the interface is non-contradictory).
- Q.8 distinguishes the intellectual gifts (understanding, wisdom, knowledge, counsel) from the appetitive (might, fear, piety) via Isaiah 11. Understanding = perception/apprehension; wisdom = judgment per divine ideas; knowledge = judgment per created ideas. The perception/judgment split is the load-bearing structure for the synthesis.
- Q.9 a.2 — the 'medium' principle: every cognitive habit regards formally the medium through which a thing is known and materially the thing known. God is known in philosophy through the natural light, in theology through the supernatural light of faith — same object, different medium, different science. Maps cleanly onto Hoffman's interface (the medium constitutes the science) and onto MacIntyre's tradition-constituted rationality.
- Q.9 a.4 — gift of knowledge corresponds to the beatitude 'blessed are they that mourn': knowledge 'befits the mourner who has discovered that he has been mastered by evil which he coveted as though it were good.' Knowledge of created things reveals their power to ensnare — an epistemics-of-disillusionment that pairs with McGilchrist (the left-hemisphere grasp that mistakes the map for the territory).
- Q.8 a.4-5 — the gift is present in all who have sanctifying grace (sufficient for salvation) and absent in those without it; understanding 'implies a certain perception of the truth which is in none except one who has sanctifying grace.' Note the tie of right cognition to rightly-ordered will (charity) — cognition is not perspective-neutral.
