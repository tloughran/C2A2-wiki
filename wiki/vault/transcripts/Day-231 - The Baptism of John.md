## Notes

- Q.37: the seven fitting reasons for Christ's circumcision all turn on *solidarity under the law* — taking on the burden of the fallen race's remedies "that he might free those who lived under that burden"; the name Jesus = Savior signifies the gratuitous gift through which "all men might be saved."
- Q.38 a.1: John's baptism is *preparation* — drawing crowds, accustoming them to the practice, then pointing them beyond himself ("Behold the lamb of God"); the forerunner's whole office is to direct attention away from himself.
- Q.38 a.3: John's baptism did not impart grace — he baptized with water so that another would baptize with the Holy Spirit; the closer old-covenant parallel to sacramental baptism is circumcision (Bede: it brought "the same saving aid"), and faith at all times saves even when its specificity differs.
- Q.38 a.5: "He must increase and I must decrease" (John 3:30) — John continues baptizing to avoid jealousy among his disciples and to keep gathering people *so as to hand them over* to Christ.
- Flag-worthy: preparation-then-handoff is a pure case of the central theme — a limited perspective (John: "I am not the Christ") that knows its own limitation, installs readiness in others, and cedes; readiness-formation reads naturally as prior-updating (Friston), and the hinge-of-the-ages structure as Wright's inaugurated eschatology.
