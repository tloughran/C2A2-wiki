## Notes

- **Pride = the inordinate desire of one's own excellence** (Q.162 a.1-2), opposed to humility (it scorns subjection) and to magnanimity (it tends to great things inordinately). A special sin by its object yet the cause of all sins, since any good can be sought to inflate one's excellence.
- **Pride is the first sin and most grievous by genus** (a.6-7): the formal essence of sin is *turning away from God*, and pride just is the will's refusal to be subject to God's rule. Gregory's four species: boasting of what one lacks; thinking one has from oneself what is from God; thinking a received good is due to one's own merits; despising others and wishing to be singularly conspicuous.
- **Subject of pride** (a.3): in embodied beings it engages the irascible passion of hope *and* the will; but it can be wholly in the will — hence pride is ascribed to the demons, who have no bodily passions. Pride is thus not tied to embodiment.
- **The first man's sin was pride** (Q.163): not gluttony or curiosity for the fruit, but the serpent's real lure — 'you will be like God,' coveting to decide good and evil by his own power, to 'rely on himself in contempt of the order of the divine rule.' Both devil and man 'wished to rely on himself.'
- Synthesis hook: pride is the perspective-limited agent *absolutizing* its own perspective and refusing guidance from those who see more — the precise inversion of the project's central claim.
