# Day 220 — Communication of Idioms

> Episode: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LzWzZZdPXuE>
> Summa reference: *Tertia Pars (III), Q.15-16*

Context: Day 220 covers III Q.15 (the defects of soul assumed by Christ — whether there was sin, the 'fomes' of sin, ignorance, sensible pain, sorrow, fear, wonder, and anger in Christ) and III Q.16 (those things which are applicable to Christ in His being and becoming — the 'communication of idioms,' i.e. which predicates true of one nature may be said of the one person, and the paradox that Christ was at once a 'wayfarer' and a 'comprehensor'). Austin Habash moves through some 22 articles, defining a wayfarer as one tending toward beatitude and a comprehensor as one already possessing it — and Christ, beholding the divine essence from conception yet still subject to earthly evils, is truly both at once.

## Notes

- Q.15: Christ assumed defects of *soul* (genuine sorrow, fear, pain, anger as righteous passions) but NOT sin or the 'fomes' (the disordered inclination); the limitation is real suffering without disorder.
- Q.16: the 'communication of idioms' — because the two natures subsist in one person, what is true of either nature may be predicated of the one Christ ('God suffered,' 'this man created'); a grammar for one person bearing two registers.
- Q.16: Christ as 'wayfarer and comprehensor' at once — He beheld the beatific vision from conception (comprehensor) yet was still on earth, subject to its evils, journeying toward glory (wayfarer).
- Flag-worthy: wayfarer-AND-comprehensor is the central-theme bridge figure — the one who already sees the whole while inhabiting the limited, suffering perspective; the divine guide of Phil 2:12 who 'perceives more of the whole than we do' yet walks the road with us.
