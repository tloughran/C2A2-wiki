## Notes
- Q.104 (Obedience): a special virtue distinct from justice — the prompt compliance of will with the precept of a superior, when the superior commands within his proper jurisdiction.
- Three grades: sufficient (what is commanded), perfect (counsels also), indiscreet (everything without judgment). Aquinas commends the second, not the third. Obedience is never owed against God or against natural justice.
- Aquinas's classic case: should the religious obey his superior in matters of soul and body? Yes in body and act; no in matters that exceed the superior's commission (forbidden goods, demanded sins).
- Q.105 (Disobedience): the corresponding vice. Distinguished from mere non-compliance; disobedience is *contempt of the precept as such*, treating the commanding authority as if it had none.
- Synthesis hook: obedience under Aquinas is bounded delegation: the inferior cedes execution but not ultimate judgment. This is the deep structure of any well-aligned multi-agent system — McGilchrist's emissary that knows it is emissary, Friston's hierarchical Bayesian model where higher levels constrain but do not replace lower-level inference, Wright's pattern of covenanted obedience to YHWH that does not abolish the human subject.
