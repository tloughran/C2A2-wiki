## Notes

- Two opposed vices: insensibility (rejecting even necessary pleasures, against nature) and intemperance (excess) (Q.142 a.1). The mean is bracketed by both defect and excess — a control-theoretic structure.
- Intemperance is a *childish* sin (a.2): unruly appetite reaches for petty things, and 'lust served became a custom, custom not resisted became a necessity' (Augustine, Confessions). A vivid account of maladaptive habit-formation / runaway precision on appetitive priors.
- Intemperance is MORE blameworthy than cowardice (a.3): more voluntary (no overpowering fear of death excuses it), more specific in object, and easier to train against since temptation is daily. A claim about culpability vs. resource-pressure worth flagging.
- Intemperance is the most disgraceful sin (a.4) because it 'dims the light of reason from which all the clarity and beauty of virtue arises.' Sets up Day 190's onestum/beauty material.
- Q.143 lays out the three-part architecture of temperance: integral parts (shamefacedness, onestum), subjective parts/species (abstinence, sobriety, chastity, purity), and annexed parts (continence, humility, meekness, modesty, etc.). A taxonomy the synthesis can mine for the self-regulation cluster.
