# Day 146 — Right and Just

*Questions 56 and 57 of the Secunda Secundae — closing the treatise on prudence and opening the great treatise on justice. Q.56 asks why the Decalogue contains no positive precept of prudence: right reason about the means is too variable from person to person and circumstance to circumstance to be legislated, though the Old Law does forbid the vices opposed to prudence (craftiness and the like). Q.57 opens the treatise on justice with its object — ius, the right: justice is “the stable quality in a man’s will which disposes him to will what is right” (a.1), and uniquely among the virtues its object is settled once equality is found in the act. Right divides into natural right (equality fixed by the nature of the thing) and positive right (fixed by private or public agreement) (a.2); the right of nations is distinguished from natural and from civil right (a.3); and paternal right and the right of dominion are special species, since son and slave are in a sense extensions of father and master (a.4).*

## Notes

- The object of justice is *ius* (the right) — uniquely external, and “settled once equality is found in the act,” needing no further inquiry into how/why (Q.57 a.1). This externality contrasts sharply with the inwardness of the prudence treatise just closed.
- Natural vs positive right (a.2): equality “determined by the nature of the thing” vs equality “altered by positive law.” A bridge to the natural-law / conventional-law seam under naturalist metaethics.
- The host’s three-branches-of-government illustration (executive / judicial / legislative ↔ natural right / right of nations / civil right) is drawn from Crean & Fimister, *Integralism* — a contemporary interpretive overlay, not Aquinas’s own.
- Aquinas’s truth-parallel (a.1): justice and truth are both “a kind of equality” / conformity — justice is conformity of act to the right, truth is conformity of thought to the world. Equality-as-conformity links metaethics to the realist theory of truth.
- a.4 (“no justice of father to son or master to slave”) rests on the part–whole / extension framing that recurs across the whole Justice treatise — the part is not strictly ‘other’ to the whole it belongs to.
