Context: Day 214 covers III Q.3 (the mode of union on the part of the Person assuming — whether it befits a divine Person to assume, whether the divine nature assumes, whether the nature abstracted from personality can assume, whether one Person can assume without the others, whether each Person could have assumed, whether several Persons could assume one nature, whether one Person could assume two natures, and the fittingness that the Son rather than the Father or Spirit should assume) and III Q.4 (the mode of union on the part of the human nature — its assumability, whether a person was assumed, whether 'a man' was assumed, abstraction from individuals, assumption in all individuals, and assumption of the stock of Adam). Austin Habash leans on Garrigou-Lagrange throughout for the harder articles.

## Notes
- Q.3 a.8 — why the SON fits the assuming: the Son is the eternal Logos/Word/'concept' by whom all was made, so the 'word of the craftsman' comes to restore man to his idea in the mind of God; fittingness via adoptive sonship; and the redemption of the sin of seeking knowledge (Eden) by the Word of true knowledge. Strong bridge to Levin's target-form/idea and to perspective-restoration.
- Q.4 a.2-3: the Word assumed a human NATURE, not a human PERSON or 'a man' — 'the divine person absorbed the [human] person inasmuch as the union hindered the human nature from having its own distinct personality.' Central image: the individuating boundary (personhood) supplied by the divine, not duplicated.
- Q.4 a.4-5: not human nature 'abstracted from individuals' (that would be 'a false and fictitious incarnation') and not in all individuals (it would thwart each human's own personality and the dignity of the one Incarnate Word). Particularity is essential — the universal saves by becoming a particular.
- Q.4 a.6: assumption of the stock of Adam — born of the fallen race (yet without original sin) to make satisfaction as a member of the race, for the glory of the race, and 'to make of our race a comeback story.'
- Speculative articles (a.5-7: could the Father/Spirit have assumed? could three Persons assume one nature? could one Person assume two natures?) are answered affirmatively as possibilities grounded in omnipotence and the person-has-nature principle; the host flags these as especially hard.
