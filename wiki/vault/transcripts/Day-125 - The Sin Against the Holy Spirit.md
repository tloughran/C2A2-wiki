# Day 125 — The Sin Against the Holy Spirit

*Questions 14 and 15 of the Secunda Secundae: blasphemy against the Holy Spirit, and the vices opposed to knowledge and understanding. Aquinas treats the unforgivable sin — read by Augustine and the Church as final impenitence, the deliberate refusal of the very mercy by which sins are forgiven — and Peter Lombard's sixfold list (despair, presumption, impenitence, obstinacy, resisting known truth, envy of a brother's grace). Q.15 then treats blindness of mind and dullness of sense as the vices opposing the gifts of understanding and knowledge.*

## Notes

- NOTE ON LOCUS: Austin twice says 'questions 12 and 13,' but Q.12-13 (apostasy/blasphemy) were Day 124. The content here — 'blasphemy against the Holy Ghost' and 'the vices opposed to knowledge and understanding' — is unmistakably II-II Q.14 and Q.15. Recorded summa_ref as II-II Q.14-15; the day-number narration error does not affect content.
- Q.14 a.1 — the sin against the Holy Spirit = sin 'through certain malice': rejecting or removing the very things that would prevent the choice of evil (hope removed by despair, fear by presumption). Since those restraints are effects of the Spirit, attacking them attacks their font. This is self-undermining at the level of the error-correcting mechanism itself — a striking bridge to Friston (a system that disables its own prediction-error correction) and to the central theme (refusing guidance from what perceives more).
- Q.14 a.3 — unforgivable = final impenitence: not a magnitude of offense God cannot match (CCC 1864: 'there are no limits to the mercy of God') but a standing refusal to *receive* mercy. The unforgivability is structural, on the creature's side — the door locked from the inside. Important for the synthesis's non-coercion thread.
- Q.15 a.1-2 — blindness of mind (loss of the supernatural light, culpably) and dullness of sense (weakness/sluggishness toward spiritual goods) are distinguished as total-loss vs degraded-acuity. Maps onto a signal/precision account (Friston: lost channel vs low-precision channel; Hawkins: failure of the cortical model to form vs to sharpen).
- Q.15 a.3 — Gregory's etiology: blindness of mind from lust, dullness of sense from gluttony; abstinence and chastity 'dispose man to the perfection of intellectual operation' (Daniel: God gave the abstinent children knowledge and understanding). An embodied-cognition claim — appetite shapes what the intellect can perceive — that pairs with McGilchrist (attention shaped by mode of engagement) and Fredrickson (affective state gates cognitive scope).
