Article four, whether holy men who are not priests have the keys. The answer is no. The power of keys

[04:11] comes by ordination within one's proper jurisdiction. and it can be exercised. It comes through conformity to Christ via the sacrament of holy orders. It is not about personal holiness. As St. Thomas puts it briefly, action belongs to that which has the power. But the key which is a spiritual power belongs to priests alone. Therefore, priests alone are competent to have the use of the keys. Article five, whether wicked priests have the use of the keys. Beginning with an argument from practicalities or practical necessity, Aquinas says, "No man could know whether

[04:43] another man is in the state of grace. If therefore no one could use the keys in giving absolution unless he were in a state of grace, no one would know that he had been absolved, which would be very unfitting." And thus we see that just as personal holiness does not make for the power of the keys, so too does personal unholiness not invalidate the power of the keys. Article six, whether those who are schismatics, heretics, excommunicate, suspended or degraded have the use of the keys. The keys are

[05:13] given to the church through Peter. That means down the hierarchy of the church, specifically the hierarchy of the ministers. And this on account of the jeritical nature of the keys. For one cannot bind and loose a man who is not under his jurisdiction. And when the church declares a man schismatic or heretic, excommunicate, suspended, or degraded, that man now no longer has jurisdiction or authority over others, and thus loses the capacity to bind and loose others as a minister of the church. Therefore, although a man's

[05:44] ordination is not invalidated or the mark of ordination erased by being excommunicated or degraded to the lay state, his capacity to bind and loose the faithful and the sacrament is suspended. In St. Thomas's words, since the use of the keys requires in the user authority over the person on whom they are used, the proper matter on whom can exercise the use of the keys is a man under one's authority. And since it is by appointment of the church that one man has authority over another, so a man may be deprived of his authority over

[06:15] another by his ecclesiastical superiors. ## Notes

- Q.19: the minister of the keys — ordination confers the power of order; jurisdiction is required for its lawful and valid exercise over a given subject.
- Q.20: those on whom the power of the keys can be exercised — only the baptized, only one's own subjects, only the living; the dead and non-subjects fall outside the priest's binding/loosing.
- The boundary of the agent's authority is set by membership and subjection — a precise statement of who counts as 'inside' the jurisdictional whole.
- Continues the delegated-authority / nested-agency thread from Day 266.
- Flag-worthy: the scope of a collective agent's corrective power is exactly the set of members coupled into it.
