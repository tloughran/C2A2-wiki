## Notes

- Q.39 a.1: "to fulfill all righteousness" = Ambrose's gloss — "this is justice, to do first thyself that which thou wishest another to do, and so encourage others by the example"; Christ submits to what he commands.
- Q.39 a.4-5: the Jordan was the entrance to the land of promise; Christ's baptism is the entrance to the kingdom — and the opened heavens show baptism opening "the door which sin closed."
- Q.39 a.6-8: the whole Trinity is manifest at the baptism (Son in the water, Spirit as dove, Father's voice) — and what is shown is what happens to *us*: "at baptism we become adopted sons, grafted into the mystical body of God's natural son."
- Q.40: the incarnation's end required association, not solitude — proclaim the truth, seek the lost, "show sinners that such access was available to them" by associating with the lowly; hence eating and drinking (vs. John's austerity), poverty, and conformity to the law he came to fulfill.
- Flag-worthy: exemplarity as causation (do-first-thyself), the fullest perspective submitting to the pedagogy of the limited (continuing Day 231's thread), adoption-as-incorporation, and presence-among as the form love takes — strong hooks for mutual modeling, embodied/incarnational epistemology, and positivity resonance.
