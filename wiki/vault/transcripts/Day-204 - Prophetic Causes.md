# Day 204 — Prophetic Causes

Q.172 treats the cause of prophecy — whether it is natural, comes through angels, requires a disposition or a good life, can exist without charity, and whether demons can prophesy. Q.173 treats the manner in which prophetic knowledge is conveyed.

[00:05] Hello, my name is Austin Habash, the founder of Think Catholic, and you're listening to the Summa podcast, where we study St. Thomas Aquinas's Summa Theologiae in a way simple and insightful for anyone to understand. The Summa podcast is brought to you by Think Catholic. Taking two questions of the Summa a day will seek to summarize St. Thomas's responses, discovering the brilliance of Aquinas and his Catholic faith. This is day 204. So let's get started. We're covering today questions 172 and 173 of the Secunda Secundae, that is of the cause of prophecy and of the manner in which prophetic knowledge is

[00:40] conveyed. The translation that I always use in these podcasts is the one generously provided to me by Ave Maria Press, a five volume set translated by the fathers of the English Dominican province, which you can now get at a discounted price from Ave Maria Press's site by typing in the code Summa10. And so here we go. Article one, whether prophecy can be natural. As St. Peter said in his first letter, "no prophecy ever came by the impulse of man, but men moved by the Holy Spirit spoke from God." Man can prophesy naturally only in the sense that he can predict those things far off by observing a certain

[01:15] relationship of cause and effect in nature. As our Lord said, "When it is evening, you say it will be fair weather, for the sky is red; and in the morning it will be stormy today, for the sky is red and threatening." But this is not the gift of prophecy as such. For the prophet knows in a supernatural way. He knows by the divine inspiration, which is not by deducing what may or may not happen due to some particular convergence of causes, but by seeing or knowing things in the future as if already present. This is how God knows the future. God knows

[01:48] with certainty what will happen, not only by being the uncaused cause of all things, but because all of time lies bare before his knowledge. The prophet similarly knows what he knows supernaturally by seeing it as if already present in the mind, for God is showing it to him. Article two, whether prophetic revelation comes through the angels. God has ordered all things in a hierarchy. The highest being God, who is beyond category; below him are entirely immaterial spirits, which are angels and demons; then to intellectual animals, that's us, to non-intellectual animals, and so forth. And because he has instituted this hierarchy, it is fitting that he would utilize it. Kind of like

[02:23] it would be strange in the workplace if you, the CEO, were to hire a manager between you and the rest of the employees and then not allow them to manage those people below you. So too, since God orders all things sweetly, as it is written in the book of Wisdom, it is fitting that he convey prophetic revelation to men not directly, but through the chain of command, which means conveying prophetic revelation through the assistance or the ministry of angels. Article three, whether a natural disposition is requisite for prophecy. The gift of prophecy is a gift of God. It is supernatural, and

[02:57] therefore, just as God can create the entire world, form and matter, out of nothing, so too he can also in the one instant dispose both a man to be able to receive prophetic knowledge and then bestow such knowledge. Therefore, a natural disposition from man is not requisite for prophecy. Article four, whether a good life is requisite for prophecy. And this is an important truth to engrave into our minds, that the gratuitous graces, such as the gift of prophecy, are given by God irrespective to the individual holiness of the possessor. As our Lord taught, "on that day many will say to me, Lord, Lord, did

[03:32] we not prophesy in your name and cast out demons in your name and do other mighty works in your name? And then I will declare to them, I never knew you. Depart from me, you evildoers." So, one can be an evildoer, a man or woman in a state of original or mortal sin, and still carry out mighty works in Jesus's name. Which is why we cannot idolize miracle workers. I know multiple miracle workers today which are either outside of Christ's church or within it yet without the fruits of holiness. As St. Paul taught just as emphatically in 1 Corinthians chapter 13, faith, hope, and love — these are the greatest, the

[04:02] virtues flowing from sanctifying grace. And so these we should pursue wholeheartedly, while desiring the gratuitous graces only if God so willed to bestow them for the profit of others. In St. Thomas's words, responding to our article, prophecy can be without charity. For prophecy pertains to the intellect, whose act precedes the act of the will, which power is perfected by charity. Secondly, for prophecy, like other gratuitous graces, is given for

[04:37] the good of the church. According to 1 Corinthians 12:7, "the manifestation of the spirit is given to every man unto profit," and it is not directly intended to unite man's affections to God, which is the purpose of charity. Then as a final note, Aquinas mentions here that although without charity a man may have the gift of prophecy, disordered passions and an inordinate attachment to temporal things does not exactly help a man think about God and utilize this

[05:05] prophetic knowledge. And therefore, in so far as vice hinders contemplation, it can be said to be an obstacle to prophecy. Article five, whether any prophecy comes from the demons. And a word from St. Thomas: "accordingly, prophecy, properly speaking, is conveyed by divine revelations alone. Yet the revelation which is made by the demons may be called prophecy in a restricted sense." So in other words, demons can take much better guesses of the future, for example, even when it involves free decisions of man, than we can. And thus, in so far as they reveal these estimations, these educated guesses to

[05:42] man, they can be said to give a kind of pseudo-prophecy and make for themselves pseudo-prophets. Article six, whether the prophets of the demons ever foretell the truth. And they do, as the demons did through the pagan prophet Balaam, who foretold, as recorded in the book of Numbers, "a star shall come forth out of Jacob, and a scepter shall rise out of Israel," which was ultimately fulfilled in Christ, in

[06:10] Jesus of Nazareth. Therefore, demons sometimes do foretell truth, but in order to accomplish their goal, which is the condemnation of human souls through sin and damnation. Therefore, although they may at times foretell truthfully, the demons are never to be contacted, never to be trusted, never to be in any kind of fellowship with. Moving then on to question 173 of the manner in which prophetic knowledge is conveyed. Article one, whether the prophets see the very essence of God. According to scripture,

[06:38] as detailed in 1 Corinthians 13, "love never ends; as for prophecies, they will pass away," which tells us that the knowledge of prophecies is not identical to the knowledge of the divine essence, because once a man sees God as he is in heaven, such a vision does not pass away, and this is what heaven consists in essentially, the vision of God. As St. John taught in his first

[07:17] letter, "Beloved, we are God's children now. It does not yet appear what we shall be, but we know that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is." We can conclude then, since prophecy ceases and the vision of the divine essence does not cease, that prophets do not see the essence of God. Article two, whether in prophetic revelation new species of things are impressed on the prophet's mind, or merely a new light. And beginning with a quick refresher on how natural knowledge works. St. Thomas says here, which we can then explain, "things are represented to the human mind under the form of species, and according to the order of nature, they must be represented first to the senses, secondly to the imagination, thirdly to the passive intellect. And these are changed by the species derived from the phantasms, which

[07:53] change results from the enlightening action of the active intellect," which is an awesome concise summary of human cognition. But said in much simpler terms, when you are teaching a child the color blue, you place a blue ball in front of them, then a blue water bottle, then a blue shoe. You point at each of these items and you say "blue" until the child gets the gist of what they all have in common, until they have abstracted from these particular instantiations of blue the general form, the essence of blue. They were able to do that because of the natural light of the mind which illuminates these particular things in order to reveal the general structure, the essence beneath it, which then informs the mind. So this essence, this

[08:32] gist, this form of blue informing the mind is what we call the intelligible species. So when it comes to the gift of prophecy, does the knowledge which comes from it stem only from a stronger light in the mind by which one can then abstract new, deeper truths from an ordinary thing, like when Daniel

[09:04] interpreted prophetically the writing on the wall in the book of Daniel chapter 5? Or does this prophetic knowledge come by the ideas, the truths themselves, simply being directly impressed into the mind, skipping that normal process of abstraction, such as might have been the case with St. Paul, who said, "For I would have you know, brethren, that the gospel which was preached by me is not man's gospel, for I did not receive it from man, nor was I taught it, but it

[09:33] came through a revelation of Jesus Christ." Or, and lastly, could prophetic knowledge come via an infused coordination of images, and as enlightened by a supernatural light, such as in the book of Jeremiah where it is written, "The word of the Lord came to me a second time, say-

[10:01] ing, What do you see? And I said, I see a boiling pot facing away from the north. Then the Lord said to me, Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land." And as the listener can probably discern from the examples we have given, revelatory knowledge can be given in all of these ways. As St. Thomas concludes, "hence it is evident that prophetic revelation is conveyed sometimes by the mere infusion of light, sometimes by imprinting species anew, or by a new coordination of species." Article three, whether the prophetic vision is always accompanied by abstraction from the senses. So by way of review, St. Thomas says here prophetic revelation can be given either by (a) the infusion of an intelligible light, (b) infusion of

[10:38] intelligible species, (c) by impression or coordination of pictures in the imagination, or (d) by the outward presentation of sensible images. And what abstraction from the senses means in this article's question would be like the suspension of the sense knowledge we experience when we are asleep, but we do not feel the bed or hear the sounds in the room when we are asleep or dreaming. And in the ways we mentioned in this article in which prophetic revelation can be conveyed, the only one that requires, in the sense that it is orderly and fitting for man, that he be withdrawn from the senses is when revelation is

[11:14] given through images in the mind. And this is because, as we know from dreaming, imagining something and seeing something with our physical eyes can be difficult to distinguish. Yet, if we began to see a man and then at the same time lost our sense of hearing and touch and smell and taste, this might clue us

[11:43] into the fact that something abnormal is happening, that we might be receiving a supernatural revelation or having a supernatural experience. And so, in order to avoid confusing the prophet, it is fitting that prophetic vision, when delivered through the imagination, accompany a withdrawal from the senses. Article four, in our last article, whether prophets always know the things which they prophesy. And we have precedents of this not being the case, of a prophet not knowing the things they were prophesying. And this was the high priest Caiaphas, as we see in the Gospel of John 11, as it is written, "But one of them, Caiaphas, who was high priest that year, said to them, you know nothing at all. You do not understand that it is expedient for you that one

[12:16] man should die for the people, and that the whole nation should not perish." And this article is quite interesting. It's kind of tucked away in the back of the third volume. Yet, it serves as a surprising meditation on God's providence and man's freedom. That the Holy Spirit can move a man so that he chooses freely, that he think and deliberate and choose as human voluntary agents do, yet choose in such a way that he will certainly speak or act according to God's wishes, what God wishes to convey prophetically by his speech and actions. Consider, for example, the soldiers who divided Christ's garments

[12:52] amongst themselves and cast lots for his tunic. Surely they did so according to their own free deliberation and choice, but at the same time were moved by the Holy Spirit to do so certainly, and in order to serve as a prophetic witness to Christ, of whom it was predicted back in Psalm 22, "Dogs encompass me. A company of evildoers encircle me. They have pierced my hands and feet. I can count all my bones. They stare and gloat over m-

[13:24] e. They divide my garments among them and for my clothing they cast lots." Therefore, St. Thomas concludes — and in this passage I'm about to quote, where Aquinas says "defective instrument," in the English I think he means "deficient of itself" instrument. And so St. Thomas says here, "Accordingly, when a man knows that he is being moved by the Holy Ghost to think something or signify something by word or deed, this belongs properly to prophecy. Whereas, when he is moved without his knowing it, this is

[13:53] not perfect prophecy, but a prophetic instinct. Nevertheless, it must be observed that since the prophet's mind is a defective instrument, even true prophets know not all that the Holy Ghost means by the things they see or speak or even do." ## Notes

- **God's knowledge of the future = "all of time lies bare before his knowledge"; the prophet sees it "as if already present."** This is the block-universe / view-from-outside-the-interface picture — strong hook for Carroll's eternalism and Hoffman's interface (time as a construction of the dashboard).
- **The angelic chain-of-command** (God → angels → us), illustrated with the CEO/manager analogy, is a hierarchy-of-mediation claim: revelation propagates through nested levels rather than directly. Maps to nested agency / multiscale mediation.
- **Best epistemology in the batch:** Aquinas's abstraction account (senses → imagination → passive intellect; phantasms transformed by the active intellect's light) and the blue-ball teaching example. Three modes of prophetic conveyance — stronger *light* (better abstraction), directly *impressed species* (skipping abstraction), or *coordinated images*. Direct hook for predictive-coding / generative-model accounts of cognition and for the interface.
- **Providence + freedom (Caiaphas, the soldiers casting lots):** the Holy Spirit moves a man "so that he chooses freely... yet certainly." Compatibilist top-down constraint — central-theme + active-inference resource.
- **The capstone line for the central theme:** "since the prophet's mind is a defective instrument, even true prophets know not all that the Holy Ghost means." Perspective-limitation stated of the most exalted knower short of the beatific vision.
- Prophecy can exist **without charity** (intellect precedes will; gratuitous grace given "for the profit of others," not to unite affections) — a clean separation of *capability* from *union*, useful against conflating intelligence with love.
