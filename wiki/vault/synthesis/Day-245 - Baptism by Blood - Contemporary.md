# Day 245 — Baptism by Blood (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (III Q.66) distinguishes baptism of water, of blood (martyrdom), and of desire — holding that blood and desire can supply the effect of baptism where water is impossible. The medieval claim is that the *disposition* (faith, charity, willingness to die) can reach the same saving terminus as the material rite. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: when many routes can reach one target state, what is the operative reality — the particular material path, or the disposition/form that the paths share?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **how can different routes converge on one and the same outcome, such that the material specifics are not strictly necessary to the end?** Aquinas needs blood and desire to be genuinely sufficient, not second-best imitations — the same terminus reached without the same matter. The contemporary frame meets exactly this in any system exhibiting *equifinality*: a target state reached by multiple, materially different trajectories.

## Resource

Levin is the leading resource. In Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's program, PRS-03 (the morphological-attractor formalism) describes outcome-equifinality directly: a target morphology reached by many distinct cellular routes; perturbed systems re-route to the same set-point. Levin's planaria reach the correct anatomical target through varied paths and after varied insults — empirical demonstration (*in its own domain*) that what governs is the attractor (the form), not the particular trajectory. This is the cleanest naturalized parallel to baptism's "many routes, one terminus."

Kastrup supplies the metaphysics of why disposition is operative. In Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup's program, PRS-02 (individual identity within monism) makes the salient reality the *state of mind* rather than the physical token — so a disposition of will (desire, charity unto death) is not a placeholder for the "real" physical event but is itself the operative reality the rite normally signifies.

On Wright's reading, salvation is inclusion in the covenant people; baptism of blood/desire is membership effected where the sign is unavailable. Rohr's Universal Christ frames grace as exceeding the measure of the rite — the pattern is not confined to the water.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the operative reality of baptism is the dispositional form (faith/charity oriented to Christ), which can reach its saving terminus by multiple routes — water ordinarily, blood or desire where water is impossible — because what governs the outcome is the attractor/disposition, not the particular material path.** This *shares* with Aquinas the sufficiency of blood and desire and the priority of charity. It *reframes* the three "baptisms" as equifinal routes to one target state rather than as a ranked hierarchy of substitutes. It *firms up* the possibility — equifinality is empirically actual (Levin) — while *marking* that the transfer to a salvific terminus is analogical, and the claim that the disposition saves is theological (Wright/Rohr), not derivable from the biology.

## The central-theme thread

This article strongly supports the project's central claim. Salvation, in the synthesis idiom, is the limited agent's inclusion in loving unity, computed toward with the resources it perceives. Baptism of desire is the limit case where the *resources* (water, a minister, time) are unavailable, yet the agent's disposition — its genuine orientation toward the whole — suffices, because grace meets the perspective at the limit of its resources. "Work out your salvation in fear and trembling" (Phil 2:12) and "baptism of desire" name the same structure: the terminus is reached by whatever route the agent's situation permits, provided the orientation is real.

- **Wright PRS-34 (justification as declaration of covenant membership), `traditions/wright/prs_triplets.md`** — *theological, Confidence High.* This is the constraint the closing paragraph asks for: membership is *declared*, and the badge practices are not the thing declared — the record's own formulation is that badges individuate sub-groups while the shared table enacts unity.
