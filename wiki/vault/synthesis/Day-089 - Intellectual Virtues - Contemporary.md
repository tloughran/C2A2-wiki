# Day 89 — Intellectual Virtues (Contemporary)

## Frame

Q.56 asks which powers of the soul are subjects of virtue. The intellect is a subject of virtue: it admits perfection by stable habit. The will is a subject of virtue: it can be ordered to its proper good by habit. The *irascible* and *concupiscible* parts of the sensitive appetite are subjects of virtue insofar as they share in reason — they can be habituated to follow reason's lead. The vegetative powers and the *outer* senses (taken in themselves) are not subjects of virtue; they execute without deliberative shaping. The *inner* senses (imagination, memory, common sense) can be partially habituated, but Aquinas treats them as intermediate cases.

Q.57 turns to the *intellectual* virtues specifically. Aristotle names five — the *speculative* triad of *understanding* (intellectus, *nous*: grasp of first principles), *science* (scientia: demonstrative knowledge of conclusions), and *wisdom* (sapientia: knowledge of the highest causes); and the two *practical* virtues — *art* (ars: right reason about things to be made) and *prudence* (prudentia, *phronesis*: right reason about things to be done). Aquinas distinguishes *speculative* virtues (whose end is the truth) from *practical* virtues (whose end is action). Wisdom is the *highest* of the speculative virtues — it governs both science and understanding, because it knows the highest cause and judges the others by reference to it. Prudence stands apart among the practical virtues because it concerns the *singular contingent action* and requires good moral disposition: a man cannot be prudent without good moral character, because the very *end* he aims at must be the right end (which the morally formed will supplies). Prudence has *annexed* virtues — *eubulia* (right deliberation), *synesis* (good judgment in standard cases), *gnome* (good judgment in exceptional cases).

Under conscious-realist monism the medieval map of intellectual virtues recovers as a remarkably articulate taxonomy of *epistemic and practical-rational competence types*. The contemporary frame can speak to each: nous as fast intuitive grasp of structure (Hawkins's reference-frame intuition; Kahneman's System 1 at its best); scientia as model-based inference under explicit constraint (Friston's hierarchical posteriors; logical-mathematical structure); sapientia as judgment over the integrated whole (McGilchrist's right-hemispheric integration; the "wisdom" tradition in the empirical decision sciences); ars as bounded-rational design competence (the contemporary design and engineering literature); prudentia as situated practical reasoning under conditions of moral character — a domain where the empirical literature on practical wisdom (Schwartz; Stump) and active-inference accounts of action-under-uncertainty converge.

## Problem

The shared problem: **what are the distinguishable kinds of cognitive-and-practical-rational excellence, what makes each an excellence (not merely a competence), and how do they hang together with the agent's moral character?** Aquinas's answer is finely articulated. Five intellectual virtues. Speculative vs. practical division. Wisdom governing the speculative triad. Prudence inseparable from moral virtue because the *end* must be right. Annexed virtues to prudence — eubulia, synesis, gnome — for the structural moments of deliberation, judgment-in-standard-case, judgment-in-exceptional-case.

The contemporary problem is exactly recognisable. The decision sciences and the empirical literatures on cognition, expertise, judgment, and practical reasoning give a remarkably converging picture: there is a hierarchy of cognitive excellences, the highest of which integrate domain-specific competence with broad sensitivity to the whole context; practical rationality is *not* reducible to instrumental rationality but requires the agent's end-orientation to be already well-formed; and the moments of deliberation, standard-case judgment, and exceptional-case judgment all admit separable cultivation.

The deeper question Q.57 poses is the *unity-of-the-intellectual-virtues* question. Are these five virtues separable? Can one have *scientia* without *sapientia*? Can one have *ars* without moral virtue? Can one be *prudent* without the moral character that fixes the right end? Aquinas's answers — wisdom integrates the speculative triad; ars is independent of moral virtue (a builder can build well morally bad); prudence cannot be separated from moral virtue — are subtle. The contemporary frame must either vindicate or revise these claims.

## Resource

**Hawkins's reference-frame intuition and pattern-completion** is the cortical-circuit account of *intellectus*. The agent's cortex, having stored thousands of reference frames, recognises the structure of a new instance rapidly and pre-reflectively. This is *intellectus* in its developed form — not the deliberative working-out of conclusions but the fast intuitive grasp of how the case structurally is. **PRS-01** in the Hawkins-tradition wiki supplies the mechanism: Hierarchical Temporal Memory and the Thousand Brains Theory — reference frames, temporal prediction, and distributed voting — recorded at **High**. (Corrected from PRS-04 on this pass; see the Bridges entry for the reasoning.) The medieval claim that *intellectus* is the habit by which we grasp first principles maps onto: the cortex's stabilized reference frames *constitute* the agent's grasp of structural primitives in its domains.

**Friston's hierarchical model-based inference** supplies the contemporary articulation of *scientia*. Science is demonstrative knowledge of conclusions from principles. Under active inference, this corresponds to the agent's hierarchical-generative-model competence at deriving lower-level posteriors from higher-level priors, under explicit conditioning. **Friston PRS-01** (the Free Energy Principle: variational Bayesian inference joined to thermodynamic free energy, recorded **High**) carries the formalism. Friston PRS-04 (organism as active modeler) gives the framing but is recorded *(Implicit)* at **Medium**, and its stated Solution is predictive coding and the Bayesian brain hypothesis, which derives nothing — it is retained here for framing only. The logical-mathematical literature (Tarski, Lakatos, the Bayesian-program tradition) gives the formal content at its own strength.

**McGilchrist's right-hemispheric integration as sapientia** is the keystone for the highest speculative virtue. Two records carry it, anchored on this pass rather than left as a bare citation of the tradition page: **PRS-01** (hemispheric asymmetry, the right primary) and **PRS-07** (division and union as creative synthesis — union at a higher level, a third thing neither hemisphere produces alone). PRS-07 is the closer match to *sapientia* specifically, because Aquinas's wisdom does not merely attend more widely, it *judges the other two virtues by reference to the highest cause* — a synthetic act rather than a receptive one. McGilchrist's account of right-hemispheric attentional contextualization — its sensitivity to the whole, its receptivity to the unfamiliar, its integration of the parts under the whole — is the contemporary articulation of *sapientia*. The "knowledge of the highest causes" that integrates *intellectus* and *scientia* is precisely the right-hemispheric mode that governs the left-hemispheric instrumental competence. McGilchrist's clinical and historical work supports the diagnostic that *wisdom-loss* is hemispheric-imbalance: a culture that loses right-hemispheric receptivity loses *sapientia* even as it expands *scientia*.

**Hoffman on the interface as wisdom-conditioning** speaks to the *highest* speculative virtue's distinctive content. Wisdom, in the medieval frame, is knowledge of the highest cause — God in the theological context; the underlying *what is fundamental* in any inquiry. Hoffman's interface theory contributes a precise formal point: if perceived spacetime is a species-specific interface and not the underlying reality, then wisdom (knowledge of the underlying) cannot be obtained by deeper looking at the interface alone. PRS-03 in the Hoffman-tradition wiki (the Interface Theory of Perception, with fitness-payoff functions replacing truth-tracking) carries this point directly; PRS-01 (conscious realism, matter as perceptual construct) supplies its ontological setting. This is one of the key contemporary moves on Q.57 a.2.

**Friston's active inference as the formal account of *prudentia*** is the deepest contemporary recovery. Prudence is right reason about contingent action — situated, particular, condition-sensitive. Active inference is *exactly* the formal account of bounded-rational action under uncertainty in a particular situation. The Q.57 a.4-5 articles on prudence as practical reason about the singular contingent action recover as the agent's policy-selection under expected-free-energy minimization given a specified goal-prior. **Friston PRS-16** is the record that carries this: expected free energy as the unified mathematics of decision under both risk and ambiguity, collapsing the two into one optimization target (**High**). That is the formal object the prudence analogy needs, and it is not what PRS-04 records; PRS-04 is retained for framing only, at *(Implicit)*/Medium. The active-inference-and-action literature applies at its own strength.

**Stump on the second-personal shape of prudence** is the keystone for Q.57 a.4-5. PRS-11 in the Stump-tradition wiki records the account of non-propositional, second-person ("Franciscan") knowledge as philosophically rigorous and irreducible to propositions; PRS-29 records the correlative ethical claim that for Aquinas the moral life is first of all a matter of standing in a right second-personal relationship rather than of acquiring dispositions. Together they give Q.57 a.4 its contemporary reading: a man without the right moral end cannot be prudent because he does not in fact know-by-second-person-knowing what the right action *toward* a person is.

**McGilchrist on phronesis as right-hemispheric situated judgment** is the parallel hemispheric-level account. The right-hemispheric mode of attention is precisely what allows the agent to read the particular case rightly — to see *this* person in *this* situation under *these* conditions. Left-hemispheric instrumental dominance produces the case-blind application of general rules; right-hemispheric integration produces situated judgment. McGilchrist's framework supplies the empirical-clinical mechanism for Aquinas's claim about prudence's particularity.

**Stump on the unity-of-virtue dynamics** clarifies Q.57's most controversial claim — that prudence cannot be separated from moral virtue. PRS-09 in the Stump-tradition wiki treats this as substantively right, and gives the reason: virtues perfect the soul's *natural powers* rather than sitting alongside them as detachable traits, so the agent's *cognitive* grasp of what to do is mediated by the agent's *affective and motivational* formation — the relevant cognition is second-personal, and the agent's relation to the person is part of what the agent must know.

**Fredrickson on positivity-resonance as cognitive-broadening** supplies an empirical anchor for the prudence-and-moral-character entailment. PRS-05 as recorded in `traditions/fredrickson/wiki.md` — *broadened attention and tradition learning* — reports that positive affective states broaden attentional and cognitive scope; chronic negativity narrows. **The record's own grade is *Implicit***, and its second half — what broadening means for tradition immersion — is entered there as an **open question**, not a result. The broadening effect is empirically well attested in the wider affect literature; the tradition-learning extension is the wiki's question and this synthesis's own step, and neither is licensed by the record at high confidence. (Day 90 states the same id correctly; this bullet previously badged it *empirical, high confidence*, and the two sibling days disagreed about one record.) The agent without good moral disposition (which in Fredrickson's terms includes the cultivated dispositions of positivity-resonance and other-directed attention) is empirically *less able* to perceive the relevant case-features that prudence requires.

**Wright on practical wisdom in the Pauline tradition** is the scriptural anchor. Wright's reading of Paul (especially Philippians, Colossians, and the Romans 12 material) treats Christian practical reason as inseparable from the agent's formation in Christ — the renewed mind that "discerns the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect" (Rom 12:2). The Pauline phronēsis is exactly Aquinas's prudentia under conditions of formation-in-Christ. *After You Believe* makes the linkage explicit.

**Rohr on contemplative wisdom and second-half-of-life integrated judgment** complements the wisdom-as-sapientia analysis. Rohr's *Falling Upward* treats the second-half-of-life integrated judgment as a kind of *settled wisdom* — situated, non-dual, attentive to the whole. This is the contemplative-tradition recovery of *sapientia* as a stabilized disposition rather than a propositional inventory.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

**(a) Shared with Aquinas.** There are distinguishable kinds of cognitive-and-practical-rational excellence: a fast intuitive grasp of structural primitives (nous/intellectus); a demonstrative-model-based competence (scientia); a synthesizing judgment under the highest-cause-or-whole (sapientia); a bounded-rational design competence (ars); and a situated practical wisdom about action in this particular case (prudentia). The speculative-practical distinction is preserved. Wisdom integrates the speculative triad. Prudence is inseparable from moral virtue — the agent's end-orientation conditions the agent's cognitive access to the right action. Ars is separable from moral virtue (a morally bad person can be a good craftsman). Prudence has structural sub-moments: deliberation (eubulia), standard-case judgment (synesis), exceptional-case judgment (gnome).

**(b) Where it reframes.** *Intellectus* recovers as fast cortical reference-frame intuition; *scientia* as hierarchical-generative-model inference under explicit conditioning; *sapientia* as right-hemispheric integration over the whole; *ars* as bounded-rational design under domain constraint; *prudentia* as active-inference policy selection under situational and moral-end constraints. The unity-of-virtues claim about prudence recovers as: agent's policy selection depends on goal-prior; the goal-prior is set by the agent's moral formation; without right moral formation, the goal-prior is mis-set and prudent action is unavailable. Stump's second-personal reading deepens this: the relevant knowing is *of persons* and is mediated by the agent's relational formation.

**(b2) Where it is superseded.** Q.56's survey of *which powers are subjects of virtue* draws a line, and the contemporary evidence moves it. At **Q.56 a.5** Aquinas asks whether the sensitive powers of apprehension can be subjects of virtue and answers by dividing them: the *interior* powers — imagination, memory, the cogitative — admit habits that dispose a man well, though these are virtues only secondarily; the *exterior* senses do not, because they operate by natural disposition rather than at reason's command. The Frame above records the ruling in those terms. **That exclusion does not survive.** Perceptual learning is among the better-replicated findings in the field: trained observers acquire stable, long-lived, domain-specific improvements in *what they see and hear* — the radiologist who resolves the nodule, the sonographer, the expert reader of the board — and the improvement is not a faster inference laid over unchanged sensory input but a change in the discriminations the perceptual system itself makes. It meets Aquinas's own criteria for a habit point for point: stable, built by repeated acts under attention, disposing its subject well with respect to a proper object, and acquired at reason's direction rather than given by nature. The vegetative powers make the same point more weakly, through interoceptive and autonomic conditioning. **What survives is the distinction; what is retired is its placement.** Aquinas is right that there is a real line between what admits deliberative formation and what merely executes, and he is wrong about where it falls — wrong for a principled reason worth naming, since he treats the exterior senses as a fixed transducer stage, which is exactly the assumption the predictive-processing frame this commentary relies on elsewhere denies: if perception is already inference, it is already trainable, and the exterior senses cannot be quarantined from habituation. The cost falls on Q.56 rather than Q.57. The five intellectual virtues stand; what does not stand is the tidy claim that *intellectus* begins where sensory execution ends, and the boundary between a trained perceptual discrimination and the fast structural grasp the Hawkins PRS-01 bullet calls *intellectus* becomes a difference of degree rather than of subject. *Evidence: perceptual-learning and expert-perception effects are empirically firm. The reading of them as specifically habit-shaped — stable, act-built, attention-gated — is this commentary's own application, and no register record carries it.*

**(c) Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.** *Firm, and the ground of (b2)*: perceptual learning and expert-perception effects in the exterior senses, absent any faculty-level exemption. *Firm*: fast cortical pattern-completion (Hawkins **PRS-01**, High; broader cortical literature); the free-energy formalism (Friston **PRS-01**, High) and expected free energy as the mathematics of decision under risk and ambiguity (Friston **PRS-16**, High); hemispheric asymmetry (McGilchrist **PRS-01**, High). *Empirical in the wider literature but recorded only as (Implicit)*: attentional-cognitive scope broadening under positive affect (Fredrickson PRS-05 in `wiki.md`) — and its tradition-learning half is an open question there, not a finding. *Framing only, at (Implicit)/Medium*: Friston PRS-04 and Hawkins PRS-04, neither of which derives or measures what the bullets above assert; both retained at Resource level. *Philosophical-cultural rather than experimental*: McGilchrist PRS-07 on synthesis-as-union. *Philosophically rigorous*: Stump on the second-personal structure of practical-moral knowing (PRS-11 **High**; PRS-09 and PRS-29 **Medium**). *Recorded High, though the application is this synthesis's own extension*: Hoffman **PRS-03** as constraint on sapientia — the record itself claims a rigorous mathematical proof that selection drives true perception to extinction, so the speculation lies in carrying it to wisdom-as-highest-cause, not in the record. *Theological, well-attested*: Wright on Pauline phronēsis; Rohr on contemplative wisdom.

## The central-theme thread

The intellectual virtues are the central theme's cognitive substrate. Loving unity is the computational task; perspective-limitation is the constraint. The intellectual virtues are the *cognitive competences* that let the limited agent compute well under those constraints. *Intellectus* lets the agent grasp the structural primitives of its perspectival situation. *Scientia* lets the agent derive conclusions about its situation from those primitives. *Sapientia* lets the agent judge the whole — including its own perspective-limitations and the wider perspective that exceeds them. *Ars* lets the agent design well within its domain. *Prudentia* lets the agent act rightly *in this particular case*.

The central theme's most striking convergence with Q.57 is the *unity-of-virtues* claim about prudence. Loving unity is the goal; perspective-limitation is the condition; the agent's *moral* orientation is what fixes the goal-prior under which the agent's cognitive resources operate. A man oriented to vengeance rather than to loving unity cannot be prudent — not because his cognitive faculty is damaged but because his goal-prior is mis-set. The "fear and trembling" of Phil 2:12 acquires another layer of mechanical sense: the working-out is cognitively constrained by the agent's moral formation, and the agent without good moral formation is *cognitively blind* to the right action.

*Sapientia* carries the central theme's deepest cognitive claim. Wisdom is knowledge of the highest cause — which under conscious-realist monism is mind itself in its widest scope (Kastrup's mind-at-large; the cosmic-Christ frame in Rohr). The agent's sapiential competence is, in part, its receptivity to *more* of the whole than its individuated perspective contains. This is the central theme's "guidance from those who perceive more of the whole" in the cognitive register: the wise agent is the one whose right-hemispheric receptivity (McGilchrist), contemplative attention (Rohr), and Pauline renewed mind (Wright) admit perspective-widening into its situated judgment. Tom's PRS-form re-description of Stump's faith-as-knowledge-of-persons supplies the second-personal version: wisdom is knowing-by-being-in-relation, not knowing-by-inventory.

One qualification is owed here, and it is recorded rather than this commentary's own. **PRS-41 in the Stump-tradition wiki** (added 2026-09-08, **Medium**) states the problem precisely: a disposition of openness to direction from a source outside the agent's own cognitive reach "is structurally identical for a good master and a manipulator; nothing in the mechanism distinguishes docility from susceptibility." Its Solution is that **openness is never a virtue *simpliciter*** — it is a virtue only under a discrimination condition administered by the recipient, which makes the moment of ratification constitutive of the relation rather than external to it. That bears directly on the paragraph above and on the constitution's own "guidance from those who perceive more of the whole": receptivity-to-more-of-the-whole is not by itself a sapiential excellence. It is one only where the agent retains and exercises the discrimination. This is Q.57's territory rather than an importation — the record's Resource cites *docilitas* as a part of prudence (II-II q.49 a.3), and prudence is precisely the virtue Q.57 a.4-5 makes inseparable from good moral disposition. The record's own Evidence line marks the difficulty as **OPEN**: the criterion is retrospective, available to one who already holds the right answer, so it does not by itself defeat the regress. The qualification is therefore stated, not resolved.

## Where this leaves us

Q.56-57 take up the intellectual virtues. Q.58-59 (tomorrow) turn to the *moral* virtues — how they differ from the intellectual virtues, and their relation to the passions. The synthesis will pivot from cognitive competence to character-as-affective-formation. Fredrickson, McGilchrist, and Stump will lead; the active-inference frame on passions-as-appraisal will reappear. The two are mutually conditioning. By the end of Q.59 the contemporary frame will be holding the full Aristotelian-Thomistic synthesis: virtues of mind and virtues of character, mutually formative, jointly constituting the agent's stabilized disposition toward loving unity under perspective-limitation.
