# Day 221 — Contemporary Parallel: The Conformed-but-Free Will

## Frame

Having settled what the Son *assumed* (Q.4-16), the *Summa* turns to how the assumed humanity *is* and *acts*. Q.17 secures unity of *being*: Christ is *one* because the one person subsists across the two natures ("one and being are convertible"), so although there are many distinguishable beings in him — his knowledge, his virtues, his body's size and shape — there is one *esse* belonging to the one hypostasis. Q.18 then secures unity of *will* against the Monothelite error in the other direction: precisely *because* Christ is true God and true man he has *two* wills, a divine will and a genuine human rational will, plus a "will of sensuality" (the sensitive appetite, "rational by participation"). The crux is a.5-6: the human will is *altogether conformed* to the divine in the thing willed — "not as I will, but as thou wilt" (Matt 26:39) — and yet the sensitive appetite, which "naturally shrinks from sensible pains," never rose up against reason, so there was *no contrariety* of wills in Christ. The contemporary version of the question: *can a perspective be genuinely limited, genuinely its own, and genuinely free, while being wholly conformed to the will of one who sees the whole — without that conformity being coercion, and without the limitation being conflict?*

## Problem

Two linked inquiry-problems sit under both the medieval and the contemporary frames. (1) **Unity across registers.** How can one *being* carry many distinguishable beings — a human intellect, a human will, a sensitive appetite, a body — without fragmenting into many subjects? (2) **Conformity without coercion or conflict.** This is the load-bearing one for the project. A human will that is *altogether conformed* to a higher will sounds like a will overridden — a perspective erased rather than honored. Aquinas's achievement in Q.18 is to deny exactly that: the human will is *free* (a.4, choice of means is its proper act), *its own* (a real second will, not a mouthpiece), *and* fully aligned, while the sensitive appetite's real shrinking from pain produces no *contrariety* because it never "prevails against the determination of his mind." The problem the contemporary frame inherits is to give that arrangement a structure: how does a bounded, limited will align to a superordinate will in a way that is alignment rather than absorption?

## Resource

The leading move comes from active inference. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston describes the organism as an *active modeler* (PRS-04) — an agent that holds its own generative model and acts to minimize the gap between that model and the way things are. PRS-06 extends this to *multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling*: agents "couple through mutual informational alignment," and a sub-agent can conform its model to a superordinate model *without being coerced*, because alignment is the agent's own action on its own model, not an external force imposed on it. This is precisely the shape of a human will freely conforming to a divine will: the conformity is the limited will's *own act* of aligning to what the higher perspective sees, not an override of it. For the *unity-of-being* problem, the consciousness-first programs carry the load. Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup (PRS-02, PRS-08) gives one consciousness holding a genuinely *individuated* point of view — a bounded "alter," a *Daimon* with its own character and vocation — that is real and its own while remaining a perspective *within* the one. One being, a bounded will: dissociation-without-fragmentation. Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-01) supplies the posture that keeps two wills from collapsing into either Monothelite fusion or Nestorian division: non-dual knowing "perceives unity-in-distinction rather than collapsing into false dichotomies" — *both/and*, two wills in one harmony. And the scriptural anchor is decisive: "not as I will, but as thou wilt" is the Gethsemane form of the Philippians hymn, the will that "emptied himself... obedient unto death" (Phil 2:7-8) — and that hymn runs straight into "work out your salvation in fear and trembling, *for it is God who works in you*" (Phil 2:12-13), the project's banner, where the limited agent's working and the higher will's working are the *same* working, not rivals.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the conformed-but-free human will is the project's model of the limited perspective freely computing toward loving unity with the One who sees the whole. The conformity is not coercion because, on the active-inference reading, alignment is the limited will's own act of bringing its model into accord with a superordinate model (Friston PRS-04/06); the unity-of-being is dissociation-without-fragmentation, one being holding a real, bounded will (Kastrup PRS-02/08); and the absence of contrariety is the non-dual both/and (Rohr) in which two wills constitute one harmony rather than a contest.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps two real wills, not one — the human will is genuinely its own, with genuine free choice of means (a.4). It keeps the conformity *total* in the thing willed (a.5). And it keeps the sensitive appetite's real shrinking from pain *without* contrariety (a.6): a perspective can be genuinely afflicted and genuinely limited without being in conflict with the higher will.

*Where it reframes.* Aquinas secures the no-contrariety result through faculty psychology and the perfection of grace in Christ; the contemporary reframe offers a *mechanism* for alignment-without-coercion (the limited agent aligning its own generative model to a superordinate one) and a *structure* for one-being-holding-a-bounded-will (dissociation). The arrangement stops being a Christological singularity and becomes legible as the general shape the project posits for every limited agent: alignment to the One who perceives more is the agent's own free computation, not its erasure.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The two-wills grammar and the no-contrariety result are theological/formal — rigorous within their own terms. The "not as I will" → Phil 2:13 anchor is scriptural (Wright, strong). The active-inference model-alignment and the dissociation structure are speculative-formal (medium): they are coherent analogues with formal backing, not empirical demonstrations that a human will *is* an active-inference sub-agent.

## The central-theme thread

This is the day the project's premise gets stated as a problem about *willing*. The central claim is that limited agents "work out [their] salvation in fear and trembling... aware of [their] need for guidance from those who perceive more of the whole than we do." Q.18's conformed human will is exactly that: a limited will that freely brings itself into accord with the will of the One who sees the whole — and the test case, Gethsemane, shows the conformity costing something real (the sensitive appetite shrinks) without the limited will being overridden. Phil 2 makes the convergence unmistakable: the same hymn that gives us the will "obedient unto death" gives us "work out your salvation... for it is God who works in you both to will and to work." The limited will's working and the higher will's working are not two competing forces but one alignment — which is precisely what active inference describes (the sub-agent's own act of conforming its model) and precisely what loving unity *means* on the project's terms: not the absorption of the limited perspective but its free coincidence with the whole. The will of sensuality that shrinks yet does not rebel is the honest picture of a finite agent under resource limitation: real aversion, real cost, no contrariety.

## Where this leaves us

Q.17-18 establish that the one person who is both comprehensor and wayfarer (Day 220) is *one being* who *wills* with a real human will wholly conformed to the divine. Tomorrow (Day 222, "Unity of Operation") the *Summa* moves from willing to *acting*: how the two natures' operations concur in one work (Q.19), and — critically for the project — how Christ's action as *Head* extends its merit to the members who are "united to him as members to a head" (Q.19 a.4). The thread to carry forward: having seen a limited will freely conform to the whole, watch the treatise on Christ's *operation* for how that conformed agency becomes *incorporating* — how the one who aligns to the whole draws the many limited perspectives into one.
