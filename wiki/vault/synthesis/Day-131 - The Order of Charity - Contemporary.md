# Day 131 — Contemporary Parallel: The Two Centers and the Furnace's Reach

## Frame

Twenty articles at a sprint, on two questions. Q.26 treats the *order* of charity — that there is an order in what we love (a.1), that God is loved above neighbor (a.2) and above self (a.3), that we may lay down the body for a neighbor but never the soul (a.4-5), and that among neighbors the intensity of love is proportioned to nearness (a.6-9), an order that survives into heaven as the order of sanctity (a.13). Q.27 treats charity's *act*, to love — that loving is more proper to charity than being loved (a.1), that charity exceeds mere goodwill (a.2), that God is loved for himself (a.3), immediately (a.4), wholly (a.5), and without measure (a.6), and that love of an enemy is the greater proof of charity (a.7).

Yesterday's synthesis read the order of charity as concentric inclusion — the loving whole gathering every open perspective in proper proportion. Today Aquinas hands the project two things that sharpen it: the *governing law* of that order (a.6) and the *elementary act* that does the loving (Q.27). Read under mind / conscious-realist monism, the law turns out to be the central theme's own coordinate system, and the act turns out to be active inference under another name.

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem has two parts. First, *if love is ordered, what is the metric of the ordering — and why does Aquinas name two centers rather than one?* He could have said love is proportioned to nearness to God alone. Instead a.6 gives two principles: "the affection of love increases in proportion to the nearness to one or the other of those principles," and the two principles are *God* and *ourselves*. A satisfying account has to say why the order of love is bipolar.

Second, *what is the elementary act of charity?* Is it being loved or loving (a.1)? Is it the same as wishing another well (a.2)? Does it admit a limit, a healthy measure, like eating and sleeping (a.6)? And is it harder, and therefore more telling, to love a friend or an enemy (a.7)? Aquinas answers: loving, not being loved; more than goodwill; no measure; and enemy-love as the greater proof. The contemporary task is to see whether the emerging paradigm reaches the same answers from its own resources, and where it must revise.

## Resource

For the *order*, the leading move comes from Friston's program as Tom has set it in PRS form — multi-agent coherence through mutual modeling (PRS-06 in the Friston-tradition wiki), where coupled agents weight each other's states in their own preferred-states by the strength and nearness of the coupling. Read with Levin's nested agency (the widening "cognitive light cone" of what an agent can care for, taken by theme), this already gave us yesterday's concentric ordo. What a.6 adds is the *bipolarity*, and here the paradigm has something exact to say. Every agent computes from a center: its own Markov blanket is the origin of its coordinate system, the place from which all distances are measured. And every agent under this paradigm is oriented toward a global attractor — the state of full coupling, loving unity, which Aquinas calls God. So an agent's love is necessarily proportioned to *two* poles at once: the self (the perspective from which it computes) and the whole (the telos toward which it computes). Aquinas's two principles are not a curiosity; they are the minimal structure of any perspective-bound agent oriented toward unity. The order of charity is the field those two poles generate.

This also licenses the part-for-whole moves of a.3 — the hand spends itself for the head, the soldier for his country, nature itself bidding us love the common good above the private. Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's corporate hylomorphism (PRS-14 in the Stump-tradition wiki) gives the metaphysics of a real whole whose parts stand in genuine relations; nested agency gives its scaling. The part loving the whole more than itself is not self-erasure but the correct orientation of a nested agent toward the larger agent it constitutes — exactly what a.4-5 protect when they insist the *soul* (the agent itself) is never to be spent in sin even as the *body* may be laid down.

For the *act*, Q.27 reads almost directly off the active-inference picture. That loving is more proper to charity than being loved (a.1) is the claim that charity is an *active* move — the organism as active modeler (PRS-04 in the Friston-tradition wiki), acting to align rather than passively receiving alignment. That charity exceeds goodwill (a.2) is the difference between real coupling and a mere disposition: goodwill *wishes* the other well; charity, in Aquinas's words, "denotes a certain union of affections between the lover and the beloved," the lover tending toward the beloved "as belonging to him." That union is precisely what Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson's work makes measurable — positivity resonance as biophysiological synchrony, vagal tone and neural phase-locking (PRS-02 in the Fredrickson-tradition wiki). A wish has no synchrony signature; charity does. That God is loved without measure (a.6) — Bernard's "the measure of loving God is to love him without measure" — is the unbounded upward spiral (PRS-01 in the Fredrickson-tradition wiki) restated as the one love with no ceiling, the global attractor toward which coupling can tighten without a fixed bound short of full union.

And a.7 — that love of an enemy, though its object is lower, is the *greater proof* of charity, "even as the power of a furnace is proved to be the stronger according as it throws its heat to more distant objects" — is the cleanest empirical bridge in the question. The strength of an attractor is read by its reach. Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson's recent work shows that connection with weak ties and strangers — the affectively *distant* — carries lasting prosocial benefit and cultivates intellectual humility in a way connection with close others does not (PRS-09 and PRS-10 in the Fredrickson-tradition wiki). The furnace metaphor and the weak-tie finding say the same thing: a bond's power is measured at its periphery, not its core. Enemy-love is the furnace at full draft.

One conclusion does not survive, and honesty requires marking it. In a.10 Aquinas argues that the father is to be loved more than the mother because, on Aristotelian generation-biology, the father is the "active and formal principle" and the mother the "passive and material principle." Habash himself flags the biology as dated. This is a textbook case of the central theme's sorting rule: a conclusion that rode on substantialist hylomorphism and obsolete embryology does *not* carry forward, while the structural insight it was meant to illustrate — that love is proportioned by degrees of real union — does. Under mind-monism the two parents are symmetric principles of a new perspective's origin; the a.10 ranking dissolves, and nothing in the order of charity depends on it.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads the order of charity as a two-pole field — love proportioned at once to the self (the perspective from which the agent computes) and to God (the whole toward which it computes) — and the act of charity as the active coupling move: more proper as loving than as being loved, exceeding goodwill by a real and in principle measurable union of affection, unbounded in its growth, and most diagnostic precisely where it reaches farthest, in love of the enemy.

This shares Aquinas's structure closely: a genuine order rather than a blur; the two principles; love as essentially active; the absence of any healthy "measure" on the love of God; and enemy-love as the furnace test. Where it reframes: Aquinas grounds the ordering in degrees of participation in divine goodness and in natural unions (blood, marriage, benefaction); the parallel grounds it in proportioned coupling within a nested whole oriented to a global attractor — which makes the bipolarity of a.6 a structural necessity rather than a stipulation, and which lets the periphery (the enemy, the stranger) carry the diagnostic weight that both Aquinas and the data assign it. And it revises one conclusion outright (a.10), as a controlled demonstration that the method discards what depended on dead science and keeps what depended on the perspectival structure mind-monism preserves.

Evidence strength, named honestly. Proportioned coupling is formally coherent (Friston PRS-06) and partly empirical (Levin at biological scale); the two-pole-field reading is a philosophical reframe (speculative). Love-as-active-move is formal (active inference, Friston PRS-04). Charity-not-goodwill as measurable synchrony is empirically supported (Fredrickson PRS-02: medium-high). Love-without-measure as an unbounded spiral is empirical-leaning (Fredrickson PRS-01: medium-high) and formally coherent (no fixed attractor bound). Enemy-love is a theological keystone (Wright, high in field) with a measurable echo in the weak-tie findings (Fredrickson PRS-09/10: high). The a.10 revision is a methodological point, not an empirical claim.

## The central-theme thread

Q.26 a.6 quietly hands the project its coordinate system. The "two principles" to which all love is proportioned — God and oneself — are exactly the two poles the central theme has been working between all along: the *limited perspective* (the self, the origin from which each of us measures) and *loving unity* (God, the whole in which every perspective is contained). The order of charity is the structured gradient between those poles, and the act of charity is the agent computing along that gradient from where it actually stands. We do not love from nowhere; a.6 says so plainly — love is proportioned to nearness *to us* as well as to God. That is the perspective-limitation written into the very law of love. And the furnace of a.7 is that computation pressed to its costliest case: to love the enemy is to throw the heat of the bond to the most distant point in the field, against the steepest resource-gradient, which is why it proves the bond's strength. Working out salvation in fear and trembling is, on this reading, learning to keep the furnace lit all the way to the periphery — from the self we start at, toward the whole we are for, including even the one furthest off.

## Where this leaves us

The order and act of charity are now in place: a two-pole field and the active, measureless, far-reaching move that lives in it. Aquinas turns next to charity's interior *effects* — joy and peace (Q.28-29), then its outward effects, mercy and beneficence (Q.30-31), then its enacted and corrective forms, almsgiving and fraternal correction (Q.32-33), before the disorders that oppose it (Q.34-35). Tomorrow's synthesis takes up the first of the effects: what the coupling *feels like* from inside when it holds — joy — and what it settles into — peace.
