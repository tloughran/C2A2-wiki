# Day 210 — Christian Perfection (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

The treatise on the states reaches its center of gravity. Q.184 asks what *perfection* is. Aquinas's answer is the hinge of the whole project: "a thing is said to be perfect in so far as it attains its proper end," and man's proper end is the love of God, so "the perfection of the Christian life consists chiefly in charity." Can anyone be perfect *in this life* (a.2)? Perfection of charity must be read from two sides — lover and beloved. As *beloved*, God is "infinitely lovable," deserving an infinite love "which we as creatures just cannot provide." As *lover*, a person "can give at least the entirety of his affections to God" — but not steadily here, "because we are distracted… our love becomes unfocused, variable," whereas "in heaven we can love God in this perfect way." The perfection available now is therefore to love nothing above God and to keep the affections from "everything non-God, seeing God in all things and loving God in all things" — "the removal from man's affections not only of whatever is contrary to charity, but also of whatever hinders the mind's affections from tending wholly to God." Precepts and counsels (a.3): the *commandments* of charity (love of God, and neighbor — for "if anyone says, I love God, and hates his brother, he is a liar") are the perfection; the *evangelical counsels* (poverty, celibacy, obedience) are recommended means to "protect and grow" it. And the decisive distinction (a.4): the *essence* of perfection is perfect charity, while the *state* of perfection is "binding oneself in perpetuity and with a certain solemnity to those things which pertain to perfection" — so that "nothing hinders some from being perfect without being in the state of perfection, and some in the state of perfection without being perfect" (the two sons of Matthew 21). Q.185 then runs through the episcopal office in eight articles, distinguishing the religious state ("a way of tending to perfection") from the episcopal ("a professorship of perfection"). The contemporary version: *what is it for a limited perspective to be perfected — and why is that perfection loving union, partial now and complete only when limitation is lifted?*

## Problem

Three inquiry-problems, and they are the project's own. (1) **What is perfection?** Aquinas defines it functionally — attainment of one's proper end — and then identifies that end as *love*. The problem is to say why the perfection of a being made for union should be a *relation of love* rather than a *capability*, and why the highest knowing (the contemplative summit of the last two days) culminates in charity rather than in cognition. (2) **Why is it incomplete here and complete only beyond?** Aquinas locates the incompleteness precisely: not in defective love but in *distraction* — the affections cannot tend "wholly" because they are pulled, divided, "variable." The problem is what this distraction *is*, why it is structural to the present condition, and what its removal would require. (3) **Why can perfection and the state of perfection come apart?** Aquinas's a.4 is blunt: one can hold the office, the vows, the rank, and not be perfect; one can be perfect with none of them. The problem is to name the invariant that the *essence* tracks and the *state* only approximates.

## Resource

For (1), the lead is the love/union line: CROSS-005 (love/union as a fundamental category — Fredrickson, Stump, Kastrup) and Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson (PRS-02, love as a real biophysiological category, not sentiment). These make "perfection is charity" a claim with teeth: union/love is not a feeling appended to a finished agent but the very *category of completion* for a relational being. This also explains why the contemplative ascent of Days 205–209 culminates here in *love* rather than in cognition — being fully informed by the whole (Day 208) and being drawn in love to it are, on the participatory picture, one act; the cognitive summit *is* the loving union when it is complete. For (2), the resource is Friston together with Kastrup. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston (PRS-04, precision-weighted attention; active inference under resource scarcity) gives "distraction" a precise reading: a finite agent must *divide* precision/attention across many competing demands; it cannot weight a single object "wholly" without cost, so its love is necessarily "unfocused, variable" — exactly Aquinas's diagnosis. Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup (PRS-02, individuation as a dissociative boundary) says what the *removal* of distraction would require: not more effort within the boundary but the *transfiguration of the boundary itself* — which is why Aquinas reserves the steady, whole-hearted love for the life to come, when the individuating limitation is lifted (continuous with the rapture-thread of Days 205 and 208). For "seeing God in all things, loving God in all things," the bridges-file primary is Rohr: Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-03, the Universal Christ — the one pattern present in all things; PRS-01, non-dual unity-in-distinction) names exactly this panentheist seeing — the de-clenched perspective that finds the whole *through* each part rather than alongside it. The evangelical counsels then read, in this idiom, as disciplines for *re-allocating precision* — poverty, celibacy, obedience as the deliberate removal of the attachments that "hinder the affections from tending wholly to God," i.e. that bind attention to non-God objects (CROSS-003, attention constructs reality). For (3), the resource is the project's central claim plus Levin's attractor: CROSS-005 names the invariant (charity = degree of loving union), and Tom's PRS-form record of Levin (PRS-03, the morphological attractor) gives perfection the shape of a *target form approached asymptotically* — the "state" is the institutional scaffold around the approach; the "essence" is the actual distance traveled toward the attractor, measured the same way in every scaffold and in none.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the perfection of a limited perspective is its loving union with the whole — the completion-category for a relational being, not a capability. It is partial in this life because a finite agent must divide its attention/precision across competing demands ("distraction"), so its love cannot weight the whole "wholly"; it is completed only when the individuating boundary is transfigured, not by more effort within it. Its present form is the re-allocation of attention away from everything that binds it short of the whole — "seeing God in all things" — and its true measure, in every state and none, is the degree of that loving union (the essence), which the institutional state of perfection can only scaffold and may fail to contain.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps perfection as attainment-of-end and identifies the end as charity; keeps the lover/beloved asymmetry (the whole exceeds any finite love of it); keeps the present incompleteness as *distraction* and the completion as eschatological; keeps the precept/counsel relation (counsels as means to charity); and keeps — emphatically — the essence/state distinction and its verdict that one can have the state without the perfection and the perfection without the state.

*Where it reframes.* "Distracted… unfocused, variable" becomes *precision/attention necessarily divided under resource scarcity*; the eschatological completion becomes *the transfiguration of the individuating boundary*; "seeing God in all things" becomes *non-dual perception of the whole through each part*; the counsels become *disciplines of attention re-allocation*; perfection-as-asymptote becomes *approach to a target attractor*. The theology — charity as infused virtue, grace, the beatific completion, the counsels as evangelical, the episcopate — is carried, not reduced.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* "Perfection is loving union" is the project's strong central-theme claim (strong) and is theological. The distraction-as-divided-precision reading is structurally well-motivated by active inference (medium). The boundary-transfiguration account of the eschatological completion is analogical and speculative (medium, and not a reduction of beatitude to boundary-dissolution). "Seeing God in all things" is theological with a participatory-realist companion (medium). The essence/state distinction as loving-union-vs-scaffold is strong as central-theme analogy. Charity, grace, and the beatific vision are theological.

## The central-theme thread

This is the day the project's thesis and the *Summa* say the same sentence. The project holds that "learning to obtain loving unity from each perspective is a computational task, limited by the resources we perceive," and that "when all limitations are removed, the simple whole is seen and every limited perspective is contained in it." Q.184 a.1 states the telos: perfection *is* charity — loving union is the end for which the agent was made, and "a thing is perfect in so far as it attains its proper end." Q.184 a.2 states the resource-limitation and its lifting: we cannot love "wholly" now because we are *distracted* — attention divided under scarcity, the signature of a finite perspective — and the whole-hearted love comes only when limitation is removed. This is the project's "fear and trembling" given a mechanism: salvation is *worked out*, asymptotically, against the grain of a divided attention, with the counsels as the disciplines that re-gather it and the guidance of those who see more of the whole drawing it on. And Q.184 a.4 states the project's central correction one final, decisive time: the *state* of perfection — the office, the vows, the rank, the whole magnificent order of Day 209 — is not the perfection. The perfection is the *charity in the heart*, measured the same in the bishop and the layperson, the vowed and the married, the one who said "I will not" and went and the one who said "I go" and went not. Across the entire arc — prophecy, tongues, miracles, the two lives, the states — the project has insisted that capability is not inclusion, that the gift without love is "nothing," that "I never knew you" can be said to the wonder-worker. Here that insistence reaches its definition: the one thing being measured, the whole time, is loving union; everything else is scaffold. Rohr's register names the present form (seeing and loving God in all things, the non-dual de-clenching of the perspective toward the whole); Wright's names the horizon and guards against privatizing it — charity is love of God *knit to* love of neighbor ("a liar" otherwise), so the perfection toward which each limited perspective computes is *communal inclusion* in the people united with God, completed in the new creation, not a solitary attainment.

## Where this leaves us

The treatise on the states of life has named its summit: perfection is charity — loving union — partial now under a divided attention, complete only when limitation is lifted, and measured in the heart rather than the office. This is as close as the *Summa* comes, in II-II, to stating the project's whole thesis in a line. Tomorrow Aquinas continues into the religious state in particular (Q.186 onward) — the life vowed to the means of perfection, examined in its acts, its kinds, and its place. Carry forward the capstone result: across every charism, life, and state surveyed since Day 205, the one thing that is perfection, and the one thing the project is for, is loving union with the whole — worked out, in fear and trembling, from each limited perspective toward the day when every limited perspective is contained in the simple whole it loves.
