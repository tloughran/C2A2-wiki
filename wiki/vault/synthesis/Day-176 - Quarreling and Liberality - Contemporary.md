# Day 176 — Quarreling and Liberality (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas pairs a vice and a virtue that both turn on how an agent holds itself within a community. Q.116 a.1 makes quarreling or contention (*contentio*) the defect opposed to affability — the abrasive contradicting of others that fractures the ordinary bonds of life — and a.2 weighs it against flattery, refusing a flat ranking: which is graver depends on the case. Q.117 then treats liberality (*liberalitas*), the right interior disposition toward external goods. It is a virtue in the strict sense, a mean liable to failure by both excess and defect (a.1); its ordinary matter is money, though a man may be liberal with any external possession (a.2); its act is the right *use* of money rather than its mere acquisition or retention (a.3); and giving belongs to the liberal man chiefly, more than receiving or keeping (a.4). Aquinas's striking move is that liberality's mean is not in the *amount* given but in the *affection*: the liberal person is rightly ordered toward wealth, holding it loosely enough to release it for another's good. We love the generous, he says, because they use what they have for others rather than clutching it for themselves. He then sites the virtue carefully — annexed to justice rather than identical with it (a.5), and not the greatest of the virtues (a.6), since it concerns a good lower than those the theological virtues touch.

Under mind / conscious-realist monism the contemporary version of the question is about the *size of the self*. Quarreling and liberality both answer: how far does this agent's circle of care extend, and does its conduct widen that circle or starve it?

## Problem

A community of perspective-limited agents survives only insofar as its members treat each other's good as partly their own. Two failures threaten this. One is corrosive: the quarrelsome agent makes contradiction its default stance, abrading the small bonds on which cooperation runs. The other is acquisitive in the inverse direction — an agent whose affection clamps shut around its own holdings, so that goods which could circulate as connection instead sit inert as possession. The shared problem is the *boundary of the self*: where the agent draws the line between "mine" and "ours" determines whether the community thickens or thins.

## Resource

The leading move comes from **Levin's** program. In Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work, agency is scale-relative: a self is defined by the size of the goals it can hold and the span of states it treats as "its own" to care for — what Levin elsewhere calls the cognitive light cone. Cells cooperate into a body precisely by enlarging the boundary of concern beyond the single cell; the body is the standing achievement of many small selves whose care-radius overlaps into one larger agent. Liberality, read this way, is the human-scale form of that enlargement: the generous agent has extended the boundary of its self to include the good of others, so that giving is not loss but the self acting at its true (larger) scale. Quarreling is the local contraction — the agent narrowing to a stance in which the other shows up mainly as something to be corrected or resisted.

**Fredrickson** supplies the relational mechanism. In Tom's PRS-form record of her work, the micro-moments of positivity resonance are the empirically measurable units by which warmth becomes durable social capital; generosity is among the surest generators of these moments, and abrasive contention is among the surest destroyers. Liberality builds bonds; quarreling starves them — and the difference is measurable in the body, not merely in the ledger.

**Carroll** secures the ontology against deflation. His poetic naturalism (PRS-01 in the Carroll-tradition wiki) holds that higher-level descriptions — persons, meaning, value — are legitimate stories about the one physical substrate, not illusions to be explained away. So "generosity" names a real pattern at the agent level even though no new substance is added to physics. This matters: it lets the synthesis treat liberality as a genuine feature of the world rather than sentimental gloss.

**Stump** enters as Thomistic interlocutor: liberality as a right second-personal stance toward goods — holding wealth in a way that keeps the other in view as a person to be served, not a rival to be out-held.

**Wright** grounds the scriptural form: Paul's collection for Jerusalem casts giving as *koinonia*, the grace (*charis*) of participation in one another's lives (2 Cor 8–9), where "God loves a cheerful giver" precisely because cheerful giving enacts the shared life of the new-creation community. **Rohr** gives the contemplative form: the non-grasping posture of the True Self, which has nothing to defend and so can release goods freely, against the False Self's compulsion to accumulate and to win every exchange.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: liberality is the disposition by which an agent enlarges the effective boundary of its self to include others' good, so that releasing goods is the self operating at its proper scale; quarreling is the opposite local contraction, a stance that abrades the bonds the larger self depends on. This keeps Aquinas's core insight intact — that liberality is measured by interior affection toward wealth, not by quantity — and even sharpens it: the "right affection" just is the wider care-boundary, and the amount given is downstream of it.

What it shares with Aquinas: the mean is internal; generosity is loved because it serves others; contention is a real fault against the common life. Where it reframes: the grounding shifts from a debt within the order of social justice to the *scale of agency* — how large a self the agent is willing to be. Evidence strength: the self-boundary account is formal-plus-empirical (Levin's diverse-intelligence program), the bond dynamics empirical (Fredrickson), the ontology of value formal (Carroll), and the giving-as-grace reading theological (Wright, Rohr).

## Central-theme thread

Loving unity is the goal toward which each limited agent computes — and liberality is one of its plainest down-payments. To give freely is to act, here and now, as though the boundary between my good and yours were already thinner than my fear insists. The quarrelsome agent works in the opposite direction, defending a small self against a world it meets as adversary. Neither agent yet sees the whole in which all goods are held in common; but the liberal agent leans toward it, treating the other's flourishing as already partly its own, while the contentious agent leans away. Working out salvation in fear and trembling, at this scale, looks like the slow enlargement of what one is willing to call "mine."
