# Day 158 — Contemporary Parallel: Religion as the Virtue of the Perspective Toward the Whole

## Frame

Q.80 enumerates the *potential parts of justice* — virtues annexed to justice that fall short of its strict definition (rendering due *according to equality*) because the debt is unpayable: religion (toward God), piety (toward parents and country), observance (toward those in dignity), gratitude (toward benefactors), truthfulness (toward all in the matter of what we say of ourselves), friendship (toward those we associate with), liberality (toward those in need), epieikeia (the equity that corrects strict justice). Q.81 opens the treatise on religion itself: religion as a virtue (a.1), as one virtue (a.2-3), as a special virtue (a.4), as a *moral* virtue (a.5 — its object is the act of worship, not God Himself who is the proper object of the theological virtues), as the highest of the moral virtues (a.6, because its object is most excellent — that which directs to God), as identical with sanctity (a.8). This is the pivot of the entire second-half treatise: from justice between humans to justice-toward-God, of which everything in II-II Q.82 onward will be the unfolding.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem, in both frames: *the rendering of due is the structure of justice, but some recipients of what is due cannot be paid back in kind (God, parents, country, benefactors) — so what is the moral virtue that renders due in the face of unpayable asymmetry, and what is its highest form?* Aquinas's answer is the architecture of Q.80-81: the *potential* parts of justice (annexed virtues that share justice's form — rendering due to another — but cannot meet its strict equality), with religion as the highest because its object (worship, which directs to God) is most excellent. The contemporary problem is to recover the same architecture without the substance ontology Aquinas inherits — to ask what *religion as the highest moral virtue* looks like when the metaphysics is mind / conscious-realist monism.

## Resource

Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr's work supplies the heart of the contemporary reframe. PRS-03 (Universal Christ) is the formal claim that the divine pattern is the actual structure of reality from the beginning — Christ "first incarnate in creation, second incarnate in Jesus" (per Rohr) — so the field-of-which-agents-are-expressions is not foreign to us but our most intimate ground. PRS-01 (non-dual) names the mode of the religious act under this metaphysics: not the propitiation of a distant authority by a separate agent, but the perspective-limited expression actively *participating in* the whole it has always belonged to. PRS-02 (contemplative tradition) supplies the practical depth: religion's interior dimension (devotion, prayer, contemplation — the territory of Q.82-83 to come tomorrow) is the mode in which the participation is consciously enacted.

Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's work supplies the scriptural and historical ground. PRS-01 (critical realism) preserves the realism Aquinas's argument requires: God is the real horizon, not a projection, even though our access is perspectival. PRS-02 (faithful improvisation) supplies the *form* religion takes in time: covenant-life is the people's improvisational rendering of God's improvised covenant — never simply transactional debt-discharge but a relational form whose specific expressions are improvised in successive cultural contexts. PRS-03 (inaugurated eschatology) names the temporal structure: religion is enacted in the *already-not-yet*, where the whole has decisively entered the part's history (in Christ) but the full alignment is still being worked out. This is the contemporary reading of Aquinas's "religion as the highest moral virtue": the moral form of the people's response to the whole's prior covenanting action.

Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work formalizes the apparatus. PRS-06 (shared generative models) makes religion the polity's collective active inference toward the highest prior — the worship-act is the polity's coordinated re-prior-setting at the level of ultimate concern. PRS-10 (active inference under unpayable debt) handles the asymmetric structure Aquinas names: a religious act is rational under FEP precisely because the agent's model includes the recognition that the source of the goods received cannot be reciprocated in kind, and the appropriate policy is the acknowledgment-and-alignment characteristic of worship (not the transactional exchange characteristic of justice between equals).

Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work supplies the second-personal scaffolding. PRS-11 (second-personal / Franciscan knowledge) is the philosophical claim that some knowledge exists only in trust-relations between persons; God is the supreme case — not objectifiable, known only second-personally. Religion as a moral virtue is the disposition that maintains the agent in the right kind of second-personal availability to the divine. PRS-14/15 (corporate substance) extends this to the polity: religion is the polity's response to its own constitutive ground — the whole the polity is part of, the divine creator-and-redeemer the polity acknowledges as constitutive.

Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work supplies the largest-scale collective-cognition reading. PRS-30 makes coordinated collective cognition real at every scale; religion is then the polity's enacted recognition that there is a scale *beyond* the polity itself to which the polity is in some sense related — the agent-at-maximum-scale that the program of "agency at every scale" leaves room for at the upper end (without committing to any particular theology). The pairing with Kastrup's mind-at-large (PRS-01/02 in the Kastrup-tradition wiki) gives the metaphysical floor: mind is the ontological primitive, the maximum-scale agent is real, and worship is the alignment of the local alter with that primitive ground.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed contemporary parallel preserves Q.80-81 substantially, with the metaphysics of "God as recipient of unpayable debt" recast in mind-monist terms. *Preserved*: religion is a real virtue (Q.81 a.1) — there is a settled disposition that orients the agent rightly toward the whole; it is one virtue (a.2-3) — its object (worship as response to the whole) is unified across its acts; it is a *moral* virtue (a.5) — its object is the act of worship, not the whole itself which is the object of the theological virtues (faith, hope, charity); it is the highest of the moral virtues (a.6) — its object directs the agent's whole life toward the field that is its source and end. *Reframed*: the metaphysics shifts from external transactional debt to participatory alignment — under mind-monism, the whole is not foreign to the agent but the agent's most intimate ground (Rohr Universal Christ; Kastrup mind-at-large; Hoffman conscious-agent network at maximum scale), and religion is the moral virtue of the agent's active acknowledgment of its perspective-limitation and its orientation toward alignment. *Extended*: the unpayable-debt structure is named more precisely as the formal mark of agent-whole asymmetry — what makes religion the *highest* moral virtue is that its object includes everything else (every other virtue is rendered, ultimately, under religion's larger acknowledgment).

Measure against Aquinas: convergence on the placement of religion (highest moral virtue), its formal structure (rendering due to one who cannot be repaid in kind), and its relation to the theological virtues (religion's object is the *act*, theological virtues' object is God Himself). The metaphysics shifts: where Aquinas's frame leaves God as a kind of supreme exterior (Creator, Lord, Judge) whose debt we cannot discharge, the contemporary frame names the relation as participation in a field that is also our ground — closer to Rohr's "we are in Christ before we know it" and to the contemplative tradition Aquinas himself acknowledges (Q.180 to come). The reframe makes religion *more* central to flourishing, not less: the agent's right orientation to the whole is the precondition of every other right orientation.

## The central-theme thread

Q.80-81 is the structural pivot of the central claim. The justice treatise has named the rendering of due between limited perspectives; religion names the rendering of due between the limited perspective and the whole. The structure exactly mirrors the central claim: the perspective-limited agent acknowledges its position; recognizes the whole that contains and exceeds it; and orients its life-policy toward alignment with that whole. The unpayable-debt structure (the *potential*-parts-of-justice classification) is the formal mark of the asymmetry the central claim depends on: the agent owes the whole everything (its very perspective is from the whole) and can render back nothing in kind. Religion is the moral virtue of *that asymmetry rightly oriented*: the perspective's active acknowledgment of its position, expressed in worship. "Working out salvation in fear and trembling" finds its name here — religion is the practical mode in which the working-out is conducted toward the highest pole of the field.
