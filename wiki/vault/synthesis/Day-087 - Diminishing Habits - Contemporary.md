# Day 87 — Diminishing Habits (Contemporary)

## Frame

Q.52 takes up the *increase* of habits — can habits grow stronger, and if so how? Aquinas distinguishes carefully. Habits are *qualities*, not *quantities*, so they do not increase in extension (more parts) the way a quantitative thing grows. They increase rather in *intensity* and in the agent's *participation*: the subject comes to share more deeply, more readily, more efficiently in the quality. The example is striking — fire increases both by spreading and by intensifying; one's knowledge of Latin grows by extension (more vocabulary) and by intensification (more rapid, fluent use). Acts cause this growth, but only when performed with *full intention* and *due intensity* — perfunctory acts neither generate nor strengthen the habit.

Q.53 takes up the inverse: *diminution* and *corruption* of habits. Aquinas argues (a.1) that a habit is corrupted *directly* by a contrary — a false reason corrupts the habit of science; a judgment of reason moving against the virtue, through ignorance, passion or deliberate choice, corrupts the virtue — and *indirectly* through corruption of its subject, which is why bodily habits like health can go both ways while the habits of first principles can go neither. (a.2) Habits diminish by the same causes that corrupt them, diminution being "the road which leads to" corruption. (a.3), the article Habash dwells on, asks whether *mere cessation from act* corrupts or diminishes, and Aquinas's answer is more specific than a simple yes: cessation is a cause only **indirectly**, in the way that removing an obstacle is a cause. Ceasing from the act stops counteracting the contrary agencies that were undermining the habit all along — for moral virtue, the inclination of the sensitive appetite and other external agencies; for intellectual habits, the "strange fancies" that arise in an unexercised imagination. His reply to the first objection states the restriction exactly: heat would be destroyed by ceasing to give heat *only if, for that same reason, cold were to increase*. Habash renders the upshot as the episode's meditation — there is no standing still; at any moment we are increasing or diminishing — and flags at the outset that Q.52-53 are not primarily about temperance, courage and justice but about habits like the knowledge of a science and the bodily quality we call health.

Under conscious-realist monism the Q.52-53 dynamics map almost verbatim onto the contemporary literatures on practice-induced plasticity, extinction, disuse atrophy, and consolidation. The strikingly correct mechanical content of the medieval account — *intensity not extension*; *full-intention acts only*; *contrary acts as the specific mechanism of corruption* — recovers as precision-weighted plasticity, attentional gating of consolidation, and counter-conditioning. The episode is, in this respect, a small triumph for the medieval method: armchair phenomenology + careful conceptual analysis gets the structure right that the experimental literature would later validate.

## Problem

The shared problem: **what are the dynamics of habit-stabilization and habit-destabilization, and what mechanisms account for each?** The Q.52-53 articles isolate three substantive claims that any complete theory of habit-dynamics must vindicate. First, that habit-growth is *intensive* rather than *extensive* — habits do not get bigger; their subject becomes more deeply patterned by them. Second, that *only full-intention acts produce growth* — distracted, perfunctory, or coerced acts neither grow nor maintain. Third, that habits *decline by contrary act* — with omission and environmental disruption not standing beside it as further mechanisms but working *through* it, since on Aquinas's account cessation only removes the guard on a contrary agency already present. (The clause that stood here also named infused virtue as a special case with differing dynamics; Q.53 makes no such claim, and the limb is escalated — see the reviewer note in §Solution (a).)

The contemporary problem is the same, and it now has substantial empirical answer. Practice-induced plasticity is real but not flat: gains scale with attentional engagement (Treisman, Posner), affective stake (LeDoux, Pessoa), and prediction-error during the act (Schultz, Friston). Extinction is real but does not erase — the original disposition persists below threshold and can be reinstated (Bouton). Counter-conditioning is more durable than mere disuse. The dual-process distinction between habitual and goal-directed control (Daw, Niv, Dayan) lets us specify *which* stabilizations resist disruption and which can be undone by deliberate counter-action.

The deeper question Q.53 raises — what does it mean for a *virtue* (as opposed to a mere skill) to diminish? — sharpens under the central theme. A diminished virtue is not just a forgotten habit. It is an attenuation of the agent's disposition toward loving unity under conditions of perspective-limitation. The contemporary frame must say *how* this attenuation works mechanically and *why* certain dispositions admit attenuation that others (the infused virtues) do not.

## Resource

**Friston's precision-weighted hierarchical learning** supplies the formal machinery for Q.52 a.1's *intensity not extension* claim. In a hierarchical generative model, learning a domain does not add a new model; it sharpens existing priors. Precision-weighting concentrates the agent's processing on the relevant variables. Full-attention acts produce strong prediction-error signals that drive consolidation; perfunctory acts produce attenuated signals and weak consolidation. Friston PRS-04 (organism as active modeler) and the precision-weighting literature directly underwrite the Aquinas claim that *full-intention* acts grow the habit while perfunctory acts do not.

**Fredrickson's longitudinal practice data** provides the empirical foothold for both Q.52 (growth) and Q.53 (decline). Fredrickson PRS-01 on loving-kindness meditation documents the dose-response of sustained practice on vagal tone, attentional defaults, and positivity resonance. *The record carries only the growth half.* Its Resource and Solution run from cultivation to upward spirals of health and connection; it says nothing about what happens when practice lapses, so the decline side of Q.53 cannot be sourced to it. The return-toward-baseline-on-lapse claim rests instead on the wider practice-cessation and detraining literature, and is asserted here at that literature's strength, not at PRS-01's. What PRS-01 does establish, at **High** confidence, is the positive limb: habitus is sustained by ongoing congruent act. Fredrickson's "upward spiral" (positivity-resonance → broaden-and-build → more positivity-resonance) gives the *positive feedback* version of Aquinas's "habit-strengthens-with-act" dynamic.

**Hawkins's reference-frame consolidation** is the cortical-level mechanism for Q.52 a.2's "habit grows by repeated act with due intensity." Reference frames consolidate with repetition under attention; without attention, the same repetition produces less consolidation. Hawkins's account gives a clean cortical-circuit story for the medieval "due intensity" requirement.

**Levin's bioelectric pattern dynamics** provides the morphogenetic-scale account of pattern stability and destabilization. Bioelectric patterns can be re-routed; they can also stabilize a new attractor under sustained perturbation. The Q.52-53 dynamic of "stabilize-by-congruent-act; destabilize-by-contrary-act" recovers at the cellular-tissue level as bioelectric attractor switching.

**McGilchrist on attentional defaults and characterological diminution** supplies the hemispheric-level dynamic. The agent's habitual mode of attention (left-instrumental vs. right-contextual) is itself a habit, modifiable by sustained practice. *Disuse* of right-hemispheric attention produces a left-hemispheric-dominant character; *contrary use* (sustained instrumental focus) produces the same drift more rapidly. McGilchrist's clinical account of attentional drift maps onto Q.53's habit-decline mechanism.

**Stump's analytic-Thomistic work on acquired versus infused virtue** is keystone for Q.52-53 read as moral psychology. PRS-24 in the Stump-tradition wiki draws exactly the distinction Q.52-53 turns on — acquired virtues are *built up by practice*, infused virtues are *received in an instant with grace*, and the two operate at different levels of the soul's powers. That is Q.52's growth-by-repeated-act and Q.53 a.3's infused-virtue exception stated as a single contemporary thesis. PRS-09 (virtue as cooperative causality) supplies the complementary half: the Holy Spirit perfects the soul's *natural* powers so that the resulting willing is genuinely the agent's own — which is why the infused virtue's sustaining cause is not the agent's accumulated act-merit, and why its diminution runs by severance rather than by lapse. PRS-23's two-stage quiescence/infusion model gives the mechanism behind that asymmetry, locating agency and divine priority in different causal roles rather than in competition for one. The contemporary frame can extend this by reading the participatory substrate as the wider mind under conscious-realist monism — without contradicting the Thomistic position.

**The behavioural-economics and clinical literatures on counter-conditioning, extinction, and relapse** (Bouton; Marlatt; Volkow) supply the mechanism for Q.53 a.1 (contrary acts as the specific destabilizer). Extinction does not erase the original disposition; it builds a competing one. Counter-conditioning is more reliable than mere disuse for habit suppression. The medieval claim that contrary acts diminish a habit recovers as the formal claim about competing-learner dynamics in the agent's policy space.

**Wright on perseverance and the formed disciple** anchors the theological dynamic of Q.53 a.3 (infused virtue's special dynamics). Wright's reading of Pauline ethics emphasises that the *life-in-the-Spirit* is sustained by ongoing relational participation rather than by accumulated act-merit. The Pauline imagery of *abiding* in Christ (John 15; Romans 8) gives the Christological shape: the disposition is sustained by ongoing connection to its source, and lost specifically by *severance* from that source rather than by ordinary disuse. This matches Aquinas's claim that infused virtue is not diminished by partial acts but by acts directly contrary to it.

**Rohr on contemplative practice and the rhythm of formation and fall** completes the theological frame. *Falling Upward* and *Immortal Diamond* treat the spiritual journey as marked by alternating consolidation and rupture; the contemplative habit deepens through ongoing practice and is interrupted not by ordinary disuse but by the agent's withdrawal from the contemplative attention itself. The dynamics Rohr describes line up with Q.53's "virtue's diminution is specifically a moral matter" claim.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

**(a) Shared with Aquinas.** Habit-growth is *intensive*: it is the increasing deep patterning of the agent by the disposition, not the addition of new components. Only acts performed with full intention (attentional engagement, affective stake, predictive-error-rich engagement with the act's object) produce growth. Habits decline by contrary act — and this is the whole of the shared claim, because for Aquinas omission is not a second mechanism alongside it. Cessation diminishes a habit only by removing what was holding a contrary agency off (Q.53 a.3; the heat-and-cold reply). Where a contrary agency is standing by, disuse is decay; where none is, ceasing does nothing on its own. That reduction is a substantive commitment and the contemporary literature bears on it directly, in both directions — see (b2).

*Reviewer note, 2026-09-13:* the paragraph that stood here also credited Aquinas with an **infused-virtue exception at Q.53 a.3** — that infused virtue cannot be diminished by lapse but only by directly contrary acts, its cause not being the agent's own acts. **Q.53 contains no such claim in any of its three articles**, which run on science, health and moral virtue; Habash's opening flags that these questions are not primarily about the virtues at all. The claim Aquinas does make in that neighbourhood lives elsewhere — the acquired/infused distinction at I-II Q.51 a.4 and Q.63, and the argument that charity is not decreased by remiss acts because its cause is God at II-II Q.24 a.10. The sentence has been withdrawn from the shared column here, but the limb it supports runs through §Resource, (b), the central-theme thread and five bridges, and unpicking it is a rewrite rather than a repair. **Escalated 2026-09-13; not removed.** The material may well be worth keeping as the commentary's own extension — it is simply not this article's content.

**(b) Where it reframes.** The medieval *quality/quantity* distinction recovers as the architectural distinction between *parameter values* (which sharpen/decay) and *parameter counts* (which would be quantitative growth — the wrong category). The "full-intention" requirement recovers as precision-weighted attentional engagement during the act. The "contrary act" mechanism recovers as competing-learner dynamics in policy space. Note what this does to a.3 rather than merely restating it: Bouton's result that extinction builds a competing association rather than erasing the original *is* Aquinas's indirect-cause reduction, arrived at independently and by experiment. Apparent decay-from-disuse turns out, in the animal and the clinic, to be the accrual of a contrary. The medieval armchair reached the right shape here and the commentary should say so plainly instead of filing it under reframing. *(The sentence that followed, reading the "special dynamics of infused virtue" as participation in a wider field rather than accumulated act-merit, is retained but is escalated — see the reviewer note in (a). It is a real claim of Aquinas's, made elsewhere; it is not a claim of Q.53.)*

**(b2) Where it is superseded.** The reduction in (b) is *bounded*, and Aquinas states it unboundedly. His reply to the first objection is the load-bearing sentence — heat is destroyed by ceasing to give heat only if, for that same reason, cold increases — and generalized to habits it says there is no such thing as decay by non-use alone: every loss must be some contrary's gain. **That universal is false, and it fails inside the scope Aquinas himself sets at a.1**, where bodily habits such as health are named as proper subjects of corruption. Detraining is the plain counterexample: cardiorespiratory capacity, strength, and motor-skill precision decline on cessation of training along well-characterized time-courses, with no contrary training and no competing habit accruing — the acquired parameter simply reverts toward an untrained set-point. The same holds for the perceptual and motor consolidation literatures, where an unrehearsed skill degrades without being displaced by a rival skill. So: what survives is the mechanism for *character* habits, where a contrary agency genuinely is always standing by — the sensitive appetite is not idle, and McGilchrist PRS-15's point is that a modern attentional environment is an active rewiring rather than a vacuum, which is Aquinas's "external agencies" with a name and a measurement. What is retired is the claim that this exhausts the field. Aquinas gives himself a partial escape in the reply to the third objection, granting that the sensitive part is in time and that "time makes us forget" — but that concession sits in a reply while the corpus makes indirect causation the whole account, so the concession does not carry the weight the contemporary evidence puts on it.

*Sourcing, stated honestly:* **this supersession has no register anchor.** The Friston, Fredrickson, Hawkins, McGilchrist, Levin, Stump, Rohr, Kastrup, Hoffman, Carroll, Wolfram and Wright registers were searched 2026-09-13 for *detrain*, *disuse*, *atroph*, *decay*, *forgetting curve*, *extinction*, *relapse*, *cessation*, *lapse* and *decondition*; the only hit bearing on learning at all is Hoffman's "swift extinction," which is about the fate of veridical perception under selection and is not a learning-theoretic result. Published literature is the anchor. Fredrickson PRS-01 remains scoped to the growth limb only, as a prior pass correctly established — do not re-recruit it here.

**(c) Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.** *Firm*: dose-response of practice-induced affective stabilization (Fredrickson; Davidson). *Firm*: extinction-as-competing-learner rather than erasure (Bouton); counter-conditioning durability literature. *Firm*: attentional gating of consolidation (Treisman, Posner; Friston precision-weighting). *Empirically supported*: reference-frame consolidation under attention (Hawkins). *Empirically supported at growing scales*: bioelectric attractor dynamics (Levin). *Philosophically rigorous, but register-graded Medium on two of three records*: Stump on virtue-maintenance (PRS-09 and PRS-24 are **Medium**; PRS-23 is **High** — the rigor is Stump's published argument, not the records' provenance). *Theological, well-attested*: Wright on abiding-in-Christ as substrate of sustained virtue (canonical works; no register record — see Bridges); Rohr on contemplative-practice dynamics (PRS-02, **High**). *Firm, and the ground of (b2)*: detraining and disuse decay in cardiorespiratory, strength and motor-skill measures, absent any contrary training — the counterexample to the unbounded form of Aquinas's indirect-cause claim. *Speculative-metaphysical*: the conscious-realist-monist reading of infused-virtue's special dynamics as participation in mind-at-large — and note that this limb is now escalated on the separate ground that Q.53 does not contain the article-claim it elaborates.

## The central-theme thread

Q.52-53 specifies the *dynamics* of the central theme's working-out. Loving unity is the computational task, and the agent computes toward it under perspective-limitation. The Q.52-53 dynamics tell us that this computation is *intensive*: the agent does not become a different agent by working out salvation, but is *more deeply patterned* by it. Each full-attention act grows the disposition; each lapse loses ground; each contrary act actively diminishes. The "fear and trembling" of Phil 2:12 makes mechanical sense — the working-out is not safe, not automatic, and not retained against contrary action.

The special dynamics of infused virtue carry the central theme's most distinctive claim. Some dispositions toward loving unity are not built up by the agent's acts alone. They are participations in a wider mind whose perspective exceeds the agent's. Such dispositions are diminished only by the agent's *active turning away* (acts directly contrary), not by ordinary lapse. This is the central theme's "guidance from those who perceive more of the whole" in the dynamic register: the agent that abides in the wider participation retains the disposition; the agent that severs the participation loses it. Wright's *abiding-in-Christ* and Rohr's contemplative-attention give the theological-and-contemplative texture; Kastrup's de-dissociative widening of the alter into mind-at-large gives the metaphysical-monist gloss.

## Where this leaves us

Q.52-53 supply the dynamics of growth and decay; Q.54-55 (tomorrow) take up the *distinction* of habits — how habits are individuated by their objects, and what specifically constitutes the *virtues* as a distinguished class of habit. The next move is from *general dynamics of habit* to *the specific kind of habit that is virtue*. The central theme will sharpen as the synthesis turns from habit-as-such to habit-ordered-to-loving-unity. The empirical literatures on practice-induced plasticity, attentional consolidation, and counter-conditioning will yield ground to Stump (virtue-as-formal-disposition), Fredrickson (positivity-as-virtue-substrate), McGilchrist (character-as-hemispheric-balance), and Wright + Rohr (virtue-in-Christ as the cosmic-scale formation).
