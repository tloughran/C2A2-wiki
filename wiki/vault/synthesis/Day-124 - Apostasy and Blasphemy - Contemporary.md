# Day 124 — Contemporary Parallel: The Object of the Will Is a Claim About the Whole

## Frame

Apostasy (Q.12) and blasphemy in general (Q.13) look like a catalogue of sins; underneath they are a single thesis about what an agent's orientation *is*. Apostasy is "standing away from" (apo-stasis) — a withdrawal complete enough to involve not just the will but the intellect, "a turning away from the faith itself." Blasphemy is "disparagement of some surpassing goodness, especially that of God": denying what befits God or affirming what does not, literally (from the Greek) "harming the reputation of." And the article that makes the whole question land is Q.13 a.4: the damned blaspheme even without words, because "this detestation of the divine justice is in them the interior blasphemy of the heart" — the very *object of their will* testifies that God is not their greatest good.

The contemporary version: what does an agent's set-point — what it actually values most — encode about its model of reality? Mind / conscious-realist monism answers that the object of the will is not a private preference riding on top of a neutral world-model; it *is* a claim about what is fundamental.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem is the relation between valuing and representing. Aquinas treats blasphemy as opposed to the *confession of faith* — that is, as a kind of false statement about God, "a lie, an untruth." But a.4 pushes further: there can be blasphemy with no statement at all, lodged purely in the orientation of the will. So the problem is: how can a non-propositional act (setting the will on something) be true or false about anything? In what sense does what you love commit you to a claim about the whole?

A second, smaller problem is political: Aquinas's a.2 of Q.12, that an apostate prince forfeits his subjects' allegiance. This belongs to the dissolved world of excommunication-as-civil-act and, like yesterday's death-penalty article, is named as a departure rather than reframed.

## Resource

The leading resource is Friston's active inference. In the PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-04, and PRS-08 on target morphology as prior belief, in the Friston-tradition wiki), an agent *is* a generative model whose preferred outcomes — its set-point, its priors over which states it expects and seeks — are not separable from its representation of the world. The agent's "valued target" is encoded in the same model that represents reality; to set the system's target is to commit to a structure of expected states. On this picture Aquinas's claim that the will's object can be an "interior blasphemy" is exact: the orientation of the will is a setting in the generative model, and a model whose deepest set-point is something-other-than-the-true-good *is* a model that misrepresents what is fundamental, whether or not the agent ever utters a word.

Hoffman and Kastrup supply the content of the misrepresentation. Blasphemy as "denying what befits God or affirming what does not" is, in the monist register, getting the ontological primitive wrong — and getting it maximally wrong, since God/the ground is what is most fundamental. Hoffman's interface theory (PRS-03, PRS-04 in the Hoffman-tradition wiki) shows that a model can be internally coherent and still systematically wrong about the underlying reality; Kastrup's analytic idealism makes the relevant error precise: to take the transient and dissociated for the fundamental is the deepest possible category mistake about mind-at-large. Blasphemy is the limiting case of that mistake, asserted (in word or in the will's set-point) against the supreme good.

Levin frames apostasy. Complete withdrawal — intellect and will turning from the target — is, in the morphogenetic register (PRS-02, PRS-03 in the Levin-tradition wiki), the loss of a set-point: a system that has left the basin entirely, not merely diverged within it. The "complete vs partial turning" distinction Aquinas insists on (apostasy proper requires the intellect, not just disobedience in particulars) is the difference between losing the target state and erring locally while still oriented toward it.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel: an agent's deepest valuation is a truth-apt commitment about what is fundamental, because under monism valuing and representing are one operation. This shares Aquinas's core claim — blasphemy is a falsehood about the supreme good, and it can live in the will as truly as on the tongue — and gives it a mechanism. The "interior blasphemy of the heart" is a generative model whose highest-precision set-point is fixed on something that is not the ground; such a model is false about reality at its most load-bearing point, and it is false *as lived*, not merely as spoken. Where it reframes Aquinas: he reaches this through faculty-psychology (a disordered will detesting divine justice); the parallel reads will and intellect as aspects of one model, so that "what you ultimately love" and "what you take to be ultimately real" are not two facts but one. The reframe makes a.4 more intelligible, not less: of course the orientation can blaspheme, because the orientation is a claim.

The apostate-prince article (Q.12 a.2) is set aside. The decoupling of civil authority from ecclesial sanction is a fixed contemporary commitment, continuous with yesterday's rejection of coerced belief; Wright's critical realism (no view-from-nowhere licensing one party's dominion-stripping of another) and Rohr's Universal Christ (the pattern at work even in the one who has turned away) reorient the response from forfeiture toward engagement.

Evidence strength: the identity of valuing and representing is formally motivated within active inference (the set-point is part of the model) and philosophically robust (high in field); its theological application — that the will's object blasphemes — is a reframe offered as illumination, not proof. The "maximally-wrong model of the fundamental" reading is metaphysical and speculative. The apostasy-as-lost-set-point analogy is exactly that, an analogy.

## The central-theme thread

This is the will-as-orientation face of the central theme. Each conscious agent is individuated by its perspective, and the deepest fact about a perspective is the target toward which it computes. Loving unity is the telos; the will's set-point is the direction of the computation. To set the will on something-other-than-the-whole is to render, in the only currency that finally counts, a false verdict about what the whole is — "interior blasphemy of the heart." "The damned blaspheme" is the central theme's shadow: a perspective so fixed away from the whole that even its silence testifies against the good. And the converse is the project's hope — the saints "praise him with their voices" because their set-point has come into alignment with the ground, the computation toward loving unity completed. Apostasy and blasphemy mark, with unusual sharpness, that the orientation of a limited perspective is never metaphysically idle: it is always already saying something true or false about the whole that contains it.

## Where this leaves us

The will-as-orientation thesis is now fully on the table. Tomorrow (Q.14-15, the sin against the Holy Spirit and the vices opposed to knowledge and understanding) takes it to its limit: the sin that attacks the very mechanism by which an agent could correct its orientation, and the cognitive failures — blindness of mind, dullness of sense — that flow from a disordered appetite. The self-undermining structure waiting there (a system disabling its own error-correction) is the natural endpoint of an account in which what you love determines what you can see.
