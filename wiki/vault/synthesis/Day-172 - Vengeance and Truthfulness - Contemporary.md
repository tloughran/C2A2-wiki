# Day 172 — Vengeance & Truthfulness (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas's Q.108 treats vengeance: the infliction of penal evil on one who has done wrong. It is lawful — indeed a part of justice — only when administered by legitimate authority, ordered to the correction of the wrongdoer or the protection of the common good, and undertaken with right intention rather than from hatred. Q.109 treats truth (*veritas*) as a moral virtue: the disposition by which a person shows himself in word and deed to be what he is, rendering to others the truth about himself, which is a kind of debt owed within the order of justice.

The contemporary question, under mind-monism, is twofold and unified: how does a community of perspective-limited agents (a) correct defection without corrupting the corrector, and (b) maintain the honest self-disclosure on which all mutual modeling depends? Vengeance is the limiting case of correction; truthfulness is the precondition of cooperation. Both govern the integrity of the shared model among agents.

## Problem

A cooperative system of agents requires two things that are easy to get wrong. First, it needs a mechanism to correct those who act against the cooperative structure; without it, defection spreads and coherence collapses. But the correction mechanism, if seized as private passion, becomes itself a defection — retaliation that escalates rather than repairs. Second, the system needs agents to render themselves accurately to one another; without honest signaling, mutual modeling degrades and cooperation becomes impossible. The shared problem is the maintenance of a calibrated, honest, defection-resistant model across many limited perspectives.

## Resource

**Friston on multi-agent coherence and honest signaling** (PRS-06 in the Friston-tradition wiki) frames both halves. Cooperation among active-inference agents depends on mutual modeling, which in turn depends on signals being honest: deception is the injection of false data into others' generative models, and it degrades the shared world the agents inhabit. Truthfulness, formally, is the maintenance of signal-integrity that keeps mutual models calibrated. Correction (vengeance's licit core) is the system's response to agents who have defected from that integrity — and the game-theoretic literature on *altruistic punishment* shows empirically that cooperation in large groups is sustained precisely by a willingness to bear costs to correct defectors. Aquinas's insistence that vengeance be ordered to correction and the common good, not to private hatred, is the normative form of the same finding: punishment maintains cooperation only when it targets the defection, not the person-as-enemy.

**McGilchrist** (PRS-01 in the McGilchrist-tradition wiki) gives the phenomenological warning. The grasping, manipulative mode of attention (which he associates with an unchecked left hemisphere) turns correction into possession — the wrongdoer becomes an object to be seized and crushed; this is vengeance-as-passion. The relational mode holds the wrongdoer as a person within a whole that the correction is meant to restore; this is vengeance-as-justice. The difference is exactly Aquinas's right-intention condition. **Levin** (PRS-17 and PRS-18 in the Levin-tradition wiki) gives the nested-agency analog. PRS-17 records bioelectric pattern memory as *cognitive glue* — the mechanism that constitutes a collective knower above the level of the single cell. PRS-18 records the failure mode of that collective: cancer reframed as a *decision problem* for the cell collective rather than as purely genetic damage, and correctable by rewriting the collective's goal state rather than by destroying the deviant cell. That is the biological form of Aquinas's right-intention condition — correction in service of the whole's integrity, aimed at restoring the shared target rather than at destroying the part. *Empirical.*

**Stump** enters here on both questions: punishment as medicinal (ordered to the good of the one corrected and the community), and truthfulness as second-personal self-revelation (PRS-11 in the Stump-tradition wiki) — the giving of oneself-as-one-is to be known by another.

**Wright** supplies the decisive scriptural reframe of vengeance. "Vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord" (Rom 12:19) does not abolish justice but *reserves ultimate vengeance to God* — and on Wright's reading of the cross, God's own response to wrong is to absorb it rather than retaliate, breaking the escalation cycle in the body of the crucified. Human correction is therefore bounded delegation (cf. Day 170): legitimate authority may correct for the common good, but final vengeance is not the creature's to seize. On truthfulness, Wright reads *veritas* as covenant faithfulness — being reliably what one is before God and neighbor. **Rohr** gives the contemplative form of both: the breaking of the retributive cycle as the heart of the gospel, and truthfulness as living from the True Self rather than performing the False Self.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response treats vengeance and truthfulness as the two guardians of a cooperative model among limited agents. Correction of defection is real and necessary, but it is licit only as bounded delegation ordered to restoration — reserved to legitimate authority, aimed at the defection rather than the person, and (theologically) with ultimate vengeance left to the whole/God who absorbs rather than escalates. Truthfulness is the honest self-disclosure that keeps mutual models calibrated; deception is not merely a private fault but a corruption of the shared world.

What it shares with Aquinas: the location of both in justice; the right-intention condition on vengeance; the account of truthfulness as a debt of self-manifestation. What it reframes: vengeance's licitness is grounded in cooperation-maintenance dynamics (empirical game theory + active inference) and bounded by the delegation structure, rather than in a free-standing penal order; truthfulness is grounded in signal-integrity for mutual modeling. Evidence: altruistic-punishment and honest-signaling results are empirical; the active-inference framing is formal; the absorb-don't-retaliate and True-Self readings are theological.

## Central-theme thread

Loving unity is computed by perspective-limited agents who must both correct defection and disclose themselves honestly. Vengeance-as-passion and deception are two ways the agent refuses the cooperative structure: the first by seizing a correction-role it has not been delegated and escalating rather than restoring; the second by feeding false data into others' models and thereby isolating itself behind a performed self. Both fracture the shared computation. The agent working out salvation in fear and trembling corrects only within commission, leaves ultimate vengeance to the One who sees the whole, and renders itself truly — because honest self-disclosure is the precondition of being known and included.

The two should not be read as one body of support.
- **McGilchrist PRS-01** (hemispheric asymmetry and cultural impoverishment) — the line between vengeance-as-possession and vengeance-as-restorative-justice. *High in the wiki, and the lateralization neuroscience it rests on is empirical — but the record's own scope is a claim about modern Western culture operating in a left-hemisphere mode, not about punishment. Reading the grasping/relational contrast onto the corrector's disposition toward the wrongdoer is this commentary's extension of the record, not something the record contains; McGilchrist's cultural thesis about the lateralization data is itself his interpretation. Grade High, scope narrower than the use made of it here.*
