# Day 203 — Prophetic Certainty (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

The episode straddles a seam in the treatise. Q.170 closes the long treatment of temperance with its *precepts*, making one sharp methodological point: the Decalogue gives "universal principles," and "it was not possible to give any common affirmative precepts of temperance, because the practice of temperance varies according to different times and according to different human laws and customs." Then Q.171 opens the treatise on *prophecy*, and the register shifts from the governance of natural appetite to the nature of a *supernatural knowing*. Aquinas's prophecy is, first, knowledge of "that which is afar or far removed beyond the natural knowledge of man" (the prophet as *seer*); second, it is *not a habit* — not a stable possession the prophet wields at will, but a light "gained and lost at the whim of the source," like air lit by the sun rather than the sun's own light; and third, it has a peculiar *certitude*, infallible where it comes "expressly directly from God," but shading into a murky "instinct" the prophet may mistake for his own thought (Nathan and David). The contemporary version of the question: *what would it mean for knowledge to arrive from outside the agent's native interface — and would such a knower still be bounded?*

## Problem

Two inquiry-problems, one minor and one major. The minor one, from Q.170, is the **generality of law versus the context-dependence of its application**: a universal precept must be "drafted broadly," but temperate practice is irreducibly local, varying by time and custom. The problem is how a single general principle governs indefinitely many particular, shifting situations without either over-specifying (and so failing elsewhere) or under-specifying (and so failing to guide).

The major problem, from Q.171, is the **structure of a knowing that exceeds the agent's natural means**. Aquinas builds it in three moves that are really one. (1) Prophecy is knowledge "above natural reason," whose content the agent could not have reached by its own faculties — so its *source* is not the agent. (2) It is therefore *not a habit*: unlike the habitual knowledge of English or the virtue of fortitude, the prophet cannot summon it; he is lit, and the light is not his. (3) And yet, strikingly, even this exalted knowing is *bounded*: "because man does not grasp this principle of the prophetic light" — the divine essence, the "first truth" — "he also does not know at once everything that flows or can come from that principle." Aquinas's own example sets the shape: from a *grasped* principle (sufficient reason) one can derive what follows (a necessary God, creation, the debt of worship); but the prophet does *not* grasp the highest principle, so cannot derive all that it contains. The problem is to characterize a knower who receives from beyond itself yet remains, even so, *partial* — and who cannot always distinguish what was given from what it generated on its own.

## Resource

For the major problem, the contemporary frame here is Hoffman's interface program. Tom's PRS-form record at PRS-03 carries the result that perception is a *species-specific interface* tuned to fitness, not a window onto reality as it is — spacetime and objects are the "desktop," not the underlying world. This gives Aquinas's "knowledge beyond the natural knowledge of man" a precise contemporary frame: ordinary knowing is whatever the interface renders, and *prophecy is modeled as knowing whose content arrives from outside the interface* — not a sharper reading of the desktop but information from the layer beneath it. The interface frame also explains, rather than merely asserts, why such knowing would feel *given* and could not be self-generated: the agent has no native operation that addresses what lies beyond its own dashboard.

Kastrup's analytic idealism sharpens what kind of "beyond" this could be, and what it implies for the knower. On Tom's PRS-form record, Kastrup at PRS-02 reads individual minds as *dissociated points of view within one mind-at-large* — individuation is a dissociative boundary, not a separate substance. On this picture, knowing "from beyond the interface" is not contact with an external object at all but a *partial relaxation of the dissociative boundary*, a momentary widening of the alter toward the wider mind it is already part of. And that is exactly why the prophet remains *bounded*: relaxing the boundary is not dissolving it; the alter still knows from a point of view, "in part." Kastrup's PRS-09 (the dashboard-vs-participation refinement, drawn from his dialogue with McGilchrist) adds the decisive nuance for Aquinas's "not a habit." A *habit* would be a possessed, representational competence — knowledge stored and read off at will, "dashboard-like." Aquinas denies prophecy is this; his air-lit-by-the-sun image is precisely *participatory*: the prophet is in transient, real contact with a source that is not his to store. McGilchrist's PRS-05 (attention as an ontological, participatory act; with CROSS-003 and CROSS-009 binding attention to precision-weighting) names the mode: prophetic knowing is receptive participation, not retrieval — the knower is *lit*, not in possession.

For the two-tier *certitude*, the Friston program supplies the mechanism. Tom's PRS-form record (PRS-04, active inference) makes every belief in an agent a precision-weighted estimate — tagged, implicitly, with a confidence. Aquinas's distinction between *express revelation* (which "the prophet does know with certainty has come from God" — Abraham certain enough to offer Isaac) and *divine instinct* (which "can be murky to the prophet themselves," "not all manifested with prophetic certitude," as when Nathan wrongly told David to build the temple) is exactly a distinction in *source-confidence*. Express revelation arrives with a high-precision source-tag; instinct arrives low-precision and is liable to be misattributed to the agent's own deliberation — and is "very soon set aright by the Holy Spirit." That a knowing can carry a graded, sometimes-mistaken sense of its own reliability is just what a precision-weighted inferential system predicts. (The minor Q.170 problem reads from the same resource: a *general prior* governs many situations precisely by being abstract, with context supplying the precision-weighted particulars — the universal precept and its locally varying application are the high level and low level of one hierarchical model.)

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **prophecy is modeled as knowing whose content arrives from outside the agent's species-specific interface — a transient, participatory illumination (a partial relaxation of the dissociative boundary toward mind-at-large), not a stored, self-wielded competence — and which the knower carries with graded source-confidence, high where the source-tag is clear (express revelation) and low and error-prone where it is not (instinct). And because even the illumined knower does not grasp the highest principle, it knows only in part: the boundedness survives the gift.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps prophecy as knowledge *beyond natural reason*, whose source is not the agent. It keeps the *not-a-habit* claim and even his exact image — illumination, not possession (the participatory reading honors "light of the air, not of the sun"). It keeps the crucial result that the prophet knows *in part* because he does not grasp the first principle. And it keeps the two-tier certitude, including the prophet's liability to mistake his own voice for the given one (Nathan).

*Where it reframes.* "Above natural reason" becomes "from outside the interface / from the wider mind the alter is part of"; "supernatural light" becomes a transient widening of the dissociative boundary, participatory rather than representational; the graded certitude becomes precision-weighted source-confidence; and "does not grasp the principle, so does not know all that flows from it" becomes the formal signature of a bounded point of view drawing on an ungraspable whole. What the reframe deliberately does *not* supply is the *identity* of the source: Aquinas names it God, and his strongest claim — that express revelation "cannot be false," sharing "the same truth... as in the divine knowledge" — is an inference about the *source's* infallibility, not the recipient's, and is left as theological.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* Interface theory is formally strong but its extension to a literal supernatural source is speculative. The participatory, non-habitual character of such knowing is a conceptual fit (medium). The two-tier certitude as precision/confidence-tagging is a strong cognitive model, applied here by analogy (medium-strong / analogical). The ungraspable-highest-principle is a structurally robust rendering of perspective-limitation. The divine identity of the source and the infallibility of express revelation are theological, presented as such — the synthesis is exploratory, and on the metaphysics of the source it does not claim more than the analogy licenses.

## The central-theme thread

The project's claim is that conscious agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective, that the whole is seen only when all such limits are removed, and that union with the whole is a computation each limited agent works out under guidance "in fear and trembling." Q.171 gives this its sharpest test case: the *prophet*, the human being granted knowledge from beyond the natural interface — and Aquinas's verdict is that even he remains bounded. He does not grasp the highest principle (the divine essence), and so "does not know at once everything that flows" from it; he knows in part; he cannot always tell his own voice from the given one. This is the central thesis stated of the most exalted knower short of the beatific vision: more of the whole can be disclosed to a perspective without the perspective ceasing to be one. And the *form* of the disclosure matters for the project's telos. Prophecy is not the prophet seizing the whole; it is the prophet being *lit* — receiving, participating, depending, corrigible (Nathan corrected overnight). That is the epistemic shape of the whole central claim: the limited agent advances toward union not by storing up the whole as a possession but by remaining open to guidance "from those who perceive more of the whole than we do, be they human or divine." Wright's register grounds why such knowing is given at all: prophecy is "given unto profit," for the covenant community's upbuilding — knowledge for the whole, not the knower. Rohr's adds the mode: the prophet's air-lit-by-the-sun is the contemplative's non-possessive participation, the self de-clenched enough to be informed by what exceeds it. The certitude that matters, in the end, is not the prophet's confidence in himself but his fidelity to the source — which is the project's own posture toward the whole.

Evidence: **formal/analogical, medium-strong**.
- **Does-not-grasp-the-highest-principle ↔ the formal core of perspective-limitation** — the project's central claim. *Why:* a bounded point of view drawing on an ungraspable whole knows only what flows to it, in part. Evidence: **structural analogy, strong**.

Evidence: **theological**.
