# Day 225 — Contemporary Parallel: Honor Tracks the Person, the Mediator Unites the Many

## Frame

Q.25 asks how Christ is to be *adored*. Aquinas's principle is that honor terminates in *persons*: "since in Christ there is but one person of the divine and human natures, he is given one adoration and one honor on the part of the person adored," though "on the part of the cause for which he is adored" there are several (he is honored for his uncreated knowledge and for his created knowledge). The humanity is adored with *latria* (the worship due to God) *because* it is the humanity of a divine person; images and even the relics of the true cross are venerated because "the honor given to an image reaches to the prototype" — we honor the *person*, "not just the flesh and bones... our eyes happen to be apprehending." The Blessed Virgin and the saints, being "mere rational creatures," receive *dulia* and *hyperdulia*, not latria; their relics are honored as "members of Christ... temples and organs of the Holy Ghost." Q.26 then names Christ "the Mediator of God and man" — and precisely *as man*: "Christ as God is in all things equal to the Father, but even in the human nature he is above all men; therefore as man he can be mediator, but not as God," his humanity "occupying a middle position... between broken sinful human beings and the Godhead." The contemporary version: *what makes honor track one individuated person across two natures — and how does a mediator unite the many to the whole precisely through a limited human nature?*

## Problem

Two inquiry-problems. (1) **Individuation by person.** The whole grammar of Q.25 turns on honor tracking the *person*, not the natures, the images, or the relics — the person is the individuated locus to which everything else is referred. The problem is what *grounds* personhood as the unit of identity and honor, such that one adoration is owed across two natures. (2) **Mediation through limitation.** Q.26's startling claim is that Christ mediates *as man, not as God* — the *limited* nature is what does the uniting, because only what occupies the "middle position" can join the two sides. The problem is how a limited perspective, precisely *as* limited, can be the bridge that unites the many to the whole.

## Resource

For individuation-by-person, the consciousness-first programs supply the structure. Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup (PRS-08, the Daimon as principle of individuation) gives exactly what Q.25 needs: a *principle of individuation* by which one consciousness is genuinely *this* irreducible person — "the tension between 'we are all one consciousness' and 'I am irreducibly myself' is dissolved through the Daimonic model of patterned, meaningful dissociation." Personhood is the individuated locus; honor tracks it because it is the real unit of identity, prior to and undivided by the natures or the images that refer to it. Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-01) supplies the posture that lets one adoration span two natures without collapsing them: non-dual knowing holds *unity-in-distinction* — one person, honored across distinct natures, "both/and" not either/or. For *mediation-through-limitation*, the resources are the integration programs. Master CROSS-013 (conscious agents integrate into a higher-order unified agent, with McGilchrist's split-brain data as the empirical leg) gives the structure of one agent uniting many; and Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman (PRS-04) adds that conscious agents "construct mutually consistent spacetime interfaces through agent-to-agent interaction" — the joining happens *at the interface*, the bounded/limited layer where agents meet, which is precisely where Q.26 locates mediation ("the middle position"). The mediator unites *through* the limited interface, not despite it. The scriptural anchor is direct: "there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus" (1 Tim 2:5) — the mediation is predicated of the *man* — and Tom's PRS-form record of Wright (PRS-05) names the mediation's end as *incorporation*: the mediator unites the many into the people among whom God comes home (Heb 9:15, "mediator of a new covenant").

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **honor tracks the one person across two natures because the person is the individuated locus of identity — the Daimonic principle of individuation (Kastrup PRS-08) — to which natures, images, and relics are all referred, and which non-dual knowing holds as unity-in-distinction (Rohr PRS-01). And the Mediator unites the many to the whole precisely *as man* — through the limited human interface where agents meet and integrate (Hoffman PRS-04, CROSS-013) — so that limitation is not an obstacle to mediation but its very organ.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps honor terminating in the *person*, one adoration across the natures, with images and relics honored because honor passes to the prototype. It keeps the latria/dulia distinction (the divine person receives the worship due to God; creatures, however exalted, do not). And it keeps Q.26's core: mediation is "as man" — the limited nature does the uniting.

*Where it reframes.* Aquinas grounds personhood in the metaphysics of hypostasis and mediation in the human nature's middle position; the contemporary reframe gives personhood a *principle of individuation* (the Daimon) and locates mediation at the *interface* where agents integrate. Mediation-through-limitation stops being a Christological peculiarity and becomes the general truth that the limited interface is exactly where the many are joined to the whole.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The one-adoration grammar and the latria/dulia distinction are theological/formal. The "one mediator, the man Christ Jesus" is scriptural (Wright, strong). The individuation-by-person and interface-mediation readings are speculative-formal (medium), with the integration claim carrying CROSS-013's empirical leg.

## The central-theme thread

The project's foundational claim is that "conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective." Q.25's whole grammar of adoration depends on individuation-by-person: honor tracks the one individuated locus, not the natures or the images that refer to it — which is the project's individuation premise stated as a doctrine of worship. And Q.26 supplies the project's load-bearing reversal: the Mediator unites the many *as man, not as God* — that is, *through* the limited perspective, not by transcending it. This is exactly the central theme's logic. The whole does not unite the limited by erasing limitation; it unites them through a perspective that occupies the "middle position," the bounded interface where agents actually meet. Loving unity is achieved not by a mediator who bypasses the limited condition but by one who *inhabits* it (the comprehensor who is also wayfarer, Day 220) and joins the many to the whole *from inside* the limitation. The interface reading sharpens it: integration happens at the bounded layer where agents interact — so the limited human nature is not the mediation's handicap but its organ. The individuated person who unites the many through his own limitation is the central theme made personal.
