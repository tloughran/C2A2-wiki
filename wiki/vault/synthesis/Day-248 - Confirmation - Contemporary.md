# Day 248 — Confirmation (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (III Q.72) presents confirmation as the sacrament of spiritual *growth* — strengthening the baptized for mature witness, giving the Spirit's boldness to confess the faith publicly. The medieval question is maturation: a second sacrament not for entry but for the deepening of an agency already begun. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: what is the developmental strengthening of an agent — the move from established identity to robust, costly, world-facing action?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **how does an already-constituted agent acquire the robustness to act decisively under cost and risk?** Aquinas needs confirmation to add something real to baptism — not a new identity but a new strength, a licensing of bold public action. The contemporary frame meets the same problem wherever a system, having a stable identity, must develop the capacity to commit to high-stakes action under uncertainty.

## Resource

Levin supplies the developmental resource. In Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's program, PRS-08 (cells as cognitive agents navigating an anatomical morphospace) establishes that a cell collective is a problem-solving agent at all; the *scaling* claim proper is PRS-10, the continuity thesis, on which goal-directedness differs across scales only in degree — complexity of represented goals, memory, interaction protocols — making competency a graded, measurable property rather than a binary one. Together they give competency scaling: agents bootstrapping from simple to more capable problem-solving, expanding the scale at which they can pursue goals. Confirmation, read through this resource, is a strengthening that scales the agent's competency for its proper goal (witness), not a re-founding of identity.

Friston supplies the decisive formal move. In Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's program, PRS-04 (the organism as active modeler) includes *precision-weighting* — the gain an agent assigns to its predictions, which determines how strongly belief translates into committed action. "Spirit-given boldness" maps onto a raising of precision on the agent's deep commitments, which is exactly what licenses costly action ("fear and trembling," Phil 2:12) rather than hesitation.

McGilchrist adds the attentional register: maturation as a re-balancing of the mode of world-engagement, the capacity to attend to the whole and act from it rather than from decontextualized caution. Rohr's second-half-of-life maturation names the spiritual arc — the move from the first-half task (identity, container) to the second-half capacity (mission, witness).

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **confirmation is the developmental strengthening of an already-constituted agent — a competency-scaling and precision-raising on its deep commitments that licenses mature, costly witness — not a new identity but a new robustness of an existing one.** This *shares* with Aquinas the maturation logic (growth, not entry) and the public-witness orientation. It *reframes* "strengthening" as competency-scaling (Levin) and precision-weighting (Friston) rather than an infusion of a quasi-substance. It *marks* that Levin's competency scaling is empirical in biology and only analogically transferred; that Friston's precision-weighting is a formal account of the *form* of bold commitment, not its supernatural content; and that the Spirit's gift is theological (Rohr, Wright).

## The central-theme thread

The project holds that loving unity is a computation each limited agent performs with its perceived resources, often under threat. Confirmation is the sacrament that *raises the agent's capacity for that computation under cost* — precisely the "fear and trembling" register. Maturation here is the agent becoming able to act toward the whole even when acting is risky, which is the difference between merely belonging to the people united with God and actively extending that unity into a resistant world.
