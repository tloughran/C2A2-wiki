# Day 122 — Contemporary Parallel: Grasping Coherence Without Grasping Essence

## Frame

Having treated the virtue of faith, Aquinas turns to the two intellectual gifts of the Holy Spirit that correspond to it: understanding (Q.8) and knowledge (Q.9). His central and easily-missed move is in Q.8 a.2. The gift of understanding does *not* comprehend the essence of God or the quiddity of the mysteries. What it grasps is that the articles of faith, "whatever be the outward appearances, do not contradict the truth of things," so that the believer "ought not to depart from matters of faith for the sake of things that appear externally." Understanding here is the grasp of *coherence under a veil*, not the grasp of essence. Q.9 then distinguishes three judging functions: understanding apprehends, wisdom judges by divine ideas, knowledge judges by created ideas.

The contemporary version of the question is: what kind of cognitive achievement is available to a finite agent who knows it does not and cannot see the thing-in-itself? Aquinas's answer — you can know that the interface is non-contradictory even when you cannot see through it — is, almost word for word, the structure that conscious-realist monism builds its epistemology on.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem is the gap between certainty and comprehension. The believer holds the articles "for certain" yet "does not comprehend them" (the triune God, the incarnation cannot be grasped as we grasp the principle of non-contradiction). Aquinas's worry is St. Paul's: "now we see in a mirror dimly." If we see only in a mirror, in what sense do we *understand* at all? The general form of the problem is the one every finite cognizer faces: how can there be a real cognitive achievement that is not a seeing-of-the-essence — that operates entirely within a representation the agent knows to be partial?

A second problem rides on top: why does Aquinas insist the gift of understanding is present in *all* who have sanctifying grace and absent in those without it, tying right cognition to a rightly-ordered will (charity)? Why would the orientation of the will gate the operation of the intellect?

## Resource

The leading resource is Hoffman's interface theory of perception (PRS-03, PRS-04 in the Hoffman-tradition wiki). Perception did not evolve to track truth; it evolved to track fitness, so what an organism perceives is a species-specific interface, not the underlying reality. The decisive consequence for our question: a finite agent never grasps the thing-in-itself, but it *can* operate reliably within an interface, and it can detect whether its interface-representations are mutually consistent. The cross-program index makes the pairing explicit. CROSS-062 (Hoffman × Stump) records the "non-veridical sensory interface as resource for Thomistic intellect-over-sense epistemology": Aquinas's insistence that intellect outranks sense, and that we ought not abandon the articles for the sake of how things "appear externally," is precisely a refusal to let the fitness-interface overrule the deeper model. The gift of understanding, on this reading, is the capacity to hold the model as coherent against the pull of the appearances.

Friston supplies the perception/judgment machinery Aquinas needs for Q.9. In the PRS-form record of Friston's predictive-coding work (PRS-04) the intellect's "two-fold act, perception and judgment" maps onto generative-model apprehension versus model evaluation; active reasoning (PRS-10) is exactly the selection among competing world-models that Aquinas assigns to the gift of knowledge ("to discern what is to be believed from what is not to be believed"). McGilchrist sharpens the perception side: the cross-program index (Batch-3) records the structural identity between Stump's second-personal narrative knowledge and McGilchrist's right-hemisphere knowing — a holistic, participatory apprehension prior to and richer than analytic decomposition. Understanding-as-perception is right-hemisphere apprehension; wisdom and knowledge as judgment add the evaluative composition Aquinas calls "saying something is or is not so."

Stump's narrative-cognition program (PRS-07 in the Stump-tradition wiki) anchors the claim that there is real, rigorous, non-propositional knowing — "Franciscan knowledge," knowing-a-person rather than knowing-that — which is exactly the register in which "understanding the articles" lives.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads the gift of understanding as the agent's capacity to grasp the *coherence* of its faith-model without grasping the *essence* of its objects. This shares Aquinas's claim almost exactly: understanding is real cognition, but it is not comprehension; it certifies non-contradiction, not quiddity. Where it reframes is the metaphysical gloss. For Aquinas the veil is the gap between the finite intellect and the divine essence in this life. Under monism the veil is structural and universal: every finite agent perceives through an interface (Hoffman) — a Markov blanket (Friston), a dissociative boundary within mind-at-large (Kastrup) — so the believer's "mirror dimly" is the general condition of finite cognition, not a special religious handicap. What grace adds is a higher-precision prior that lets the agent trust the deeper model against the appearances, plus the cognitive act of confirming that the model coheres.

The medium-principle from Q.9 a.2 — every cognitive habit regards "formally the medium through which things are known and materially the things known" — is the load-bearing bridge. God is known in philosophy through the natural light and in theology through the supernatural light; same object, different medium, different science. This is interface-relativity: the medium constitutes the science. It is also MacIntyre's tradition-constituted rationality — recorded in PRS-form as PRS-08 in the Loughran-tradition wiki (tradition-crossing inquiry), a theme Stump also carries: what can be known, and how it is judged, is relative to the inquiry-medium one occupies. The gift of knowledge (judging created things by the light of faith) and the gift of wisdom (judging by union with the divine things themselves) are two media trained on overlapping objects — exactly the philosophy/theology distinction generalized.

The second problem — why right cognition requires a rightly-ordered will — falls out of the monist picture rather than sitting as a pious add-on. If cognition is model-based agency, and the agent's model is shaped by what it values (precision is allocated according to the agent's goals), then a will turned away from the highest good will not allocate the precision needed to perceive that good. Aquinas's claim that "understanding implies a certain perception of the truth, which is in none except one who has sanctifying grace" becomes: the perception is gated by the orientation, because perception is not value-neutral. The gift of knowledge corresponding to the beatitude of mourning makes the same point from the other side — knowledge of created things reveals their power to ensnare, "befitting the mourner who has discovered that he has been mastered by evil which he coveted as though it were good." This is an epistemics of disillusionment, the right-hemisphere correction of the left-hemisphere grasp that mistook the appealing appearance for the good.

The scriptural anchor deserves its own register. The verse Aquinas is quoting — "now we see in a mirror dimly" — sits inside 1 Corinthians 13, Paul's argument that prophecies, tongues, and knowledge are the partial media of the present age while love alone persists into the age to come; Wright's reading of that now/then structure (developed at length in *Surprised by Hope* on new creation) makes the verse say what this whole article needs: the cognitive gifts are real but interim, and the one that survives the removal of the limitation is charity. That is the same gating Aquinas asserts when he ties the gift of understanding to sanctifying grace — the cognition that matters is the one ordered by love, because love is the mode of knowing that belongs to the unveiled state. Rohr's contemplative tradition (*The Universal Christ*) names the practice-side of the same claim: apophatic, non-dual knowing holds the mystery as coherent without collapsing it into concepts — understanding-without-comprehension as a discipline rather than only a doctrine. Both are theological resources and are marked as such.

Evidence strength, marked honestly: interface theory is formal and evolutionarily motivated but contested (medium-high). The perception/judgment mapping onto active inference is formal and has neuroscientific support. The identification of the religious "veil" with the universal interface is a philosophical reframe, not a finding. The will-gates-perception claim is well-motivated within active inference (precision is goal-relative) but its application to charity-gates-understanding is theological extension, marked speculative.

## The central-theme thread

This article is where the project's epistemology becomes explicit. Conscious agents are individuated by the limitation of their perspective; that limitation is, concretely, the interface through which each one perceives. The gift of understanding is the answer to the obvious worry the central theme raises: if every perspective is partial, can the limited agent know anything of the whole at all? Aquinas's answer, kept and generalized, is yes — not by seeing the essence, which would require removing the limitation, but by grasping that the model of the whole given to it is coherent and by trusting the higher-perceiving node that supplied it. "We see in a mirror dimly" is the central theme's epistemic motto. The agent does not abandon the deeper model for the sake of the appearances; it holds the coherence and continues the computation toward the day when the limitations are removed and "then face to face."
