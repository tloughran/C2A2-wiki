# Day 273 — Anointing Members (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas asks three practical questions about Extreme Unction: who is its minister (Q.31), and on whom and on *what parts of the body* it should be conferred (Q.32). His answer to the last is striking — the anointing is applied to the organs of the senses (eyes, ears, nostrils, lips, hands, feet), because these are the avenues through which sin characteristically enters and acts. The medieval rationale is moral-physiological: sin comes in through the senses, so grace is applied at the same gates. The contemporary version under mind/conscious-realist monism is sharper than it first looks: if the senses are not windows onto reality but a constructed *interface*, then anointing them is treating the very apparatus of a limited perspective as the site of repair.

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **the status of the senses as the boundary of a perspective.** Aquinas already treats the senses as more than passive receptors — they are the active gates of the soul's traffic with the world, and therefore morally weighted. The deeper problem he is feeling toward is one any account of perception must face: are the senses a transparent pipe delivering the world as it is, or are they a *constructed* boundary that both enables and limits what an agent can know and do? On the answer hangs the meaning of anointing them.

## Resource

The emerging paradigm answers the problem decisively, and in Aquinas's favor on the substantive point. Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman's program, PRS-03 (the Interface Theory of Perception), carries a mathematical proof that natural selection drives *truth-tracking* perception to extinction: perception evolved to track fitness payoffs, not objective reality, so the senses are an adaptive interface — a species-specific desktop — rather than a mirror of the world. The senses are exactly the apparatus that constructs a limited perspective; they are the limitation, rendered as biology.

the Levin-tradition wiki's PRS-08 adds the bodily map: cells are cognitive agents navigating an "anatomical morphospace," so the body is a distributed information-bearing structure, not undifferentiated tissue — there *are* privileged loci, organs that carry distinct functional meaning. the Friston-tradition wiki's PRS-05 supplies the scaling principle: cognition is distributed across scales, and the sense organs are the body's prediction-error boundary, the surface where the internal model meets the world and updates. And the Rohr-tradition wiki's PRS-04 (embodied love as epistemology; the body as a site of knowing) refuses to spiritualize this away — knowing is bodily, so the gates of bodily knowing are theologically serious places.

Put together: the senses are the interface (Hoffman) at the boundary of a form-bearing body (Levin) where the agent's model meets the world (Friston), and that boundary is a genuine site of knowing and therefore of grace (Rohr).

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel reading: **anointing the senses is consecrating the body's interface points — the very loci where a limited perspective is built — as the places where grace re-enters.** What it *shares* with Aquinas is precisely his instinct: the senses are not neutral, they are the morally and epistemically loaded boundary of the self, so it is fitting to apply the sacramental sign there. The monist resources *vindicate* that instinct — the senses really are the constructed limit of a perspective, not a clear pane, which makes "sin enters through the senses" less a moral metaphor and more a claim about where distortion is admitted into an agent's interface.

Where it *reframes*: Aquinas treats the senses as gates of sin to be repaired; the interface reading lets us say something subtler — the senses are not defective by accident but *limited by design*, the price of being an individuated agent at all. Repair, then, is not the removal of the interface but its re-consecration toward the whole. Where the evidence is *firm*: that perception is an adaptive interface rather than a truth-mirror is formally proven (Hoffman PRS-03) and consonant with active inference (Friston PRS-05). Where it is *theological/canonical*: that the anointing of these specific members conveys grace, and the rules governing the minister and recipient, are matters of sacramental theology and Church order, not findings.

## The central-theme thread

This is one of the most literal appearances of the project's central claim in the whole Supplement. Conscious agents are individuated *by the limitations of their perspective* — and the senses are that limitation made flesh: the species-specific interface through which, and only through which, this agent meets the world. Anointing the senses is therefore the sacrament reaching for the exact organ of individuation. It does not promise to dissolve the interface and grant the unlimited view; it asks that the limited interface be turned toward the whole — that the eyes, ears, and hands of a perspective that can only ever see in part be re-pointed, in fear and trembling, toward the loving unity they cannot yet perceive entire. Grace re-enters where the limitation lives.

## Bridges

- **Hoffman PRS-03** (formal + empirical): the Interface Theory of Perception — the senses as an adaptive, species-specific interface rather than a truth-mirror. Why this pairing: it gives Aquinas's "sin enters through the senses" a rigorous reading as distortion admitted at the constructed boundary of a perspective.
- **Levin PRS-08** (empirical, in-domain): the body as anatomical morphospace navigated by cognitive cells — privileged, meaning-bearing loci rather than uniform tissue. Why: it grounds the choice of *specific members* for anointing.
- **Friston PRS-05** (formal): distributed cognition across scales; the senses as the prediction-error boundary where model meets world. Why: it specifies the senses as the update-surface that repair would target.
- **Rohr PRS-04** (theological): embodied love as epistemology; the body as a site of knowing. Why: it keeps the anointing from being spiritualized into mere symbol — bodily gates of knowing are real places of grace.

## Where this leaves us

Having located grace at the interface, Day 274 turns to permanence and repetition — whether unction may be repeated, and then opens the sacrament of Order, with its claim to an *indelible* character. The open thread: if the senses are a re-consecrable interface, what kind of change is one that *cannot* be repeated or undone — the question morphogenetic memory (Levin PRS-01) is poised to address next.
