# Day 145 — Contemporary Parallel: The Same Machinery, the Wrong End

## Frame

The prudence treatise ends on its shadows. Q.54 treats *negligence* — "a lack of due solicitude," a failure of the act of command in which the negligent man "fails through lack of a prompt will." Q.55 then treats the vices "opposed to prudence by way of resemblance": practical reason rightly structured but wrongly aimed. "Prudence of the flesh" is the skill of a man "adept in discovering and executing the best means" who "looks upon carnal goods as the last end of his life." *Craftiness* (astutia) pursues an end "by means that are false and counterfeit"; *guile* is its execution in words, *fraud* in deeds. And there is *undue solicitude* about temporal things and about the future, against which our Lord warns, "be not solicitous for tomorrow." All, Aquinas says, "arise chiefly from covetousness." Under the mind / conscious-realist monism this project writes from, this closing question is the sharpest statement of the seam the whole batch has tracked: the gap between *competence* and *the end it serves*.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem is one the project has been circling since Day 141 a.13, and Q.55 states it cleanly: *a perspective can compute brilliantly toward the wrong end, or by corrupt means — so what distinguishes true practical wisdom from its high-functioning counterfeits?* Aquinas is explicit that "prudence of the flesh" is genuinely *adept*: the man of the flesh is no bungler; he is "adept in discovering and executing the best means," only "to a worldly end." The counterfeit is not a deficiency of skill. It is defined entirely by the *end* to which the skill is directed (flesh-prudence), or by the *means* through which it works (craftiness, guile, fraud). This is the same point Aquinas made with Luke 16 on Day 141 — "the sons of this world are wiser in their generation than the sons of light" — now turned into a full taxonomy of how competent practical reason goes wrong.

## Resource

The governing resource is the end-neutrality of intelligence. On the lineage this project follows — Levin's agents-with-goals, inherited via William James as problem-solving under limited resources — competence is precisely the capacity to navigate a means-space, and it is *silent about which end is navigated toward*. That silence is exactly what makes "prudence of the flesh" possible: the machinery of counsel, judgment, and command can run flawlessly toward any goal, including a worldly last end. So what converts competent computation into *virtue* cannot be the competence itself; it must be the end — which returns us to Day 141 a.13, true prudence only in those with the right end. The whole of Q.55 is the formal converse of that article.

For *prudence of the flesh* specifically (a.1), the resource is the fitness-versus-truth distinction. Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman's work holds that perception is tuned to track fitness payoffs, not truth (PRS-03) — the interface optimizes for what pays, not for the whole it hides. "Prudence of the flesh" is that optimization made deliberate: a perspective expertly maximizing its own interface-payoffs, "carnal goods as the last end," with the deep variable — the highest cause, the whole — dropped from the objective entirely. It is the folly of Day 141 (Q.46) raised to a competent vice: not the dull mind that cannot taste the highest cause, but the skilled mind that has stopped trying to.

For *undue solicitude* (a.6–7), the resource is precision. Tom's PRS-form record of PRS-16 in the Friston-tradition wiki frames risk and ambiguity as the agent's weighting over outcomes; solicitude that "sets the heart on the world" or "desires too much, or fears too much to lose" temporal goods is misweighted precision — gain piled on temporal variables that providence has not asked the agent to carry. Aquinas's own image is exact: "every time has its own proper solicitude," and to be solicitous about the vintage in summer is "needlessly forestalling the solicitude belonging to a future time." That is precision misallocated across the temporal horizon — anxiety as the wrong prior over tomorrow.

For *craftiness, guile, and fraud* (a.3–5), the resource is the instrumentalizing mode untethered from the real good — practical reason optimizing means while severed from any honest relation to the end, the manipulation of signs ("guile is ascribed chiefly to speech") to move others toward one's own advantage. And for the *root* (a.8), Tom's PRS-form record of MacIntyre's work supplies the structural diagnosis: a practice has *internal goods* proper to it, which are corrupted when *external goods* — "money, power, status" (P3) — displace them. Covetousness is precisely that displacement, the external good clutched until it governs the means. Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr's work names the remedy Aquinas reaches for: detachment, the refusal of the false self's grasping; and Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's critical realism, with the Sermon on the Mount (Matt 6, "seek first the kingdom"), anchors the same diagnosis scripturally — covetousness as a kind of idolatry, the absolutizing of a partial good.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads false prudence as *fully competent practical computation severed from the right end* — directed at a worldly last end (flesh), or pursued by counterfeit means (craft, guile, fraud), or misweighting temporal goods (undue solicitude) — with covetousness, the absolutized partial good, as the common root, and negligence (Q.54) as the same machinery failing to fire at all. With Aquinas throughout: these are real vices, distinguished by end and means; they are genuinely *adept*, not deficient in skill; and they are healed at the root, by detachment from the partial goods that distort the objective. Where it reframes: "prudence of the flesh" is read as optimization for interface-payoff rather than for the whole (Hoffman), and undue solicitude as precision misallocated across time (Friston) — but the diagnosis is Aquinas's own. Where the evidence is firm: that competence is end-neutral, so that skill alone never makes prudence virtuous, is as robust as any claim the project holds. Where it stays theological: that the right end is loving union with God, and that covetousness is finally idolatry, is carried by the field — and the synthesis leaves it there.

The measure against Aquinas: he needs no correction here, only mechanism. The contemporary frame explains *why* competence and virtue come apart (intelligence is end-neutral), *why* the flesh-prudent optimize so well for the wrong thing (the interface rewards fitness, not truth), and *why* anxiety is a disorder of practical reason rather than a mere mood (misweighted precision). What it cannot supply is the end that would make the machinery virtuous — and that, exactly, is charity's, not the computation's.

## The central-theme thread

This is the negative image of the whole project, and it belongs at the close of the prudence treatise. The central claim says loving unity is the goal each limited perspective computes toward; false prudence is the computation aimed *away* from the whole, at a partial good seized as if it were the whole. "Prudence of the flesh" is a perspective optimizing its own interface-payoffs — the fitness-over-truth bias the project names as the standing danger of any limited viewpoint — and undue solicitude is that bias misweighting the present against a future the agent was asked to entrust. Covetousness, Aquinas's root, is the absolutizing of one's own partial good; detachment, his remedy, is the discipline of *not* absolutizing it — holding one's perspective's goods loosely enough to be reoriented toward the whole, which is the practical face of the receptivity Day 144 called the gift of counsel. The batch's lesson lands here: competence is not salvation. The same machinery that, oriented toward loving union and counseled by the one who comprehends all things, *is* the working-out of salvation, becomes — pointed at a partial good and closed against guidance — its precise counterfeit. The difference is never the skill. It is always the end.

## Where this leaves us

The prudence treatise closes with its counterfeits named and their root exposed: competent practical reason is not enough; severed from the right end and the guidance of the whole, it becomes flesh-prudence, craft, and anxious grasping. Aquinas turns next in the Secunda Secundae toward *justice* — the virtue that renders to each what is due, and the largest of the moral-virtue treatises ahead. The through-line this batch has drawn carries into it intact: a limited perspective computes its way toward a goal it did not set (Day 141), with components built for contingency (Day 142), scaling across nested communities and reaching past its own rules where they fail (Day 143), needing counsel from the one who comprehends all things (Day 144), and going wrong — never by losing competence, only by losing the end — into the counterfeits that close the treatise here.
