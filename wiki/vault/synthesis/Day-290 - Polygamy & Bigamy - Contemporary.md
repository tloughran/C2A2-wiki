# Day 290 — Polygamy & Bigamy (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.65–66 close this stretch with **plurality of wives** — whether polygamy was ever lawful, and the impediment of bigamy. Aquinas's position is layered. Plural marriage is *against the secondary precepts* of natural law because it compromises the goods of marriage — especially *fides* (the undivided fidelity proper to the one-flesh union) and the equal mutual self-gift established in the marriage debt (Day 289) — yet he concedes it was *tolerated* under the Old Testament dispensation, by a kind of dispensation ordered to the multiplication of God's people, without ever being a positive ideal. Monogamy, for Aquinas, expresses what marriage most fully *is*; polygamy is a deficient mode tolerated for a time. The contemporary version: **why is the *dyad* — exactly two — the unit of complete mutual union? Is there a structural reason that total, symmetric self-gift cannot be divided among several?**

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **the cardinality of complete union**. Day 289 established that the deepest union is *symmetric and total* mutual self-gift — each cedes sole self-authority to the other. The polygamy question asks whether that relation can hold among three or more. Aquinas's instinct is that it cannot hold *fully*: a husband with several wives cannot give himself *wholly* and *symmetrically* to each, and the equal marriage debt of 1 Cor 7:4 becomes structurally impossible to honor in all directions at once. The contemporary sciences of coupled agency face a precise version of this: is there something about *complete, symmetric* mutual modeling that is proper to a dyad and degrades in a triad or larger group?

## Resource

The Friston-tradition program supplies the leading structural move. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-06, *multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling*; PRS-16, *expected free energy as a unified objective*) lets us state the dyadic point carefully. In a *symmetric* coupling, each agent selects its policy with the other's preferences fully internalized as its own — control over the joint state is shared *equally*, and each agent's whole modeling capacity can be devoted to the one other. As soon as a third party is added under the same demand of *complete, symmetric* self-gift, the structure cannot be satisfied in all directions at once: finite modeling and self-giving capacity must be *divided*, and the equal, total cession of self that defines the deepest union (Day 289) becomes mathematically impossible to render to each partner simultaneously. Polygamous configurations are not *incoherent* as collectives — large coherent collectives plainly exist — but they cannot instantiate *complete symmetric dyadic self-gift* with each member; they are necessarily a different, lower-symmetry kind of union. This gives Aquinas's "polygamy compromises *fides* and equal self-gift" a structural reading rather than a merely customary one. The Levin-tradition program supplies the precedent that higher individuals come in determinate integration-structures (PRS-32): not every grouping yields the same kind of whole; the *form* of integration matters. The Wright-tradition program governs the scriptural-historical register, and here it does essential corrective work: PRS-02 (*faithful improvisation*) and PRS-03 (*inaugurated eschatology*) frame the biblical handling of polygamy as a *trajectory* — the texts depict (without endorsing) patriarchal polygamy and move, across the canon, toward the monogamous one-flesh ideal reaffirmed by Jesus ("from the beginning it was not so") and Paul. This both supports Aquinas's "tolerated, not ideal" reading and supplies a more developmental account than a flat natural-law verdict. Rohr's PRS-7 (*relationship ontologically prior*) and PRS-04 (*embodied love*) keep the analysis personal: the issue is not arithmetic but the wholeness of self-gift.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the dyad is the unit of *complete, symmetric* mutual union because total equal self-gift cannot be divided among several without ceasing to be total or ceasing to be equal — polygamy yields a real but lower-symmetry union, not the full one-flesh mutuality.** What it *shares* with Aquinas: monogamy as the full expression of what marriage is; polygamy as a deficient mode compromising *fides* and equal self-gift; and the historical acknowledgment that it was tolerated under the old dispensation. Where it *reframes*: Aquinas grounds the verdict in secondary natural-law precepts plus dispensation; the monist reframe grounds the *core* of it structurally (the impossibility of complete symmetric self-gift to more than one), while Wright's trajectory reading reframes the biblical data as a *developmental* movement toward the dyadic ideal rather than a static prohibition — which is both more honest to the texts (which contain polygamy) and more aligned with inaugurated eschatology. Where evidence is *firm*: that symmetric, capacity-saturating coupling is proper to a pair and must be divided among more is a clean structural point. Where *speculative*: the leap from "complete symmetric coupling" to a normative verdict on human marriage forms is theological-conceptual, not derivable from the formal model alone; and the claim should not be inflated into a denigration of the many faithful non-dyadic family structures history records — the argument is about the *specific* relation of total symmetric one-flesh self-gift, not about which households can be loving.

## The central-theme thread

The project's loving unity is the union of perspectives in which each gives itself wholly and is wholly received. Day 289 showed self-gift must be symmetric; this article shows it must, at its most complete, be *paired* — because a self given *wholly* cannot be given wholly to several at once. The dyad is thus the elementary cell of the project's vision of unity: the smallest community in which complete mutual self-gift is structurally possible. The larger unity the project seeks — all perspectives held in the one whole — is not built by diluting self-gift across many at the dyadic level, but by the multiplication and interlinking of such complete unions (recall affinity, Day 285) into an ever-wider communion. Monogamy, on this reading, is not a restriction on love's reach but the preservation of love's *completeness* at the point where two perspectives become one.
