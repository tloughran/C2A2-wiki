# Day 287 — Marrying Non-Believers (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.59–60 treat **disparity of worship** (disparitas cultus) — marriage between a believer and an unbeliever — and the related impediments touching faith. Aquinas regards disparity of worship as a genuine impediment to the *sacramental* good of marriage: a shared faith orients the union toward its supernatural end (the *sacramentum*, Day 282), and a marriage divided at that deepest level is impeded in reaching it, even where the natural goods remain. He is careful, following Paul (1 Cor 7), that an *existing* marriage in which one party later believes is not dissolved — the believing spouse may sanctify the union — but he treats the *contracting* of a religiously divided marriage as gravely impeded. The contemporary version: **is a shared deep framework — a common tradition or generative model of the world's meaning — a genuine precondition of the most complete union between two agents, or only a contingent preference?**

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **whether deep coupling requires a shared interpretive framework**. Aquinas's worry is not bigotry but structure: the highest good of marriage is a *shared orientation to ultimate ends*, and two people who disagree at that level cannot pursue *that* good together, however well they pursue the natural ones. The contemporary sciences of coupled agency and the philosophy of tradition meet here. Two agents form a coherent higher unit by sharing a generative model; the deeper and more comprehensive the shared model, the deeper the achievable coupling. A union between agents whose *most comprehensive* models diverge can be coherent at many levels and yet structurally barred from the deepest shared computation — which is precisely what "impeded as to the sacramental good" names.

## Resource

The Friston-tradition program supplies the leading move. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-18 in the Friston-tradition wiki, *what it would mean, formally, to widen a community so it can engage rival traditions*) is a program on exactly the cost and possibility of coupling across divergent comprehensive models. Its lesson cuts both ways and must be reported honestly: divergence at the level of the most comprehensive priors does raise a real structural barrier to the *deepest* joint inference (supporting Aquinas's structural point), *and* PRS-18's whole thrust is that such widening is *possible and valuable* — communities (and couples) can extend their shared model to engage a rival framework rather than treating divergence as simply disqualifying. PRS-06 (*multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling*) locates the depth of any union in the extent of the shared model. The **MacIntyre** bridge node supplies the philosophical spine: rationality and the deepest agreements are *tradition-constituted*; two agents who do not share a tradition can still reason together, but only by the harder work of mutual translation between traditions — there is no neutral ground. (Note: MacIntyre functions here as a bridge to `traditions/macintyre/`; he is not yet in the formal C2A2 roster, so this is a form-level citation pending confirmation.) The Wright-tradition program governs the scriptural register: PRS-05 (*salvation as covenant inclusion*) and his reading of 1 Cor 7 establish that the unbelieving spouse is not to be expelled — the believer's faith can sanctify the union — which qualifies any structural pessimism with a theology of patient inclusion. Rohr's PRS-01 (*non-dual knowing*) and PRS-03 (*the Universal Christ*) push the qualification further: the cosmic Christ is already at work beyond the visible boundaries of explicit faith, so "disparity" is never simple exclusion.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **a shared comprehensive framework is a genuine precondition of the *deepest* union — disparity at the level of ultimate ends really does impede the sacramental good — but the same science of community-widening shows such disparity is a barrier to be worked across, not a simple disqualification, and the Pauline tradition insists the divided union can still be sanctified.** What it *shares* with Aquinas: the structural recognition that the highest marital good requires a shared orientation to ultimate ends, and the Pauline refusal to dissolve an existing mixed marriage. Where it *reframes*: Aquinas treats disparity primarily as an impediment to be dispensed or avoided; the monist/MacIntyrean reframe treats it as a *translation problem* — a real and costly barrier to deep coupling that can nonetheless be addressed by the labor of mutual modeling across traditions, reframing the impediment as a graded difficulty rather than a binary bar. Where evidence is *firm*: that depth of coupling tracks extent of shared model is well-defined formally, and tradition-dependence of deep rational agreement is a strong philosophical result. Where *speculative*: the theological verdict that a *specific* shared faith is required for the sacramental good is normative and internal to the tradition, not derivable from the formal models, which speak only of shared-model depth in general.

## The central-theme thread

The project holds that limited agents need *guidance from those who perceive more of the whole*, and that loving unity is computed within, and across, traditions richly informed about one another's perspectives. This article is the marital test case of the project's own method. Disparity of worship is the place where the central theme's two commitments meet: the realism that a shared framework deepens union, and the missionary-dialogical hope that divergent frameworks can be bridged by patient mutual translation rather than walled apart. The very C2A2 architecture — traditions interacting when richly informed about one another — is the program-level answer to the marital question: difference at the deepest level is a barrier to be lovingly worked across, and sometimes, as Paul says, the believing partner sanctifies the whole.
