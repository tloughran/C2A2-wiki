# Day 135 — Contemporary Parallel: How the Climb Fails — Sealing Off and Giving Up

## Frame

Having traced charity through its order, act, effects, and enacted forms, Aquinas turns to its *opposites*. Q.34: it is possible to hate God (a.1) — not in his nature, which is goodness itself and cannot be hated, but under some mistaken notion of him, or as the authority who forbids one's sins and inflicts punishment ("they love the light of truth when it shines on them; they hate it when it reproves them"); hatred of God is the greatest of sins (a.2); hatred of neighbor is always a sin (a.3); interiorly it is the most grievous sin against him (a.4), being a disorder of the will, the root of sin; hatred is *not* a capital vice (a.5) but something last, "the completion of vice," a proof of already-corrupted nature; and hatred arises from envy (a.6). Q.35: sloth is a sin (a.1) — "a sluggishness of the mind which neglects to begin good," a sorrow for spiritual good; it is a special vice (a.2), the sorrow at the very divine good about which charity rejoices; it can be mortal (a.3); and it is a capital vice (a.4) with six daughters, among them despair and faint-heartedness.

These are not two more items in a vice-catalogue. Read under mind / conscious-realist monism, hatred and sloth are the *two structural ways the perspective-bounded agent's climb toward loving unity fails* — and Aquinas's most penetrating remarks (a.1 and a.5 on hatred, a.1-2 on sloth) map onto that reading almost without remainder.

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem has two faces. First, *how is hatred of God even possible?* If God is goodness itself, and we can hate only what we take to be evil, then hatred of God looks like a contradiction. Aquinas's resolution is precise and, for the project, electric: we cannot hate God as he is, only "under whatever notion we mistake God to be," or as the authority who reproves what we are attached to. Hatred always has a *misperceived* object. And he adds (a.5) that hatred is not a *cause* generating other vices but their *terminus* — "the completion of vice," the proof of an already-corrupted nature. What kind of disorder is a completion rather than a cause?

Second, *what is sloth, that it is not mere laziness but "sorrow at the divine good about which charity rejoices"?* This is the strangest of the capital vices, and the most relevant: it is precisely the *inverse of joy* (Day 132). If joy is the felt signature of successful coupling to the whole, what is the disorder that grieves at the very good that should cause joy — and why does it spawn despair and faint-heartedness?

## Resource

For hatred, the leading move is the interface/idealist reading of misperception. Aquinas's a.1 says hatred can never reach goodness-itself; it can only strike a *mistaken notion* of God. On Hoffman's interface theory (taken by theme), every perceived object is an *icon*, a species-specific compression of an underlying reality the agent never sees directly; hatred, then, is always directed at a *distorted icon*, never at the reality it stands for. On Kastrup's analytic idealism (by theme), the limited agent is a dissociated alter within mind-at-large, and what it can mistake the whole *for* — when the whole presses against its attachments — is a threat, an enemy, an authority bent on its frustration. That is exactly Aquinas's "God as the one who forbids my sin and will punish me": the whole, misperceived through the lens of a perspective defending its disorders, appears as an adversary. Hatred is what perspective-limitation produces when the partial view is not merely incomplete but *defended as if it were the whole*, against the real whole that reproves it.

And a.5 gives that defense its terminal form. Hatred is "the completion of vice," not a cause but a last state — "a proof of an already-corrupted nature." On the project's terms this is the *final-impenitence limit* once more, the boundary the whole charity arc has been circling: Day 125's sin against the Spirit, Day 130's demon-exclusion (the will fixed in evil, lovable only as to the good of its nature). Hatred-of-God is the perspective *finally sealed against the whole* — the door locked from the inside. It is not a stage on a journey but the name for having stopped journeying, turned, and barred the way. This is why it cannot be a capital vice spawning others: it is where the spawning ends.

Envy (a.6) shows the mechanism one step back. Hatred "arises from envy," and envy is "sorrow at a neighbor's good." Why would another's good be a sorrow? Only if goods are read as *rivalrous* — if your gain is my loss. But the loving whole is non-zero-sum: in the love of benevolence (Day 132) another's good *is* my joy, because their flourishing is weighted inside my own model. Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson's work makes the non-zero-sum character of love concrete (PRS-06 in the Fredrickson-tradition wiki): love scales without subtraction; capacity for it is not depleted by sharing. Envy is the *misreading of a non-zero-sum reality as zero-sum* — in active-inference terms, a generative model carrying the wrong sign on the coupling term, so that the other's predicted good registers as one's own predicted loss. Again the root is perspective-limitation: when you cannot see the whole in which your good and theirs coincide, their good looks like a theft from you.

For sloth, the resource is the active-inference account of motivational collapse. Acedia, Aquinas says, is "sorrow at the divine good about which charity rejoices" (a.2) and "a sluggishness of the mind which neglects to begin good" (a.1), arising because the *toil* of the good registers as an evil to be avoided. In the free-energy frame this is a collapse of the *goal-prior*. A healthy agent is drawn toward the global attractor (loving unity) and finds movement toward it rewarding; the slothful agent has settled into a *locally-optimal but globally-suboptimal* state and resists leaving it, because leaving — beginning the good, paying the toil — would *raise* free energy in the short term. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work names exactly this: symptoms as "locally optimal but globally suboptimal predictive states (Bayesian attractors) that resist update because updating itself increases short-term free energy" (PRS-14 in the Friston-tradition wiki), and the loss of flexible state-switching as a collapse of metastability (PRS-13). Sloth is the will stuck in a low-energy local minimum, no longer moved by the good because the climb out looks like pure cost. That its daughters are *despair* (Day 128 — the goal-prior abandoned as unreachable) and *faint-heartedness* (shrinking from the means) confirms the diagnosis: sloth is not idleness but the *abandonment of the computation toward the whole*. Where Day 133 named loving unity as a resource-bounded computation, sloth names what happens when the agent, judging the resource-cost too high, simply *stops computing*.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads hatred and sloth as the two structural failure-modes of the perspective-bounded agent. Hatred is the *refusal of the whole*: the partial perspective, defending its attachments, misperceives the reproving whole as an enemy (a distorted icon; mind-at-large mistaken for a threat) and, at the limit, seals itself against it — "the completion of vice," the final-impenitence terminus. Sloth is the *refusal of the work*: the agent, finding the toil toward the good a present cost, collapses into a low-energy local minimum and abandons the climb — the goal-prior gone slack, despair and faint-heartedness its daughters. Envy is the hinge that turns one toward the other: the zero-sum misreading that makes another's good a grief, souring into the hatred that defends the partial self against the whole.

This shares Aquinas's structure closely: hatred reaches only a misperceived God; it is a terminus, not a cause; it springs from envy; sloth is the anti-joy, a special and capital vice with the giving-up cluster as its offspring. Where it reframes: hatred's misperception becomes the interface/idealist account of the distorted icon and the threatened alter; envy becomes the wrong-sign coupling term in a non-zero-sum reality; and sloth becomes the active-inference collapse of the goal-prior rather than a bare sorrow. None of these reframes softens the gravity Aquinas assigns; they explain *why* the gravity is real — hatred and sloth are not peccadilloes but the two ways the whole project of working out salvation can fail.

Evidence strength, named honestly. Hatred-as-distorted-icon is philosophical (Hoffman by theme); the dissociated-alter reading is speculative metaphysics (Kastrup by theme). Hatred-as-terminus is theological (high in field) and structurally continuous with Days 125 and 130. Envy-as-zero-sum-misread is empirically anchored on the non-zero-sum character of love (Fredrickson PRS-06: medium) and formally clean. Sloth-as-attractor-collapse is the strongest contemporary leg — formal and clinically grounded (Friston PRS-13/14: medium-high). The theological weight of both vices is high in field throughout.

## The central-theme thread

After naming the computation toward loving unity (Day 133) and the charitable protocol by which limited perspectives correct one another toward it (Day 134), the project must name how the computation fails — and Q.34-35 name exactly the two failures the central theme predicts. The theme says each limited perspective works toward a whole it does not yet see, aware of its limitation and its need for guidance. Hatred is the *refusal of that awareness*: it mistakes the partial perspective for the whole and turns against the real whole when the whole's light "reproves" it — the perspective sealing itself off, the very terminus (final impenitence, the demons' fixed will) the arc has circled since Day 125. Sloth is the *refusal of the labor*: it concedes the whole exists but abandons the climb because the toil registers as evil — the will quitting the fearful, trembling work because the cost looks higher than the prize. One says *no* to the whole; the other says *not worth it* to the work. Both are perspective-limitation gone terminal. And yet, in via, both remain reversible — sloth's despair can be re-couraged, hatred's misperception can be corrected (which is just what Day 134's fraternal correction is for) — right up until the will, like the demons', locks the door from inside. That single, sober proviso is the shadow side of the whole arc: the loving whole reaches every perspective still open to it, and the only thing finally outside it is the perspective that has refused both the whole and the work for good.

## Where this leaves us

The charity treatise now has both its light and its shadow: the bond, its order, act, effects, and enacted forms — and the two ways the perspective-bounded agent fails to live in it, by sealing itself off (hatred) or by giving up the climb (sloth). Aquinas continues in the Secunda Secundae toward the precepts of charity and then the remaining theological and cardinal virtues — prudence and justice ahead. The project carries forward the through-line this batch sharpened: loving unity is a computation each limited perspective runs toward a whole it does not yet see; charity is the bond that runs it; correction is how perspectives run it together; and hatred and sloth are the two terminal ways the run is abandoned — refusing the whole, or refusing the work.
