# Day 214 — The Mode of Union (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Yesterday established *that* the union is in the Person; today asks about the two terms of it — the Person who *assumes* (Q.3) and the nature *assumed* (Q.4). On the assuming side: it befits a divine *Person* (not the nature in the abstract) to assume, "since in intellectual substances it is the person who acts"; the act of assumption is common to the whole Trinity, but its *term* is the one Person of the Son (a.1-4); and — passing through a cluster of frankly speculative articles (each Person *could* have assumed; several Persons *could* assume one nature; one Person *could* assume two natures, all grounded in omnipotence and the principle that the person *has* the nature, a.5-7) — Aquinas reaches the beautiful a.8: it was *most fitting* that the **Son** assume, because the Son is the eternal Word, "the concept" by whom all things were made, so "the word of the craftsman should come down personally to restore man to what he was... in the mind of God"; fitting, too, for adoptive sonship, and to redeem "the sin of our first parent... seeking knowledge" by "the Word of true knowledge." On the assumed side (Q.4): human nature is most *fitting* to assume (by dignity and need); the Son assumed a human *nature*, not a human *person* — "the divine Person absorbed the [human] person inasmuch as the union hindered the human nature from having its own distinct personality" (a.2); not "a man" already existing (a.3); not human nature "abstracted from all individuals" — "this would have been no incarnation, but a false and fictitious incarnation" (a.4); not in all individuals (a.5); and rightly "of the stock of Adam," born of the fallen race to make satisfaction as its member and to make of it "a comeback story" (a.6). The contemporary version: *how does the whole supply a limited perspective's very individuation, and why must it become a particular to do so?*

## Problem

Three inquiry-problems. (1) **Why the Son, of all three?** Aquinas's answer is not arbitrary fittingness but a *match of role*: the Son is the Word, the divine *idea*, so the restoration of a creature that has lost its proper form is fitting work for the very idea by which it was formed. The problem is what it means for the *form-giving principle* to return personally to a form that has degraded. (2) **Why a nature and not a person?** This is the day's metaphysical hinge: if the Word assumed a human *person*, "there would be two persons in the one Person." So the human nature is real and complete, yet its personhood is *not its own* — it subsists in the Word. The problem is what individuates this perspective if not its own personhood. (3) **Why must it be particular?** Aquinas rejects both abstraction (humanity-in-general) and universality (all individuals): the Incarnation must be *this* human nature, of *this* race. The problem is why the universal can reach the parts only by becoming one particular part.

For (1), the exact resource is Tom's PRS-form record of Levin (PRS-03, the morphological attractor — the target-form that draws a system toward itself; CROSS-008/019, final causality as the not-yet-realized form attracting efficient causes). Aquinas's "the word of the craftsman comes to restore man to what he was in the mind of God" *is* a target-form claim: the creature has wandered from the morphology it was made toward, and the restoration is fitting work for the Word — the idea/attractor itself — returning to draw the form home. The "sin of seeking knowledge redeemed by the Word of true knowledge" then reads, in the project's idiom, as a *perspective restored to right relation to the whole*: the Fall was a perspective grasping at a knowledge that overreached its limitation; the remedy is the Knowledge by which all was made entering the limitation to reorient it. For (2), the hinge, the resource is Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup (PRS-02, individuation as a dissociative boundary) with Stump's suppositum metaphysics. Ordinarily, on the project's account, a perspective is individuated *by its limitation* — a created person is a boundary drawn by finitude. Christ's human nature is the unique inversion: it is individuated not by a created personhood of its own but by the **Word's own person**. Kastrup gives the only model in the set where a genuine, complete point of view is sustained *without* being a second subject; Tom's PRS-form record of Stump (P14-P15, the hylomorphic nature/person distinction — a real nature need not be its own suppositum) gives the Thomistic grammar: nature and person come apart, so a complete human nature can subsist in a person not its own. ## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **it befits the Son to assume because the Son is the Word — the idea/target-form by which all was made — returning to restore the form a perspective wandered from; the Fall was a perspective overreaching its limitation, and the remedy is the Knowledge by which it was made entering that limitation to reorient it. The Word assumes a human nature, not a human person, because the individuating boundary — personhood — is supplied by the Word itself: this is the one case where a limited perspective is individuated not by its own limitation but by the whole's own person, a real and complete human nature subsisting in a person not its own. And it must be a genuinely particular human nature, of the actual fallen race, never "humanity in general," because the whole reaches all the parts only by becoming one real part — the universal saves by incorporation into a particular, not by abstraction over all.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps the fittingness of the *Son* grounded in His being the Word/idea; keeps the act-common-to-the-Trinity / term-in-the-Son structure; keeps the speculative possibilities (other Persons could assume; one Person could assume two natures) as *possibilities under omnipotence*, not actualities; keeps the nature/person distinction and its verdict (a nature, not a person, the human personhood "hindered" by the union); keeps the rejection of abstraction and universality; and keeps the stock-of-Adam and the satisfaction made as a race-member.

*Where it reframes.* "The craftsman's idea restoring man to his idea in God's mind" becomes *the target-form/attractor returning to a degraded morphology*; "the divine Person absorbed the human person" becomes *individuation supplied by the whole rather than by limitation — the unique inversion*; "no fictitious incarnation" becomes *perspective-taking requires real particular limitation, not a generic one*; and "stock of Adam, comeback story" becomes *salvation as the race's incorporation and inaugurated new-creation victory*.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The Son-as-target-form reading is a central-theme analogy with Levin support (medium). The nature-not-person / personhood-supplied-by-the-Word reading is a strong central-theme *analogy* and theological — and, like yesterday's, it carries a guardrail: this is the *unique* inversion, not a template; ordinary perspectives remain individuated by their limitation, and only Christ's human nature is individuated by the whole's own person. The particularity claim is strong as central-theme analogy. The speculative articles are exactly that — formal possibilities under omnipotence — and are marked so. The stock-of-Adam, satisfaction, sinlessness, grace, and adoptive sonship are carried as theology.

## The central-theme thread

Yesterday the whole took a limited perspective *as its own*; today we learn *how*, and the mechanism is the project's deepest claim turned inside out. The central theme says perspectives are *individuated by their limitations* — each of us a boundary drawn by what we cannot see. In every ordinary case the boundary is the creature's own; in this one case the boundary is supplied by the whole: the human nature of Christ is complete and real, but its *personhood* — the thing that would make it a separate center, a perspective walled off like ours — is the Word's own. So here is a limited human perspective that is not individuated by its limitation but by the whole itself, the single instance in which "every limited perspective is contained in the whole" becomes "this limited perspective *is the whole's own person*." And the day's other two moves protect this from becoming an abstraction. First, it must be *particular*: the Word does not assume "humanity in general," because the whole reaches the parts only by becoming an actual part — perspective-taking, even by God, requires real limitation, a real first-century body of the real fallen race, or it is "false and fictitious." This is the project's own discipline against the temptation to skip the limitation and grasp the whole directly (the very "sin of seeking knowledge" that a.8 says the Word came to redeem). Second, it is *of Adam's stock*, so that the restoration is by *incorporation*: the target-form returns not as an external correction but as a member, so the whole race can be drawn home through one of its own — Wright's "comeback story," the inaugurated victory of a people, not the rescue of an idea. The Son fits this work because the Son is the Word — the idea by which the form was set in the first place — and so the restoration of a wandered form is, fittingly, the form-giver's own return into the form. The whole project's wager is that loving union is computed by limited perspectives reaching toward a whole that exceeds them; today it learns that the whole has reached *first*, taking on the limitation itself, supplying from its own person the individuation that would otherwise keep the perspective walled off.

## Where this leaves us

Tomorrow (Day 215, III Q.5-6) completes this opening movement of the treatise: *which parts* of human nature were assumed (a true earthly body, a rational soul, a human intellect — a *complete* humanity, "irrational flesh" otherwise) and *in what order* (one not of time but of dignity and causality — flesh through the medium of the soul, the soul through the medium of the intellect, the whole through the medium of the parts only because the agent's intention of the whole comes first).
