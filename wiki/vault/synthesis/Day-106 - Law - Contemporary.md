# Day 106 — Law (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas opens the long treatise on law (Q.90 a.1-4) with a definition that has lasted, assembled one article at a time: law belongs to reason (a.1), is ordered to the common good (a.2), is made by one who has care of the community (a.3), and must be promulgated (a.4). He then sets the five-fold map (Q.91) — eternal, natural, human, divine (old and new), and the *fomes* (the disordered law of the members). The question for us is not whether this map is right in its medieval terms; it's whether the *underlying structure* it identifies — a real pattern that constrains thriving, accessible in different ways at different levels — picks out something that contemporary inquiry has independently reconstructed.

The contemporary version of Aquinas's question is: *Are there objective patterns that govern how complex agents flourish, accessible to agents in proportion to their perspective, and binding in something stronger than mere convention?*

## Problem

The medieval frame and the contemporary frame share an underlying inquiry-problem: agents are not simply free-floating constraint-violation engines; they exist in a structured field of possibility where some trajectories are flourishing and some are not. Aquinas's *fomes* — Romans 7's "law in my members" — names the empirical fact that even agents who recognize the higher pattern do not consistently track it. Their *perspective* is divided.

The problem can be stated more sharply: what is the relation between (a) a real pattern that governs flourishing, (b) the agent's access to that pattern, and (c) the agent's actual behavior? Aquinas's answer involves eternal law (the pattern in the divine mind), natural law (the agent's natural access), human law (locally-implemented heuristics), and divine law (revealed access). The contemporary version of the problem is whether there is *anything* like (a) — whether there are objective attractors of well-being for agents — and if so, how cognitive access to such attractors works.

## Resource: form as ontologically real and causally active

The most important contemporary move available to this question is the recovery of *form* as ontologically real and causally active — not a folk-psychological projection onto value-neutral matter.

**Levin's morphogenetic work** (see traditions/levin/wiki.md) is the empirical case in point. Bioelectric fields encode anatomical *targets* (eyespots, limbs, organ topology) that are causally responsible for the development that achieves them. The frog-tadpole regenerates its eye in a non-canonical location and the field directs cells to rebuild the correct functional structure. The "target morphology" is not a metaphor — it is an attractor in a real state-space, and the developmental dynamics relax toward it. This is precisely Aquinas's Q.93 a.1 "type/exemplar/model" cashed out scientifically: a real pattern, prior to and shaping the becoming of the thing.

**Hoffman's interface theory** (traditions/hoffman/wiki.md) cashes out the perspective-side: agents do not perceive the deeper reality directly; they perceive a species-specific *interface* — fitness-relevant signals organized in a format the agent can navigate. The natural law in Aquinas's account is *exactly* this: not the eternal pattern as such, but the eternal pattern *as accessible to rational creatures by their natural light*. Different agents see different interfaces; the pattern itself is invariant.

**Kastrup's analytic idealism** (`wiki/traditions/kastrup/prs_triplets.md`, PRS-01 — the hard problem answered by an inverted ontology in which consciousness is primary and matter its expression; recorded Explicit, Confidence High) supplies the metaphysical reframe Aquinas needs: the eternal law is *in the divine mind* not because mind is a privileged container but because mind is what reality fundamentally is. The pattern is mind-shaped because patterns *just are* mind-shaped. This dissolves the Cartesian worry about how a normative pattern could be real in a value-neutral universe.

**Friston's active inference** (traditions/friston/wiki.md) supplies the agent-side dynamics. Each agent operates with a generative model of its world; behavior is the minimization of long-term prediction error against priors. The *fomes* is exactly what active inference predicts when an agent's priors are locally adapted to a corrupt training distribution: the agent will reliably pursue what locally minimizes prediction-error even when the locally-adapted priors are systematically miscalibrated relative to the deeper truth. Aquinas's "wounded nature" is a generative model whose priors have drifted from the true target distribution.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

Under mind / conscious-realist monism, Aquinas's five-fold map can be re-described as follows:

**Eternal law** = the pattern as it is in itself — the deepest computational specification of well-functioning for kinds of agents. Under Kastrup, this is a structure within the universal mind. Under Levin, it is the target morphology of well-functioning lives. Under Hoffman, it is what the interface tracks (the reality "beyond" the desktop).

**Natural law** = the species-specific interface available to rational creatures by their natural cognitive apparatus. Each rational agent has *some* access — minimal at the level of the first principle of practical reason, increasing with virtue-formation and attention. Hoffman's interface theory predicts both the *reliability* of natural-law access (it is selected to track fitness-relevant aspects of the deeper pattern) and its *limits* (it is not the pattern itself).

**Human law** = local coordination heuristics built from the natural-law signal. These are real but tradition-dependent, contingent on the specific community, and revisable as the community's understanding grows or degrades.

**Divine law** = revelation as additional information from outside the species-specific interface — Wright's Pauline reading: covenant communication that supplements what natural law could not access on its own.

***Fomes*** = the agent's generative-model miscalibration — the locally-adapted priors that systematically pursue what reason recognizes as not-the-good. Friston names this precisely; Aquinas saw it in Romans 7.

### What this shares with Aquinas

The five-fold structure, taken seriously as a typology of *how a pattern is accessed*, survives translation. Aquinas's claim that there is a real eternal pattern, that natural reason gives partial access to it, that local communities make law from that partial access, that revelation extends access, and that agents systematically fall short — every one of these claims has independent contemporary support in the convergent paradigm.

### Where it reframes

Two key reframings:
1. **Form is internal to the world, not above it.** Aquinas locates the eternal law in the divine mind external to creation; the monist reading dissolves the gap: mind is what creation *is*, and the eternal pattern is the deep structure of that mind. This is closer to Rohr's *Universal Christ* than to a strict Thomistic externalism.
2. **Natural law is *interface-real*, not pattern-real.** The natural law is reliable but species-specific. Different rational species (or different cognitive types within a species) might access different aspects of the same deeper pattern. This puts a humility constraint on natural-law arguments: tradition-specific applications (sexual ethics, drug policy, etc.) should be held more loosely than the bare claim that *some* objective pattern is being tracked.

### Where it is empirically firm vs. speculative

*Empirical*: Levin's morphogenetic fields are reproducibly demonstrated; Friston's free-energy framework is mathematically formalized and yields predictions tested in computational neuroscience. The *fomes*-like behavior of organisms with corrupted priors is observable.

*Formal*: Hoffman's interface theorems (FBT — Fitness-Beats-Truth) are mathematical results, but their interpretation as global metaphysics is contested.

*Speculative*: Kastrup's idealism is philosophical argument, not empirical demonstration. The identification of eternal-law-as-pattern with structure-of-universal-mind is theological/metaphysical interpretation.

*Theological*: The Christological reading (Rohr) and the covenant reading (Wright) are interpretations of biblical material requiring acceptance of revelation as data.

## The Central-Theme Thread

This is where the central theme of the project surfaces: *conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective; when all limitations are removed, the simple whole is seen, and every limited perspective is contained in it. But learning to obtain the goal of loving unity from each perspective is a computational task, limited by the resources we perceive.*

Q.91's natural law is precisely the *limited-perspective* access to the eternal pattern. We "participate" in the eternal law to the degree our perspective allows — and our limitations are not bugs but the very condition of our individuation. The work of law and the work of grace (anticipated in Q.91 a.5 with the contrast of the Old and New Laws) is to bring increasingly limited perspectives into alignment with the deeper pattern, under the constraint of finite resources.

We "work out our salvation in fear and trembling" (Phil 2:12) — the project's animating verse — because we *cannot directly see* the eternal pattern. We have natural-law access (limited but real), tradition-mediated guidance (the human law of communities), revelation (divine law for the relevant communities), and the practical-rational task of bringing our behavior into closer correspondence with the pattern in the resources we have.

The *fomes* (Q.91 a.6) is the empirical fact that this is hard. The Levin/Friston bridge gives this its computational form: priors miscalibrated by inherited cultural and evolutionary history yield reliable but locally-distorted behavior; the work of correction is irreducibly developmental.
