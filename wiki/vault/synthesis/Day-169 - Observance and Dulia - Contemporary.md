# Day 169 — Observance and Dulia (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas's Q.102 (Observance) treats the virtue by which we render honor and obedience to those who excel in dignity — superiors in office, the elderly, those who have responsibility for our well-being. Q.103 (Dulia) then treats more closely the distinction between *latria* (worship due to God alone, as the source of being-itself) and *dulia* (honor due to created excellence as participated-in the divine excellence). The hidden engine of both questions is a careful gradient: who gets what kind of response, scaled to the rank-of-being from which the response is owed.

In contemporary terms, the question is: in a graded ontology where agency, consciousness, and excellence scale up and down across many levels of being, what is the correct calibration of warranted response at each level? The mind-monist convergence — Hoffman's graded moral ontology, Levin's agency-at-scale, Kastrup's daimon as principle of individuation, McGilchrist's right-hemispheric mode of recognizing the whole — supplies exactly the apparatus for this calibration.

## Problem

The shared underlying problem: a perspective-limited agent must respond differently to entities of different rank-of-being without (a) flattening the gradient — treating everything as if it were either fully sacred or fully banal — or (b) misallocating the highest response (treating a creature as if it were the source). Modern intuitions tend in both wrong directions at once. The iconoclastic intuition flattens by refusing honor to any creature; the celebrity-and-cult intuition flattens in the opposite direction by lavishing latria on whatever interface is most vivid. Aquinas's careful gradient — latria to God alone, hyperdulia to Mary, dulia to saints and to excellent persons, observance to those who hold offices — is a precision instrument that contemporary discourse has largely lost.

## Resource

**Hoffman on graded moral ontology** (Hoffman PRS-06 in the Hoffman-tradition wiki, recorded at Medium as an implicit problem — a proposal within Conscious Realism rather than a measured finding) gives the contemporary frame directly. Conscious Realism treats moral status as scaling with the structure of the perception-decision-action loop: any system with such structure is a conscious agent to some degree, and the appropriate response from another agent scales accordingly. The trace-logic extension (Hoffman PRS-07) makes this even more precise: there are multiple observer-windows on the same underlying agency, each appropriate to a different scale. Aquinas's gradient — latria-to-God, dulia-to-saints, observance-to-office-holders — is, in contemporary terms, a precision-tuned response to a graded conscious-agent ontology. The error of flattening (modern celebrity-cult or modern iconoclasm) is the trace-logic error of confusing observer-windows.

**Levin on agency at scale** (Levin PRS-09 and PRS-10 in the Levin-tradition wiki — a principled, falsifiable, non-vitalist basis for attributing cognition at any scale, and the traced continuity of goal-directedness from unicellular origins to human cognition, both recorded at high confidence; with PRS-08, cells as cognitive agents in anatomical morphospace) gives the empirical grounding. Levin's program demonstrates that real agency exists at cellular, tissue, organ, organism, and collective levels — and that each level has its own warranted response from the levels above and below. A cell that "worships" a tissue (treating the tissue as terminus rather than as itself part of a larger morphogenetic field) becomes cancerous (Levin PRS-02, cancer as defection). *QC 2026-08-24: the agency-at-scale claim was previously anchored to Levin PRS-04. PRS-04 is the generalised basal-cognition entry, "(Implicit) Cognition substrate," recorded at Confidence Medium; the cross-scale attribution claim it was carrying belongs to PRS-09/PRS-10, and the cancer clause to PRS-02. PRS-04 remains apt only for the background framing that cognition is not neuron-bound, at its true Medium.* A tissue that fails to give the cell its proper coordinative response degrades. The graded ontology Aquinas constructs theologically has, in Levin's program, an empirical analog all the way down.

**Kastrup on the daimon as principle of individuation** (Kastrup PRS-08 in the Kastrup-tradition wiki) gives the metaphysical extension. Each dissociated alter within mind-at-large is not generic but specifically characterized — it has a *daimonic signature* that warrants specific recognition. Dulia in Aquinas's sense is the honor owed to a particular excellence-instantiated-in-a-particular-person; the daimon-account gives a metaphysical reason why that honor is not merely sentimental but tracks something real about the patterned-individuation of mind-at-large. Latria, by contrast, is owed only to the *whole* of which alters are alters — which in mind-monist terms is the only fully appropriate object of unconditional response.

**McGilchrist on relational ontology with neurological grounding** (McGilchrist PRS-02 in the McGilchrist-tradition wiki) supplies the phenomenology. The right-hemispheric mode encounters created excellence in its wholeness, as a window-onto-the-whole; the left-hemispheric mode encounters it as a manipulable object. Genuine dulia requires the right-hemispheric mode — the saint's image is encountered as window, not as object. The iconoclastic worry that all such honor is idolatry is a left-hemispheric over-correction; the celebrity-cult error is the left-hemispheric reduction of the person-as-window to the person-as-stimulus.

**Wright** in *Surprised by Hope* and the broader Second-Temple work documents how this gradient operated within early Christianity. Second-Temple Judaism preserved the worship-due-only-to-God constraint with notable precision; the early ascription of worship to Jesus presupposed exactly the latria-dulia gradient and used it to make a christological claim. **Rohr** in *The Universal Christ* reads the cosmic-Christ pattern as the deepest justification for the gradient: every created excellence is a real participation in Christ's universal pattern, warranting real (but not ultimate) reverence; ultimate reverence is reserved for the pattern itself.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: in a graded ontology of conscious agents at multiple scales, the correct response from one agent to another is scaled to the rank-of-being at which the recipient instantiates excellence. Latria is appropriate only to the *whole* in which all agents participate — the ontological ground itself. Dulia and observance are the family of appropriately-scaled responses to created excellences, calibrated by what kind of participation in the whole the created excellence carries. Flattening the gradient in either direction produces specifiable pathologies: iconoclasm (refusal to honor what is honorable) destroys the recognition of participation; idolatry (treating creature as terminus) destroys the recognition of participated-from.

What this shares with Aquinas: the careful preservation of a gradient between unconditional response and graded response; the recognition that gestures of honor (bowing, kneeling, addressing-by-title) are not in themselves idolatrous but become idolatrous only when their inner referral is misdirected; the insistence that latria-to-God-alone is a *protection*, not a deprivation, of created excellence.

What it reframes: rather than ground the gradient in a metaphysics of being-in-itself versus participation, the contemporary frame grounds it in the structure of a mind-monist ontology where individuated agents are patterned-alters of one consciousness, and warranted response tracks the scale of the agency-pattern. The shift preserves Aquinas's distinctions while reframing their metaphysical justification.

Evidence strength: the graded moral ontology Hoffman defends and the agency-scaling Levin defends are empirically tractable. The neurology of relational mode (McGilchrist) is empirical. The metaphysical reading of latria-dulia within a daimonic-individuation frame is interpretive.

## Central-theme thread

The perspective-limited agent participates in loving unity from within a network of agents of varying scale and excellence. Recognizing this requires the daily calibration of warranted response — neither flattening everyone-and-everything to interchangeable instances, nor treating any single created excellence as the whole. Latria-to-God-alone is the contemplative recognition that the only adequate response to the whole-as-whole is unconditional; dulia and observance are the contemplative recognitions that participation in the whole genuinely shows up in created persons, and warrants specifically-tuned response. Salvation-in-fear-and-trembling, on Wright's reading, is participation in a covenanted people who together get this calibration right; on Rohr's reading, it is the contemplative awakening to the universal-Christ pattern as the ground of all warranted reverence.

## Where this leaves us

Q.102–103 has carefully calibrated the gradient of warranted *recognition*. Tomorrow's Q.104–105 on obedience and disobedience extends the same logic into the gradient of warranted *compliance*. If dulia is the correct response to created excellence within the gradient, obedience is the correct response to legitimate authority within the gradient — bounded by the same constraint that latria is owed only to God, so obedience is owed only within the limits of the office's legitimate commission. The pair sets up Aquinas's last word in this stretch of II-II: how an embedded agent honors the authority of office without surrendering its ultimate judgment to it.
