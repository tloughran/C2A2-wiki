# Day 173 — Kinds of Lies (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas's Q.110 anatomizes lying. A lie is speech contrary to one's mind, and it is always a sin because speech is by nature a sign of thought: to assert what one does not hold is to misuse the very finality of the sign. He divides lies threefold by intent and effect — the *officious* lie told to help, the *jocose* lie told in play, and the *mischievous* lie told to harm. All are sins, but they are not equally grave: the mischievous lie can be mortal, while officious and jocose lies are ordinarily venial. Q.111 treats dissimulation and hypocrisy — the lie enacted not in word but in deed, the performance of a virtue or holiness one does not possess.

The contemporary version, under mind-monism and continuing yesterday's honest-signaling frame: speech is the channel through which perspective-limited agents share what they alone can see. A lie is the deliberate corruption of that channel. The right question is not only whether lying is wrong but *how to grade* its wrongness — and the answer turns out to track the magnitude of the lie's damage to the shared model on which cooperation depends.

## Problem

A community of limited agents can only approximate the whole by pooling perspectives, and pooling requires that each agent's reports be reliable indices of what it actually holds. Deliberate misreporting injects false data into other agents' generative models, degrading the shared world. The problem has two layers. The first is the *act*: a single false assertion, whose harm varies enormously with intent and consequence. The second is the *disposition*: the standing performance of a self one is not — hypocrisy — which corrupts not a datum but the agent's entire signal, making everything it reports unreliable. Grading the wrong requires distinguishing these, and distinguishing among the acts by their actual cost to the cooperative structure.

## Resource

**Friston on mutual modeling** supplies the spine, and the register supports one half of it. PRS-06 in the Friston-tradition wiki (multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling, Confidence Medium, and labelled Implicit — it is a reconstruction rather than a stated position) holds that agents couple by reducing prediction error in each other's behaviour, so that mutual informational alignment is what makes a collective agent out of individuals. That is the coupling claim. The *honest-signalling* step — that this coupling requires signals to be reliable indices of internal states, so that deception is a distinct pathology of it — is **not** in the register: the 40-entry Friston register was searched on 2026-08-24 for deception, deceit, honesty, signalling, communication, lying and trust, and returns nothing on topic. The step is this commentary's own extension of PRS-06, and should be read as suggestive rather than formal. Granting it: if cooperation among active-inference agents depends on signals being reliable indices of internal states, then deception is precisely the decoupling of signal from state — the injection of false evidence into others' models. This gives a principled gravity scale: a lie's seriousness is a function of how much it corrupts the shared model and how much harm that corruption causes. Aquinas's threefold division maps onto it cleanly. The *mischievous* lie aims its corruption at another's harm — maximal damage to the cooperative structure, hence potentially mortal. The *officious* lie corrupts the channel but in service of a perceived good; it still degrades signal-integrity (which is why it remains a fault) but its damage is bounded. The *jocose* lie operates inside a frame both parties understand as play, where the signaling convention is locally suspended by agreement — minimal corruption, hence the lightest fault. The medieval intent-based gradient and the contemporary harm-to-model gradient are the same gradient seen from two sides.

**McGilchrist** (PRS-01 in the McGilchrist-tradition wiki) supplies the account of hypocrisy. The manipulative mode of cognition can construct and maintain a performed self — a presentation optimized for effect and detached from what is actually disclosed. Hypocrisy is this detachment made chronic: the agent's whole self-signal becomes a manipulation. McGilchrist's diagnosis of a culture that mistakes the performed presentation for the real is, at the individual scale, exactly Aquinas's hypocrite.

**Hoffman** (PRS-03 in the Hoffman-tradition wiki, Confidence High) gives a careful boundary. Interface theory holds that perception *normally* decouples appearance from underlying reality — natural selection drives true perception to extinction, so perception is an adaptive interface rather than a truth-mirror. But the lie is a different thing: not the species-level interface doing its adaptive work, but a *deliberate* decoupling of one's own signal from one's own state, aimed at another's model. The contrast sharpens what is wrong in lying: it is the weaponizing, against a fellow agent, of the very gap between appearance and reality that the interface ordinarily manages honestly.

**Stump** enters here: a lie is a betrayal of the second-personal relationship, a refusal to let oneself be truly known by the one addressed; hypocrisy is the counterfeit of personal presence — offering a self that is not there to be known. The second-personal knowing this leans on is PRS-11 in the Stump-tradition wiki (Confidence High), the account of non-propositional "Franciscan knowledge" of persons. The register develops it for biblical suffering-narratives rather than for truthfulness, so the transfer to the lie is this commentary's step, not a citation of one.

**Wright** grounds the scriptural form: truth as covenant faithfulness, and Jesus' sharpest denunciations reserved precisely for hypocrisy — the "whitewashed tombs" of Matthew 23, beautiful in signal and corrupt in state. This stays a canonical anchor rather than a numbered one: the 41-entry Wright register was searched on 2026-08-24 for truth, truthfulness, covenant faithfulness, hypocrisy, Matthew 23 and integrity, and holds nothing on personal truthfulness. The nearest entry, PRS-28 on the cruciform test for politically instrumentalised Christian claims, diagnoses a corporate rather than a personal hypocrisy and should not be substituted here.

**Rohr** gives the depth reading: hypocrisy is not merely occasional but structural — the False Self, the performed identity that every agent is tempted to mistake for itself (PRS-15 in the Rohr-tradition wiki, Confidence High, which identifies the ego/false-self as the thing being liberated from). The contemplative path is the slow exposure of that performance and the recovery of the True Self that has nothing to hide (PRS-05, Confidence Medium, on the mechanism by which the false self becomes the true one — not erasure of the wounded pattern but its redemptive re-reading). Neither entry is sourced to *Immortal Diamond*, the work this commentary's frontmatter had named; both are anchored here by content.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: lying is the deliberate corruption of the shared signaling channel by which limited agents pool their perspectives, and its gravity scales with the harm that corruption does to the cooperative structure. This preserves Aquinas's threefold gradient and his conclusion that all lies are faults while not all are grave. Hypocrisy is the gravest form not because any single act is worst but because it corrupts the agent's entire signal, making the agent unmodelable and so unincludable.

What it shares with Aquinas: that lying is always a fault; the threefold gradient by intent/effect; the special seriousness of enacted, standing falsehood (hypocrisy). What it reframes: rather than ground the wrong solely in the natural finality of speech as a sign of thought, it grounds it (also) in the role of honest signaling in maintaining shared models among agents — which yields the same gradient on independent grounds and explains *why* harm scales the gravity. Evidence, as corrected by the QC sweep of 2026-08-24: the mutual-modeling substrate is formal but Medium-confidence and Implicit in the register (Friston PRS-06); the honest-signaling and gravity-scaling steps built on it have **no** register support and are this commentary's own extension, so they are suggestive rather than formal or empirical; the fitness-beats-truth boundary is formal (Hoffman PRS-03, High); the natural-finality argument remains a philosophical resource; the hypocrisy-as-False-Self reading is theological (Rohr PRS-15/PRS-05).

## Central-theme thread

If loving unity is computed by perspective-limited agents pooling what each can see, then truthful speech is the medium of the computation and the lie is its sabotage. The officious and jocose lie are small leaks; the mischievous lie is targeted damage; the hypocrite is a corrupted node whose every output must be discounted. The deepest danger, as Rohr names it, is that the agent comes to *believe its own performance* — the False Self that no longer knows it is performing — at which point the agent has lost not only honesty with others but the self-knowledge it needs to receive correction. Working out salvation in fear and trembling requires keeping one's signal honest enough that one can be truly known, corrected, and included.

## Where this leaves us

Q.110–111 anatomized the failures of truthfulness in word and enacted deed. Day 174 turns to two failures that lie at the borders of truthfulness about *oneself*: boasting (Q.112), the overstatement of one's own excellence, and irony or self-deprecation (Q.113), its understatement. Both are species of untruth about the self — the same signal-integrity concern, now turned reflexively onto how an agent reports its own standing, where the temptation is not to harm another but to distort the picture others (and oneself) hold of one's own worth.
