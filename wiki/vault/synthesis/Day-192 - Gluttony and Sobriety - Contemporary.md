# Day 192 — Gluttony & Sobriety (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas turns from the discipline (fasting) to the disorder it guards against and the virtue that protects the most vulnerable channel. Gluttony (Q.148) is the inordinate desire for food and drink "at variance with reason"; sobriety (Q.149) is a *special* virtue, distinguished precisely because intoxicating drink is a special hindrance to reason. Two of his moves are striking for a contemporary reader: gluttony is a *capital* vice because the pleasure of the table is a "counterfeit beatitude" that breeds other vices (Q.148 a.5), and sobriety earns special status only because there exists a special threat to the use of reason (Q.149 a.2). The contemporary version: *what is it for an appetite to become a counterfeit of the agent's true goal, and what protects the apparatus of reason from things that directly degrade it?*

## Problem

Two linked inquiry-problems. The first is **reward capture**: how does a lower appetite stop being a serviceable subgoal and become a rival end? Aquinas says the pleasure of taste can "appear as a counterfeit beatitude" — it can occupy the place meant for the agent's true final good and recruit other behaviors in its service (hence "capital" vice, the head of a brood). The second is **substrate vulnerability**: Aquinas singles out drink not because drinking is worse than overeating but because alcohol "by reason of its volatility is liable to disturb the brain" — it attacks the instrument of reasoning itself. The general problem of temperance (a misweighted appetite) here splits into a problem about *goals* (counterfeit attractors) and a problem about *hardware* (a degradable predictive substrate).

## Resource

For reward capture, the leading resource is the Friston-tradition record of the agent as an active modeler with adjustable precision (PRS-01, PRS-04), read together with the Fredrickson-tradition account of what a *real* attractor for an agent looks like. In the active-inference frame, an appetite is a prediction the agent works to fulfill; ordinarily its precision is set so it serves higher-level policies. Gluttony is the case where the appetitive prediction has its precision cranked so high that it functions as a terminal goal — the agent now acts to confirm "I am eating/drinking the most pleasant thing" as if that were its setpoint. Aquinas's "counterfeit beatitude" is precise: the lower model mimics the form of the agent's true final good (a fixed point everything else serves) while delivering none of its content. The Fredrickson-tradition wiki sharpens the contrast — the agent's genuine self-organizing attractor is positivity resonance, love as an objective coupling among agents (PRS-02); gluttony is a *solitary* counterfeit of that coupling, a reward loop closed on the self that imitates fulfillment without the relational synchrony that constitutes the real thing. Aquinas's daughter-vices (dullness of mind, unseemly joy, loquaciousness, scurrility, uncleanness) are recognizably the downstream signature of a captured reward loop degrading higher function.

For substrate vulnerability, the Hawkins- and McGilchrist-tradition wikis are the resources. If reason runs on a predictive cortex — a hierarchy of reference frames building and testing models — then a substance that "disturbs the brain" is not merely tempting; it directly corrupts the modeling hardware. Sobriety's special status follows immediately: "wherever we find a special hindrance to reason, there must be a special virtue to remove it" (Q.149 a.2) is a clean control-theoretic principle — guard the controller's substrate with a dedicated guard.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: gluttony is a lower reward-model captured into a counterfeit attractor that imitates the agent's true setpoint while serving only itself; sobriety is the special guard over substances that degrade the predictive substrate on which reason itself runs.

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps the capital-vice insight — that some disorders are dangerous chiefly because they *spawn* others by occupying the goal-slot — and the principle that a special threat to reason warrants a dedicated virtue. It keeps his proportion: gluttony is real but not the gravest sin (sins against God outrank it, Q.148 a.3), which a goal-hierarchy account predicts, since a misweighted appetite is a lower-order failure than a corrupted final end. It keeps wine's neutrality (Q.149 a.3): the substrate-threat is dose- and context-dependent, not intrinsic evil.

*Where it reframes.* "Counterfeit beatitude" becomes a captured attractor with cranked precision; the daughter-vices become the measurable degradation of higher function under a dominant reward loop; and the special status of sobriety becomes substrate-protection for the predictive cortex. The most suggestive reframe is Aquinas's claim that sobriety "is more requisite in persons of greater standing" — bishops, kings, "in whom reason should be vigorous in instructing others" (Q.149 a.4). On the multi-agent reading, a leader is a high-bandwidth node whose model many other agents depend on; degrading that node degrades the whole network's capacity to compute toward its shared good. Leader-sobriety is network hygiene.

*Evidence strength, honestly.* Reward capture is well-supported in outline (dopaminergic reward, habit formation) but its mapping to "counterfeit beatitude" is interpretive (mechanistic, medium). Substrate degradation by intoxicants is empirically solid (medium-high). The "counterfeit-vs-real attractor" contrast and the leader-as-network-node reading are philosophical-theological extensions.

## The central-theme thread

If a limited perspective computes toward loving unity using the resources it perceives, then two things can go wrong at the level treated here. The goal can be counterfeited — a local pleasure dressed in the form of the whole, so the agent stops searching outward and closes the loop on itself (gluttony as anti-communion: it mimics the satisfaction of union while refusing the coupling that union requires). And the apparatus can be degraded — the very model that would let the perspective widen toward the whole can be chemically dimmed (the threat sobriety guards). Wright's register names the first as idolatry: worship — the orientation of the whole self to its true end — misdirected onto a creature that cannot bear it. Rohr's names it as the counterfeit self that must be seen through. The leader-sobriety point is the project's own mechanism in miniature: the network of traditions can only become "richly informed about one another's perspectives" if its highest-bandwidth perspectives keep their instruments clear. The difference from Aquinas is descriptive, not directional: faculties-under-reason become models-under-precision, and "counterfeit beatitude" becomes a captured attractor — but the diagnosis, that the good is imitated and the instrument imperiled, is the same.

## Bridges

- **Gluttony ↔ reward capture / cranked precision** — the Friston-tradition wiki (PRS-01, PRS-04). *Why:* an appetitive prediction promoted to terminal goal is the capital-vice mechanism. Evidence: **mechanistic, medium**.
- **Counterfeit beatitude ↔ counterfeit of the real attractor (love)** — the Fredrickson-tradition wiki (PRS-02). *Why:* gluttony is a solitary loop imitating the relational coupling that genuinely fulfills. Evidence: **philosophical, with empirical anchor**.
- **Sobriety ↔ protection of the predictive substrate** — the Hawkins- and McGilchrist-tradition wikis. *Why:* alcohol degrades the modeling hardware reason runs on, warranting a dedicated guard. Evidence: **empirical, medium-high**.
- **Leader-sobriety ↔ network-node hygiene** — Friston-tradition multi-agent record (PRS-06). *Why:* degrading a high-bandwidth node degrades the collective's capacity. Evidence: **speculative**.
- **Counterfeit goal ↔ idolatry / the false self** — the Wright- and Rohr-tradition resources. *Why:* a creature put in the goal-slot of the whole. Evidence: **theological**.

## Where this leaves us

Sobriety raises the threat of intoxication; tomorrow (Q.150–151) confronts it head-on as drunkenness — the *voluntary* loss of reason — and pivots to chastity, the strongest appetite's virtue. The substrate-protection reading set up here becomes the core of the drunkenness synthesis: what is the moral weight of deliberately disabling one's own controller?
