# Day 247 — Baptism & Circumcision (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (III Q.70) compares baptism with circumcision: both are signs of faith in Christ (one prospective, one accomplished), both confer covenant membership, yet baptism supersedes and exceeds circumcision in efficacy. The medieval question is continuity-with-revision: how a later sign can fulfill and replace an earlier one without the earlier being a mistake. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: how does a community *revise its own generative model* — the very signs by which it tracks reality — while remaining the same tradition faithfully extended rather than a different one?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **how can the model itself be revised — not just beliefs within a fixed scheme, but the scheme's defining signs — without rupture of identity or admission of prior error?** Aquinas needs circumcision to have been genuinely efficacious *and* genuinely surpassed. A tradition revising its sacramental signs faces exactly this: deep revision that is nonetheless continuous.

## Resource

Friston supplies the formal move. In Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's program, PRS-04 (the organism as an active modeler) gives revision of estimates *within* a generative model; the structural case — revision of the scheme itself — is PRS-10, whose problem field is precisely that active inference "lacks formal treatment of reasoning — the process by which agents evaluate and select among competing hypotheses or world models." Structural revision is model *selection*, not model updating: a reconfiguration that preserves the system's deep priors (its preferred states) while changing the surface variables through which it tracks the world. Baptism-replacing-circumcision is, formally, a structural model revision that conserves the deep prior (covenant membership, faith in Christ) while changing the tracking sign.

Wright is the closest fit. His five-act hermeneutic and "faithful improvisation" describe precisely a community that *extends* its founding story into new territory, acting consistently with the trajectory while responding to what the earlier authors could not anticipate. His new perspective on Paul reads the circumcision-to-baptism transition as covenant continuity, not supersession-by-rejection. Rohr's order-disorder-reorder names the same arc: a received order passes through crisis into a deeper reorder that preserves what was true.

The *form* of this analysis — a tradition as a narrative of Problems, Resources, and Solutions that progresses rationally — is **Tom's** PRS framework, lineage Kuhn (solved problem as unit of progress) + MacIntyre (traditions as P/R/S narratives) + Levin via William James. It is cited here as Tom's re-description, not as any thinker's self-account.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the move from circumcision to baptism is a faithful structural revision of the covenant community's generative model — the deep prior (membership through faith in Christ) is conserved while the tracking sign is reconfigured — so the earlier sign was efficacious in its act of the drama and is fulfilled, not falsified, by the later.** This *shares* with Aquinas the continuity-and-supersession both at once. It *reframes* "fulfillment" as conserved-prior-under-revised-model and "faithful improvisation" (Wright) rather than replacement-by-correction. It *marks* that Wright's covenant reading is theological (high within his critical-realist method), the Friston structure is a formal analogy, and the PRS/tradition-progress frame is Tom's methodological lens, not empirical data.

## The central-theme thread

The project holds that limited agents need guidance from those who perceive more of the whole, across time. A tradition that revises its signs while conserving its telos is the social-temporal form of that guidance: no single generation perceives the whole, so the community computes toward unity across acts of an unfinished drama (Wright), each revision a better-resourced approximation. The circumcision-to-baptism transition models how perspective-limited communities make genuine progress without rupture — the same shape the C2A2 project itself enacts across its traditions.
