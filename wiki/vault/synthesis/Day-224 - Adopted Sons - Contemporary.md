# Day 224 — Contemporary Parallel: Adoption as Divinization, the Exemplar of the Limited

## Frame

Q.23 asks whether it is fitting that God should *adopt* sons, and what adoption is. Aquinas's working definition: "A man adopts someone as his son inasmuch as out of goodness he admits him as heir to his estate" — and grace does this supernaturally, so that "by God's gift we begin to share in a supernatural knowledge of God through faith, a supernatural love of God through charity," and as adopted heirs we are "due to be heirs if we persevere in grace." The inheritance is staggering: "heaven for us... will be knowing God and knowing his essence... insofar as the creature is able by God's grace" — the limited creature elevated to share, by participation, in the very beatitude that *is* God's own happiness. Why fitting? "God is infinitely good... and the good is self-diffusive," so the whole Trinity adopts. But a.4 marks the asymmetry: Christ is *not* adopted — he is the *natural* Son, because the person is the eternal Word ("adoption is of persons, not natures"). Q.24 then attributes *predestination* to Christ "in respect of his human nature," and makes him the *exemplar* of ours ("like Christ, our human nature is elevated by sanctifying grace... our beatitude consists in the vision of God's essence") and, "as to its term," the *cause*: "God so decreed our salvation that it should be achieved through Jesus Christ." The contemporary version: *how can a limited, finite perspective come to share in the whole's own vision — and what grounds the limited's destiny toward that sharing?*

## Problem

Two inquiry-problems. (1) **Divinization: the finite sharing the infinite's life.** Adoption is the claim that the limited rational creature can be *elevated* to participate in the beatitude proper to God — knowing the divine essence. The problem is how a bounded perspective could come to share the unbounded vision without simply being the unbounded vision (which would erase its individuation). (2) **What grounds the destiny?** Q.24 makes Christ the exemplar-and-cause of the limited's predestination: the limited perspectives' trajectory toward the shared vision is grounded in, and modeled on, the one whose human nature already holds it. This is the central theme's structure — the limited guided by, and incorporated into, the one who sees the whole — stated as *predestination*.

## Resource

Because adoption-and-elevation is a question about what the limited creature is *drawn toward becoming*, the leading move is Levin's morphogenetic teleology. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin (PRS-01, PRS-03) describes biological systems *computing toward a target form not yet realized* — cells navigating "anatomical morphospace" toward an attractor, a goal-state that pulls the present state toward itself. Adoption-as-divinization has exactly this shape: the graced creature is drawn toward a beatitude it does not yet possess, elevated toward a "form" (sharing the divine vision) beyond its current nature — teleology not as metaphor but as the real structure of a system under attraction toward a goal. For *heirship without loss of individuation*, Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup (PRS-02) supplies the structure: a dissociated "alter" can *share in the life of the whole* — partake of mind-at-large — while remaining a bounded point of view; the heir shares the inheritance without ceasing to be a distinct heir. Tom's PRS-form record of Stump (PRS-05) enters here: suffering and the whole trajectory are "instrumentally permitted for the highest intrinsic good — union with God and persons," so the elevation toward shared beatitude is intelligible through its *telos*, which is precisely the loving-unity the project names. The scriptural anchor is the adoption corpus: "you have received the spirit of adoption as sons... heirs of God and fellow heirs with Christ" (Rom 8:14-17), "he predestined us for adoption" (Eph 1:5) — and Tom's PRS-form record of Wright (PRS-05) frames the inheritance as *incorporation into the people among whom God comes home*, not relocation of souls. Rohr's Universal Christ (PRS-03) keeps the adoptive pattern cosmic: the divine pattern present in and drawing all creation toward itself.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **adoption is the limited perspective being elevated by grace to share, by participation, in the whole's own vision — drawn toward a beatitude not yet possessed the way a morphogenetic system is drawn toward a target form not yet realized (Levin PRS-01/03), and sharing in the life of the whole without losing its individuation the way a dissociated alter partakes of mind-at-large while remaining bounded (Kastrup PRS-02). Christ, who already holds the shared vision in his human nature, is the exemplar-and-cause that grounds the limited's destiny — the bridge figure whose perspective the limited are predestined to be incorporated into.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps divinization as *participation*, not identity — the creature shares God's beatitude "insofar as it is able by grace," remaining a creature (Kastrup's bounded alter, not the absorption of the perspective). It keeps the asymmetry of a.4: Christ is the *natural* Son, the one whose vision the adopted *share* rather than originate. And it keeps Q.24's exemplar-and-cause: the limited's destiny is grounded in the one who already holds the end.

*Where it reframes.* Aquinas grounds adoption in grace-elevating-nature and predestination in the divine pre-ordination; the contemporary reframe reads the elevation as attraction-toward-an-unrealized-telos (morphogenetic) and the heirship as alter-participation-in-the-whole (dissociation). Predestination stops being only a decree and becomes the structure of limited systems drawn toward the goal-state held by the one who sees the whole.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* Divinization-by-grace and the natural-vs-adopted-Son grammar are theological. The adoption/heirship corpus (Rom 8, Eph 1) is scriptural (Wright, strong). Morphogenetic attraction toward an unrealized form is *empirical as biology* (Levin's planaria/xenobot data) but applied to grace by analogy (medium). The alter-participation structure is speculative-formal (medium).

## The central-theme thread

The project's claim is that "every limited perspective is contained in" the simple whole, and that the limited compute toward loving unity under guidance from those who see more. Adoption is that containment named as *inheritance*: the limited creature is admitted as heir to share the whole's own beatitude — "knowing God and knowing his essence... insofar as the creature is able by grace." This is not the erasure of the limited perspective but its elevation — Kastrup's alter sharing mind-at-large while remaining itself — which is exactly what "contained in the whole" must mean if individuation is preserved. And Q.24 grounds the trajectory in the bridge figure: Christ's predestination is the *exemplar* (the limited are drawn toward the form he already holds) and the *cause* ("achieved through Jesus Christ") — the one who sees the whole is the attractor and the ground of the limited's destiny toward it. The morphogenetic reading sharpens the point: the limited are not merely *told* to seek the shared vision; they are *drawn* toward it, as a system under attraction toward a goal-state — and the goal-state is held, exemplarily, by the comprehensor. Loving unity, here, is the inheritance the limited are predestined to share.
