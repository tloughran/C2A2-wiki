# Day 185 — Vainglory (Contemporary)

> Sequencing note: Habash flags that this episode (Q.132–133) is out of broadcast order and should precede Day 184's magnificence (Q.134–135). By locus it sits here, between ambition (Day 183) and magnificence.

## Frame

Q.132 treats vainglory (*inanis gloria*): the inordinate desire for glory, the *manifestation* of one's own excellence. Glory is the public shining-forth of a good; it is "vain" — empty — when it is sought for things not truly excellent, or from those whose judgment is worthless, or for oneself rather than referred to God. Aquinas catalogs its "daughters" — boasting, love of novelty, hypocrisy, contention, disobedience, presumption of novelties — the brood of behaviors that grow from organizing a life around being *seen* as excellent. Q.133 treats pusillanimity (*pusillanimitas*), the defect opposed to magnanimity: smallness of soul, the failure to attempt great goods one is in fact capable of, shrinking from what reason and one's real powers would warrant.

Under mind / conscious-realist monism the two are mirror errors about the self. Vainglory over-invests in the self's *image*: the agent optimizes for how it is represented in the generative models of others — not merely desiring honor (yesterday's ambition) but actively *performing* excellence for display. Pusillanimity under-invests in the self's *real capacity*: the self-model set below the truth, so the agent declines goods within its reach.

## Problem

The self can be mis-perceived in two opposite ways, and both are failures of the same magnanimous calibration. The first is to over-weight one's *appearance*: an agent for which the dominant term is how it shows up in others' eyes will shape its conduct toward display — and because the audience's recognition is the goal, the conduct hollows out, since what is rewarded is the *appearing* and not the *being*. Aquinas's word for the emptiness is exact: the glory is *vain* because it refers to nothing real, or refers the real good to the wrong end. The second failure is to under-weight one's *capacity*: an agent that judges itself smaller than it is will shrink from great goods, not from fear of danger (that was timidity) but from a false reading of its own resources. The problem is that these two — chasing the image and refusing the real largeness — look like opposites (vanity vs humility) but are *the same disorder*: a self-estimate decoupled from the truth, once inflated toward the image, once deflated below the capacity.

## Resource

**Friston** gives the formal account of both. In Tom's PRS-form record of his work, an agent's generative model includes a model of how it is modeled by others (a second-order, social self-model). Vainglory is high precision on *that* term made terminal: the agent's policy is selected to optimize its represented-excellence in others' models, with the represented standing in for the real. This is ambition's proxy-capture taken one step further — not just seeking the honor signal but *manufacturing the appearance* that elicits it. Pusillanimity is the inverse of magnanimity's calibration: precision on the self-capacity model set *below* the truth, so high-value, ambitious policies score as infeasible and are never selected. The two vices flank magnanimity on the self-estimate axis exactly as fearlessness and timidity flanked fortitude on the threat axis.

**Fredrickson** supplies the relational diagnosis. In Tom's PRS-form record of her work, genuine positivity resonance is real coupling between agents; *display* is its counterfeit — a performance of connection or excellence aimed at being witnessed rather than at the witnessed reality, and it does not build the durable resource that genuine resonance does. Pusillanimity, on her framework, is a repertoire kept chronically narrow not by threat (the timid case) but by a low self-estimate that never licenses the broadened, ambitious action — the upward spiral never started because the agent does not believe it has the resources to begin.

**McGilchrist** names the cognitive signature of vainglory directly: attention captured by one's own *representation* — the explicit, abstractable image of oneself in others' minds — rather than by the living work or the living other. It is the left hemisphere's attachment to the token in its purest social form. Pusillanimity, conversely, is a failure of the right hemisphere's reverent confidence: the agent cannot let itself be drawn out toward the great good because it has mis-read its own standing before it.

**Stump** enters as Thomistic interlocutor, holding the precise sense of "vain": glory is the manifestation of real excellence, and its vanity is *emptiness* — manifestation referred to nothing real, or to judges whose recognition is worthless. The disorder is not visibility as such but visibility *decoupled from the reality it should show*.

**Wright** grounds the re-sourcing. Paul's resolution of the glory problem is not to seek no glory but to relocate its source: "Far be it from me to boast except in the cross of our Lord" (Gal 6:14), and "let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord" (1 Cor 1:31). The cure for vainglory is not self-effacement but a true object of glory — God's act, not the self's image. **Rohr** gives the contemplative form on both sides: vainglory is the False Self's *whole economy*, the self constructed for and sustained by being seen; and pusillanimity is, in his terms, the failure to claim the True Self's *given* largeness — a false humility that declines the greatness God has actually conferred. For Rohr both are healed in the same move: worth received in union, neither performed for an audience nor disbelieved.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: vainglory is high, terminal precision on the self's *appearance* in others' models — the agent performing excellence for display, optimizing the represented good in place of the real (Friston, Fredrickson, McGilchrist). Pusillanimity is the self-capacity model set *below* the truth, so the agent shrinks from great goods within reach (Friston) — the upward spiral never started for lack of self-estimate (Fredrickson). The two are mirror decouplings of self-estimate from truth: inflated toward image, deflated below capacity.

What it shares with Aquinas: vainglory as the inordinate desire for the *manifestation* of excellence, vain because empty; pusillanimity as the defect opposed to magnanimity. Where it reframes: the surprising unification — vanity and false humility as the *same* disorder (a self-estimate decoupled from truth), which Aquinas's structure implies but the precision language makes explicit. Where it goes beyond the formalism: both are healed by re-sourcing worth from the whole — God's verdict rather than the audience's, the True Self's given largeness rather than the performed or the disbelieved one (Wright, Rohr). Evidence strength, marked honestly: the appearance-precision and self-estimate accounts are formal and empirical; the claim that worth should be sourced in God is theological, firm within Wright's and Rohr's programs and openly so beyond them.

## Central-theme thread

Here the central theme cuts cleanly through a pair that looks like opposites. A perspective is individuated by its limits — and the right response to one's limits is neither to perform a largeness one does not have nor to disbelieve the largeness one does. Vainglory is the perspective curved entirely onto its own image: its whole computation runs toward how it appears to other limited perspectives, an audience that cannot confer the worth it seeks. Pusillanimity is the perspective that has under-counted the resources actually given it, and so refuses the great goods through which it might have moved toward unity. Both miss loving unity — one by self-display, one by self-diminishment — and both, on the central claim, can only be healed the same way: by re-sourcing worth from the *whole* rather than from the self or its audience. The agent that knows its worth is held in a whole that sees all perspectives at once has no need to perform for the part, and no warrant to disbelieve the largeness that whole has actually given it. That is the magnanimous center these two vices flank: working out salvation neither by spectacle nor by shrinking, but by accurate self-knowledge held within union.

## Bridges

- **Friston (precision on appearance; sub-true self-estimate)** — vainglory as terminal precision on represented-excellence; pusillanimity as capacity under-estimate. *Formal.*
- **Fredrickson (display as counterfeit resonance; stalled spiral)** — performed excellence that does not build; the upward spiral never started for low self-estimate. *Empirical.*
- **McGilchrist (attention captured by one's representation)** — vainglory as the social token prized over the living reality. *Empirical / interpretive.*
- **Stump (the vanity of empty manifestation)** — visibility decoupled from the reality it should show. *Theological / metaphysical.*
- **Wright + Rohr (boast only in the Lord; True Self's given largeness)** — worth re-sourced to God's verdict; vanity and false humility healed in one move. *Theological.*

## Where this leaves us

With vainglory and pusillanimity, the self-estimate axis around magnanimity is complete: presumption and ambition on the excess side (Day 183), pusillanimity on the defect side, vainglory as the display-counterfeit, and magnanimity (Day 182) as the calibrated center. By locus this pair precedes magnificence (Day 184), and the two readings interlock — magnificence is the right *outflow* of resource into great works, vainglory the outflow diverted into the *display* of one's excellence; reading them as a pair sharpens both. Open thread, now strong enough to act on: across this entire bloc — timidity, fearlessness, foolhardiness, presumption, ambition, vainglory, pusillanimity, meanness — the vices keep resolving into a *small set of mis-set precisions* (threat, capacity, appearance, scarcity) flanking a calibrated virtue, with the theological term supplying the ranking the formalism cannot. That recurrence is worth a dedicated cross-cutting note before the treatise leaves fortitude, as the clearest structural payoff the fortitude bloc has produced.
