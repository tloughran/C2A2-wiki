# Day 152 — Contemporary Parallel: Accusation as Distributed Truth-Finding

## Frame

Q.68 turns from the substance of an injustice (theft, robbery, killing) to the *procedure* by which the political whole finds out what has been done. Is one bound to accuse (a.1)? What distinguishes accusation from denunciation (a.2)? What are the corruptions of accusation — calumny, collusion, evasion (a.3)? Q.69 turns to the defendant: must one answer truthfully (a.1), is perjury lawful (a.2), may one defend oneself with falsehood (a.3), is escape from a just sentence permitted (a.4)? Together they form the medieval account of the *adversarial yet cooperative* apparatus by which a polity learns what one of its parts has done.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem in both frames: *no single agent in the polity can see what every other agent has done, yet the whole's response to a defection requires knowing what was actually done — so what procedure allows partial perspectives to compose into a finding the whole can act on?* Aquinas answers from the categories of office and duty: accusation is what one is bound to *do* (when the crime injures the common good); the defendant has rights of self-defense; perjury and false witness corrupt the procedure; escape from a just sentence subtracts from the whole's restoration. Under mind-monism the question becomes: how does a distributed inference apparatus made of bounded role-occupants converge on a finding that the polity-as-whole could not directly perceive?

## Resource

Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work provides the formal apparatus. The polity's truth-finding procedure is a multi-agent active-inference system in which each role-occupant has weighted priors and constrained inputs (PRS-28 precision-weighting; PRS-06 multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling). The accuser supplies an *observation* drawn from a perspective the rest of the apparatus cannot directly access; the defendant supplies a *competing model* from the perspective only the agent has; witnesses supply triangulating evidence; the judge integrates. The three corruptions Aquinas enumerates (Q.68 a.3) — calumny, collusion, evasion — map cleanly onto three structural failure modes of such an apparatus: calumny corrupts the input (false signal injection); collusion corrupts the integration (two role-occupants coordinating to bias the inference); evasion withholds the role-occupant's required contribution (a node that should report goes silent). PRS-04 (intention as model-target) sharpens the moral reading: each role exists *for* the truth-finding goal; defection from the role-target is what the three corruptions formally are.

Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work supplies the biological metaphor that makes the apparatus more than analogy. PRS-17 (collective cognition) is the explicit empirical claim that real bodies *do* this: components hold local information and the body integrates it without any one component seeing the whole. PRS-02 (defection) names what accusation tracks — a part has stopped acting for the field — and the courtroom is the polity's analogue of the immune system: the apparatus by which the field detects, evaluates, and decides how to respond to defection.

Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work supplies the social-ontological backing. PRS-14/15 (corporate substance) makes the procedural roles real parts of a real whole — not legal fictions but the actual structure of how the polity acts. PRS-11 (second-personal knowledge) supplies the defendant's standing: the defendant is not merely an object of inquiry but a *person* whose perspective is constitutive of the case; this is why Aquinas grants lawful self-defense (Q.69 a.2) — every accusation is provisional until the accused's first-person testimony is integrated.

Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's work provides the scriptural ground. Biblical *mishpat* (justice) is right-relation, and the burden-of-proof structure runs through Torah (multiple witnesses required for capital offense; the accuser tested for malice). The procedure is not a Western legal artifact; it is the form of a covenant community's response to alleged wrong, recognizable across cultures because it answers the same problem.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed contemporary parallel preserves the substance of Q.68-69 with the metaphysics made monist. *Preserved*: there is a duty to accuse when the common good requires it (Q.68 a.1) — a node of the apparatus may not withhold information critical to the whole's correction; calumny, collusion, and evasion are real wrongs (a.3) — each corrupts the apparatus in a distinct way; the defendant has lawful self-defense (Q.69 a.1-2), short of perjury (a.3); escape from a just sentence is wrong (a.4) — it withdraws the part from the whole's restorative response. *Reframed*: the courtroom is not an arbitration imposed on contestants but the polity's distributed inference apparatus — a real instrument of the collective cognition (Levin PRS-17) made of bounded role-occupants whose specialization is precisely the polity's way of seeing what no single perspective could see. *Extended*: the three corruptions (calumny, collusion, evasion) gain a formal taxonomy as the three structural failure modes of any distributed inference engine, predictable from the apparatus's design.

Measure against Aquinas: convergence on the substance and on most of the moral readings, with the metaphysics of "office" recast from juridical category to functional component of the polity's truth-tracking machinery. The reading sharpens why Aquinas's enumeration (calumny / collusion / evasion) is exhaustive at the level he draws it: those are exactly the three places a distributed inference apparatus can fail. Where the contemporary frame extends Aquinas: it lets us see why procedural injustice in any one role propagates — corrupted input cascades into corrupted integration, which corrupts subsequent decisions — and why repairing the procedure matters as much as repairing any particular wrong it failed to find.

## The central-theme thread

Accusation is one of the project's clearest cases for the central claim. No part of the polity can see what every other part has done; the parts that can see something the rest cannot are bound to report; the apparatus that integrates partial views is the polity's way of approximating the perspective only the whole could have. Calumny, collusion, evasion are three ways a part can absolutize its own perspective against that apparatus — falsifying input, coordinating against the integration, withdrawing the input the integration requires. Each is a small-scale version of the defection theft and murder are at larger scale: the part acting as if its own perspective were the whole, rather than offering its partial view as the apparatus requires. The defendant's lawful self-defense is the converse safeguard — the apparatus *requires* the accused's perspective, and any procedure that suppresses it defects from the whole-tracking telos.
