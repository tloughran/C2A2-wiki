# Day 215 — The Order of Assumption (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

The opening movement of the treatise on the Incarnation closes by asking *which parts* of human nature were assumed (Q.5) and *in what order* (Q.6). Q.5 insists on completeness: a true earthly body (a.1-2), a rational soul (a.3), and a human mind/intellect (a.4) — "if Christ had had a soul without a mind, he would not have had true human flesh, but irrational flesh." Nothing human is left out, or He would not be "true God and true man" nor the savior of the race as its member. Q.6 then clarifies that the "order" in question is *not temporal* — every part is assumed at the one instant of conception — but an order of *dignity* and *causality*: the flesh is assumed "through the medium of the soul" (the soul is nearer God in dignity, and the body exists *through* the soul), the soul "through the medium of the intellect/spirit" ("the purest part of the soul," Damascene), and — decisively (a.5) — the whole is assumed "through the medium of the parts" only in the sense that *the agent's intention of the whole comes first in the order of nature*: "the Word assumed the parts through the medium of the whole." Q.6 a.6 closes on grace: "the grace of union is the personal being given *gratis* from above... and is the term of the assumption," while habitual grace (the man's holiness) follows it. The contemporary version: *how complete is the assumed perspective, and what is the priority of whole over parts in a union?*

## Problem

Two inquiry-problems. (1) **How complete must the assumed perspective be?** Aquinas is emphatic — body, soul, *and* intellect, or it is "irrational flesh." The problem is why nothing can be omitted: why a *partial* humanity would fail the work. (2) **In what sense is the whole prior to the parts?** Aquinas denies a temporal order and asserts an order of dignity and causality, culminating in the claim that the agent's intention of *the whole* is first in the order of nature. The problem is to give this whole-priority a precise sense.

## Resource

For (1), the resource is Wright. Tom's PRS-form record of Wright (PRS-05, salvation as incorporation into the people, as a true member of the race) requires the completeness directly: only a *complete* humanity can incorporate the race, so the assumed perspective must include everything human — the savior saves what He assumes, and "what is not assumed is not healed" (the patristic axiom standing behind a.4). For (2), the exact resource is Tom's PRS-form record of Levin (PRS-03, the morphological attractor; CROSS-008/019, final causality as the not-yet-realized whole that draws and organizes the parts). Aquinas's "the Word assumed the parts through the medium of the whole — on account of the whole that was first intended" *is* a final-cause/whole-priority claim: the target whole is prior to the parts and organizes them, exactly as a morphological attractor is prior to and organizes the cells that realize it. And Tom's PRS-form record of Stump (P14-P15, the hylomorphic form that organizes its constituents without dissolving them) gives the order-of-dignity its grammar: flesh-through-soul-through-intellect is a *hierarchy of form*, the higher organizing the lower, not a clock. Rohr's register (PRS-03/PRS-04, the Universal Christ truly *in matter*, a real earthly body) keeps the completeness from spiritualizing away the flesh — the whole takes on the body, not merely the mind.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the whole assumes a *complete* limited perspective — body, soul, and intellect, with nothing human left out, because a partial humanity could neither be a true member of the race nor heal what it failed to assume. And the "order" of assumption is not a sequence in time but a hierarchy of form: the whole, intended first by the agent, is prior to and organizes its parts — the morphological whole before the parts, the target-form before the material — so that flesh is held through soul and soul through intellect as lower-through-higher, all at the one instant of conception.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps the completeness (body, soul, intellect; "irrational flesh" otherwise); keeps the order as one of dignity and causality, not time; keeps the whole-priority ("parts through the medium of the whole," the agent's intention of the whole first in nature); and keeps the grace of union as the *term* of assumption with habitual grace following.

*Where it reframes.* The completeness becomes *the whole taking a full perspective, not a partial one*; the order-of-dignity becomes *a hierarchy of form/levels rather than a temporal sequence*; and "parts through the medium of the whole" becomes *the morphological whole prior to its parts — final cause before material* (Levin's attractor).

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The completeness claim is strong as central-theme analogy and is theological (carrying "what is not assumed is not healed"). The whole-priority-as-morphological-attractor reading is a strong central-theme analogy with Levin/Stump support (the final-causality bridge is among the better-attested in the set, CROSS-008/019). The order-of-dignity as a hierarchy of form is analogical (medium). The grace of union and the term/follows distinction are carried as theology.

## The central-theme thread

The treatise's opening movement closes by guarding two things the project cares about most. First: the assumed perspective is *complete*. When the whole takes on a limited perspective it does not take a convenient fragment — not a body without a mind, not a mind without a body — but the full human nature, "or it would be irrational flesh." For a project whose wager is that the whole can contain every limited perspective, this matters: containment is not abstraction, and the perspective contained is real, entire, embodied, intellectual — a whole human way of seeing, taken on in full. Second: *the whole is prior to its parts*. Aquinas denies that the assumption runs part-by-part in time and insists that "the whole that was first intended" comes first in the order of nature, the parts assumed *through* it. This is the morphological-whole reading the project has leaned on since the metaphysics days — the target-form prior to and organizing the material that realizes it — now applied to the supreme union: the whole does not assemble a perspective bottom-up out of parts; it intends the whole perspective first and holds the parts within it. That is the project's own picture of how "the simple whole contains every limited perspective" — not as a sum of fragments but as a whole that is prior to, and organizes, all that it contains. And the grace of union as the *term* of assumption — the personal being given *gratis* — keeps the asymmetry of Day 213 in view: the union is sheer gift from the side of the whole, and the perspective's own holiness (habitual grace) *follows* from being taken in, rather than earning the way in. Rohr's register keeps the flesh real (the Universal Christ truly in matter); Wright's keeps the purpose in view (a complete humanity so the race can be incorporated). The opening of the Incarnation treatise thus ends where the project began: a whole, prior to its parts, taking a complete limited perspective wholly into itself, by grace.
