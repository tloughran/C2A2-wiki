# Day 130 — Contemporary Parallel: The Order and Reach of the Loving Whole

## Frame

Two long questions on charity's subject and object. Q.24 treats charity in its growth: it is seated in the will (a.1), infused not acquired (a.2), it can increase (a.4) — and increases by the subject's *ever-deeper participation*, not by the addition of parts (a.5) — it has no fixed limit to its increase in this life (a.7) though it cannot be perfect in this life (a.8), it admits of three degrees: beginners, the proficient, and the perfect (a.9), and it cannot decrease by degrees though it is lost entirely by one mortal sin (a.10, a.12). Q.25 treats charity's object — *what charity loves*: the same act loves God and neighbor (a.1) and can take itself as object (a.2); God first, then oneself (a.4), one's own body (a.5), one's neighbor; whether we love sinners (a.6), our enemies (a.8-9), the angels (a.10), and even the demons (a.11); closing with the order of charity (a.12) — God, then one's own soul, then the neighbor's soul, then the body, all loved for God's sake.

The contemporary version completes the arc. Yesterday charity was defined as the coupling that binds many limited perspectives into one loving whole. Today asks two things of that bond: *how does it grow?* and *how far does it reach?* Mind / conscious-realist monism, and the central theme it serves, has a clean reading of both — and the order of charity turns out to be the very structure the central theme names.

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem has two parts. First, *what kind of quantity is love, that it can grow without limit and yet by deepening rather than accumulation?* Most quantities grow by addition and hit ceilings; Aquinas says charity grows by intensification of the subject's participation and has no ceiling in this life. Second, *if love binds agents into a whole, what is the structure of that whole — who is included, in what order, and is anyone excluded?* Aquinas gives a definite ordo (God, self, neighbor's soul, body) and a definite reach (sinners and enemies are in; demons are out). A satisfying account must explain both the *shape* of the inclusion and its *one limit* without making either arbitrary.

## Resource

For the growth question, the resource is Fredrickson's program. In Tom's PRS-form record of her work, positivity resonance generates *upward spirals* — micro-moments of connection that build vagal tone and capacity for further connection, a self-reinforcing deepening with no obvious ceiling (PRS-01). This is the empirical shape of Aquinas's a.5 and a.7: charity increases not by adding discrete units of love but by the subject becoming a more capacious participant in the bond, each act of love widening the capacity for the next. Friston's coupling (PRS-06) gives the formal version — mutual model-alignment tightens iteratively, and there is no fixed bound on how aligned coupled agents can become short of full union. The three degrees (a.9 — beginning, progress, perfect) describe a coupling that moves from chiefly avoiding decoupling (the beginner resisting sin), through active deepening (the proficient), to stable union (the perfect, longing to be wholly with the source). Rohr's order-disorder-reorder pattern (PRS-02, high) is the nearest record in that tradition, and the resemblance should be stated with its limit: PRS-02 is about *epistemological crisis* — a received order disrupted, and a reorder that is deeper because it was tested — whereas Aquinas's middle term is progress, not disorder. The register holds no maturation-stages record as such. Reading the three degrees as a developmental arc is therefore this commentary's own extension of PRS-02, not a claim the record makes.

Aquinas also holds that charity has no ceiling *in this life* (a.7) and yet cannot be perfect in this life (a.8) — the increase is unbounded and the terminus is not reached. That pairing is precisely the already/not-yet structure Wright's inaugurated eschatology names (PRS-03, high): the decisive event has occurred and its full implications are still being worked out, so the present is neither repetition nor blank slate but the space between the decisive insight and its full elaboration. Charity in via is that space, and the "no ceiling" of a.7 is what open-endedness looks like from inside it. Charity grows like a spiral, not a stack.

One further pair of articles belongs here, and the contemporary frame has a sharp reading of it. Aquinas holds that charity cannot *decrease* (a.10, a.12): since it comes from God, nothing lessens it by degrees — and yet it is lost entirely through a single mortal sin. So the quantity is asymmetric. It rises without ceiling and by gradations; it does not fall by gradations at all. On the coupling account this is not a paradox but a recognisable shape: intensification of participation is continuous, while the bond itself is a *binary* condition of the agent — either the agent's ordering is toward the whole or it is not. A coupling can tighten by any increment; whether an agent is coupled at all is not an increment. The falling-away is a phase transition, not an attenuation, which is why Aquinas can say charity is never diminished and can still be wholly lost. Marking this matters for the rest of the arc: it is the same asymmetry the despair and presumption articles (Day 128) turn on, and the same one the final-impenitence limit below depends on.

For the order-and-reach question, the resources are Levin, Stump, and Friston together. The order of charity — God, then one's own soul, then the neighbor's soul, then the body — is a *concentric* structure, and the contemporary set reads concentric inclusion as *nested agency*. Levin's program gives the picture, and it can be named to the record rather than gestured at by theme. PRS-30 holds the nesting claim directly — subjective vantage points distributed continuously across nested cognitive scales, cells to tissues to organisms to constructs (high; it is recorded as a live disagreement with Kastrup over whether that gradient is continuous or resolves into discrete alters, which is worth keeping in view rather than smoothing over). PRS-80 states the containment ordering explicitly — cognition as the outermost circle, physical systems and living systems as nested subsets within it (high, from Levin's own words) — which is the mind-first version of a concentric order and not merely a biological analogy for one. So a self is already a nested whole, and caring scales outward in proportioned shells. The second half of the usual gloss needs a caution: the "cognitive light cone" records in that register (PRS-40, PRS-41) are an *AI-alignment* record and a *cancer* record, whose shared criterion is whether the light cones of subunit and whole overlap on the relevant homeostatic variables. The formalism transfers to the order of charity; the empirical credit those records carry — cancer biology, alignment failure — does not, and is not claimed here. Friston's multi-agent coupling (PRS-06) makes the proportion precise: an agent's preferred-states include others' states weighted by the strength and nearness of coupling. Stump's corporate hylomorphism (PRS-14, and PRS-16, the tradition as a real united-something) gives the metaphysics: the whole that charity forms is a genuinely ordered body whose members stand in real relations of nearness, not an undifferentiated blur. So Aquinas's ordo is not favoritism; it is the structure any real whole-of-coupled-agents must have — inclusion proportioned to nearness to the center, which for Aquinas is God himself.

Before the order, the unity. Q.25 opens by asking whether the love of charity stops at God or extends to the neighbour (a.1), and Aquinas answers that it is *specifically the same act* by which we love God and by which we love our neighbour — one habit, one act, two terms. This is the article the whole coupling reading rests on and it should be named rather than assumed: if loving the neighbour were a second, different act, then the "one loving whole" of yesterday's synthesis would be a federation of separate bonds rather than a single one. Aquinas's a.1 says it is one bond, and a.2 adds the reflexive case — the love of God can itself be loved, since an immaterial act can take itself as object. On the coupling account that reflexivity is not a curiosity: an agent that can model its own coupling can deliberately cultivate it, which is exactly what the upward-spiral evidence describes people doing.

Two features of the order matter for the project. First, self and body are *included* (a.4-5): proper self-love is part of the order of charity, not its enemy — on the coupling account, an agent must remain a well-formed agent (a maintained Markov blanket, a cared-for body) to be a node in the loving whole at all. The order of charity is not self-erasure; it is the right placement of the self within the whole. Second, the reach extends to sinners and enemies (a.6, a.8-9). We love sinners by loving the good of their nature and their possible restoration, not their sin; and charity *requires* love of enemies, at least in the common love of all and in readiness, and in its perfection by showing them the signs of love. This is the Jesus-distinctive that Wright locates at the historical center of Jesus' kingdom-announcement (read through the Gospels and Paul) — the love that refuses to let the boundary of the in-group define the boundary of the loved. Fredrickson supplies the empirical echo: love of *strangers* and out-group members (PRS-15, PRS-06) carries lasting prosocial benefit and is biologically available to all — radical inclusivity is not only commanded but measurable. Rohr gives the cosmic scope: even irrational creatures are loved out of charity (a.3), because the divine pattern is present in all of it. The record for that is PRS-21 — Universal-Christ-in-creation, "creation as the first incarnation," wild others as sacred (medium). Aquinas's own limit on that article should be kept in view — irrational creatures are loved out of charity in so far as we wish their preservation to God's honour and to human use, but not *as friends*, since charity is a friendship with God and friendship extends only to what can share the common end.

And then the one limit. We are *not* bound to love the demons out of charity (a.11), because their will is fixed finally in evil; what remains lovable is only the good of their nature, not them as possible friends. This is not an arbitrary exception — it is the exact structural mirror of Day 125's final impenitence. Charity reaches every perspective that can still be drawn into the whole; the only thing outside its friendship is the perspective *permanently sealed against the whole* — the will that has, in the language of Day 125, locked the door from the inside. The reach of charity and the limit case of the sin against the Spirit are the same boundary seen from opposite sides: love extends as far as a perspective remains open to union, and no further, because friendship cannot be one-sided where the other has finally refused it.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads charity's growth as an unbounded upward spiral of deepening coupling (intensification of participation, not accretion of parts), and the order of charity as the concentric structure of nested agency — inclusion proportioned to nearness to the center (God), embracing self and body (the well-formed node), neighbor, stranger, and even enemy (the good of every nature still open to union), and excluding only the will finally fixed against the whole. This shares Aquinas's structure closely: growth by participation without ceiling; a real ordo rather than a blur; self-love rightly placed rather than denied; the radical reach to sinners and enemies; and the single, principled exclusion.

Where it reframes: Aquinas grounds the order in degrees of how a thing shares in the divine goodness and stands to the lover. The parallel grounds it in proportioned coupling within a nested whole (Levin, Friston, Stump) — which makes the ordo legible as the necessary structure of any real loving whole rather than a ranking of worth, and which makes the demon-exclusion legible as the final-impenitence limit rather than a failure of love's generosity. It also reframes "no limit to charity's increase in this life" as the open-endedness of a self-reinforcing spiral whose only terminus is full union — exactly Aquinas's "perfect" degree.

Evidence strength, named honestly. The upward-spiral growth of love is empirically supported (Fredrickson PRS-01: medium-high) and formally coherent (Friston PRS-06). The concentric/nested order is theologically firm (high in field) and reframed through nested-agency and coupling models that are partly empirical (Levin PRS-30 and PRS-80, both high, though PRS-30 records a live disagreement rather than a settled result), partly formal (Friston), partly speculative at the corporate scale (Stump PRS-14/16: medium). The light-cone criterion is borrowed formalism from alignment and cancer records (Levin PRS-40/41) and carries none of their empirical credit here. Love of enemies and strangers is a theological keystone (Wright: high in field) with a measurable echo (Fredrickson PRS-15: medium-high). The demon-exclusion-as-final-fixity is theological (high in field); its identity with the Day-125 limit is offered as a structural illumination.

## The central-theme thread

This is where the central theme receives its ordo. The claim has always been that "when all limitations are removed, the simple whole is seen and every limited perspective is contained in it." Q.25 shows what that containment looks like from within, before the limitations are removed: a concentric inclusion proportioned to nearness to God — self rightly placed, neighbor and body embraced, stranger and even enemy drawn in by loving the good of their nature, all of it loved for God's sake. The order of charity is the in-via structure of the loving whole the project has been computing toward. And its one exclusion completes the symmetry the whole stretch has been building: charity reaches every perspective still open to union (the reach is, in principle, total — even enemies, even the cosmos), and stops only at the perspective finally sealed against it. The boundary of love is not love's failure; it is the structural shadow of freedom — the same door, seen from outside, that final impenitence locked from within. Hope held the whole as reachable; charity is the bond that reaches it; and the order of charity is the shape that bond takes as it gathers every limited perspective, in its proper place, into the simple whole.

## Where this leaves us

The arc from hope to charity is complete: hope as the goal-prior (Day 126), fear as its precision (127), despair and presumption as its two failures (128), charity as the coupling that binds the many into one (129), and now charity's growth and reach — an unbounded spiral gathering every open perspective, in proper order, into the loving whole. Aquinas continues in charity (II-II Q.26 onward: the order of charity in detail, then its acts — joy, peace, mercy, beneficence, almsgiving, fraternal correction). The synthesis is now positioned to take up charity's *acts*: what the binding does once it holds — how the loving whole, having gathered its perspectives, lives.
