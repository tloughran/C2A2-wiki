# Day 279 — Defining Marriage (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.43–44 ask Aquinas to *define* marriage and to fix what betrothal (the espousals) is and when it binds. His definition turns on the joining of consenting wills directed to an undivided partnership of life: betrothal is a promise *de futuro*, marriage proper is constituted by consent *de praesenti*. The bond, crucially, is made by the *joining of wills*, not by consummation — a real entity called into being by an act of mutual intention. The contemporary version of the question is: **what kind of act can bring a genuine higher-order unity into existence between two agents — can a unity be constituted by an act of joint intention rather than by mere physical fact?**

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is the **constitution of a real relation by an act**. Aquinas must explain how words of present consent *make* something — a bond with effects no prior agreement had — without that something being a mere physical event (since the bond exists before consummation) or a mere fiction (since it has real binding force). The contemporary sciences of joint agency face the parallel: when do two coupled agents *become* a unit? A merely behavioral account (they're married once they act married) under-determines the bond exactly as consummation under-determines it for Aquinas; something must constitute the unit before its characteristic behavior appears.

## Resource

The Friston-tradition program supplies the leading move. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-06 in the Friston-tradition wiki, *multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling*) locates the birth of a dyadic unit at the moment two generative models mutually adopt one another into their predictions — when each begins to model the world *as half of a pair*. That mutual adoption is an *act of joint intention*, not a physical event, which is a strikingly exact naturalistic gloss on consent-*de-praesenti*-makes-the-marriage. The Kastrup-tradition program supplies the metaphysics of how two can be joined while remaining distinct: PRS-02 (*individual identity within monism*) and PRS-25 (*dissociative constructs that are real yet not ultimate*) hold that individuation is a real but soft boundary within one underlying mind, so that two individuals coming into a constituted unity is not the merger of two substances but the partial relaxation of a dissociative boundary. PRS-7 in the Rohr-tradition wiki (*relationship ontologically prior*) keeps the bond from being a mere add-on. PRS-32 in the Levin-tradition wiki (*cognition scaling cell→organism*) supplies the lower-scale precedent: a higher individual is constituted when components commit to a shared target, before the organism "behaves."

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the marriage bond is constituted by an act of mutual intentional adoption — two generative models electing to be one — and this constituting act is precisely why consent, not consummation, makes the marriage.** What it *shares* with Aquinas: consent as the efficient cause of the bond, and the bond's reality prior to any consummating behavior. Where it *reframes*: Aquinas treats the joined wills as producing a quasi-juridical bond; the monist reframe treats consent as the *coupling event* that initiates a real shared generative model (and, on Kastrup's reading, a softening of the dissociative boundary between two perspectives). Where evidence is *firm*: that joint commitment, not shared behavior, is what individuates a higher agent is well-modeled at biological scales. Where *speculative*: the application to a human marital "we" rests on analogy; the phenomenology of two people coming to think as a pair is real, but its identification with formal dyadic coupling is interpretive.

## The central-theme thread

Consent is the central theme's defining act in miniature. Two perspectives, each unable to see the whole, *elect* a shared whole and bind themselves to compute their lives from it together. The project's claim that loving unity is a task taken up under limitation is nowhere clearer: marriage begins not with achieved union but with a vow to pursue it — "fear and trembling" is the native key of the wedding consent, which promises a unity the partners cannot yet see.

## Bridges

- **Friston PRS-06 (formal / speculative):** the dyadic unit is born when two models mutually adopt each other — a naturalistic reading of consent-makes-marriage.
- **Kastrup PRS-02 / PRS-25 (metaphysical, speculative):** union as relaxed dissociative boundary, not merged substances — two remain distinct while constituting one.
- **Rohr PRS-7 (theological):** relation is prior, so a constituting act produces something real, not a fiction.
- **Levin PRS-32 (empirical at biological scale):** higher individuals are constituted by committed shared targets before characteristic behavior — the precedent for consent-before-consummation.

## Where this leaves us

Having argued that consent *constitutes* the bond, the next question is what makes consent *valid as a coupling act*. Open thread: the Kastrup "relaxed-boundary" reading of union needs to be squared with marriage's indissolubility claims, since a relaxed boundary suggests reversibility the sacrament denies.
