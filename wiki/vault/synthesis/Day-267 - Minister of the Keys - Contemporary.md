# Day 267 — Minister of the Keys (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (Suppl. Q.19-20) moves from the *power* of the keys to its *minister* and its *subjects*. The power of order, conferred in ordination, makes one able to wield the keys; jurisdiction is required to wield them lawfully and validly over a given person. And the power reaches only so far: it is exercised over the baptized, over one's own subjects, over the living — not over non-members, not over those outside one's jurisdiction, not over the dead. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: what fixes the *scope* of a collective agent's corrective authority — over whom can the whole act, and why does the reach stop exactly where it does?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **what determines the boundary of a corrective authority — the set of members on whom the higher-order whole can actually act?** Aquinas answers with membership, subjection, and life (baptism, jurisdiction, the living state). The contemporary question is whether that triple limit is an arbitrary canonical rule or the expression of something structural about how wholes act on their parts.

## Resource

Friston's program gives the structural answer. In Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work, PRS-22 names the *group-level* Markov blanket — a statistical boundary maintained by the collective as a whole, distinct from the blankets of its individual members — and holds that a collective sustaining one thereby satisfies the formal conditions to be modeled as a single active-inference agent. That boundary is what makes an interior: the whole's dynamics act on its internal states, and what lies across the blanket is, by definition, not interior to it. A collective agent, coupled by the mutual modeling of PRS-06, can therefore act only on the members inside that boundary; a system outside it is not a subject the whole can bind or loose, because it is not part of the self the whole regulates. PRS-23 sharpens this into a matter of degree — a community is "one agent" to the extent that it sustains the group-level blanket, and degradation of the boundary marks fragmentation. Read this way, "only one's own subjects" is not a bylaw but a statement of what jurisdiction *is*: the corrective power reaches precisely the members inside the boundary, and no further.

Levin's program gives the biological instance. In Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work, PRS-02 reads cancer as *defection* — a cell's departure from the body's cooperative morphogenetic community — and names the remedy as bioelectric coherence, restoring the cell to the collective field rather than editing its genome. The corollary is the point here: the morphogenetic field exercises top-down control over the cells coupled into the body's bioelectric network, and only those. A cell electrically isolated from the field is not commanded by it; the whole's authority is exactly co-extensive with the coupling. The keys' limit to "one's own subjects" mirrors the field's limit to its own cells.

Wright supplies the ecclesial sense of the boundary: covenant membership defines who is "inside" the community improvising the five-act story — the church acts within its own covenanted body. Rohr keeps jurisdiction from hardening into ownership: the minister's authority over his subjects is service of a pattern that exceeds him, not dominion over persons. That valid exercise *requires* ordination and jurisdiction, and that the power does not reach the dead (whose state is fixed), remain sacramental-theological claims.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the minister of the keys can wield them only by a power proper to the office (order) and a coupling to the subject (jurisdiction), and the power reaches exactly the members inside the boundary of the whole — the baptized, the subject, the living.** This *shares* with Aquinas the order/jurisdiction distinction, the limit to one's own subjects, and the exclusion of the dead and the unbaptized. It *reframes* the scope-limit as a structural fact about collective agency: a whole acts on what is inside its own group-level Markov blanket (Friston PRS-22, on the coupling of PRS-06), as the morphogenetic field commands its own coupled cells (Levin PRS-02). It *firms up* that "jurisdiction" names a real coupling, not a paper permission — the authority is genuinely powerless across the boundary. It *marks* that the *validity* tied to ordination, the grace conveyed, and the fixed state of the dead are theological; the resource explains why corrective reach tracks membership, not what the sacrament effects within it.

## The central-theme thread

The scope of the keys states the central claim's converse. If a perspective-limited agent is guided toward loving unity by a whole that perceives more than he does, then that guidance can reach him *only insofar as he is coupled into the whole* — only the member sharing the community's life can be bound and loosed within it. The minister's authority stops at the boundary of the perspective-sharing body because guidance presupposes belonging: one cannot be oriented toward a unity one has not entered. The keys bind the living subject — the agent still computing his way toward the whole — and not the stranger outside the coupling or the dead whose computation is complete.
