# Day 142 — Contemporary Parallel: The Anatomy of a Practical Agent

## Frame

Having defined prudence as right reason about the means (Day 141), Aquinas takes it apart. Q.48 distinguishes three senses in which a virtue has "parts": *integral* parts (like ingredients — all required for the perfect act), *subjective* parts (its species — individual, domestic, political prudence, taken up on Day 143), and *potential* parts (allied virtues subordinate to its chief act). Q.49 then names the eight integral parts: memory, understanding, docility, shrewdness, reason, foresight, circumspection, caution. Catalogued as powers of a soul, the list reads as medieval inventory. Catalogued as the competencies an agent needs in order to act well on contingent particulars, it reads almost as a design specification — and that is how the mind / conscious-realist monism this project writes from will take it.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem is the one Q.47 a.3 opened and Q.49 now answers part by part: *what does it actually take for a finite agent to apply a universal rightly to a singular, contingent case?* Aquinas's framing is itself the clue. Prudence "regards contingent matters of action," where one is "directed not by those things that are simply and necessarily true but by those which occur in the majority of cases" — and "we need experience to discover what is true in the majority of cases." The problem is not deduction from certainties; it is good-enough inference from accumulated, uncertain experience, fast enough to act on. To name the parts of prudence is to specify the sub-capacities such inference requires.

## Resource

Take the parts in their functional clusters. **Memory** (a.1) is the first and most telling. Aquinas grounds it in "the majority of cases," which is to say in *base rates learned from experience* — exactly the learned priors that Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work treats as the agent's generative model (PRS-04), and that the cortical-modeling line in the Hawkins-tradition wiki treats as a structure *built up* from repeated exposure, not given a priori. Prudence's memory is not rote recall; it is the trained prior over how the world tends to go.

**Understanding** in a.2 — which Aquinas carefully distinguishes from the intellect's grasp of essences and identifies with the *cogitative sense* or "particular reason," the "right estimate about a particular end" — is the binding problem: how a general model meets *this* concrete state. On Tom's PRS-form record of McGilchrist's work, this is the apprehension of the living particular that attention performs (PRS-05/01), the place where the universal premise ("it is good to go to Mass") becomes a particular one ("I should go *this* Sunday"). It is the interface between the model and the case.

**Foresight, circumspection, and caution** (a.6–8) are one forecasting cluster. Foresight "implies the notion of something distant to which the present has to be directed" — forward modeling toward a goal. Circumspection asks "whether a means be suitable in view of the circumstances" — context-fit, the precision-weighting of the present situation. Caution handles the case where "evil is mingled with good" and "evil has the appearance of good" — risk-aversion under ambiguity. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-16) unifies exactly this: decision under both risk (known distributions) and ambiguity (volatile ones). The three parts are a bounded agent's planning under uncertainty, divided by what they hedge against.

**Shrewdness** (*eustochia*, a.4) — "the discovery of the middle term," the quick supplying of the connecting inference ("friends of an enemy are also enemies") — is abductive search: finding the link that lets a conclusion close. **Reason** (a.5) is the discursive movement of counsel itself, "proceeding from certain things to others."

**Docility** (a.3) stands apart, and it is the deepest. Because "such matters are of infinite variety, no one man can consider them all," so "man stands in very great need of being taught by others, especially by old folk who have acquired a sane understanding of the ends." Tom's PRS-form record of MacIntyre's work names this directly: practical reason is learned within a practice, by apprenticeship in a craft-tradition (PRS-01, P3), not invented alone. And the Stump-tradition wiki's record of narrative cognition (PRS-07) adds why the transmission cannot be mere propositional summary: some practical knowledge is held only in the shape of lived cases, transferable only by being taught through them. Docility is the channel by which one perspective's hard-won priors become another's inheritance.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads the eight integral parts as *the component competencies of a bounded-rational agent acting on contingent particulars*: learned priors (memory), universal-to-singular binding (the cogitative sense), prior-acquisition from others (docility), abductive search (shrewdness), discursive inference (reason), forward modeling (foresight), context-fit (circumspection), and risk-avoidance (caution). With Aquinas: all are genuinely *parts of one virtue*, jointly necessary for the perfect act of command, and ordered finally to the end charity supplies. Where it reframes: the parts are functions of an agent rather than powers of a substantial soul, and "the majority of cases" is read frankly as the base-rate structure of a learned model. The reframe is unusually low-friction here, because Aquinas's own framing — contingent matters, the majority of cases, experience, the need to be taught — is already the vocabulary of inference under uncertainty.

Evidence, named honestly: that practical competence rests on learned base rates (memory) is robust; that planning decomposes into forecasting, context-fit, and risk-handling (foresight/circumspection/caution) is a strong formal analogy; the cogitative-sense-as-binding reading is interpretive but well-motivated; and the claim that these perfect *one* virtue ordered to charity's end is theological, carried by the field, not by the model.

## The central-theme thread

Docility is where this question touches the spine of the project. The central claim insists that each agent's perspective is incomplete and that we therefore need "guidance from those who perceive more of the whole than we do." Aquinas says the same thing as a structural feature of prudence itself: the field of particulars is of "infinite variety," no single perspective can survey it, and so the prudent agent *must be teachable* — must receive its priors from those whose longer experience has seen more of the field. Memory is the store of cases one perspective has accumulated; docility is the link that lets that store be pooled across perspectives. This is the tradition working as the central claim says it must: not many isolated agents each recomputing the whole from scratch, but a community in which the limited share what none alone could see, the elder's "sane understanding of the ends" becoming the novice's starting model. The eight parts are the anatomy of a single limited perspective; docility is the part that connects it to the others.

## Where this leaves us

The single virtue has been resolved into its working components. Tomorrow Aquinas turns to the *subjective* parts — the species of prudence that scale from the individual to the household, the city, and the kingdom — and to the connected virtues of deliberation and judgment that feed into command. The thread sharpens: if today's question showed how one limited perspective is built to act well, the next shows the same competency running at nested scales, and the moment when the learned rule fails the singular case and judgment must reach past it.
