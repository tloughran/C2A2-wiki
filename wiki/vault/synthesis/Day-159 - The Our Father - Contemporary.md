# Day 159 — Contemporary Parallel: The Our Father, or, Prayer as the Perspective's Active Alignment

## Frame

Q.82 takes up the *interior acts* of religion, beginning with devotion: devotion as a special act (a.1), of religion (a.2), as the cause of joy (a.4); its principal cause is divine goodness, but the secondary cause is meditation on God's goodness *and* on our own defects (a.3). Q.83 takes up prayer, the longest single article-set on prayer in the Summa (17 articles): prayer as an act of religion (a.3), as proper to the rational creature (a.1), whether we should pray to God alone (a.4), for what (a.5-6), for whom (a.7-8), the Lord's Prayer as the most fitting prayer (a.9), its parts (a.9 ad finem), whether prayer ought to be vocal (a.12), whether attention is necessary (a.13), how long it should be (a.14), whether prayer is meritorious (a.15), whether prayer obtains what it asks (a.16), the four divisions of prayer following 1 Timothy 2 (a.17). The two questions are the inward depth of religion the previous day's reframe opens onto.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem on devotion: *what is the interior disposition that makes the agent's religious acts genuinely religious rather than mere external performance?* Aquinas answers from the *will*: devotion is the will's prompt readiness to give itself to God's service, caused by meditation on what makes such giving fitting. The inquiry-problem on prayer: *if providence is unchanging and God knows what we need before we ask, what can prayer actually be doing, and what is its right shape?* Aquinas's answer is the structurally crucial one: prayer does not inform God or bend providence; *providence has been arranged from eternity to include our prayers as among the causes of certain effects*, so prayer is the act by which the perspective-limited agent participates in the providential ordering rather than altering it. The Lord's Prayer is the exemplar because its structure is the right ordering of the perspective-limited agent's petitions.

## Resource

Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup's work supplies the metaphysical ground for Aquinas's hardest claim. PRS-01 (mind as ontological primitive) and PRS-02 (the alter as dissociation within mind-at-large) give the contemporary frame the resources to say cleanly *why* prayer cannot be informational: the alter is not informing the larger mind of anything because the alter *is* a mode of the larger mind. Prayer is then the alter's deliberate *self-recollection* of its position within the field — a re-orientation that is itself one of the field's processes, fully real and fully consequential, but not a transaction between separate entities. Aquinas's "providence has been arranged to include our prayers as causes of certain effects" lands cleanly: the prayers *are* part of the field's self-articulation in time.

Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr's work supplies the contemplative-theological reading, and it has to be taken in two pieces that the earlier draft of this commentary ran together. PRS-01 (non-dual knowing, Confidence *High*) is an entry about **epistemology** — its solution is contemplative knowing as a complement to analytical reason, a mature consciousness that perceives unity-in-distinction instead of collapsing into false dichotomies. It licenses the *mode* in which prayer is undertaken; it does not by itself license the ontological claim that the praying agent and the field are not two separate things. That claim is **PRS-21** (Confidence *Medium*), which is explicitly the contemplative-Christian articulation of the network's mind-monist metaphysics — the Universal-Christ-in-creation reading ("creation as the first incarnation") offered as "the lived-practice side of *mind as primitive*." With PRS-21 supplying the ontology and PRS-01 the epistemic mode, the reframe stands: prayer is participatory rather than transactional, because agent and field are not separated across a transactional boundary, and the agent's knowing of that fact is contemplative rather than discursive.

PRS-9 (contemplative practice as the operational pathway by which agents move from individuated perspective-limitation toward inhabited loving unity — Confidence *High*, and note the register genuinely writes this id unpadded) supplies the practical depth: Aquinas's devotion (Q.82) is the dispositional ground of contemplative attention; the centuries of practice in the Christian contemplative tradition (Cassian, the Cistercians, the Carmelites, the Hesychasts, and Rohr's own Franciscan reading) elaborate exactly the moves Q.82-83 prescribes formally. **PRS-20** (Confidence *Medium-High*) sharpens this from devotional to operational: the contemplative stance is the *position from which* leveraged action becomes possible — a precondition for non-reactive participation rather than a private experience. That entry is worth naming precisely because it makes, in the register's own words, the three-way join this commentary is otherwise making on its own authority: it says it "pairs with Friston's precision-weighting frame and with McGilchrist's right-hemisphere-as-stable-attention frame," which is the exact triad assembled below. PRS-03 (Universal Christ, Confidence *Medium*) supplies the cosmic frame — though its own solution is a warrant for multi-tradition inquiry, so what it licenses here is the Resource-line claim that the divine pattern is present throughout creation, not more. **PRS-27** (Confidence *Medium*) is the entry that speaks most directly to petitionary prayer: the creature's inability to fully reciprocate divine love is generative rather than defective, since the asymmetry is what sustains desire, humility, and continued growth — which is why the four creature-ward petitions are not a lesser appendix to the three God-ward ones.

Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's work supplies the scriptural and historical ground for the Lord's Prayer. PRS-02 (faithful improvisation): the Pater is the form of Christian prayer Jesus gave, paradigm and not template — the improvisation of Christian prayer through history is shaped by it without being limited to verbatim recitation. PRS-03 (inaugurated eschatology) clarifies the structure: "your kingdom come, your will be done on earth as in heaven" is the explicit declaration of the already-not-yet — the kingdom has decisively come (the agent prays it because Jesus has inaugurated it) and is yet to come fully (the agent prays for its full coming). For the deliverance-from-evil petition this commentary previously cited PRS-04, and that citation was wrong: PRS-04 (Confidence *Medium*) is "Resurrection as historical and cosmological," an argument about **historical method** — that naturalistic historiography excludes certain explanations by fiat, and that Wright's evidential case for the bodily resurrection challenges Carroll's Core Theory completeness. It shares the word *resurrection* with the claim made here and none of its content. The claim actually needed is **PRS-11** (Confidence *High*): Wright's "homecoming" reading of the canon, in which Christian hope is the renewal of creation through God's arrival rather than souls departing to a non-physical elsewhere, and the church's present task is "participate in the arrival now." On that reading the deliverance petition is the agent's prayer for protection *within* an arriving new creation it is already asked to participate in — which is also what keeps "your kingdom come" from collapsing into escapism.

Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work formalizes the apparatus, with one anchor corrected and two confidence disclosures. The gloss "intention as model-target" that this commentary had attached to PRS-04 is not that entry's content: PRS-04 (Confidence *Medium*, label *Implicit*) is "Organism as active modeler" — predictive coding and the Bayesian brain, the living system as a continuous hypothesis-testing agent. It licenses the general picture of the agent as a modeler; it contains no notion of a *preferred outcome* against which a model is targeted, which is the notion prayer-as-realignment actually requires. The register carries that notion in exactly one place: **PRS-16** (Confidence *High*), which identifies risk as the KL divergence between **prior preferences** and predicted outcomes, and expected free energy as the unified optimization target over both risk and ambiguity. Prior preferences are formal objects there, and re-setting them is a well-defined operation. So the formal role of prayer belongs to PRS-16 — with the disclosure that PRS-16's own domain is decision under risk and ambiguity in finance, conflict, and competitive strategy, so the transfer to a devotional act is this commentary's application of the formalism and not something the entry proposes. PRS-04 stands behind the weaker and safer half: that the praying agent is a modeler at all. PRS-10 (Confidence *High* — active reasoning via expected free energy, with perception, action, and model selection unified as instances of one minimization) supplies the dynamics under which such a re-setting is intelligible. PRS-28 supplies the vocabulary of *precision-weighting* — the term is genuinely the entry's own, precision being the inverse variance of prediction error, modulated over selected channels — but the entry (Confidence *Medium*) is a **precision-psychiatry** proposal, mapping neuromodulator profiles to symptom phenotypes via aberrant precision dynamics. It describes precision going wrong in pathology, not an agent deliberately re-weighting its own highest priors in an act of devotion. That last step is this commentary's extension, and is offered as structural isomorphism rather than as a result Friston's program has established. The four-fold division of prayer (1 Tim 2: supplications, prayers, intercessions, thanksgiving; Q.83 a.17) maps cleanly onto four kinds of prior-update: requesting (a forward-looking adjustment), declaring intent (a present-tense alignment), advocating for others (extending the agent's model-care to other agents), acknowledging received goods (a backward-looking integration). The Lord's Prayer compresses all four into seven petitions.

Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work supplies the second-personal scaffolding. PRS-11 (Confidence *High*) names what address-prayer is: the agent's *addressing* of God is a second-personal act, irreducible to the third-personal "thinking about" God. The entry's own material is Stump's Franciscan, non-propositional knowledge as applied to seven biblical **suffering narratives**, so the transfer from narrative-mediated knowledge of suffering to the address-structure of prayer is this commentary's step; what the entry supplies is the second-personal category itself, which is what the step needs. PRS-14 (Confidence *High*) and PRS-15 (Confidence *Medium*) — the hylomorphic metaphysics of corporate substance, and the non-metaphorical reading of "body of Christ" as a real hylomorphic composite — extend to the "Our" of the Pater: the prayer is the polity's prayer, the collective body of Christ addressing its Father — not a private individual transaction but a corporate liturgical act in which each individual praying joins the corporate body's continuous prayer.

Tom's PRS-form record of McGilchrist's work supplies the cognitive-mode reading. PRS-11 (religious ritual, communal worship, and contemplative discipline as what cultivates right-hemispheric attention — receptive, holistic, oriented to whole persons and contexts) names the disposition Aquinas calls *devotion*: the readiness of will is, at the cognitive layer, the right-hemisphere-led mode of receptive attention to the whole. The contemplative tradition's practical instruction (slow attention; refusing the left-hemispheric urge to grasp and possess; receptive availability to what arrives) is empirically the cultivation of this mode.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed contemporary parallel preserves Q.82-83 substantially, with Aquinas's hardest claim (prayer doesn't change God but is included in providence) given metaphysical traction by mind-monism. *Preserved*: devotion is a real virtue/act (Q.82) — there is a settled disposition that makes prayer genuinely prayer; meditation on God's goodness *and* on our own defects is the proper cause of devotion (a.3) — the two-pole acknowledgment of the whole and the part is the structure of right religious attention; devotion is the cause of joy (a.4) — alignment with the whole *is* what joy is the felt signature of (Day 132). Prayer is real and consequential (Q.83 a.2), proper to the rational creature (a.1) — the perspective-limited agent's distinctive form of participation; we pray to God alone for what God alone can give (a.4); we should pray for definite goods (a.5), for others including our enemies (a.7-8); attention matters (a.13) but lapses don't destroy the prayer (a.13 ad finem); the Lord's Prayer is the most fitting prayer (a.9) and its structure is canonical for how the perspective-limited agent ought to order its petitions.

*Reframed*: the metaphysics of "providence has been arranged to include our prayers as causes of certain effects" becomes the natural reading under mind / conscious-realist monism — the alter's prayer is one of the field's processes; the prayer's causality is real because it is the field's own self-articulation in time, not a separate-entity transaction. The two-pole structure of the Pater (three God-ward petitions + four creature-ward petitions) is the explicit form of the central claim's structure: orientation toward the whole + petition for the resources to maintain the orientation.

*Extended*: the four-fold division of prayer (1 Tim 2; Q.83 a.17) gains a formal gloss as four kinds of prior-update; the right-hemispheric reading of devotion (McGilchrist PRS-11) gives the contemplative tradition's practical instruction a cognitive correlate; the corporate "Our" of the Pater (Stump PRS-14/15) explains why the prayer is liturgical-paradigmatic rather than merely individual.

Measure against Aquinas: deep convergence on substance — Aquinas's account of prayer is one of the most contemporary-friendly sections of the Summa precisely because the central claim ("prayer doesn't inform God; providence includes the prayers") presupposes the kind of immanent, participatory metaphysics mind-monism makes explicit. Where the contemporary frame extends Aquinas: it gives him the metaphysical ground he uses without explicitly thematizing, and it lets the formal structure of the Lord's Prayer become readable as a precise specification of the central claim's structure.

## The central-theme thread

Q.83 is the Summa's most explicit articulation of the central claim. The perspective-limited agent's active alignment with the providence already at work — not informing the whole, not bargaining with it, but *aligning the perspective's priors with the field's self-articulation* — is exactly what the central claim names. The Lord's Prayer is the exemplar because its structure is the central claim made explicit. The first three petitions name the orientation: *your name be hallowed* (the whole's character honored at the apex of the agent's recognition), *your kingdom come* (the alignment of the field acknowledged as the goal), *your will be done* (the policy adopted by the limited agent matches the policy the whole is enacting). The next four petitions name the resources the limited perspective needs to maintain the orientation: *daily bread* (sustenance for the perspective's continued existence), *forgive us as we forgive* (re-integration of the perspective with other perspectives where defection has occurred — the central claim's social form), *lead us not into temptation* (resource-bounded request not to be tested beyond capacity — the FEP-friendly recognition of the agent's actual limits), *deliver us from evil* (final petition for the whole's protection of the perspective against what would undo it). The two-pole structure of self+God that ran through Day 131's charity treatise reappears here as the structure of the prayer that founds Christian life. Working out salvation in fear and trembling has, at its center, this active alignment in address.
