# Day 257 — Penitential Restoration (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (III Q.89) asks whether the virtues and grace *lost* through mortal sin are *restored* by penance, and whether they return in the same measure as before. His answer: grace and the infused virtues are restored, sometimes more abundantly (the fervent penitent may rise higher than before), sometimes less, depending on the intensity of the penitential movement — so restoration is real but not mechanically identical. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: how does an agent that has lost a prior configuration recover it, and need the recovered state equal the one lost?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **can a system regenerate a lost target configuration, and is the restored state identical to the original or path-dependent?** Restoration presupposes that a target form persists to be recovered toward; the "equal measure" question presupposes that recovery may over- or under-shoot depending on the recovery process.

## Resource

Levin's program is the sharpest resource. In Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's program, PRS-03 (a formal language for target morphology) plus the demonstrated phenomena of regeneration and pattern-completion show living systems actively rebuilding a lost target form: the set-point persists even when the structure realizing it is destroyed, and the system computes its way back toward it. Penitential restoration of lost virtues reads as exactly this — the target form (right ordering) was not annihilated by sin, and penance is the regenerative return toward it.

Friston frames the "equal measure" question. In Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's program, PRS-04 (goal-directedness) and the general point that priors are *history-shaped* make recovery *path-dependent*: the restored prior carries the trace of the deviation and the repair, so the recovered state can be deeper (a stronger prior, having corrected through error) or shallower than the original — never guaranteed identical. This is precisely Aquinas's "sometimes more, sometimes less."

Rohr (PRS-05) reads the recovered self as *transformed*, not reset — the penitent rises as a true self refined through the falling, which is why restoration can exceed the original. Wright grounds the *content*: restoration is re-inclusion in covenant membership, a real relation re-established, not a private state reloaded.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **penitential restoration is regenerative return to a persisting target form, and because the recovered prior is history-shaped it need not equal the lost one — it may run deeper for having corrected through error.** This *shares* with Aquinas the reality of restoration and the variability of its measure. It *reframes* restoration as pattern-completion toward a persisting set-point (Levin) and the unequal measure as path-dependence of the recovered prior (Friston). It *firms up* a premise empirically: that a lost target configuration can be actively regenerated is demonstrated in Levin's regeneration work, not merely posited. It *marks* that grace as the agent of restoration, and the *content* of the recovered relation (covenant membership), are theological; and that "deeper than before" is a structural possibility in the model, but the gift that makes it so is not computed.

## The central-theme thread

The motif appears as re-inclusion after deviation. The limited agent that has strayed is not discarded; the whole holds the target toward which it is regenerated, and the recovered perspective — refined by its correction — can participate more fully than before. Restoration is the whole drawing a deviated part back and, in the drawing, deepening its share. Wright's "salvation as inclusion" is enacted as restored membership, and Rohr's "falling upward" names why the return can surpass the starting point: the perspective that has computed its way back through error holds the whole more knowingly.

## Bridges

- **Levin PRS-03 (target morphology; regeneration / pattern-completion)** — *empirical-in-domain.* Restoration as active return to a persisting set-point; sharpest resource.
- **Friston PRS-04 (goal-directedness; history-shaped priors)** — *formal.* The unequal measure as path-dependence.
- **Rohr PRS-05 (transformed, not reset)** — *theological / contemplative.* Why restoration can exceed the original ("falling upward").
- **Wright PRS-05 (salvation as incorporation, not relocation) + PRS-13 (*God's Homecoming*)** — *scriptural, high within method.* The content of the recovered relation: membership re-established, not a private state reloaded.
