# Day 284 — Impediment of Consanguinity (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.53–54 treat **consanguinity** — kinship by blood — as a diriment impediment, and work out the degrees within which marriage is barred. Aquinas gives two kinds of reason: a *reverence* owed to those naturally close (marrying them would disorder the proper affections of family), and, strikingly, a *social* reason — the prohibition forces marriage *outward*, multiplying the bonds of friendship and charity between families who would otherwise remain strangers. The impediment is not merely a fence; it is an engine of social widening. The contemporary version: **why would a flourishing collective build rules that *prevent* unions among the already-bonded in order to *compel* the formation of bonds with the distant?**

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **the widening of the circle of union**. Aquinas sees that if people married only within the close circle, charity would stay local and the human community would fragment into isolated clans; the impediment is a device for extending the reach of love beyond the near. The contemporary sciences of collective intelligence face the structurally identical design problem: a collective that only couples its already-similar, already-connected members becomes brittle and parochial; robustness and intelligence require mechanisms that force new couplings across distance. The impediment of consanguinity is the marital instance of a general principle about how collectives stay open.

## Resource

The Friston-tradition program supplies the leading move with unusual directness. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-18 in the Friston-tradition wiki, *what it would mean, formally, to widen a community so it can engage rival traditions*) is a research program *on exactly this*: the conditions under which a collective extends its shared generative model to incorporate the distant rather than collapsing inward on the familiar. Read onto Q.53–54, the consanguinity prohibition is a coherence-and-openness rule — it blocks the low-cost local coupling precisely to drive the high-value distant one, widening the community's model. PRS-06 (*multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling*) explains the cost of *not* doing so: a collective that only models its near neighbors never builds the cross-links that make a larger unity coherent. The Levin-tradition program supplies the biological precedent: PRS-17 (*the bioelectric interface to collective intelligence*) and PRS-09 (*cognitive attribution across scales*) describe how collectives integrate across distance to form larger competent individuals — integration *requires* reaching beyond the immediately adjacent. Rohr's program names the theological telos: PRS-01 (*non-dual knowing*) and PRS-7 (*relationship ontologically prior*) read the widening as charity refusing to stop at the boundary of the familiar — the kin-rule serves the universal communion.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the consanguinity impediment is best read as a community-widening mechanism — a rule that suppresses the easy near coupling to compel the bond-extending distant one — and this aligns with how collective intelligences stay open and coherent at larger scales.** What it *shares* with Aquinas: the explicit social rationale (the prohibition exists partly to multiply bonds of charity beyond the family) and the natural-reverence rationale. Where it *reframes*: Aquinas presents the social reason as one consideration among several; the monist reframe foregrounds it as an instance of a general law about open collectives, giving the medieval intuition a wider warrant. Where evidence is *firm*: exogamy as a near-universal human institution that demonstrably extends alliance networks is well-attested anthropologically, and the general principle that integration requires reaching beyond the adjacent is supported in collective-intelligence research. Where *speculative*: the identification of canonical *degrees* of consanguinity with any optimal widening parameter is not derivable — the specific degrees are historical-juridical, and the genetic rationale modern readers assume is largely absent from Aquinas's own reasoning, which should be marked rather than retrofitted.

## The central-theme thread

The project holds that loving unity is to be extended until every limited perspective is included in the whole. Consanguinity's impediment is that extension made into law: love is forbidden to curl up among the already-near so that it must reach the stranger. The rule is the central theme's missionary edge — the refusal to let the circle of union close at the boundary of the familiar, which is exactly the move from clan to *catholic* (universal) communion.
