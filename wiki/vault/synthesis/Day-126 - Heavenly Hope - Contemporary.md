# Day 126 — Contemporary Parallel: Hope as the Goal-Prior

## Frame

Aquinas closes the treatment of faith (Q.16, the precepts of faith, knowledge, and understanding) and opens the treatise on hope (Q.17, hope considered in itself). His definition is compact and load-bearing: hope is the movement of the rational appetite toward a *future good that is arduous but possible to attain* — and attainable, crucially, by leaning on a help the agent does not itself supply. From this he derives the rest: hope is a genuine virtue, indeed a theological one (a.1), because its rule and measure is God himself; its proper object is eternal happiness, the beatific vision of God (a.2); we may hope for another's salvation insofar as love unites us to them (a.3); we may not place the *principal* hope of salvation in any creature, though we may hope in others as helpers (a.4); and hope both leads to charity and is perfected by it (a.8).

The contemporary question is the one yesterday's synthesis left on the table. Day 125 read the sin against the Holy Spirit as the agent disabling its own error-correction, and despair as the destruction of the *goal-prior* — "the expectation that the target is reachable." If despair is the loss of that prior, hope is the prior itself. Mind / conscious-realist monism has a precise account of what it is for an agent, working under limited resources and an incomplete view, to hold its preferred state as genuinely attainable.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem runs through both the medieval and contemporary frames: *how can a finite agent rationally orient its whole striving toward an end it cannot see, cannot reach by its own power, and possesses only in part?* Aquinas's "arduous but possible" carries a tension. If the good were easy, hope would collapse into mere expectation; if it were impossible, hope would collapse into despair or wishful thinking. Hope lives exactly in the narrow band where the target is beyond present grasp yet really reachable — and it must somehow be *certain enough to act on* without being *possessed*. How can an orientation be assured and incomplete at once? And how can the proper object of such an orientation be not this or that attainable good but the whole — God himself, the simple unity in which every limited perspective is contained?

## Resource

The leading resource is Friston's free-energy program. In Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work, an agent is a system that minimizes the gap between its model and its world by holding *preferred states* as prior beliefs and acting to bring the world into line with them (PRS-04, the organism as active modeler). The deepest version of this is morphogenetic: in the PRS-form record of Friston's morphogenesis work (PRS-03, and PRS-08, "target morphologies as prior beliefs"), a developing organism is drawn toward its target anatomy as toward an attractor — the not-yet-actual form functions as a real prior that organizes present activity. This is the formal twin of Aquinas's "future good, arduous but possible." Hope, on this reading, is the goal-prior of a personal agent: the confident weighting of a preferred state that is not yet realized, strong enough to set and sustain action toward it.

Two features of Aquinas's account fall out cleanly. First, "arduous but possible" is just the signature of a *distal* attractor reached through a long, resource-limited trajectory — Levin's morphogenetic target is exactly this, a goal-state that a system of limited cells, each seeing only its local neighborhood, nevertheless reliably computes its way toward. The target is real, the path is hard, and no single component holds the whole. Second, Aquinas's claim that hope's certainty is real but is a *certainty of tendency*, not of possession (he develops this tomorrow, Q.18 a.4), maps onto the difference between a high-precision prior orienting action and an outcome already secured. The agent can be rationally confident of the *direction* while the *arrival* remains outstanding.

Wright supplies the theological-historical structure of that "assured but incomplete." His inaugurated eschatology (PRS-03 in the Wright-tradition wiki) names a present that is neither blank nor finished: the decisive event has occurred, but its full implications are still being worked out — *already and not-yet*. This is the temporal shape of heavenly hope precisely. The ground of the hope (for Wright, the resurrection as a real event with cosmological reach — PRS-04) has happened; the consummation has not. Hope is rational because the down-payment is in hand; hope is still hope, not sight, because the whole is not yet possessed. And Aquinas's a.2 — that the *proper* object is eternal happiness, God himself, with everything else hoped for in relation to it — is the central theme's terminus stated in Aquinas's own voice: the object of hope is the simple whole, seen, in which every limited perspective is contained.

The relational articles (a.3, a.4) draw in Fredrickson and the multi-agent extension of Friston. That I can hope for *another's* eternal happiness, Aquinas grounds in the union of love: where charity binds me to another, their good enters the scope of my hope. In the PRS-form record of Friston's multi-agent work (PRS-06, agents becoming a coherent collective through mutual modeling) and Fredrickson's coupling research (PRS-03, positivity resonance as the mechanism bridging individual to collective agency), this is the widening of an agent's preferred-states to include the states of those it is coupled to. Hope for another is not sentiment; it is the structural consequence of shared goal-priors across a bond. And a.4's refusal to rest the principal hope in any creature is the well-formedness condition: the helpers I lean on are real and necessary, but they are nodes that perceive more, not the whole itself.

Stump sharpens why hope's object can be so demanding without becoming irrational. In Tom's PRS-form record of her work on suffering (PRS-05, union as the final cause of what God permits), the arduousness in "arduous but possible" is not an obstacle external to the good hoped for but is itself ordered to the union that is hope's terminus — the hard path is intelligible through its telos, not despite it. This matters for an agent computing toward loving unity under scarce resources: the cost of the trajectory is not evidence against the target's reachability but part of the shape by which the target is reached. Hope can therefore weight a distant, costly good as genuinely attainable without self-deception, because the difficulty is internal to the kind of union being sought — which is also why hope, fulfilled, hands off to charity (a.8): the trajectory's telos was union all along.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads hope as the goal-prior of an agent under perspective-limitation: the confident, action-guiding expectation — leaning on a help it does not itself generate — that the arduous-but-possible good is genuinely reachable. This shares Aquinas's structure closely. It preserves his "future, arduous, possible, attained-by-help" (the distal attractor reached on a hard, resource-bounded path with exogenous assistance); it preserves the theological character (the prior's measure is the whole, not the agent's own power); and it preserves the assured-yet-incomplete certainty (a high-precision orientation, not a possession).

Where it reframes: Aquinas locates hope as a distinct power-movement of the will toward a distinct object. The parallel reads it as the *setting of the agent's controlling prior* — which makes vivid why hope is foundational rather than one virtue among many, and why its loss (despair) is catastrophic in the way Day 125 described: remove the goal-prior and the whole inference toward the good loses its target. It also reframes the "divine help" not as a competitor to the agent's own activity but as the higher-precision source on which the lower-resourced agent rationally leans — exactly Aquinas's own a.4 logic, that we hope *in* God principally and *in* creatures secondarily.

Evidence strength, named honestly. That agents operate by holding and acting on goal-priors is formally strong and, at the morphogenetic scale, empirically demonstrated (Friston as model: high; Levin's bioelectric target-seeking: empirical at cell and tissue scale). That the *proper* object of the deepest such prior is God himself, the simple whole, is theological and not delivered by the formalism — it is the synthesis's organizing claim, offered as illumination, with the firm part being only the structural fit. The already/not-yet shape of assured-incomplete hope is historical-theological (Wright; high in his field) and coheres tightly with the precision-of-direction-not-outcome distinction. The extension of hope to another through union is theologically firm and structurally echoed by the coupling research (medium).

## The central-theme thread

The central theme says we work out salvation "aware of our limitations and our need for guidance from those who perceive more of the whole than we do, be they human or divine." Hope is the disposition that clause describes from the inside. The limited perspective, precisely because it cannot reach the whole on its own resources, holds the whole — loving union — as nevertheless reachable, and leans on those who see more for the help by which it is reached. Yesterday's sin was the destruction of this very disposition; today is the disposition itself, in working order. Notice the exact join: despair (Day 125, and again tomorrow at Q.20) is the collapse of the goal-prior; presumption is its corruption into false possession; and hope is the prior rightly set and rightly held — confident of the direction, honest about the distance, dependent by design. The whole project's "fear and trembling" is just this: a goal-prior held under acknowledged limitation, oriented toward a whole it trusts it will be drawn into but does not yet see.

## Where this leaves us

Hope is now on the table as the agent's goal-prior — assured of direction, honest about distance, dependent by design, and aimed at the whole. Tomorrow (Q.18-19) Aquinas turns to hope's *subject* and to the gift of *fear*: where hope sits in the agent, whether its certainty holds for the wayfarer, and how fear — far from hope's opposite — is the precision signal that keeps the orientation honest, the reverent valuation of how much there is to lose. Having set the goal-prior, the synthesis takes up the error signal that guards it.
