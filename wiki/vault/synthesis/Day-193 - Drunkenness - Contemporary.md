# Day 193 — Drunkenness (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas now treats the act sobriety guards against — drunkenness (Q.150) — and pivots to chastity (Q.151), the virtue for the strongest of the bodily appetites. His account of drunkenness is unusually sharp: it is a mortal sin because "a man willingly and knowingly deprives himself of the use of reason whereby he performs virtuous deeds and avoids sin" (Q.150 a.2). The wrong is reflexive — one sins by discarding the very thing that keeps one from sin. And he immediately tests the principle against a counter-case (anticipated by Austin's surgical-anesthesia example): voluntarily losing consciousness for an operation is not sinful, because it is ordered by reason. The contemporary version: *what is the moral structure of an agent deliberately disabling its own governing model, and what distinguishes that from licit, reason-ordered suspensions of awareness?*

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **self-disabling of the controller**. Every other temperance failure misweights an appetite; drunkenness does something categorically stranger — it takes the regulator offline. Aquinas locates the malice not in the pleasure of drink but in the *target*: the act aims (or knowingly risks) the loss of reason itself. This raises the puzzle his anesthesia case exposes: two acts can be physically identical in outcome (reason suspended, awareness gone) yet opposite in worth. So the problem has two faces: what makes voluntarily switching off one's own governor gravely disordered, and what feature — present in surgery, absent in carousing — flips the valuation?

## Resource

The Friston-tradition record is the leading resource. There the agent simply *is* a self-model — a hierarchy that minimizes long-run prediction error by acting and updating (PRS-01, PRS-04). Reason, in Aquinas's sense of the faculty that "performs virtuous deeds and avoids sin," maps onto the high-level controller that sets policies and keeps lower loops ordered to the agent's true setpoint. On this reading drunkenness is the agent deliberately degrading its own highest control level — not pursuing a bad goal but dismantling the apparatus by which any goal is pursued well. That is why its wrong is reflexive in a way no other temperance failure is: the gluttonous agent still has its governor and could in principle correct; the drunk agent has voluntarily removed correction itself. Aquinas's verdict — "one sins by discarding that which keeps him from sinning" — is, in this frame, the recognition that disabling the controller forfeits the very capacity for re-alignment on which working-out-salvation depends.

The anesthesia case is where active inference earns its keep. The difference is not in the local state (reason offline either way) but in the *policy that selected it*. In surgery, a higher-order policy — preserve the organism, restore function — selects a temporary, bounded, reversible suspension as a means to the agent's genuine setpoint; the loss of reason is itself an act of reason. In drunkenness, no higher policy ratifies the suspension; the controller is sacrificed to a lower reward. The Fredrickson-tradition wiki adds the embodied note: regulation is a real vagal-bodily control system (PRS-01, PRS-05), and disabling it has measurable downstream cost — the impairment is not metaphorical.

For chastity (Q.151), the resource is the same precision/top-down picture: chastity "chastises concupiscence, which like a child needs curbing" — top-down regulation of the strongest, most vehement appetite, distinct from abstinence only because it governs a different and more intense pleasure of touch.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: drunkenness is an agent deliberately disabling the self-model that minimizes its own long-run error; its gravity is the gravity of dismantling one's own controller, and what redeems the structurally identical anesthesia case is that there the loss of reason is itself ordered by reason — selected by a higher policy serving the agent's true setpoint.

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps the exact location of the malice — in the targeting of reason, not in drink — and the reflexive structure ("discarding what keeps one from sinning"). It keeps the proportion: drunkenness is grave but not the gravest, since spiritual vices that corrupt the *end* outrank a carnal vice that disables the *means* (Q.150 a.3). And it reproduces his excusing conditions (a.4) cleanly: involuntary intoxication carries no culpability because no policy of the agent selected the loss.

*Where it reframes.* "Reason" becomes the high-level controller / self-model; "depriving oneself of reason" becomes taking the governor offline; and the anesthesia/drunkenness contrast becomes a difference in the *policy that selected the suspension*, not in the suspended state — which is a cleaner account of the same intuition than appetite-faculty language alone can give. Chastity becomes top-down precision-control of the most vehement appetite.

*Evidence strength, honestly.* The self-model-as-controller reading is mechanistically plausible and conceptually tight (medium); the policy-difference account of the anesthesia case is a strong conceptual result. The mapping of "mortal sin" onto "forfeiting the capacity for re-alignment" is a philosophical-theological interpretation, offered as such; the gravity-ranking is theological.

## The central-theme thread

The project's claim is that a limited agent works out its salvation — its inclusion in the union with God and others — by computing toward loving unity under scarce resources, "in fear and trembling," needing guidance from perspectives wider than its own. That computation requires a running controller. To voluntarily extinguish reason is therefore the in-via catastrophe peculiar to this stretch: not choosing a wrong end (worse) and not merely misweighting an appetite (lesser), but abandoning the computation midstream by switching off the agent that performs it. The anesthesia case shows the saving difference is *ordering*: a suspension folded inside a higher policy that serves the true setpoint is not abandonment but stewardship. Wright's register: the self is entrusted with reason as a steward is entrusted with a charge; to disable it wantonly is to bury the talent. Rohr's: the curbing of the "child" appetite is the patient discipline by which the false self is kept from seizing the controls. The difference from Aquinas is descriptive — controller and policy in place of faculty and reason — but the moral shape, that disabling one's own governor forfeits the very thing salvation is worked out with, is preserved.

## Bridges

- **Drunkenness ↔ self-disabling of the controller** — the Friston-tradition wiki (PRS-01, PRS-04). *Why:* taking the high-level self-model offline forfeits the capacity for re-alignment. Evidence: **mechanistic, medium**.
- **Anesthesia vs. drunkenness ↔ the selecting policy** — Friston-tradition active-inference record. *Why:* identical suspended states differ in worth by the higher-order policy that selected them. Evidence: **conceptual, strong**.
- **Impairment ↔ measurable regulatory cost** — the Fredrickson-tradition wiki (PRS-01, PRS-05). *Why:* disabling vagal-bodily regulation has real downstream cost. Evidence: **empirical, medium**.
- **Chastity ↔ top-down control of the strongest appetite** — the Friston- and McGilchrist-tradition wikis. *Why:* "chastising" the most vehement pleasure is precision-control at its hardest. Evidence: **mechanistic, medium**.
- **Reason ↔ entrusted stewardship; appetite ↔ the curbed child** — the Wright- and Rohr-tradition resources. *Why:* the governing self as a charge to be kept, not squandered. Evidence: **theological**.

## Where this leaves us

Chastity opens here as the regulation of the strongest appetite; tomorrow (Q.152–153) ascends to virginity — the perpetual, kingdom-oriented renunciation — and to lust as the disorder of the generative appetite, with its remarkable map of how lust corrupts the whole practical-reasoning chain. The controller-and-policy reading carries directly into that map. Open thread: the "policy that selects the suspension" formalism is strong and reusable; a later synthesis on obedience or vows (II-II Q.104, Q.186 ff.) should reuse it for the structure of a self binding its own future choices.
