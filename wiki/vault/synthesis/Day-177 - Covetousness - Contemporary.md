# Day 177 — Covetousness (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas treats the two ways the affection toward external goods fails. Q.118 is covetousness or avarice (*avaritia*): the immoderate love of possessing, which he follows Paul in calling "the root of all evils" (1 Tim 6:10) because it subordinates every other good to acquisition. Q.119 is prodigality, its opposite — wasteful giving and spending — which Aquinas judges the lesser and more curable fault, since the prodigal at least loosens his grip on goods, even disorderedly, and tends to exhaust the disorder, whereas the covetous grip tightens with time.

Under mind / conscious-realist monism the contemporary version asks what *goes wrong in an agent* when it over-clutches resources. Yesterday's liberality named the rightly-sized self. Covetousness is its pathological contraction: the self shrinks to the boundary of its own holdings and begins to treat the surrounding community as outside the circle of care.

## Problem

Every agent must estimate the resources it needs and hold some against future scarcity — this is not yet a vice. The vice appears when the estimate detaches from any real need and the agent's whole motivational economy reorganizes around accumulation. Two pathologies bracket the right disposition: the covetous agent contracts, hoarding goods that could circulate as connection; the prodigal agent dissipates, releasing goods without order. The shared problem is the *calibration of an agent's relationship to resources* — and, beneath it, the size of the self that is doing the estimating.

## Resource

The sharpest contemporary resource is **Levin's** account of cancer. In Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work, a tumor is a *defection from the cooperative morphogenetic community*: cells whose bioelectric integration into the larger body fails revert to "selfish unicellular behavior," pursuing their own proliferation at the expense of the whole — Levin even frames it as a kind of dissociative collapse of collective identity, the cell's care-boundary shrinking back to the single cell. Covetousness is the human-scale isomorph. The covetous agent withdraws from the cooperative whole into a self defined by what it can hold, and like the cancerous cell its local "success" (more possession, more proliferation) is precisely what corrodes the larger body it depends on. This is why Aquinas can call it a *root*: defection at the level of the self's boundary disorders everything that boundary touches.

**Friston** supplies the formal diagnosis. In Tom's PRS-form record of his work, an agent under the free-energy principle continually estimates the resources it must secure against modeled future surprise. Covetousness is this machinery run open-loop: a chronically inflated estimate of needed reserves, so that the agent over-provisions without bound, mistaking accumulation for the minimization of an existential prediction-error that no amount of holding can actually resolve. The covetous never reach "enough" because the quantity was never tracking a real need.

**Carroll** locates the category error. His poetic naturalism (PRS-01 in the Carroll-tradition wiki) holds that meaning and value are real patterns at the level of persons and stories, not properties of the underlying stuff. Goods are useful, but the value an agent actually seeks — significance, connection, a life that means something — is never *in* the accumulated substrate; the covetous agent makes a level-confusion, looking for an agent-level good in a pile of core-ontology objects.

**Stump** enters as Thomistic interlocutor: avarice as disordered love that fixes on possessing rather than rightly using, so that the person's second-personal relations are bent to serve acquisition. PRS-22 in the Stump-tradition wiki carries the general form of this move — the vices as predictable failure modes of an intellect-will system oriented toward goodness, rather than as arbitrary prohibitions (Confidence Medium). The register holds no entry specific to possession or avarice, so the application to Q.118 is the general account applied to this vice, not a citation of it.

**Wright** grounds the scriptural form: the "love of money" as *idolatry* (1 Tim 6:10; Col 3:5), and Jesus's "treasure in heaven" — store value where the new creation is heading, not where moth and rust corrode (Matt 6:19–21). This bullet keeps a canonical anchor rather than a numbered one because the 41-entry Wright register carries no entry on wealth, possession, treasure, mammon, riches or economic life; the declination was tested against the live register on 2026-08-24 with those search terms and returned nothing on topic.

**Rohr** gives the contemplative form, and the register splits it. The second-half-of-life movement is PRS-02 (Confidence High), whose source is *Falling Upward* itself and whose content is the order–disorder–reorder passage: the reorder that follows genuine disorder is deeper than the original order because it has been tested. The release the second half asks for is PRS-17 (Medium-High), the obstacle-side articulation naming what the agent must let go of to participate in the loving-unity telos, with PRS-15 (High) carrying the False Self as the thing being let go of. The task is no longer accumulation but release — letting the grip open so that the deeper life can be received rather than seized.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: covetousness is the agent's contraction into a self bounded by its holdings — a defection from the cooperative whole (Levin) driven by a resource-estimate that has detached from real need (Friston) and a level-confusion that seeks agent-level meaning in accumulated objects (Carroll). Prodigality is the opposite mis-calibration, dissipating goods without order, and Aquinas is right that it is the lesser fault: a loosened grip, even a careless one, has not made acquisition its god.

What it shares with Aquinas: covetousness as a root disorder, immoderate love of possessing, more corrosive than its opposite; right use rather than mere quantity as the standard. Where it reframes: the wrong is specified as defection-plus-miscalibration at the level of the self's boundary and its resource-model, which gives the medieval "immoderate love" a mechanism. Evidence strength: the defection account is empirical-by-analogy (Levin's cancer biology is empirical; its transfer to a moral agent is analogical), the over-provisioning account is formal (Friston), the value-level-confusion is formal (Carroll), and the idolatry reading is theological (Wright, Rohr).

## Central-theme thread

The synthesis holds that conscious agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective, and that loving unity is the goal toward which each limited agent computes under scarce perceived resources. Covetousness is what happens when an agent mistakes the resources for the goal. It reads its scarcity rightly — we *are* limited, we *do* lack — but answers it by contracting rather than by trusting the larger whole, so that the very limitation meant to drive the agent toward cooperation instead drives it into defection. To work out salvation in fear and trembling is to feel the scarcity and still keep the self's boundary open; covetousness is fear and trembling curdled into a clenched fist.
