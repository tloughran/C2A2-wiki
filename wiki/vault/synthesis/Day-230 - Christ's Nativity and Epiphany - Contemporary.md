# Day 230 — Contemporary Parallel: The Whole Made Known to Every Limited Perspective

## Frame

Q.35 brings the conceived one to birth and settles the grammar: *nativity regards the person*, not the nature. "Natures are not born merely of a woman, but people are" — therefore it was *the person* who was born in Bethlehem, and therefore Mary is truly *Mother of God* (Theotokos, Ephesus): she bore the person who is the eternal Word, though she contributed only to his human nature. There is a two-fold nativity (eternal from the Father, temporal from the mother), but only the eternal sonship is a "real relation" in Christ; the temporal sonship to Mary is a "relation of reason" — yet she is genuinely his mother in respect of it. Birth came "without his mother suffering" (no corruption, "much joy therein"); in Bethlehem ("house of bread"); at a time of imperial peace fitting the Prince of Peace. Q.36 then asks who the birth was *manifested* to. Not to all — for if all knew, "they would not have crucified the Lord of glory," and "the very nature of faith would have been destroyed, since faith is the evidence of things that appear not" (Heb 11:1). But not to none either — "faith comes through what is heard." It was made known to representatives of *every condition of humanity*: Simeon and Anna (male and female), the Jewish shepherds and the Gentile magi (Jew and Gentile, poor and rich) — "that no condition of man was to be excluded from Christ's redemption." The contemporary version: *what does it mean that the whole, in coming, is made known to a limited and representative set — and that it is made known across every category of limited perspective, that none be excluded?*

## Problem

Three linked inquiry-problems, and the episode closes the Christmas arc by hitting the project's themes squarely. (1) **Individuation by person, again.** Q.35's whole grammar — Theotokos, the two nativities, the one real filiation — turns on *nativity regarding the person*, the individuated locus. The problem is what grounds personhood as the unit such that "the person was born," not the nature. (2) **The whole made known under limitation.** Q.36's refusal to manifest the birth *to all* — "lest faith be destroyed" — is a striking endorsement of *perspective-limitation as constitutive*: the whole does not flood every perspective with full vision, because faith (computing toward the unseen whole) requires that the whole *not* be simply given. (3) **Radical inclusion.** Yet the manifestation reaches *every category* — "that none be excluded." The problem is how the loving-unity telos can be both *partial* (not to all, preserving faith) and *universal in scope* (to every kind, excluding none).

## Resource

For *individuation by person*, Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup (PRS-08, the Daimon as principle of individuation) grounds personhood as the irreducible individuated locus — "the person was born" because the person is the unit of identity, prior to the natures. For *radical inclusion*, the leading resources are Fredrickson and Rohr. Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson (PRS-06, *democratizing participation in reality* — "ground conscious-realist ethics in the universal capacity for resonance: all beings capable of shared positive emotion participate in reality-constituting processes — love as the basis for radical inclusivity") is almost a gloss on "that no condition of man was to be excluded"; and PRS-09/10 (weak-tie cross-category connection — deliberately cultivating connection across difference: stranger, acquaintance, the other group) maps onto the deliberate spread of the manifestation across Jew and Gentile, poor and rich, male and female. Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-03, the Universal Christ — "the divine pattern present in all of creation, not confined to any single tradition") gives the cosmic-scope warrant: the newborn is manifested to every condition because the pattern is present to all. For the *perspective-limitation* point — faith preserved by partial manifestation — the resource is the central theme's own premise plus Tom's PRS-form record of Wright (PRS-03, inaugurated eschatology: "the decisive event has occurred, but its full implications are still being worked out"; PRS-04, faith as warranted commitment beyond what naturalistic method can compel): faith just *is* the limited perspective's mode of relating to a whole not yet fully seen — "the evidence of things that appear not." And the same record of Wright (PRS-05, incorporation) names the end of the inclusion: the manifestation to every category is the first-fruits of the people among whom God comes home — the Gentile magi "grafted into the faith of Abraham," the Theotokos bearing the person through whom the many are incorporated.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the Nativity and its manifestation state, at the close of the Christmas arc, the project's whole shape. Nativity regards the *person* — the individuated locus (Kastrup PRS-08) — so it is a person, not a nature, who enters the limited world. The whole is made known *under limitation*: not flooded to all, because faith — the limited perspective's mode of relating to a not-yet-fully-seen whole — would be destroyed by full vision (the perspective-limitation premise, endorsed by the text itself). And yet the manifestation reaches *every category of limited perspective* "that none be excluded" — radical inclusion grounded in the universal capacity to participate (Fredrickson PRS-06), spread deliberately across difference (PRS-09/10), because the divine pattern is present to all (Rohr PRS-03). Partial in mode, universal in scope: the loving-unity telos as the inclusion of every kind of limited perspective in the whole that contains them.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps nativity regarding the person (Theotokos) and the careful relation-grammar of the two filiations. It keeps the manifestation *partial* — to some, not all — and for Aquinas's stated reason: to preserve faith. And it keeps the inclusion *total in scope*: every condition represented, "that none be excluded."

*Where it reframes.* Aquinas grounds the partial manifestation in the fittingness of preserving faith and the providential plan; the contemporary reframe reads it as a structural feature of how a whole relates to limited perspectives — faith is the limited perspective computing toward an unseen whole, which requires the whole *not* be simply given. And it reads the "every condition" inclusion as the loving-unity telos's *universality of scope* (Fredrickson's democratized participation, Rohr's Universal Christ), the deliberate spread across difference that the project's accelerator design aims to model.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* Nativity-regards-the-person and the filiation grammar are theological/formal. The Theotokos, the Gentile-inclusion, and "faith is the evidence of things not seen" are scriptural (Wright, strong). The radical-inclusion reading (democratized participation, Universal Christ) is theological with a speculative-formal analogue (medium). The individuation-by-person reading is speculative-formal (medium). The perspective-limitation point is a structural fit with the project's own premise (strong as fit, not as external evidence).

## The central-theme thread

This is the day the Christmas arc hands the project both halves of its central claim at once. The claim is that "when all limitations are removed, the simple whole is seen and every limited perspective is contained in it," but that "learning to obtain... loving unity from each perspective is a computational task, limited by the resources we perceive." Q.36 endorses *both* halves explicitly. The whole is *not* simply shown to all — "lest faith be destroyed" — because faith is precisely the limited perspective's mode of relating to a whole it does not yet fully see ("the evidence of things that appear not"); remove the limitation prematurely and you remove the very computing-toward that the project says is our task "in fear and trembling." And yet the whole *is* made known across *every category* of limited perspective — male and female, Jew and Gentile, poor and rich — "that no condition of man was to be excluded." This is loving unity stated as *radical inclusion*: not the collapse of the limited perspectives into one undifferentiated vision, but the gathering of *every kind* of limited perspective into the whole that contains them all (Fredrickson's "all capable of resonance participate"; Rohr's pattern "present in all"). Nativity-regards-the-person keeps the inclusion from being absorption: it is *persons*, individuated loci, who are gathered — the many contained in the one without ceasing to be many. The arc that began with a free fiat (Day 227), passed through form-bearing matter organized by Love (Day 228), and reached the whole-seeing perspective at the first instant (Day 229), here opens outward: the whole, having become a person in the most limited condition, makes itself known to every limited perspective, that none be left outside the loving unity it came to constitute.

With Q.36 the *Summa* finishes the infancy and turns next toward Christ's life and ministry (Q.37 onward — the circumcision, baptism, and public mode of life).
