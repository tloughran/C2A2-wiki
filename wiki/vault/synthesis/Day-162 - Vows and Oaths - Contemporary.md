# Day 162 — Vows & Oaths (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Yesterday's question concerned resource flow *across* agents at different scales. Today's concerns commitment *within* one agent across time. Aquinas treats vows and oaths as paired species of the virtue of religion: a vow is a promise *to* God, an oath calls God as witness *for* a present-or-future fact promised to a fellow human. Both fix the will. Both invoke the unlimited witness as anchor for a limited agent's words.

Under mind/conscious-realist monism, the contemporary version of the question is: *what does it mean for a perspective-limited agent — who is, after all, a temporal sequence of slightly different perspectives — to bind its own future self?* Why is the vowed act more meritorious than the spontaneous act? What does an oath *do* that a mere statement does not? Aquinas reaches for the will's act of fixing itself on the better good and for the dignity-of-witness due to God's unfailing truth. The contemporary parallel reaches for the precision-weighting of policies in active inference (Friston), the canalization of developmental trajectories (Levin), and Wright's reading of Pauline *pistis* as covenant fidelity rather than mere assent.

## Problem (P)

The problem is the **temporal-coherence problem**. A present self can see clearly what is good now. A future self under different precision-weightings — under tiredness, distraction, new appetites, fresh fears — may be unable to see what the present self can. If the agent only acts on what each momentary self can see, the long arc of becoming any particular kind of person is impossible. There is no monk, no spouse, no scholar, no convert, because each role requires sustained policy commitment across precisely the perceptual states that would undo it.

This is the *same* problem developmental biology solves when canalization locks a tissue into a body plan against perturbation. It is the *same* problem Friston-style active-inference models solve when they assign high precision to a long-horizon policy so that local prediction errors don't immediately overturn it. It is also the *same* problem Aquinas's reader is solving when she swears, vows, or simply makes the renewing morning act of intention. The medieval church refined the vow-and-oath protocol; the underlying problem long predates the protocol and continues to surface wherever there are temporally-extended agents.

## Resource (R)

The C2A2 paradigm offers a convergent multi-tradition resource.

**Friston** supplies the formal account (see PRS-02 and PRS-03 in the Friston-tradition wiki on policy selection under expected free energy). High-precision long-horizon policies survive local perturbation because the precision weight makes the agent treat short-term prediction error as noise relative to the longer arc. A vow, formally, is a *precision-fixing act* — it tells the agent's own generative model that this policy is not to be revisited at every fork. The will is not a separable faculty; it is the precision the agent grants its own long-horizon model.

**Levin** supplies the biological analogue (see PRS-04 on canalization and PRS-08 on developmental commitment in the Levin-tradition wiki). Tissues commit to body plans through bioelectric and biochemical settings that resist perturbation; the commitment is real, located in the substrate, and reversible only at cost. A solemn vow is what canalization looks like at the level of a person.

**McGilchrist** supplies the hemispheric reading. The left hemisphere is the instrument of commitment-to-the-chosen-path — focused, narrow, willing to ignore counter-evidence in order to execute. The right hemisphere is the instrument of recalibration — open, holistic, attentive to what doesn't fit. A healthy vowed life requires both: the left's grip on the policy, the right's awareness of when the world has changed enough that the vow itself must be revisited. Aquinas's distinction between vows admitting dispensation and solemn vows that do not maps onto this: solemn vows assert that the world *will not change enough* to warrant revisiting.

**Stump** supplies the personalist reframe (Stump's *Wandering in Darkness* and her work on faith as second-personal knowledge — see PRS-11 in the Stump-tradition wiki, Tom's PRS-form record of her account of faith). A vow is not a contract with a proposition; it is a sustained orientation toward a Person. The Aquinian definition of vow as a *promise made to God* presupposes the Person-relation that Stump's work makes contemporary.

**Wright** supplies the scriptural-historical anchor. Jesus's apparent prohibition of oaths in Matthew 5 ("let your yes be yes") is, on Wright's reading, not the abolition of vows or oaths but a critique of the Pharisaic *casuistry* that exploited oath-categories to evade real commitment. Pauline *pistis* in *Paul and the Faithfulness of God* is precisely covenant-fidelity-as-vow: God's *pistis* toward Israel and Israel's expected *pistis* in return. The vow-structure is the architecture of covenant.

**Rohr** supplies the contemplative complement: the mature contemplative has "said yes once," and the daily life is the unfolding of that yes rather than a repeated re-deciding. The vow simplifies the spiritual life precisely by removing the perpetual decision from the agent's daily computational load.

## Solution (S, proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: *a vow is the precision-fixing act by which a temporally-extended limited agent binds its own future precision-weightings so that a long-horizon policy survives the moment-by-moment fluctuations of perspective*. An oath is the same act extended to a context where the limited agent's words alone cannot bear the social weight of what it claims; calling God as witness extends the perceptual horizon of the speech-act beyond the speaker's own credibility.

**What it shares with Aquinas:** the recognition that the vowed life is more meritorious than the spontaneous because the vow consecrates not only the act but the *power to act*. Aquinas's threefold reason — giving God the act and the power; analogy with habituated vice's stronger evil; vow as belonging to the highest moral virtue, religion — survives the reframe almost intact. Aquinas's distinction between declaratory oaths (about past/present, irrevocable) and promissory oaths (about future, dispensable under changed circumstance) tracks the active-inference distinction between updating an observation-model versus updating a policy.

**Where it reframes:** Aquinas treats the vow as an act *of the will* in a faculty-psychology that is no longer the contemporary working frame. The contemporary parallel reframes the will as a *precision-weight* the agent assigns to its own long-horizon generative model. This is not a deflation — it is a relocation. The vow is no less real; it is realized in a substrate we can now describe. The Levin canalization analogue makes the relocation concrete: just as tissue commits to a body plan in measurable bioelectric settings, a vowed person commits to a way of life in measurable patterns of attention, perception, and policy.

**Where evidence is firm vs speculative:** the precision-weighting account of policy stickiness is formally and empirically robust within the active-inference research program. Developmental canalization is empirically robust at the cellular level. The hemispheric reading of commitment-and-recalibration is suggestive — McGilchrist's clinical and historical evidence is real but not the controlled-experiment kind. The Pauline-pistis reading is theologically strong within Wright's tradition. The integrative claim — *that all these are descriptions, at different levels, of one phenomenon* — is speculative and central to the project.

## The central-theme thread

The vow is the cleanest case in the entire *Summa* of the central-theme structure: a limited agent, knowing her present perspective is partial and her future perspectives will be partial in different ways, *uses the unlimited witness* (God) *as anchor for a binding she cannot internally sustain*. The vow does not pretend the future self will see what the present self sees. It precisely concedes that the future self will not, and routes the binding through an anchor that can see what neither self alone can — the loving unity toward which the policy aims. Wright's Pauline reading puts this in the strongest theological register: *pistis* is precisely the covenant-fidelity by which a perspective-limited Israel is included in God's people-being-saved.

Aquinas's article on the more-meritorious-because-vowed act now reads with new force: the vow is more meritorious not because God needs the act more, but because the vowed act enacts the agent's *acknowledgment* that her perspective is limited and that loving unity must be computed across more of her than the present moment alone contains. Working out salvation in fear and trembling (Phil 2:12, Wright's translation: "carry into effect the rescue you have been given") is exactly the vowed structure — the present self entrusts the future self to an anchor that exceeds either.
