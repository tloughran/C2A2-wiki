# Day 302 — General Judges (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas asks who will judge alongside Christ and who will be judged (Suppl. Q.89), and then in what form the Judge appears (Q.90). The answers cluster around two principles. First, **conformity**: judges-assessors must share the nature of the judged — they must have human nature so as to be "visible to all, both good and wicked," which is why the angels do not judge in the strict sense and the demons merely *execute* the sentence. Second, **the right end**: those who "left all and followed Christ" judge by a fittingness earned through voluntary poverty, and — the load-bearing article — unbelievers' works are not all sinful, yet they are not *meritorious*, because they lack the correct ultimate end. The episode closes on a metaphysical jewel: "it is impossible to see the Godhead without joy," because seeing the truth is itself delightful (Aristotle: "all naturally desire to know").

The contemporary version of Aquinas's question is about **what orients an agent toward the good at all** — what it is for an action to be aimed at the right end, and what it is for one agent to be fit to evaluate another.

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **the evaluability of agency by its end**. Aquinas's whole apparatus assumes that an act's worth is fixed primarily by the *end for which it is done*, not merely by its external character — paying a fair price at the market is just, but it is not meritorious unless ordered to the true final good. The contemporary sciences of agency face the same problem from below: how do you distinguish a system that is merely behaving adaptively from one that is genuinely *aiming* — and by what standard does any modeler judge whether another agent's policy is well-directed?

## Resource

The leading move comes from the active-inference program. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-04 in the Friston-tradition wiki, the organism as active modeler; extended in PRS-15/16 to the full OODA loop and expected free energy as the joint mathematics of risk and ambiguity) gives end-directedness a formal shape: an agent acts to minimize *expected* free energy with respect to its preferred states — its prior preferences, the "end" written into its generative model. On this account an action is not evaluable in the abstract; it is evaluable only against the agent's preference distribution. Carroll's program supplies the complementary caution: the C2A2 network records in PRS-04 of the Carroll-tradition wiki a deflationary compatibilism — agency is a real but high-level description — and in PRS-18 the open demand for a metaphysics of levels that would say why the higher-level "aim" is more than a convenient gloss.

Put together, the resource is this: **to have an end is to carry a preference structure that makes some outcomes count as success; to judge an agent is to read it against the end it actually carries.** Aquinas's "correct habitual end" is, in this register, the claim that there is a *true* preference distribution — union with the source of all good — relative to which every other end is partial.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary parallel proposes: an agent is rightly evaluated by the end it computes toward, and the *fittingness to judge* that Aquinas grants to those who "left all" is, formally, the alignment of a judge's preference structure with the true one. This is why conformity matters. A judge that does not share the nature — the form of life, the generative model — of the judged cannot make the judgment *legible* to the judged; the angels are disqualified not for lack of insight but for lack of shared nature. Evaluation requires a common interface.

What it shares with Aquinas: the priority of end over external act, and the insistence that the highest evaluation belongs to the whole (God alone judges in the strict sense; the saints judge only by participation). Where it reframes: it treats "the correct habitual end" not as a credal gate but as a claim about the *attractor* toward which mature agency tends — loving union as the preference no finite agent fully holds but all are drawn toward. Where the evidence is firm: that agency is end-relative and formally modelable under free-energy minimization is well-developed (formal, with empirical traction in neuroscience and morphogenesis). Where it is speculative: that there exists one *true* end relative to which all others are deficient is a metaphysical-theological claim the formalism does not establish; active inference is silent on whether any preference distribution is privileged.

The closing article is the place where the two registers nearly touch. "The Godhead cannot be seen without joy" says that apprehending the deepest truth is *intrinsically* rewarding — there is no gap between knowing the highest good and delighting in it. In free-energy terms this is the vanishing of the distinction between epistemic value (resolving uncertainty) and pragmatic value (reaching preferred states): at the limit, to know the whole *is* to occupy the preferred state. Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson's work (PRS-02 in the Fredrickson-tradition wiki, recovering love from mere sentiment; PRS-14 on the conditions for positivity resonance) supplies the embodied counterpart — that the highest goods are felt, relational events, not bloodless cognitions. CROSS-005 in the network index already names love/union as a convergent fundamental across Stump, Fredrickson, and Kastrup. The "joy" of the beatific vision is, on this reading, the affective signature of an agent that has reached the end it was computing toward.

## The central-theme thread

This article sits very close to the project's center. The claim that conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective entails that no finite agent yet holds the true end completely — each computes toward loving unity "from its own perspective," under the resources it perceives. Aquinas's distinction between the *judged* and the *not-discussed* maps onto this: the perfected need no inquiry because their preference structure has already converged on the whole; those with "an admixture of good and evil merits" must be discussed precisely because their orientation is mixed, still partial. And the disqualification of a judge who does not share the nature of the judged is the same point in reverse — evaluation only lands between agents who share enough perspective to make the judgment intelligible. That is the whole rationale for a system that works by *richly informing agents about one another's perspectives*: legible judgment is a function of shared interface.

Salvation, in N.T. Wright's frame (PRS-05 in the Wright-tradition wiki), is incorporation into the people among whom God comes home — not a verdict relocating souls but inclusion in a community oriented to the true end. "Fear and trembling" is the honest name for computing toward that end while one's preference structure is still mixed, still partial, still being judged.
