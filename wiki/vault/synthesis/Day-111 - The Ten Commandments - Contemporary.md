# Day 111 — The Ten Commandments (Contemporary)

## Frame

Q.100-101 are dense. Q.100 examines the moral precepts of the Old Law in detail, with the Decalogue at its center; Q.101 opens the ceremonial precepts. Three claims stand out:

1. The Decalogue's *order* is meaningful: end-before-means (God-ward precepts before neighbor-ward), and within each block from gravest to least grave (Q.100 a.5-6).
2. The mode of charity *falls under* the precept of the divine law as a precept *to acquire* charity, and as the *formal mode* of fulfilling other precepts; without charity, fulfillment-of-the-law in any deep sense is impossible (Q.100 a.10).
3. The ceremonial precepts are *figurative* — they prepare a people to recognize the pattern when the pattern arrives (Q.101 a.2). The graded-revelation principle: "the things of God are not to be revealed to man except in proportion to his capacity."

The contemporary question: *what does the convergent paradigm say about (a) the cognitive role of figurative / typological representation, (b) the necessary role of charity in robust moral functioning, and (c) the costly-signaling and identity-forming function of ritual observance?*

## Problem

The shared problem here is the *grammar of formation*: not just what an agent should do but what kinds of representations and practices produce the agent capable of doing it. Aquinas distinguishes moral precepts (what), ceremonial precepts (figures pointing-to), and judicial precepts (local determinations). The contemporary reframe needs to make sense of all three together as a developmental ecology.

A nested problem: Q.100 a.10's careful balance — charity-as-precept *and* charity-as-formal-mode-of-other-precepts *and* charity-as-impossible-without-grace — is precisely the kind of integrated moral-psychological claim that single contemporary frameworks struggle to hold all together.

## Resource: figurative cognition + costly-signaling + moral-foundations + charity-as-precept

**Hoffman's interface theory** (FLAG-02) gives the *figurative* its place: figures (ceremonies, typology) are interface-level representations that point at a deeper reality the agent cannot perceive directly. Q.101 a.2's "the things of God are not to be revealed to man except in proportion to his capacity" is precisely Hoffman's claim that the format of perception is constrained by what the agent can use. The Old-Covenant ceremonies are interface-level glyphs of New-Covenant realities. Aquinas's example (paschal lamb without blemish foreshadows Christ unblemished, etc.) is a typological pattern-match across different interface-layers.

**Friston's active inference** (FLAG-05): curriculum-staged learning requires interface-appropriate representations at each stage. Ceremonial precepts are precisely the right-level training data for a community whose generative-model priors are not yet ready for the full New-Covenant content. The pedagogical question "what level of revelation does this learner currently need?" is exactly the question the host names with Q.101 a.2.

**Fredrickson's broaden-and-build** (FLAG-11): the Decalogue's two affirmative precepts (Sabbath, honor of parents) versus the eight negative precepts is psychologically right. Negative precepts (don't kill, don't steal) define the *floor* — what counts as catastrophic violation. Affirmative precepts of *endless* debt (gratitude to God, gratitude to parents) point toward the *positive-affect space* in which agents are *broadened* into more-than-mere-rule-following. Aquinas already sees this structure in Q.100 a.7's "negative precepts extend to more persons… [but] those two affirmative precepts, gratitude to God and parents, are endless."

**McGilchrist's hemispheric asymmetry** (FLAG-09): ritual is embodied, contextual, relational — right-hemispheric. The ceremonial precepts work *because* they engage the right hemisphere's pattern-recognition and the body's affective resonance, not because they are propositionally enriched. Q.101 a.4's division (sacrifices, sacred things, sacraments, observances) covers four different embodied registers of formation.

**CROSS-09 (costly-signaling theory in master cross-program index)**: contemporary cultural-evolution research shows that costly ritual practices reliably distinguish in-group commitment and produce measurably higher group cohesion. The ceremonial precepts on dress, food, time — what looks like arbitrary tribal marking from outside — track exactly the structures cultural-evolution research has identified as functional. Q.102 a.4's "man's tendency is to reverence less those things which are common… whereas he admires and reveres those things which are distinct from others in some point of excellence" is the medieval anticipation of costly-signaling theory.

**Charity as precept (Q.100 a.10)**: this is where the synthesis must work hardest. Aquinas's careful position is that *fulfilling the law requires charity*, *charity requires grace*, *therefore the law cannot be fulfilled without grace* — without making the natural law dispensable. The Pelagian heresy is precisely the position that grace is *not* needed. The synthesis-friendly contemporary frame:

- Friston: priors that are sufficient to direct rule-compliance are *not* sufficient to direct loving compliance; the latter requires *additional* priors that the agent cannot generate from within (grace as imported prior-update).
- Fredrickson: positive affect (love) is *upstream* of robust prosocial behavior; agents in chronic negative affect can comply with rules but cannot produce the broaden-and-build dynamic that makes compliance *flourishing*.
- McGilchrist: rule-compliance (left hemisphere) without relational integration (right hemisphere) is the Pharisee's failure mode Jesus explicitly criticizes; charity is the right-hemispheric integration the rules were always pointing toward.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The moral and ceremonial precepts together constitute a *formation ecology* aimed at producing agents capable of *charity*. Moral precepts define the rule-system (the left-hemispheric floor); ceremonial precepts produce the embodied identification and pattern-recognition (right-hemispheric scaffold) that prepares agents to recognize the pattern when it is realized. Both are necessary; neither is sufficient without grace.

### What this shares with Aquinas

- The Decalogue's order is meaningful, with a defensible rational structure.
- Charity is required for fulfillment-in-the-deep-sense of the law.
- Charity is impossible without grace; the Pelagian denial is mistaken.
- Ceremonial precepts are figurative, pointing at what is to come, and graded-revelation is essential pedagogy.
- The law in its highest form is "love your neighbor as yourself" because that summary contains the deeper-form-as-pattern recognition.

### Where this reframes

- **The Decalogue's confident specific delineation (Augustine vs. Jerome on the order, the contemporary Catholic numbering) is one community's tradition-specific articulation.** The *structural* claim (end-before-means; love of God before love of neighbor) is invariant; the specific count and division is tradition-specific.
- **The ceremonial / moral distinction is now empirically informed.** Costly-signaling theory shows why specifically *embodied, costly, distinctive* practices function as community-formation mechanisms. The medieval theological account of ceremonial as figure plus identity-formation lines up.
- **Q.100 a.10's nuanced position on charity is the central theological claim of the entire law-and-grace section.** The contemporary reading must hold it: the law is not abolished by grace; the law cannot be fulfilled without grace; grace fulfills the law in the agent. This is precisely the Pauline summary Wright reads (Rom 13:10: "love is the fulfillment of the law"; Gal 5:14).

### Where this is firm vs. speculative

*Empirical*: Cross-cultural moral-foundations work; costly-signaling theory; affect-broadening effects.

*Empirical*: Predictive-processing accounts of curriculum learning; interface-level cognition (Hoffman). 

*Speculative*: The full integration of all the empirical findings into the single Aquinas frame is interpretive philosophical work.

*Theological*: Charity-impossible-without-grace is a theological claim. The convergent contemporary picture *supports* it (you cannot bootstrap from rule-compliance to love by adding more rules) but the supernatural-grace claim is theological.

*Theological*: Q.100 a.12's claim that pre-Christic salvation depends on faith in Christ as mediator (varyingly explicit) is a specific Catholic position, well-articulated by Trent. Wright's covenant-theology is congruent. Other Christian and non-Christian readers will engage this differently.

## The Central-Theme Thread

The central theme attaches firmly here: *perspective-limitation requires graded revelation*. Each agent and each community is in some stage of capacity; the law and the figures meet the agent at that stage and stretch the agent toward the next. The pattern (the eternal law, the deep priors, the universal Christ) is invariant; the *interface* by which the agent accesses the pattern is staged.

The loving-unity telos requires charity. The Pelagian failure mode is to think rule-compliance suffices — that one can bootstrap into the loving-unity state by hard work. Q.100 a.10 says no: the law itself reveals that *love* is what the law was always pointing at, and love requires that the agent's priors be updated by something outside the agent's own resources. This is the central-theme claim in its sharpest form: the agent cannot solve the loving-unity computational task using only the resources it perceives from its own perspective; it requires the import of new priors (grace, charity, the indwelling Spirit).

"We work out our salvation in fear and trembling" here means: we recognize that fulfillment is not within our perspective-bounded resources, but we work nonetheless, *receiving* the additional priors that grace supplies in the practices and revelations and community that we inhabit.

Wright's covenantal reading attaches: the *people* in whom this work is being done is the people in whom God is operating to bring humanity into the loving-unity end. Salvation is *inclusion* in that people, which is *not* a status achieved by rule-compliance but a relational reality made possible by the indwelling Spirit (Q.106 territory tomorrow on the New Law). Rohr's universal-Christ reading attaches: the pattern that the figures pointed at, the law that charity fulfills, is *Christ* — the eternal Logos in whom and through whom all things were made.

- **FLAG-05 (Friston curriculum-staged generative-model formation)**: graded revelation as pedagogical staging. *Empirical/formal*.
- **FLAG-11 (Fredrickson broaden-and-build)**: positive affect scaffolds robust prosociality; charity as the broadening principle. *Empirical*.
- **FLAG-09 (McGilchrist hemispheric integration)**: rule (left) + ritual (right) → integrated charity. *Empirical*.
- **CROSS-09 (master wiki, costly-signaling theory)**: ceremonial precepts as in-group commitment markers with functional payoffs. *Empirical cross-disciplinary*.
- **Wright, *Paul and the Faithfulness of God***: Decalogue and charity in covenantal frame; love as fulfillment. *Exegetical/theological*.
- **Rohr, *The Universal Christ***: the figurative-typological pattern is Christ. *Contemplative-theological*.

## Where this leaves us

Tomorrow (Day 112, Q.102-103) unpacks the causes and duration of the ceremonial precepts — and lands on the supersession claim (Q.103 a.4: legal ceremonies after Christ as religious acts would be mortal sin). The contemporary synthesis must hold this with care: the theological logic is precise (the figure has been realized; clinging to the figure rejects the reality), but the modern Catholic position post-Nostra Aetate refines significantly how Christians relate to ongoing Jewish observance. Open thread: how does the synthesis present "figurative-to-real" transitions without flattening into supersessionist polemic? Wright's reading of ongoing Jewish covenant-vocation (not erased by Christ but fulfilled in a way that reframes both) is the right place to look.
