# Day 226 — Contemporary Parallel: A Tradition Perceives More of the Whole Than Any One Perspective

## Frame

Q.27-28 take up the Mother of God: her *sanctification* (sanctified before birth, full of grace, preserved from all actual sin) and her *virginity* (virgin in conceiving, in giving birth, and ever-virgin). But the most striking feature of this episode is not a single doctrine — it is the spectacle of *the tradition correcting one of its greatest masters*. Habash flags it twice and plainly: on whether Mary was sanctified before *animation* (a.2) and on whether she was cleansed of the *fomes* of concupiscence (a.3), "St. Thomas is actually going to be incorrect." Aquinas, noting himself that "there is a diversity of opinion on the question," held that Mary contracted original sin after ensoulment and was then *cleansed* in the womb, with concupiscence remaining until Christ's birth redounded upon her. The Church later defined the opposite: *Ineffabilis Deus* (1854) teaches that "from the first moment of her conception... [she] was preserved free from every stain of original sin," in the "state of original innocence." Aquinas reached the edge of the insight (Mary's unique fullness of grace, "nearest of all to the Author of grace... by bringing him forth she in a manner dispensed grace to all" — the title *mediatrix of grace*) but not its completion. The contemporary version is therefore a *meta*-question, and it is the project's own: *how does a tradition come, over centuries, to perceive more of the whole than any single perspective within it — even its best — could perceive alone?*

## Problem

The inquiry-problem here is the project's central engine, surfaced explicitly by the text. (1) **A perspective reaching its limit.** Aquinas, working with the best available framework, perceives the *direction* of the truth (Mary's singular preservation, her fullness of grace) but is held back by a particular limitation — his account of how sanctification relates to ensoulment, and an assumption that *everyone* descended from Adam contracts original sin. His perspective is genuinely limited, and he says so. (2) **A tradition exceeding the perspective.** The Church, over six centuries, *reorders* around a fuller perception — preservation from the first instant — that no single thirteenth-century vantage could secure. The problem is what kind of process this is: how does the move from Aquinas's view to the defined dogma count as *development* (a tradition perceiving more of the whole) rather than either rigid repetition (which would have frozen Aquinas's error) or unfaithful innovation (which would have invented something foreign)?

## Resource

The leading resources here are the project's own theory of tradition. Tom's PRS-form record of Wright (PRS-02, *faithful improvisation*; PRS-03, *inaugurated eschatology*) is almost purpose-built for this case: a tradition extends itself into questions its founders never settled by "deep immersion in the preceding acts (the tradition's internal logic) and creative responsiveness to the present scene" — "neither fundamentalism nor liberalism but a third way." The development of the Immaculate Conception is faithful improvisation exactly: the dogma is *drawn out* of the tradition's internal logic (Mary's fullness of grace, the Theotokos, the new-Eve typology Habash cites) rather than imposed on it, and it completes what Aquinas could only approach. Inaugurated eschatology names the shape: "the decisive insight has already occurred, but its full implications are still being worked out" — the present is "the space between the decisive insight and its full elaboration," "a framework applicable to research programs as well as theology." Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-02) supplies the developmental pattern and, explicitly, the MacIntyrean bridge: the *order-disorder-reorder* sequence, noted in the wiki as "parallels MacIntyre's epistemological crisis" — "the reorder that follows genuine disorder is deeper than the original order because it has been tested and survived." Aquinas's flagged-and-uncertain articles are the *disorder* (a perspective registering that it cannot fully resolve the question); the dogma is the *reorder*. Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-05, *limitation-becomes-gift*) reads even Aquinas's error productively: the very point where his perspective fell short became the site where the tradition's deeper wisdom emerged. And the MacIntyrean tradition in the wiki supplies the rigorous version: a living tradition is *constituted* by working through epistemological crises, advancing when a successor account explains both the world *and why the predecessor account failed*. The dogma does exactly that: it explains Mary's preservation *and* why Aquinas, lacking the later framework, could not see it. Tom's PRS-form record of Stump (PRS-06, *tradition vitality and the MacIntyre criterion*) closes the loop: Thomism counts as a *live* tradition precisely because it can absorb such a correction "without dissolving" — Aquinas corrected is Thomism *alive*, not Thomism refuted.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the development from Aquinas's view to the defined dogma is a tradition perceiving more of the whole than any single perspective within it could perceive alone — the project's central engine displayed in miniature. It is faithful improvisation (Wright PRS-02): the dogma is drawn from the tradition's internal logic, not imposed; and it has the order-disorder-reorder shape of a resolved epistemological crisis (Rohr PRS-02 / MacIntyre): Aquinas's flagged uncertainty is the disorder, the dogma the deeper reorder that explains both Mary's preservation and why his perspective could not reach it. The limitation of his perspective becomes the gift through which the tradition saw further (Rohr PRS-05).**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It honors what Aquinas *got* — Mary's singular fullness of grace, her preservation from actual sin, her role as the one who "dispensed grace to all." It treats his uncertainty as integrity, not failure (he flagged the diversity of opinion himself). And it keeps the substantive doctrines of Q.28 (the virginal conception, ever-virginity) intact.

*Where it reframes.* The synthesis reads the episode not primarily as a set of Marian doctrines but as a *case study in how traditions advance* — the meta-level the project exists to study. Where Aquinas's own frame would treat his error simply as an error later corrected by authority, the contemporary reframe sees a tradition-constituted inquiry working exactly as the project predicts: a limited perspective reaching its edge, a disorder honestly registered, a deeper reorder that the whole tradition (not any one mind) achieves over time.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The historical fact of the development (Aquinas's view, the 1854 definition) is firm — it is the strongest evidence in this episode. The theory that frames it (faithful improvisation, epistemological crisis, tradition-vitality) is formal-philosophical: it is the project's own meta-claim, well-articulated and internally rigorous, but it is a *model* of how traditions advance, not an empirical law. The Marian doctrines themselves are theological.

## The central-theme thread

This is the day the project's deepest premise appears *not* as a claim about Christ or about individual agents but about *traditions* — and it could hardly be more on-theme. The central claim holds that limited perspectives "work out [their] understanding... aware of [their] limitations and [their] need for guidance from those who perceive more of the whole than [they] do." Here the one whose perspective is limited is *Aquinas himself* — the project's own master interlocutor — and the one who "perceives more of the whole" is not a greater individual but the *tradition across time*, the community of inquiry that, immersed in its own internal logic and responsive to the question, reorders around a fuller vision than any single vantage could reach. This is the C2A2 wager in concentrated form: that a tradition richly informed by its own depths, holding its disorders honestly, computes toward a fuller perception of the whole than its individual members possess. The Immaculate Conception is, for the project, less interesting as Mariology than as *proof of concept*: a perspective (Aquinas's) genuinely contained in the whole the tradition is approaching, exceeded not by being discarded but by being *completed*. And the substance is itself on-theme: "full of grace, dispensing grace to all," the new Eve whose limitation (a creature, not the Author of grace) is the very site of her unique participation — limitation-becomes-gift, the perspective most nearly transparent to the whole.

## Where this leaves us

Q.27-28 show a tradition perceiving, over centuries, more of the whole than even Aquinas could alone — the project's engine in miniature — while establishing Mary's fullness of grace and ever-virginity. Tomorrow (Day 227, "The Annunciation") the *Summa* turns to the *Annunciation* (Q.29-30): Mary's free *act of faith* and her *fiat* — "be it done unto me according to thy word" — received, Aquinas says, "on behalf of humankind." The thread to carry forward: having watched a tradition exceed a single perspective, watch the Annunciation for the complementary moment — a *single* limited perspective freely consenting, on the community's behalf, to the One who sees the whole.
