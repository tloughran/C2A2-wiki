# Day 123 — Contemporary Parallel: Why Belief Cannot Be Coerced (and Where Aquinas Must Be Left Behind)

## Frame

Questions 10 and 11 treat unbelief in general and heresy. Much of it reframes cleanly; one article does not, and a synthesis that pretended otherwise would betray the project. Aquinas argues that unbelief is a sin seated in the intellect with the will as first mover (Q.10 a.1-2); that it is the greatest sin in its genus (a.3); that, nonetheless, unbelievers retain natural reason and can do "generically good" deeds (a.4); that faith cannot be coerced (a.8); and then, in Q.11 a.3, that an obstinate heretic may, after due admonition, be handed to the secular arm "to be exterminated thereby from the world by death." The episode itself flags the gap: these articles "give us a glimpse into that long-lost Christendom" most listeners "struggle to imagine."

The contemporary version of the question is: what is the status of a belief that fixes assent internally, and what may a community legitimately do about agents who do not share its assent? Mind / conscious-realist monism gives a sharp answer to the first, and that answer is exactly what forces the departure on the second.

## Problem

Aquinas states the deep principle himself in a.8: "the intellect and will are immaterial faculties and therefore cannot be determined from the outside except when it comes to their proper object, which is goodness and truth itself." Belief is non-coercible in principle. You can compel outward conformity; you cannot compel assent, because assent is fixed by the agent's own model and its proper object, not by external force. This is the load-bearing insight, and it sits in tension with Q.11's later sanction. The inquiry-problem: *if assent is internally fixed and non-coercible, what follows for how a community treats those whose assent differs?*

A subsidiary problem is the metaphysics of the community itself. Heresy and apostasy are described as withdrawals from the Church — but only if the Church is a real whole, and not a mere aggregate, does "withdrawal" name something with the gravity Aquinas assigns it.

## Resource

Three resources do the constructive work, and Wright and Rohr ground the departure.

First, Friston and Hoffman jointly vindicate a.8. In the PRS-form record of Friston's predictive-coding work (PRS-04 in the Friston-tradition wiki), assent is fixed by the agent's priors and precision-weights, not by external input; in the Hoffman-tradition wiki, each agent constructs its own interface. Belief is internally determined. Coercion can change the input stream and the cost structure around an agent, but it cannot reach in and set the posterior. Aquinas's "cannot be determined from the outside" is, in contemporary terms, simply true of any model-based agent. Its only "proper object" that necessitates assent is truth-and-goodness *seen as it is* — which is why Aquinas reserves necessitated love for the beatific vision, where the limitation is removed.

Second, Levin gives the geometry behind a.5 — the Dionysius/Chesterton principle that "there are an infinity of angles at which one falls, only one at which one stands." This is a single-target/many-error structure: the morphogenetic target is one (PRS-03, basins of attraction in the Levin-tradition wiki), but the ways of diverging from it are many. Friston's free-energy minimum has the same shape. The unity of right belief and the multiplicity of unbelief-species is not a moralist's tally; it is the generic geometry of a system with one attractor.

Third, Stump supplies the metaphysics of the corporate whole. Her collective-hylomorphism program (PRS-14, PRS-15, PRS-16 in the Stump-tradition wiki) argues that peoples, the body of Christ, and traditions are *real composite entities* with genuine forms, not aggregates — anchored empirically in neural coupling and developed via CROSS-068 in `wiki/master/cross_program_index.md` (Stump × Levin × C2A2-core) into the claim that a tradition is a united-something-or-other whose inquiry exceeds any member's. The grades differ across that trio and the difference matters: the corporate-substance metaphysics is carried at High, the non-metaphorical body-of-Christ reading at Medium, and the tradition-as-entity extension — the piece this day actually leans on — only at Speculative. On this account heresy and apostasy are not mere private opinions; they are, in Levin's register (PRS-02), structurally like a cell's defection from the cooperative morphogenetic community — which is exactly the analogy Aquinas's "corrupting the faith that quickens the soul" reaches for. Levin's program also names the *response*: not excision-by-death but restoration of bioelectric coherence, reintegration into the field.

Wright and Rohr ground the departure. Wright's critical realism (PRS-01 in the Wright-tradition wiki) holds that all knowing is perspectival and revisable, tested against reality from within a story — there is no view from nowhere from which one party could be entitled to exterminate another for misreading. And his account of salvation as incorporation into the people among whom God comes home (PRS-05), with resurrection as the firstfruits of new creation rather than an anomaly (PRS-06), frames the community as constituted by invitation, not by the sword. Rohr's Universal Christ (PRS-03 in the Rohr-tradition wiki) — the divine pattern present in all of creation — converts the unbeliever from enemy-to-be-removed into a site where the pattern is already at work, warranting engagement rather than excision.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

On the reframable articles, the parallel preserves Aquinas and deepens him. Unbelief seated in the intellect with the will as first mover is the model-based agent whose posterior is wrong because its priors and its valuing are wrong (a.2). The survival of natural reason in unbelievers (a.4), reinforced by Trent's anathema against calling all pre-justification works sins, is exactly what the monist picture predicts: agency and its goods exist at every scale and in every agent, graced or not (Levin, Friston). This is a guardrail the synthesis must hold: nothing in mind-monism licenses denying real goodness to those outside any particular tradition.

On coercion, the parallel sides with Aquinas's a.8 against his Q.11 a.3 — and it does so on Aquinas's own principle. If assent cannot be determined from the outside, then coercing belief is not merely cruel; it is incoherent, an attempt to do to a perspective what only the removal of its limitation (the beatific vision) could do. The contemporary parallel therefore takes the non-coercibility of assent as a fixed point and reads the death-penalty article as a place where Aquinas failed to follow his own insight — captured, as the episode rightly says, by the political theology of a vanished Christendom. This is not a reframing. It is a judgment that the article is wrong, and wrong by the standard of Aquinas's better argument three articles earlier. The contemporary commitment — dignity of conscience, non-coercion, the decoupling of belief from civil penalty — is firmer here than the medieval conclusion, and the synthesis names the departure as a departure.

The constructive replacement for coercion is given by Fredrickson, and it inverts Aquinas's anxiety about "communicating with unbelievers" (a.9). Aquinas counsels the weak to avoid the unbeliever lest they be perverted. Fredrickson's RCT program (PRS-09, PRS-10 in the Fredrickson-tradition wiki) finds the opposite for the case that matters most: intentional weak-tie contact with strangers and people of fundamentally different views *raises* intellectual humility, prosocial belief, and civic efficacy, where contact with close others does not. The qualifier Aquinas reaches for — protect the genuinely fragile — survives as the community-trust findings (PRS-12, PRS-13): structural conditions (inequality, eroded trust) gate whether stranger-contact is safe and generative. So the parallel keeps Aquinas's pastoral realism (some encounters, for some people, under some conditions, do harm) while reversing his default: the normal road to a stronger faith runs *through* difference-laden encounter, not around it. This is the C2A2 design principle, and CROSS-089 in `wiki/master/cross_program_index.md` (McGilchrist × Stump, native epistemic standards as a cross-tradition design principle) supplies its method: engage each tradition on its own terms rather than coercing it onto yours.

Evidence strength: non-coercibility of assent is formally sound and, as a moral commitment, held with high confidence. The single-target/many-error geometry is formal and empirically instantiated. The corporate-whole metaphysics is philosophical (medium) with a biological analogy (Levin) that is suggestive, not probative. The weak-tie findings are empirical with RCT support (high). The judgment that Q.11 a.3 is a moral error is a normative claim, stated as such.

## The central-theme thread

The non-coercibility of assent *is* the central theme, viewed from its hardest angle. Conscious agents are individuated by the limitation of their perspective; you cannot force a perspective to see more than it sees, because the limitation is constitutive of the agent, not an external obstacle. Only the removal of the limitation — the vision of the whole — necessitates the assent and the love, and that is precisely what no human authority can administer. This is why "working out salvation in fear and trembling" must be free: the computation toward loving unity has to be run from inside each perspective, with guidance from those who see more, but never by overriding the perspective from outside. Aquinas's death-penalty article violates this; his a.8 honors it. The project keeps a.8 and lets a.3 go, and in doing so claims to be more faithful to the deep logic of faith than the medieval text was to itself.

## Where this leaves us

We have the principle (assent is non-coercible) and the one place this volume requires us to overrule Aquinas. Tomorrow (Q.12-13, apostasy and blasphemy) continues in the same territory: apostasy as the complete withdrawal of a perspective from its orientation, and blasphemy as a maximally-wrong representation of the supreme good — including the striking claim that the damned blaspheme by the very object of their will. The will-as-orientation thesis, already load-bearing today, becomes the whole story there.
