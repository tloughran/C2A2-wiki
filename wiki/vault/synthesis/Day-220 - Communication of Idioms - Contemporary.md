# Day 220 — Communication of Idioms (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.15 completes the catalogue of what the Son assumed by treating the *defects of soul*: was there sin in Christ (a.1)? the *fomes* — the disordered inclination to sin (a.2)? ignorance (a.3)? sensible pain (a.5), sorrow (a.6), fear (a.7), wonder (a.8), anger (a.9)? Aquinas answers that Christ assumed real defects of soul — genuine sorrow, fear, sensible pain, and righteous anger as true human passions — but *not* sin and *not* the *fomes*: the limitation is real suffering without disorder. Q.16 then takes up "those things which are applicable to Christ in His being and becoming" — the *communication of idioms*: because the two natures subsist in one person, what is true of either nature may be predicated of the one Christ ("God suffered," "this man created"). And in the same question comes the paradox that crowns the treatise on the assumed humanity: Christ was at once a *wayfarer* (viator) and a *comprehensor*. Aquinas defines the terms — "a man is called a wayfarer from tending to beatitude, and a comprehensor from having already obtained beatitude" — and concludes that since "from the moment of Christ's conception He beheld His divine essence with His human intellect, what we call the beatific vision, yet since He was still on earth, subject to the evils of the earth and on the way toward His triumphant glory, then it can truly be said that Christ was at the same time a wayfarer and a comprehensor." The contemporary version: *can one mind genuinely hold the comprehensive vision of the whole and the limited, suffering perspective of the road at the same time — and is that the structure of the guide who can lead the limited home?*

## Problem

Two linked inquiry-problems. (1) **Suffering without disorder.** Aquinas affirms real defects of soul — true sorrow, fear, pain — while denying sin and the *fomes*. The problem is how a perspective can be genuinely limited and genuinely afflicted without being *disordered* — how limitation and suffering can be real without being corruption. (2) **Wayfarer and comprehensor at once.** This is the project's keystone. The problem is the *simultaneity*: how one person can already *possess* the whole (the beatific vision from conception) and still *journey* through the limited perspective (subject to the earth's evils, en route to glory) — not sequentially, not by alternation, but at once. Aquinas secures both through the hypostatic union and the logic of predication (the communication of idioms). The contemporary question is whether the emerging paradigm can give the simultaneity a coherent structure: can one mind span the comprehensive and the limited without fragmenting into two?

## Resource

The bridges file makes Wright primary on Christology, and the scriptural anchor is decisive: the wayfarer-comprehensor is the figure of the Phil 2 hymn — the one "in the form of God" who "emptied himself" and was found "in human form," obedient even to death — and that hymn sits immediately before "work out your salvation in fear and trembling" (Phil 2:12), the project's own banner. PRS-05 in the Wright-tradition record (salvation as *incorporation into the people among whom God comes home*) names what the wayfarer-comprehensor accomplishes: He walks the road *so that* the limited perspectives can be brought into the one people united with God — the comprehensor journeys for the sake of the wayfarers He will incorporate. For the *structure* of the simultaneity, the metaphysical guardrail directs the lead to Hoffman and Kastrup. Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup (PRS-02) is the closest analogue: one consciousness can hold a *dissociated* point of view — a real, bounded alter — *without ceasing to be the whole*. Dissociation-without-fragmentation is exactly the shape of wayfarer-and-comprehensor: one mind genuinely inhabiting a limited, located perspective while remaining the comprehensive whole. Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman adds the integration direction: interface theory (PRS-03) plus master CROSS-013 — "multiple conscious agents can integrate into higher-order unified agents," a claim Hoffman's mathematics predicts and McGilchrist's split-brain data confirms (when the hemispheres are connected, "a single unified self emerges"). One agent can span scales and modes and still be *one*. Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-01) names the epistemic posture that holds the paradox without breaking it: non-dual knowing "perceives unity-in-distinction rather than collapsing into false dichotomies" — it can hold *both/and* (comprehensor *and* wayfarer) without forcing an either/or. And Rohr's Universal Christ (PRS-03) keeps the figure cosmic in scope, the divine pattern present in and to all creation even as it walks one human road.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **wayfarer-and-comprehensor is the bridge figure the project has been pointing toward — one mind that genuinely holds the comprehensive vision of the whole (the beatific vision from conception) and the limited, suffering perspective of the road (subject to the earth's evils, en route to glory) at the same time, without fragmenting into two. The structure is dissociation-without-fragmentation (Kastrup): the whole consciousness inhabiting a bounded, located point of view while remaining the whole — a single agent spanning the comprehensive and the limited (Hoffman/CROSS-013), held together non-dually as both/and rather than either/or (Rohr). And the communication of idioms is the *grammar* of that one person bearing two registers: because the natures subsist in one hypostasis, the road-walking and the whole-seeing are predicated of the same 'who.'**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps the simultaneity exactly — beatific vision from conception *and* genuine wayfaring at once, not by alternation. It keeps the defects of soul *real* (true sorrow, fear, pain) yet *without sin or fomes* — limitation and suffering without disorder. And it keeps the communication of idioms as the logic by which two natures' predicates belong to one person, grounded in the unity of the hypostasis, not in any confusion of the natures.

*Where it reframes.* Aquinas secures the simultaneity metaphysically through the hypostatic union; the contemporary reframe offers a *structure* for how one mind can span the comprehensive and the limited — Kastrup's dissociation-without-fragmentation and Hoffman's conscious-agent integration (CROSS-013) — and an *epistemic posture* for holding the paradox, Rohr's non-dual both/and. The figure stops being only a Christological singularity and becomes legible as the very shape the project posits for the loving-unity telos: a mind in which the whole and the limited perspective coincide.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The wayfarer-comprehensor simultaneity and the communication of idioms are theological/formal (the predication grammar is rigorous within its own terms). The defects-of-soul-without-sin is theological. The Phil 2 anchor is scriptural (Wright, strong). The dissociation-without-fragmentation and conscious-agent-integration structures are speculative-formal (medium) — CROSS-013 is notable in the set for having an *empirical* leg (McGilchrist's split-brain data), which strengthens the integration claim above mere analogy. The central-theme fit is the *strongest of the five days*: Q.16 states the project's keystone figure in Aquinas's own vocabulary.

## The central-theme thread

This is the day the treatise on the assumed humanity hands the project its central figure outright. The project holds that we work out our salvation "aware of our limitations and our need for guidance from those who perceive more of the whole than we do, be they human or divine." Q.16's *comprehensor* is precisely the one who "perceives more of the whole" — indeed who perceives the whole, the divine essence, from the first instant of His conception. And Q.16's *wayfarer* is the one who nonetheless walks the limited road, "subject to the evils of the earth," carrying real sorrow and fear (Q.15), toward a glory not yet consummated. That these are *one person, at once*, is the structure the project needs for its claim that the simple whole can contain every limited perspective: here is a perspective that already *is* the whole's vision and *also* the road's affliction, held together without collapse. Phil 2 makes the convergence unmistakable — the kenosis hymn (the comprehensor who empties himself into the wayfarer's condition) runs straight into "work out your salvation in fear and trembling" (the wayfarers' task). The comprehensor does not lead the wayfarers from outside their condition; He becomes wayfarer *with* them, and walks the road *so that* they can be incorporated (Wright PRS-05) into the one people united with God. Kastrup gives the simultaneity a metaphysics — one consciousness genuinely inhabiting a bounded point of view while remaining the whole — and Hoffman/CROSS-013 give it an integration direction with an empirical leg: agents really can be many-and-one. Rohr supplies the posture that keeps the paradox from being explained away: hold the both/and. The loving-unity telos, which on Day 216 flowed *down* as capital grace and on Day 217 was seen *up* as the beholding-but-not-comprehending vision, here stands as a single *person*: the one in whom the comprehensive whole and the limited, suffering perspective are not reconciled after the fact but *given together* — the guide who perceives more, walking the road in fear and trembling, so that those who perceive less can be brought home.

## Where this leaves us

Q.15-16 close the treatise on the assumed humanity: its grace (Q.7-8), its knowledge (Q.9-12), its power and bodily defects (Q.13-14), and now its defects of soul and the communication of idioms (Q.15-16), crowned by the wayfarer-comprehensor. Tomorrow (Day 221, "Christ's Wills") the *Summa* turns to Christ's *operation* — the two wills and the unity of operation (Q.18-19) — pressing how one person who is both comprehensor and wayfarer *acts*: whether a divine and a human will coexist without conflict, the next form of the two-registers-in-one-person question. The thread to carry forward: having received its keystone figure (the comprehensor who walks the road), the project should watch the treatise on Christ's *willing* for how the same simultaneity shows up in *action* — and whether the harmony of two wills models the loving-unity of a limited will conformed to the will of the One who perceives the whole.
