# Day 101 — Satan's Influence (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas opens Q.80 by setting a remarkably tight constraint on what an immaterial agent outside the human soul can do to that soul. Only God can move the will *ab interiori*; only the human intellect can propose an object as appetible to that will. Therefore Satan, who has access to neither the divine interior nor the proper proposition of objects to intellect, can affect us only by persuasion working on what we share with irrational animals — *imagination and the passions*. He concludes that the devil is a cause of sin "neither directly nor sufficiently but only by persuasion or by proposing the object of appetite." Aquinas then pivots in Q.81 to the cause of sin on the part of man, where original sin enters: Adam's first sin is transmitted to his offspring by *generation*, not by *imitation*.

The contemporary version of this question, under a mind / conscious-realist monism, is: *what is the actual topology of influence between minds, and what does that topology imply about the existence and reach of the kinds of sub-agents classical theology names "the devil"?* The two halves of Aquinas's locus — the structure of intra-agent susceptibility (Q.80) and the inheritance of disordered initial conditions (Q.81) — both translate cleanly once perceived spacetime is treated as a species-specific interface and mind-at-large as the ontological primitive.

## Problem

Across both medieval and contemporary framings, the inquiry-problem is the same:

> An agent whose will, by structural necessity, wills only its own perceived good can nonetheless reliably arrive at choices that damage its own telos toward loving unity. How is this possible, what is the mechanism, and where are the points at which an "outside" can introduce the systematic misappraisal that yields disordered choice?

Aquinas's answer is a *boundary geometry of the soul*: the will's interior is closed to all but God; the intellect's object-proposition is internal to the agent; the imagination and passions are the *only* surface through which a non-divine outside can operate. Sin therefore always requires a misperception or misappraisal that originates in the imagination–passions surface and is then ratified by intellect and will. Q.81 adds an inherited bias: Adam transmits a disordered initial condition through generation — a tilt in the agent's defaults before any first-person choice.

The contemporary statement does not soften this geometry; it *re-derives* it. Active inference describes the agent as a generative model maintaining its own boundary by minimizing variational free energy. The model has priors (defaults inherited through development, including genetic and culturally transmitted ones), a likelihood (how it expects sensory input to behave given its hypotheses), and an action policy (how it acts to reduce expected free energy). External influence on such an agent can only enter through the *sensory-and-affective interface* — perception, interoception, the passions. The deep update — the change in *how* the system updates — is structurally interior. Aquinas's "only God moves the will from within" survives as: *the meta-priors that determine which priors get rewritten are not modifiable from outside the agent's boundary at the same level at which sensory input is.*

## Resource

Three convergent resources from the C2A2 network supply the contemporary reframe.

First, **Kastrup's analytic idealism**, particularly his dissociation-integration model, which the network's `master/cross_program_index.md` (CROSS-027 and the cortical-columns-as-alters extension) extends downward to the brain itself. If mind-at-large is the substrate and individual perspectives are dissociated alters, then *some* such alters will, by the structural necessity of perspective-limitation, develop in ways orthogonal or hostile to others. The classical demonological vocabulary names a subset of these: alters whose perspective is so impoverished that their characteristic action on neighboring alters is to drag them toward narrower, more isolated, more fear-saturated regions of the state space. This is not the only possible cosmology, but it is one where Aquinas's geometry — interaction only through the perceptual interface — is preserved at the metaphysical level, because alters communicate only through the shared interface, never by direct will-to-will manipulation.

Second, **Fredrickson's broaden-and-build research** (PRS-01 isolation-and-disconnection; the Love 2.0 finding that positivity resonance is a measurable biophysiological event mediated by vagal tone, oxytocin, and neural synchrony). The empirical bite of broaden-and-build is its inverse: *negative emotion narrows attention.* When the vagal-tone substrate of an agent collapses — through isolation, threat, fatigue — the agent's predictive model loses the capacity to broaden over its priors. The likelihood weighting of fearful and self-protective hypotheses goes up; the meta-prior favoring exploration goes down. Aquinas's "the passions make whatever their object is seem more suitable to us when we are under their sway" is precisely the precision-weighting story: under high precision on threat-priors, the agent's posterior collapses around appetite-aligned interpretations of the situation. The empirical strength here is high: vagal tone, attentional-scope measures, and prior-modulation effects in active-inference paradigms are all directly testable.

Third, **McGilchrist's hemispheric account of attention** (PRS-01 hemispheric asymmetry; PRS-05 attention as ontological act; the corpus-callosotomy data the wiki flags as empirical proof of conscious-agent integration at Hoffman-style scale). McGilchrist's claim that the left hemisphere "re-presents known fragments" while the right "attends to the living whole" supplies a within-agent topology that converges remarkably with Aquinas's. When attention collapses into left-hemispheric closure under stress, the agent's perceived environment becomes a finite stack of decontextualized affordances — exactly the field of fragmented, abstract goods that classical temptation literature describes. The right-hemispheric reopening is what Rohr names as non-dual contemplative awareness. Empirically the hemispheric-shift findings are mid-strength: real, replicable in some paradigms, contested in their cultural-scale extrapolation; the within-individual attention-narrowing-under-stress effect is robust.

To these the network adds two further resources. **Wright** on the second-temple cosmology of "the powers" — the New Testament's nuanced refusal to either deny or anthropomorphize the supra-individual loci of evil; he treats them as real socio-spiritual structures whose grip is mediated by the formation of imagination and desire (a striking match with Aquinas's "Satan acts on the imagination and the passions, not the will"). **Rohr** on the shadow and the non-dual practice that lets the agent hold its own complicity in disorder without collapsing into either denial or despair — the practical implication of the broaden-and-build story for a community that wants to remain capable of perceiving its own wrongness without losing the wide attention that loving unity requires.

## Solution (proposed, speculative where speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response replaces Aquinas's demonology of separate immaterial substances with a *topology of mind-at-large* in which:

1. *(Theological, aligned with Wright and Rohr)* The reality named by "Satan" and "the demons" is not a population of independent immaterial wills but a structural feature of mind-at-large under perspective-limitation: alter-like regions whose impoverished perspective characteristically draws neighboring perspectives into narrower attention, fear-weighted priors, and isolation. This is what one would *predict* from a mind-monist cosmology where individuation is by perspective-limitation: some perspectives will be more limited than others, and the dynamics between them will systematically resemble what the contemplative traditions report.
2. *(Empirical, well-evidenced)* The mechanism by which any such "outside" influence reaches a particular agent runs through the perceptual–affective interface — never through direct manipulation of the will's interior. This is exactly the mechanism Aquinas names: imagination and passions, not will and intellect. The contemporary version specifies the channels: precision-weighting under stress (Friston), vagal-tone collapse under isolation (Fredrickson), hemispheric-attention closure under threat (McGilchrist), and bioelectric-pattern coupling at the body-environment boundary (Levin).
3. *(Empirical, mid-evidenced)* Original sin (Q.81) becomes the empirically tractable claim that *every* agent inherits a non-chosen initial condition — biological, developmental, cultural — that tilts the priors before any first-person decision. This is the modern reading of "transmitted by generation, not imitation" that Habash flags in Adam-as-source: the inheritance is structural, not exemplary. Where Aquinas needs the doctrine to explain universality, contemporary developmental science supplies it: prior tilts inherited through gene-culture co-transmission are empirically universal and pre-volitional.
4. *(Constructive, central-theme thread)* The proper response — "salvation in fear and trembling" (Phil 2:12, Wright's reading: inclusion in the people united with God) — is computational at the agent level and communal at the supra-agent level. Each perspective-limited agent works toward loving unity through the local actions that broaden attention (Fredrickson's positivity-resonance practices; Rohr's contemplative habits; the McGilchrist right-hemispheric retraining). But because the meta-priors are not externally modifiable at the same level as sensory input, the agent cannot save itself by force of intellect alone. It needs *guidance from perspectives less limited than its own* — the Christian tradition's "grace," translated into the network's vocabulary as the inflow of corrective signal from less-impoverished alters of mind-at-large.

**Difference from Aquinas, explicitly.** Aquinas's geometry of the soul is *preserved*; what changes is the metaphysics of what occupies the field outside that soul. He posits a population of immaterial substances with their own histories and wills; the mind-monist reframe replaces that population with a topology of perspectives within a single mind-at-large. The reframe does *not* eliminate the reality of the influences Aquinas names. It replaces "many independent immaterial agents acting on us through imagination" with "many perspective-limited regions of mind-at-large whose characteristic action on neighboring regions is mediated by the perceptual–affective interface." Functionally — and pastorally — the two accounts converge on the same warnings and the same remedies. Metaphysically they part company.

## The central-theme thread

The Q.80–81 locus is one of the cleanest illustrations of the project's central claim. *Conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective.* The Aquinas–Habash account already presupposes this: Satan acts on us only because we are perspective-limited along the imagination–passions axis; God can move the will from within only because God is the perspective that contains every other perspective. The mind-monist reframe just makes the structural claim explicit. *Loving unity*, the goal toward which the limited agent computes, is reached through the gradual relaxation of perspective-limitation — broaden-and-build, right-hemispheric reopening, contemplative non-duality, the inflow of grace from less-limited alters. The "fear and trembling" is the recognition that this computation runs under genuine resource scarcity: under isolation, fatigue, and threat, the agent's own attention narrows in ways that lock-in the very perspective-limitations the work seeks to relax. The remedy is communal: it requires standing inside a tradition (Wright, MacIntyre) and an embodied practice (Rohr) that re-supply the broadening signal the isolated agent cannot generate on its own.
