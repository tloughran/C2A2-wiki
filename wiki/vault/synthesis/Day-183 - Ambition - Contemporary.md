# Day 183 — Ambition (Contemporary)

## Frame

Having set magnanimity as the calibrated center of the self-estimate, Aquinas now treats its two *excesses*. Q.130 covers presumption (*praesumptio*): attempting things beyond one's power, the vice that overshoots one's real greatness. Q.131 covers ambition (*ambitio*): the inordinate desire for honor — desiring honor that is not due, or not referred to God and the good of others, or sought for oneself as one's own rather than received as a gift. The hinge of Aquinas's treatment is his account of honor itself: honor is a *sign* of virtue or excellence, owed in recognition of a real good. Ambition's disorder is to treat the sign as the thing — to pursue the recognition rather than the reality it should mark.

Under mind / conscious-realist monism the contemporary version splits cleanly along two parameters. Presumption is the self-model set *above* the truth: precision on one's own capacity over-weighted, so the agent selects policies its real resources cannot carry. Ambition is the *honor signal* mis-weighted: a social-feedback term that should be read as evidence of worth is instead promoted to a terminal reward and maximized for itself.

## Problem

Two distinct failures share a surface. The first is over-estimation: an agent that judges its capacity larger than it is will attempt the disproportionate and fail, not from too little courage but from a false reading of itself. The second is subtler and is the real heart of ambition: honor is genuinely *valuable* as information — it is how a community registers and transmits the recognition of real excellence — but precisely because it is valuable and pleasant, it can be detached from what it signals and pursued on its own. The agent then optimizes for *appearing* excellent in others' models rather than for *being* excellent, and the two come apart. The problem is that the honor signal is a legitimate input that becomes pathological the moment it is treated as the output.

## Resource

**Friston** gives the formal account of both. In Tom's PRS-form record of his work, presumption is the self-model over-precise relative to the truth — the mirror of pusillanimity's under-precision — so the agent's policy selection runs on a capacity estimate the world will not support. Ambition is a different error: honor functions as a social prediction signal (how the agent is modeled by others, fed back to the agent), and in a well-ordered agent this signal is *instrumental* — evidence that updates the self-model and guides contribution. Ambition promotes the signal to a *terminal* term in the value function and maximizes it directly. This is the formal shape of "seeking the sign rather than the thing": optimizing the estimate-of-being-excellent instead of the being-excellent that the estimate was tracking — a classic case of a proxy captured and gamed.

**Fredrickson** supplies the social diagnosis. In Tom's PRS-form record of her work, genuine positivity resonance is a real coupling between agents that builds durable shared resource. Status-seeking is its counterfeit: honor sought *for the self* runs on a circuit that does not build connection and is chronically insecure, because the supply of recognition is external and rivalrous. The ambitious agent is, on this reading, optimizing a metric that cannot deliver what genuine connection delivers — which is why the pursuit of honor for its own sake tends to a kind of starvation.

**McGilchrist** locates the cognitive signature. In his key, the craving for the explicit token — title, rank, public recognition — is the left hemisphere's characteristic move: it prizes the abstractable mark of a thing over the thing's living reality. Ambition is attention captured by one's own representation in others' minds rather than by the work itself; presumption is the same hemisphere's overconfidence about what it can grasp.

**Stump** enters as Thomistic interlocutor, supplying the structure the whole reframe rests on: honor as a sign ordered to a real good, virtue as ordered to the good itself and not its appearance. The disorder is precisely a mis-ordering — the appearance pursued as if it were the substance.

**Wright** grounds the theological re-ranking. The Gospel makes the seam explicit: "How can you believe, who receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from the only God?" (John 5:44). The choice is between two sources of honor — the rivalrous, external supply that ambition chases, and the verdict of God, which is given as gift and cannot be gamed. **Rohr** gives the contemplative form: the False Self is *built* out of titles, roles, and recognition; ambition is its native motion, and the only cure is the True Self, whose worth is received in union and therefore needs no honor to constitute it.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: presumption is the self-model over-precise relative to truth — the agent attempts beyond its real capacity (Friston) — and ambition is the honor signal promoted from an instrumental input to a terminal reward and maximized for itself (Friston, Fredrickson), the proxy captured in place of the good it tracked. Both overshoot magnanimity: presumption on the capacity estimate, ambition on the recognition signal. McGilchrist names the shared signature — attention captured by the abstractable token rather than the living reality.

What it shares with Aquinas: presumption and ambition as real vices of excess opposed to magnanimity; honor as a *sign* whose disorder is to be sought as an end. Where it reframes: the disorder becomes a proxy-gaming dynamic — optimizing the estimate-of-worth instead of the worth — with a measurable mechanism (terminal vs instrumental weighting of a social signal). Where it goes beyond the formalism: the resolution requires *re-sourcing* honor — from the rivalrous external supply to the verdict of God (Wright) and the received worth of the True Self (Rohr) — which the formalism can describe as "a non-gameable, non-rivalrous value source" but cannot supply. Evidence strength, marked honestly: the over-precise self-model and the proxy-capture of social signals are formal and empirical; the claim that honor *ought* to be referred to God is theological, firm within Wright's and Rohr's programs and openly so beyond them.

## Central-theme thread

Ambition is, in the project's terms, a perspective curved in on its own standing. Where magnanimity's large self-estimate was *in service of the great good*, ambition's motion is back toward the self — toward the agent's image in the models of others. The perspective collapses onto its reputation: it computes not toward the shared good but toward its own glory, mistaking the sign of worth for worth. This is the precise inverse of loving unity, which is the perspective opened *outward* into a whole it does not contain. And the central theme explains why ambition starves: a perspective individuated by its limits cannot fund its own worth from inside, so an agent that seeks worth from the rivalrous opinions of other limited perspectives is drawing on a supply that cannot satisfy. The only non-rivalrous source is the whole itself — the verdict of the One who sees all perspectives at once — which is exactly the re-sourcing Wright and Rohr name.

## Bridges

- **Friston (over-precise self-model; signal vs terminal reward)** — presumption as capacity over-estimate; ambition as the honor signal maximized for itself. *Formal.*
- **Fredrickson (status-seeking as counterfeit resonance)** — honor for the self runs on a brittle, rivalrous circuit that cannot build. *Empirical.*
- **McGilchrist (the explicit token over the living reality)** — attention captured by one's representation in others' minds. *Empirical / interpretive.*
- **Stump (honor as sign ordered to the good)** — the disorder as mistaking appearance for substance. *Theological / metaphysical.*
- **Wright + Rohr (glory from God vs from men; the False Self)** — honor re-sourced to a non-gameable verdict and to received worth. *Theological.*

## Where this leaves us

Presumption and ambition complete the *excess* side of the self-estimate axis. The defect side — pusillanimity, the soul too small to attempt what it could — arrives with Day 185 ("Vainglory"), which pairs it with vainglory, ambition's near neighbor (the desire not for honor as such but for its outward *display*). Between them sits Day 184's magnificence, the great-works virtue (the episode order is inverted here, as Habash notes). Open thread, sharpening from yesterday: ambition is the clearest instance yet of a vice that is a *proxy-capture* — a legitimate signal promoted to a terminal goal — which suggests a cross-cutting note worth writing on how several of the fortitude-bloc vices share that exact structure (the sign pursued in place of the thing), and whether that maps onto a single mis-design in the value function the active-inference frame could name.
