# Day 217 — Christ's Knowledge (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.9 asks whether Christ's soul had any *human* knowledge besides the divine, and answers with a threefold scheme: the *beatific* knowledge (the vision of the divine essence), the *infused* or imprinted knowledge, and the *acquired* or empirical knowledge. The opening argument is Aristotelian: "everything is for the sake of operation," so a human intellect "utterly devoid of knowledge" would exist to no purpose, and God does nothing in vain — Christ's human mind must be a real, operative knower. Q.10 then probes the beatific knowledge to its limit: Christ's created soul truly *beholds* the divine essence, knows things in the Word, yet — decisively (a.1) — does *not comprehend* it. "Comprehension means getting one's mental arms around something totally; God is infinite; only God comprehends God." The created finite mind of the incarnate Word knows God "as He is," beholding His essence, yet "could not exhaust, comprehend that essence," for "the infinite is not comprehended by the finite" (Damascene). The contemporary version: *can a finite knower know the whole truly without ever containing it — and is that limitation a defect or the very structure of a created perspective?*

## Problem

The inquiry-problem is the project's own keystone. Aquinas needs the highest possible finite knowledge — a created soul seeing the very essence of God — to remain genuinely *finite*: real vision of the whole, never exhaustive grasp of it. The distinction between *visio* (beholding) and *comprehensio* (exhaustive containing) is exactly the distinction the central theme depends on. The problem: is the non-comprehension a mere *shortfall* (the finite mind would comprehend if only it were bigger), or is it *constitutive* — is being a bounded perspective the very condition of being a created knower at all? This is the metaphysics and epistemology of knowing, where Hoffman's interface theory and Kastrup's analytic idealism do the most work.

## Resource

Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman supplies the leading move. Interface theory (PRS-03) holds that a creature's perception tracks *fitness*, not the whole unvarnished truth — the perceiving agent constructs a serviceable interface, never the totality behind it; and conscious realism (PRS-01) makes consciousness primary while every *finite* agent's grasp of it remains an interface. Read against Q.10, this says: a finite knower can be in genuine contact with what is real (Hoffman's agents really do interface with the underlying conscious reality) and yet never render it exhaustively — the interface is true contact, not total possession. Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup sharpens the "why" (PRS-02): individual minds are *dissociated alters* of one universal consciousness — a real but bounded point of view *within* the whole. To be an alter is precisely to know from within the one mind while not being the whole of it; perspective-boundedness is what makes one an individual knower at all. Wright's critical realism (PRS-01) gives the epistemology its respectable shape: all knowing is perspectival, tradition-embedded, revisable — "always from within a story" — yet stories *test against reality*; knowing-from-a-perspective is not knowing-falsely. Rohr's non-dual register (PRS-01) names the contemplative form of the same point: mature consciousness "perceives unity-in-distinction rather than collapsing into false dichotomies" — it can hold the real-but-not-exhaustive vision without forcing it into either skepticism ("we know nothing of the whole") or hubris ("we contain the whole").

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **a created finite mind can behold the whole truly without ever comprehending it, because perspective-boundedness is constitutive of being a created knower — not a shortfall to be removed by sheer size. Christ's human soul, at the summit of finite knowing, beholds the divine essence really (it is genuine contact with the whole, Hoffman's true interface; a real point of view within the one mind, Kastrup's alter) and yet does not exhaust it (the infinite is not comprehended by the finite). To be a finite perspective at all is to see the whole really yet never to contain it.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps the visio/comprehensio distinction exactly: real vision of the divine essence, no exhaustive grasp. It keeps the reason — the infinite is not comprehended by the finite (Damascene). And it keeps Christ's human mind as a *real, operative* finite knower (the Aristotelian "for the sake of operation"), not a mind bypassed by divine omniscience.

*Where it reframes.* Aquinas treats the non-comprehension as a metaphysical fact about finite-versus-infinite intellect. The contemporary reframe adds that perspective-boundedness is what *individuates* a knower in the first place (Kastrup's alter; Hoffman's interface): the limitation is not only compatible with real knowledge of the whole, it is the condition of being *a* knower of it. The finite mind's vision is true contact, not partial fiction — and its non-comprehension is its very signature as a creature.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The visio/comprehensio claim and the beatific vision itself are theological. Critical realism (perspectival-but-real knowing) is philosophically firm (strong). The identification of perspective-boundedness with individuation (interface, dissociation) is speculative-formal (medium). The central-theme fit is the strongest available: Q.10 is the doctrine of beholding-without-comprehending, which is the project's claim verbatim.

## The central-theme thread

The project holds that agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective, and that when all limitations are removed "the simple whole is seen and every limited perspective is contained in it." Q.10 stations the highest possible finite knower at exactly the boundary the project cares about. Christ's human soul *sees* the simple whole — the divine essence itself — and so the vision is real, not a guess from the outside. And yet, because the soul is finite, it does not *comprehend*: it does not get its arms around the whole. This is the central claim in its purest form: perspective-limitation is constitutive *even at the summit*. There is no created vantage so high that beholding becomes comprehending; the creature who saw the whole exhaustively would no longer be a creature. Hoffman's interface and Kastrup's alter make the same point from below — a finite agent is a true-but-bounded window onto a reality it never contains. The good news folded into the doctrine is that the limitation is not a wall against the whole: the finite mind really *does* behold it. Our working-out "in fear and trembling" is the condition of beings who see truly and partially at once, and who therefore need guidance "from those who perceive more of the whole than we do" — including the One whose human mind beheld the essence from the first instant of His conception.

## Where this leaves us

Q.9-10 give the beatific summit; tomorrow (Day 218, "Christ's Infused Knowledge") the *Summa* descends through the other two modes — the infused/imprinted knowledge (Q.11) and the acquired/empirical knowledge (Q.12), where Christ "advanced in wisdom" and learned through sense like any finite mind. If Q.10 showed that even the highest finite vision does not comprehend, Q.11-12 will show the ordinary finite pathway preserved intact — "broken up knowledge of the world" built piecemeal under bounded resources. The thread to carry: the project should watch whether infused and acquired knowledge are two distinct modes or two ends of one continuum of resource-bounded knowing.
