# Day 149 — Contemporary Parallel: Restitution as Repair, Respect of Persons as the Wrong Variable

## Frame

Q.62 treats *restitution* — the restoration of what injustice has taken. It is "an act of commutative justice," the "arithmetical" justice of strict equality; it is "necessary for salvation," since "unless a man restore what he has stolen, his sin is not forgiven"; the exact amount suffices for a loan, but theft additionally incurs penalty once condemned; for a good one merely *prevented* another from gaining — the neighbor's destroyed seed and its hoped-for harvest — one owes a partial, "virtual" compensation, since "to have a thing virtually is less than to have it actually"; restitution goes to the owner or his heir; and crucially, it binds not only the one who took but everyone who "cooperated" — "by command, counsel, consent, flattery, by receiving, by participation, by silence, by not preventing or by not denouncing." Q.63 treats *respect of persons*, the corruption of *distributive* justice: bestowing goods on the basis of features "that do not amount to a reason why this man be worthy of this gift." Under mind / conscious-realist monism, Q.62 is a conservation-and-repair law for the relational order, and Q.63 is the wrong-variable failure of proportional distribution.

## Problem

Two inquiry-problems, one structure. For restitution: *how is the disturbed balance of the relational order restored — and how far does responsibility for the disturbance extend?* Aquinas treats the imbalance as something that must be made level again; the language is conservational ("render," "restore," "make good"). And he extends culpability strikingly far — to all who command, counsel, consent, or merely fail to prevent or denounce. For respect of persons: *what makes a proportional distribution just rather than corrupt?* The answer turns on relevance. Distributive justice rightly gives unequally — "based on need, dignity, office" — but respect of persons gives unequally on an *irrelevant* basis ("because he is rich or because he is a relative"). The problem is to say precisely what makes a feature relevant to a distribution, and why distributing on the wrong feature is an injustice rather than a mere mistake.

## Resource

For restitution as repair, the leading resource is Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's cancer work. PRS-02 and PRS-11 frame disease as a part's defection from the cooperative whole, and — decisively — frame the *therapy* as restoration: "restoring cells to the body's collective morphogenetic field," re-establishing "bioelectric identity coherence." That is restitution's exact shape. Injustice (Day 147) was defection of the part; restitution (today) is the *re-integration* of the part into the whole — the disturbed coherence made whole again. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work gives the same on the inference side: PRS-06's multi-agent coherence "via mutual modeling" means agents cohere by aligning their models; an injustice de-aligns them, and restitution re-aligns — restoring the shared attractor that makes a collective one agent. Restitution, on both, is a conservation-and-repair operation on a real cooperative system, which is why Aquinas can call it necessary for salvation: on Wright's reading, salvation is inclusion in the people united with God, and restitution is precisely the act that returns the defector to the community's coherence.

For the *virtual-versus-actual* argument (a.4), the resource is a graded ontology of potency. Aquinas's reasoning — a thwarted future harvest is possessed only "virtually or potentially," so its loss is compensated partially, not at full actual value — is Aristotelian act/potency, which Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work (PRS-04, final causality real) keeps live. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's Platonic-space proposal (PRS-16) gives a contemporary cousin: a form not yet actualized is a real but graded target, "with varying degrees of agency" — exactly the ontological status of the harvest-not-yet-grown. The potential good is real enough to owe *something* for, but not actual enough to owe the full value.

For the cooperation network (a.7), the resource is corporate substance. Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work (PRS-14/15) models communities as real formed wholes; if the wrong is done by a *body* of cooperating agents — commanders, counselors, silent bystanders — then culpability is a property distributed across that body, not lodged solely in the principal. Aquinas's long list is a theory of distributed responsibility in a real relational web. For respect of persons (Q.63), the resource is relevance-of-variable. Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's critical realism (PRS-01) grounds the *real* warrant for a distribution: there is a fact of the matter about which feature the good answers to (need, dignity, office), and respect of persons substitutes a feature that does not track it. Tom's PRS-form record of Carroll's poetic naturalism (PRS-01) sharpens the diagnostic vocabulary — distributing on wealth where the relevant level of description is *desert* is a category error, tracking the wrong variable. The wrong is not that the distribution is unequal (distributive justice is rightly unequal) but that the inequality is keyed to the wrong relation.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel: restitution is the **conservation-and-repair law** of the relational order — the disturbed equality made level by returning the part to its right relation with the whole (Levin's re-integration; Friston's re-alignment) — and respect of persons is **distributive injustice as wrong-variable weighting**, a distribution keyed to a feature irrelevant to the relation the good answers to. With Aquinas: restitution restores arithmetical equality and binds the whole network of cooperators; the virtual good is owed partially; distributive justice is rightly unequal but corrupt when keyed to the wrong feature. Where it reframes: Aquinas treats restitution juridically and theologically; the contemporary frame adds the mechanism — repair as re-integration of the part into a real cooperative whole — and reads the cooperation network as distributed culpability in a corporate substance. Where the evidence is firm: re-integration/repair as a real dynamic of cooperative systems (cell biology, strong at scale). Where it stays speculative or theological: that social restitution and biological re-integration are one kind (analogical, marked); the graded ontology of the virtual good (metaphysical, medium); and the salvation-necessity of restitution (theological), which the synthesis carries on Wright's reading rather than reduces.

The measure against Aquinas: no correction, much mechanism. The contemporary frame explains *why* restitution is repair and not mere punishment (it restores the coherence of a real whole), *why* responsibility spreads across cooperators (the wrong is a property of the corporate body), and *why* respect of persons is an injustice and not a faux pas (it weights the wrong variable in a real proportional ordering). It cannot supply the theological premise that withholding restitution bars forgiveness — that the relational order in question includes God, and that re-inclusion in it is salvation — which the synthesis takes from Wright and Rohr and marks as theological.

## The central-theme thread

Restitution is the central claim's repair-mechanism. The claim holds that each limited perspective has its real, proportionate place in the whole, and that the telos is the loving unity in which all are included. Injustice ejects a part from its right relation; restitution returns it. This is why Aquinas can bind restitution to salvation: on Wright's reading salvation *is* inclusion in the people united with God, and to refuse restitution is to refuse re-inclusion — to remain, by one's own act, a defector. Respect of persons is the same insight failing from the distributive side: it fractures the whole by honoring a feature that has nothing to do with the relation the good is meant to serve, sorting persons by wealth or kinship rather than by their real place. Both Q.62 and Q.63 are, at bottom, about honoring and restoring the proportionate place of each perspective in the whole — the practical, resource-bounded labor of "working out salvation in fear and trembling," where the work is repairing the web one's injustices have torn and refusing the false weightings that would tear it further.

## Where this leaves us

Justice has been shown repairing itself (restitution) and failing in its distributive form (respect of persons) — both turning on the proportionate place of each part in a real whole. Tomorrow (Day 150, Q.64–65) Aquinas reaches the vices most directly opposed to commutative justice: *murder* and the other injuries to the person — where the whole-over-part logic that has governed the entire treatise meets its hardest case, the taking of a life, and where the treatise's anthropocentric ranking of beings stands in sharpest tension with a mind-monist account of agency at every scale. The repair-over-excision theme opened here will press directly on that question.
