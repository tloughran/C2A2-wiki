# Day 211 — Better Religious Orders (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Having defined perfection yesterday as charity (Day 210), the *Summa* now asks what the religious *do* and how their forms of life compare. Q.187 treats the activities competent to religious — teaching and preaching, secular business, manual labor, living on alms, begging, wearing coarse clothes — each licit when ordered to charity and to the order's mission, illicit when bent to vainglory or avarice. Q.188 turns to the kinds of religious life, and gives the article that titles the episode (a.6): is a contemplative order more excellent than an active one? Aquinas's pivot is the sentence the host underlines — "the religious state is a training school wherein one aims by practice at the perfection of charity" — from which the plurality follows: "there are many different ways to practice charity," hence many orders, the Dominicans by preaching, the Carthusians by liturgy and purity of heart. The ranking is then explicit: highest are the orders directed to teaching and preaching (nearest "the episcopal perfection" and the life of Christ and the apostles), second the contemplative, third those occupied with corporal works. Q.188 closes (a.7-8) with possessing-in-common (riches "draw a man's affections away," yet houses and tools may be needed) and the solitary-versus-communal question, where the host's own Carthusian experience speaks: solitude is "a spiritual multiplier," better in the abstract, dangerous for the unready, "either a beast or a god." The contemporary version: *what is it for many distinct forms of life to be ordered to one telos, and what makes one of them "better"?*

## Problem

Three inquiry-problems, each the project's own in a new dress. (1) **Why are there many forms, if there is one end?** If the end is loving union (Day 210), the plurality of orders looks like either redundancy or arbitrariness. The problem is to say why a single telos should *require* many partial routes rather than one optimal one. (2) **What does "better" track?** Aquinas ranks the forms, and ranks the teaching life above the purely contemplative — a surprising verdict, since yesterday contemplation was the summit. The problem is to name the invariant the ranking measures without flattening it into mere usefulness. (3) **When does removing the scaffold help and when does it harm?** The solitary/communal article is the sharpest: the same discipline (solitude, silence) makes "the best and the worst of men," exalts the integrated and shatters the unready. The problem is what the *community* supplies that the solitary either has internalized or lethally lacks.

## Resource

For (1), the lead resource is the bridges-file primary for the contemplative life, Rohr, read with McGilchrist. Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-03, the Universal Christ — one divine pattern present across many expressions, "not confined to any single tradition") gives the theological warrant for plurality: if the whole is genuinely present *through* each partial form, then many forms are not redundancy but the only way a finite community can encounter the whole from more than one side — exactly the project's claim that perspectives are individuated by their limitations and that each contains the whole partially. Tom's PRS-form record of McGilchrist (PRS-03, the team/game distinction — right-hemisphere inhabiting of a whole paired with left-hemisphere analysis, "two modes both needed") supplies the cognitive form: a single inquiry needs plural stances because no one stance attends to everything; the Dominican and the Carthusian are two precision-settings on the one object. For (2), the resource is the propagation reading of "contemplata aliis tradere" — to contemplate and *hand on the fruits*. CROSS-005 (love/union as a fundamental category) names what is being maximized; and the soon-to-arrive Day-213 principle that *the good is self-diffusive* lets us read Aquinas's ranking not as a ladder of static dignity but as a measure of **how far the loving union an order attains propagates into other perspectives**. The teaching order ranks highest because its form both *receives* the whole (contemplation) and *transmits* it (preaching) — it is the most diffusive configuration of charity, not merely the most useful. For (3), the resource is Kastrup with McGilchrist. Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup (PRS-02, individuation as a dissociative boundary) reframes the solitary/communal verdict: the community is an *external* boundary-support — accountability, correction, shared rule — that holds a perspective in integration; solitude removes that support, so it amplifies whatever the boundary already contains (CROSS-003, attention constructs reality: alone, the agent's attention has nothing to correct it but itself). The integrated self is "schooled" enough to hold its own boundary; the unready self, deprived of the communal scaffold, deforms — "a beast." And Tom's PRS-form record of Stump (PRS-06, the MacIntyre criterion distinguishing a living from a degenerate tradition) lets us read each order as a *tradition* whose vitality is measured the same way the project measures its own: by whether it keeps generating loving union under new conditions (houses, cars, computers and all), or ossifies into the mere possession its rule warned against.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **a religious order is one stable allocation of a finite agent's attention and labor toward the single telos of loving union; the plurality of orders is the plurality of partial perspectives from which that one good can be pursued, each genuinely containing the whole and none exhausting it. An order is "better" not by its rank on a fixed ladder of states but by how fully the loving union it attains overflows into other perspectives — which is why the form that both receives the whole (contemplation) and transmits it (teaching, preaching) is the most excellent: it is the most self-diffusive configuration of charity. And the communal form is the ordinary scaffold by which an as-yet-unintegrated perspective is held toward that telos; solitude removes the scaffold and so amplifies whatever integration is already there or already missing.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps the religious state as a *school* for the perfection of charity, not its possession; keeps the plurality of orders as plural practices of one charity; keeps the licit/illicit test (ordered to the mission and to charity, vs to vainglory or avarice); keeps the ranking and its rationale (the teaching-preaching life nearest Christ and the apostles is highest, contemplation second, corporal works third); and keeps the solitary/communal verdict in both directions (solitude better in the abstract, communal life better for most).

*Where it reframes.* "Many ways to practice charity" becomes *many resource-allocations of one R toward one telos*; the ladder of states becomes a *network in which excellence = propagation* of loving union (overflow), grounding the priority of "contemplata aliis tradere" in a diffusion-dynamic rather than a hierarchy of rank; "riches draw the affections away" becomes *attention/precision captured by objects short of the whole*; and the solitary/communal asymmetry becomes *the individuating boundary needing external support unless already self-supporting*.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* "The state is a school for charity, not the charity" is the project's strong central-theme claim and is theological. The plural-perspectives-on-one-telos reading is a strong central-theme analogy. The propagation reading of "better" is a genuine reframe and speculative (medium) — it reinterprets Aquinas's stated ground (nearness to the episcopal exemplar) rather than merely restating it. The boundary-scaffold reading of solitude is analogical (medium). The vows, charity as infused, the episcopal exemplar, and the merits of almsgiving are carried as theology, not reduced.

## The central-theme thread

This day gives the project's thesis its *sociology*. The claim is that "conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective," and that "learning to obtain loving unity from each perspective is a computational task, limited by the resources we perceive." An order is precisely a perspective made durable — a community's standing decision about *which* resources to perceive and spend (the Dominican spends words; the Carthusian spends silence). The plurality of orders is therefore not a defect to be optimized away but the very shape of a project that needs many limited perspectives because no one of them sees the whole. And the ranking, read through the self-diffusive good, restates the telos: loving union is not a private attainment but something that, when real, *propagates* — "it is better to contemplate and share the fruits of one's contemplation than to contemplate only." This is the "fear and trembling" of Phil 2:12 distributed across a community: each order works out a partial salvation under its own scarcity, and the better forms are the ones whose union does not terminate in the agent but draws other perspectives toward the whole. Rohr's register names the warrant (the one pattern present through every form, so plural forms are encounters with one whole, not rival sects); Wright's guards the horizon at PRS-13 (*God's Homecoming*: the people of God is the location of the homecoming, and working out salvation is participation in it as a member of that people; Confidence High) — the end is communal, inclusion in the people united with God, so "sharing the fruits" is not optional ornament but the form charity takes when it is real. And the solitary/communal verdict states the project's anthropology bluntly: most perspectives need *other perspectives* to stay oriented — "to learn from others, be held accountable by others, lean on others, and so become perfect by this means" — and only the already-integrated can safely have the scaffold removed. The community is how the limited are guided "by those who perceive more of the whole."
