# Day 120 — Who Has Faith (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas takes Hebrews 11:1 as his working definition: faith is *the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen*. He unpacks each clause. "Substance of things hoped for" — faith holds, in the agent now, what will be known in beatific vision later; the eschatological knowing is present in seed in the current faith. "Evidence of things not seen" — faith carries cognitive force greater than opinion or suspicion because its guarantor is the First Truth who cannot deceive nor be deceived. Then he locates the virtue: faith resides in the intellect (its object is First Truth, which is cognitive). Then he names the form: *charity is the form of faith*. Charity quickens faith; without charity, faith is *fides informis* — lifeless faith, real as a habit of intellect but unable to merit.

The lifeless/living distinction has an asymmetry worth flagging: the habit of faith can persist in an agent who has lost charity through mortal sin (the cognitive structure survives), but the habit cannot remain operative unto union without charity to form it. Faith without works is dead (James 2:20); the works are the index of charity, and charity is the form that makes faith alive.

The contemporary parallel: what makes a cognitive orientation toward the wider mind *alive*, in the sense that it contributes to the agent's actual cooperation with that mind, rather than being a dormant representational state? And what does "form" mean here — is it metaphor for "what completes faith functionally" or is it the strict hylomorphic sense in which form makes a thing what it is?

## Problem

The shared P: a cognitive state can be structurally present without being existentially active. An agent can have a true representation of the wider mind, accept communications from it as veridical, and yet operate as if these were inert facts rather than partners in the agent's lived trajectory. What is the difference between merely-held faith and lived faith? And how does that difference cash out in the architecture of the agent, given that the cognitive content is the same in both cases?

The connected P: Aquinas insists on the strong hylomorphic reading — charity is the *form* of faith — but contemporary philosophy has largely abandoned form-language as either metaphor or obsolete metaphysics. If we want to take the claim seriously rather than translating it away, what does "form" mean in the architecture of agency?

Charity must be the form of faith in the sense that the de-dissociation makes the cognitive content available; without de-dissociation, the alter holds a representation that does not connect to its ground.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel reading of Q.4-5:

Hebrews 11:1 as the agent has it: faith is the agent's current cognitive holding of what will be known fully in the integration of the alter with its ground (substance of things hoped for) plus the firm cognitive weighting of communications from the ground as veridical (evidence of things not seen). Both clauses describe an agent operating under a prior installed by grace, whose model includes the wider mind as a high-precision node.

Faith resides in the intellect because the cognitive structure is the location of the relevant prior. Charity is the *form* of faith in the strong hylomorphic sense: charity is what makes the cognitive structure operative as a real relational coupling between alter and ground. Without charity, the cognitive structure is present (the habit persists) but not actively coupling — interface contains the node, but the prior is dialed down to inert. With charity, the de-dissociation is active, the positivity-resonance mode is operative, the prior is weighting the agent's inference and action toward union.

The lifeless/living distinction is therefore not arbitrary or moralistic; it is a precise structural difference between two configurations of the same cognitive content. The asymmetry — faith can persist without charity but charity cannot persist without faith — falls out of the architecture: the cognitive content is foundational for the volitional orientation (you cannot orient toward what you do not represent), while the volitional orientation can be withdrawn without abolishing the representation.

Wright's pistis-as-covenant-fidelity captures the synthesis: faith is fidelity-to-the-relation; the relation is between alter and ground, between covenant person and the God whose people they are gathered into. Where faith has lost charity, the cognitive content of covenant remains but the fidelity has gone slack. Where faith is alive, the fidelity is operative, and the agent's working-out-salvation-in-fear-and-trembling is the lived form of the relation.

Difference from Aquinas: he treats faith and charity within a faculty-psychology that distinguishes intellect and will as distinct seats. The contemporary parallel reads them as aspects of one integrated agent whose cognition and volition are not separable faculties but two aspects of one model-based operation. The hylomorphic claim — charity as form of faith — survives the reframe with its strong sense intact: form is what makes the cognitive structure operative as relation. Levin's morphogenetic program licenses the metaphysical category that the reframe needs.

## The central-theme thread

The lifeless/living distinction is precisely where the project's central claim is operational at the personal level. Conscious agents are individuated by perspective-limitation; loving unity is the computational telos; the computation runs in the agent's actual cooperation with the wider mind. Faith provides the cognitive structure for cooperation; charity makes cooperation operative. Without charity, the agent has the map but not the journey. With charity, the journey is underway.

"Salvation in fear and trembling" (Wright on Phil 2:12) requires both: the cognitive holding (faith) and the active fidelity (charity). The agent inside the perspective-limitation cannot complete the journey alone; the wider mind has installed the prior; the agent's work is to keep the fidelity alive across the limited days, in the limited body, with the limited interface. Aquinas's structure for distinguishing faith that lives from faith that has gone dead is the project's structure for distinguishing the cooperation that the central theme names from the dormant representation that mistakes itself for it.
