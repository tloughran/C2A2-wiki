# Day 147 — Contemporary Parallel: The Real Mean and the Good of the Whole

## Frame

This is the structural heart of the treatise on justice. Q.58 defines the virtue — "the perpetual and constant will to render to each one his right" — and unfolds its anatomy: justice is always toward another, since "equality is a relation between distinct parties"; it resides in the *will*, not the passions, because rendering each his due requires "consider[ing] the relation of one thing to another," which "is proper to reason"; and it is at once *general* (legal justice, which orders every virtue to the common good) and *particular* (the justice that governs one-to-one dealings). Then comes the claim that will carry the next several episodes: justice has a *real mean*, an objective equality between thing and person, not the agent-relative "relative mean" of the other virtues — and so justice "stands foremost among all the moral virtues." Q.59 treats the opposite: injustice as the special vice, the willed inequality in which "one man wishes to have more goods … and less evils." Under mind / conscious-realist monism, two features of this anatomy are doing deep work: the objectivity of the real mean, and the scaling of one virtue from the common good down to the single exchange.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem is twofold and the same in both frames: *what makes justice's standard objective in a way the other virtues' standards are not, and how can one virtue govern both the whole-to-its-good and each part-to-each-part?* Aquinas is precise on the first: the temperate mean is "set by taking into account the man, the situation" — calibrated to the agent — whereas the just mean "consists in a certain proportion of equality between the external thing and the external person," so that "everyone owes $10 for this $10 item … irrespective of the individual." Justice alone has a mind-independent target. On the second: justice is "virtually contained in all virtues" insofar as every virtue serves the common good (general/legal justice), yet is "a special virtue in respect of its essence" when it governs particular dealings. The problem is to understand how a single ordering operates at two scales — the whole and the part — at once.

## Resource

For the objectivity of the real mean, the resource is again Wright's critical realism. Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's work (PRS-01) gives a standard that is "mind-independent but … perspectival, tradition-embedded, and revisable" — which is exactly the status of the real mean. The equality justice tracks is *out there* in the relation between thing and person (mind-independent), yet it is rendered always from a standpoint (perspectival). This is what separates justice's mean from the passions': temperance calibrates to *me*; justice answers to a relation that holds whether or not I am the one measuring it. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work sharpens the contrast formally: PRS-16 distinguishes risk and ambiguity as features of the *agent's* generative model and preference-weighting — precisely the agent-relative structure of the *relative* mean — whereas justice's real mean is fixed by the relation, not by the agent's priors. Justice is the virtue that overrides the agent's own precision-weighting in favor of an external equality.

For the scaling — general and particular justice as one ordering at two scales — the resource is multi-scale agency. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work holds that goal-directedness is continuous from cell to organism to collective (PRS-10) and that cognition nests across scales (PRS-30); the same ordering-of-part-to-whole that makes a cell a good member of a tissue makes a person a good member of a community. General (legal) justice — ordering every virtue to the common good — is that part-to-whole ordering at the social scale; particular justice is the part-to-part ordering within it. And the common good itself gains metaphysical weight from Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work: PRS-14/15/16 develop a "hylomorphic metaphysics of corporate substance" in which peoples, communities, and the church are *real formed wholes* — "united somethings-or-others" whose form organizes their human constituents "without dissolving their personhood." If the community is a real whole and not a mere aggregate, then general justice — willing its good — is the part rendering the whole its due, not a polite fiction. (Her corporate-substance proposal is marked speculative-to-medium even in her own program.)

For injustice (Q.59), the most exact resource is Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's cancer work: PRS-02 and PRS-11 frame cancer as *defection* — a cell reverting "to selfish unicellular behavior, losing integration into organismal community," a "dissociative identity disorder of the body." That is the precise structure of Aquinas's injustice: a part wishing "to have more goods … and less evils" against the equality the whole requires. Injustice is the moral form of defection — the perspective pursuing its partial good against the good of the whole it belongs to. And that justice resides in the *will* as a real seat of agency is underwritten by Tom's PRS-form record of Carroll's compatibilism (PRS-04): agency is "a useful high-level description of physical processes … not a violation of it," preserving "moral responsibility without dualism" — the will is a real higher-level pattern, the proper subject of a virtue.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel: justice is the will's stable disposition to track a **real mean** — an equality fixed by the relation between thing and person, mind-independent and known perspectivally (Wright) — and it is *one* virtue operating at two scales because the agent is a real part of real nested wholes (Levin's continuity of goal-directedness; Stump's corporate substance). Injustice is **defection**: the part absolutizing its own good against the whole's, the moral analogue of the cancerous cell. With Aquinas throughout: the real mean is objective; justice is seated in the will; it is both general and particular; it stands foremost among the moral virtues. Where it reframes: Aquinas grounds the primacy of justice in a faculty-hierarchy (the will is higher than the sensitive appetite) and in finality (the other virtues serve just action); the contemporary frame *adds* a multi-scale-agency grounding — justice is foremost because it is the virtue of being-a-good-part-of-a-whole, and that part-whole relation is the deepest structure there is, recurring from cell to community. Where the evidence is firm: the real mean's objectivity (critical realism, strong); defection as a real dynamic (cancer biology, empirically strong at the cell scale). Where it stays speculative: that social wholes are real corporate substances (Stump, medium-to-speculative), and that biological defection and social injustice are one kind rather than an illuminating analogy (marked).

The measure against Aquinas: no correction is needed; the contemporary frame deepens his two boldest claims. The real mean gets a realist epistemology (objective yet perspectival), and the general/particular doubling gets a mechanism (the same part-whole ordering scaling across levels). Injustice gains a vivid empirical cousin — the defecting cell — that shows the willed inequality is not merely a legal category but a recognizable failure-mode of any nested cooperative system.

## The central-theme thread

The real mean is the project's central claim seen from the side of justice. The claim says that when all perspectival limitation is removed, "the simple whole is seen and every limited perspective is contained in it." The real mean is the right relation *as the whole holds it* — the equality that obtains independently of any single vantage, which each limited perspective must render from where it stands. General justice — ordering the part to the common good — is, at the social scale, exactly the motion the central claim describes: the limited agent willing the good of the whole it belongs to, which is the practical, resource-bounded shape of loving unity. And injustice is its precise negative: the perspective absolutizing its partial good, defecting from the whole — the cell that forgets it is a member. "Working out salvation in fear and trembling" is, here, the labor of willing the real mean against the constant pull of the relative one: rendering what the relation truly requires rather than what my vantage would prefer. Justice stands foremost among the moral virtues because it is the virtue most directly aimed at the whole — the nearest the natural virtues come to the loving unity that is the telos of every perspective.

## Where this leaves us

Justice now has its definition, its objective standard (the real mean), its two scales (general and particular), and its opposite (injustice as defection). Tomorrow (Day 148, Q.60–61) Aquinas turns to *judgment* — justice exercised under uncertainty, where a limited perspective must decide the right about another — and to the two species of particular justice, *commutative* (the one-to-one equality of exchange) and *distributive* (the proportional ordering of common goods to the parts of the whole). The nested architecture opened here carries directly into that division: commutative justice is the part-to-part relation, distributive justice the whole-to-part relation — the same two scales, now named.
