# Day 175 — Affability & Flattery (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas's Q.114 treats friendliness or affability (*amicitia* in the limited civic sense): the social virtue of behaving agreeably and fittingly toward those one lives among. It is a part of justice — a real debt of pleasant, becoming conduct owed in the ordinary commerce of life, distinct from the love of friendship proper. Q.115 treats flattery (*adulatio*), its excess: pleasing others beyond the bounds of truth, especially for the sake of gain or mere approval. (Its defect — being disagreeable or quarrelsome — Aquinas handles next, under Q.116, in tomorrow's episode.)

The contemporary version, under mind-monism, completes the signal-integrity arc at the social surface: what is the right texture of everyday interaction among cooperating agents, and how is it corrupted? Affability turns out to name the empirically real micro-mechanism by which warmth builds the bonds a cooperative community runs on; flattery is its counterfeit.

## Problem

A community of limited agents is not held together only by justice in the strict sense; it is held together, moment to moment, by the small warmth of ordinary interaction. Too little of it — coldness, abrasiveness — starves the bonds the community needs. But warmth can also be faked: simulated agreeableness aimed at advantage, which feeds the other's model false positive data and instrumentalizes the relationship. The problem is sustaining genuine warmth — the kind that actually builds connection — without sliding into the performed warmth that degrades it.

## Resource

**Fredrickson on positivity resonance** carries most of the contemporary weight here, and the fit is close to exact. Her program identifies the micro-moments of shared positive affect — warmth, attention, mutual care in ordinary encounter — as the empirically measurable building blocks of social connection, with downstream effects on health, resilience, and the durability of bonds. Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson's work names this resonance as the mechanism by which fleeting warm exchanges become enduring relational capital. Affability, in Aquinas's sense, just *is* the disposition to generate these micro-moments fittingly. The medieval "debt of pleasant conduct" and the contemporary "positivity resonance that builds social resources" describe the same thing from the normative and the empirical sides.

**Friston on multi-agent coherence through mutual modeling** (PRS-06 in the Friston-tradition wiki) diagnoses the corruption. That triplet has agents cohere by reducing prediction error in each other's behavior — coupling through mutual model-building — and names love as the mechanism by which they synchronize models into a collective agent. Genuine affability is that coupling working: honest warmth, a signal that indexes real regard. Flattery decouples the warm signal from the regard, deploying it to manipulate the other's self-model for the flatterer's gain. As with lying and boasting (Days 173–174), the wrong is the corruption of signal-integrity; here it is specifically the corruption of the *warmth* signal, which is doubly damaging because warmth is the very currency of bond-building. **McGilchrist** (PRS-01) gives the cognitive contrast: relational attunement (genuinely meeting the other) versus the manipulative mode that simulates attunement to extract advantage.

**Stump** enters here: affability as a genuine, if limited, mode of second-personal regard; flattery as its instrumentalization — treating the other as a means while wearing the face of friendship.

**Wright** grounds the scriptural form: "Let love be genuine" (Rom 12:9) — *anhypokritos*, literally un-hypocritical love — and the recurring Pauline charge to build one another up (1 Thess 5:11). The community of the new creation is bound by sincere warmth, and flattery is its hypocritical counterfeit. **Rohr** gives the contemplative form: authentic loving-kindness flows from the True Self that has nothing to gain, while flattery is the False Self's people-pleasing — warmth deployed to secure approval rather than offered freely.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: affability is the disposition to generate honest warmth — positivity resonance — in ordinary interaction, building the bonds a cooperative community depends on; flattery is the corruption of that warmth into a manipulative signal. This preserves Aquinas's account of affability as a real social debt and flattery as its excess. It reframes the pair by identifying affability's empirical substrate (positivity resonance) and locating flattery's wrong in honest-signaling terms — the corruption of the very signal that builds connection. Evidence: positivity resonance is empirical; the honest-signaling account of flattery is formal; sincere-love-in-community is theological.

What it shares with Aquinas: affability as owed, fitting, real warmth; flattery as pleasing-beyond-truth for gain. What it reframes: the grounding shifts from a debt within the order of social justice to the bond-building dynamics of warmth among agents — which gives the virtue an empirical mechanism and the vice a precise diagnosis as warmth-signal corruption.

## Central-theme thread

Loving unity is not only computed; it is *lived* among perspective-limited agents, and the texture of that life is warmth. Affability is the small, daily form of the love toward which the whole computation tends — the micro-moments in which agents actually meet. Flattery counterfeits that meeting: it offers the appearance of warmth while withholding the regard, and so isolates the flatterer behind a performance even as it pretends connection. To work out salvation in fear and trembling within a community is, in part, to keep one's warmth honest — to let love be genuine — so that the bonds being built are real and the agents are actually being drawn into one another rather than managed.
