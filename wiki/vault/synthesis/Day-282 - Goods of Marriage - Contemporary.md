# Day 282 — Goods of Marriage (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.49–50 name the three *goods* (bona) that justify and structure marriage: **offspring** (proles), **fidelity** (fides), and **sacrament** (sacramentum, the indissoluble bond signifying Christ and the Church). For Aquinas these goods are what marriage is *for* — its ends, in ascending order, the last of which (the sacramental bond) makes Christian marriage not merely a natural good but a sign of a supernatural one. (The episode's ASR mislabels the locus as "45 and 50"; the questions are Suppl. Q.49–50.) The contemporary version: **what does it mean to specify the telos of a higher-order union — the goal toward which the joined unit is ordered — and can a dyad have genuine ends of its own?**

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **the reality of goal-directedness at the level of the union**. Aquinas treats the goods as ends *of the marriage*, not merely of the two individuals — the union itself is ordered toward offspring, fidelity, and sign. The contemporary sciences of life face precisely the question of whether a higher-order individual has goals that are *its own*, not just the summed preferences of its parts. If a marriage is a real higher unit (Day 278), then naming its goods is naming *its* telos — and that requires that collective-level goal-directedness be a real feature of the world, not a projection.

## Resource

The Levin-tradition program is the leading resource. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work (PRS-01, *morphogenetic control / target morphology*; PRS-32, *goal-directed cognition scaling from cells to organisms*) establishes that higher-order biological individuals possess *genuine* target states — an organism pursues a morphology no cell represents, correcting perturbations toward *the whole's* goal. This is the empirical warrant for saying a union can have ends proper to it: goal-directedness is real at the collective level, demonstrated in regeneration and morphogenesis. Mapped onto marriage, the three goods read as a layered target state of the dyadic individual: *proles* as the union's generativity (its capacity to produce new individuals, as organisms do), *fides* as the integrity-maintenance of the unit (the fidelity that keeps the higher individual coherent against defection), and *sacramentum* as the union's orientation beyond itself toward the whole. The Friston-tradition program formalizes the goal: PRS-16 (*expected free energy as the unified objective*) treats an agent's "preferred outcomes" as priors the system acts to realize — the goods are the dyad's preferred-outcome priors. Theologically, PRS-04 in the Rohr-tradition wiki (*embodied love as epistemology*) reads *fides* and *proles* as love made bodily and fruitful, and PRS-03 (*the Universal Christ*) reads *sacramentum* as the union's participation in the cosmic sign; Wright's inaugurated eschatology (PRS-03) reads the sacramental good as new-creation sign rather than legal indissolubility alone.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the three goods name the real telos of the marital higher-individual — its generativity, its integrity, and its self-transcending orientation — and collective-level goal-directedness, demonstrated in biology, is what makes this more than a figure of speech.** What it *shares* with Aquinas: that the goods are ends of the union itself and ascend toward the sacramental. Where it *reframes*: Aquinas grounds the goods in natural law plus revelation; the monist reframe grounds the *possibility* of union-level ends in demonstrated multiscale goal-directedness, then lets theology specify *which* ends are proper to the sacramental case. Where evidence is *firm*: goal-directedness at the level of a higher individual is empirically established in morphogenesis. Where *speculative*: that a marriage's goods are goals in the *same* sense as an organism's target morphology is analogical — human partners can consciously revise the union's ends in ways cells cannot, so the parallel illuminates without identifying.

## The central-theme thread

The project's telos is loving unity computed by limited agents. The goods of marriage are that telos specified for the dyad: *fides* is the agents' commitment to compute together despite partiality; *proles* is the union's overflow into new perspectives; *sacramentum* is the union's confessed orientation toward the larger whole in which all perspectives are held. The ascending order of the goods is the central theme's own arc — from cooperation, through faithful integrity, to participation in the unity that exceeds the pair.

## Bridges

- **Levin PRS-01 / PRS-32 (empirical at biological scale, analogical for the dyad):** higher individuals have real target states; warrant for union-level ends.
- **Friston PRS-16 (formal):** preferred-outcome priors as the formal shape of "goods"/ends.
- **Rohr PRS-04 / PRS-03 (theological):** fides/proles as embodied fruitful love; sacramentum as cosmic-Christ sign.
- **Wright PRS-03 (scriptural/theological):** the sacramental good as new-creation sign, not bare indissolubility.

## Where this leaves us

Q.51–52 (tomorrow) shift from the goods to the *impediments of error and condition* — what can corrupt the consent that aims at these goods. Having named what the union is for, we turn to what can falsify the act that pursues it. Open thread: the disanalogy that partners can *revise* their union's ends (whereas cells cannot revise the body's) deserves its own treatment — it is where freedom re-enters the teleology.
