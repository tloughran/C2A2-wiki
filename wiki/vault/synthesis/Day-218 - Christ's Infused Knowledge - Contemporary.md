# Day 218 — Christ's Infused Knowledge (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.11 treats the *infused* or imprinted knowledge of Christ's soul: knowledge given directly to the soul rather than abstracted from the senses. Did He by it know all things (a.1)? Could He use it without turning to phantasms (a.2)? Was it discursive — reasoned step by step — or held all at once (a.4)? Was it distinguished by diverse habits (a.6)? On a.6 Aquinas draws the contrast that organizes the whole question: habits are distinguished by their objects, and "for God, in knowing Himself, He also knows everything else, whereas for us, when I know what a dog is, I don't also know what an angel is... We do not have a universal sweeping knowledge of things. We have a broken up knowledge of the world." Q.12 then treats the *acquired* or empirical knowledge: did Christ know all by it (a.1), did He *advance* in it (a.2), did He learn from men or angels (a.3)? Aquinas affirms a real acquired knowledge — Christ genuinely learned through sense and "advanced in wisdom and age and grace" (Luke 2:52) — even as He also possessed infused knowledge. The contemporary version: *why does a mind that has knowledge given from above also build knowledge piecemeal from below, under the finite resources of sense — and what is that "broken up" knowing?*

## Problem

The inquiry-problem is the epistemology of *limited resources*. Aquinas sets two modes side by side: a *sweeping* knowledge (God's, in which knowing oneself is knowing all) and a *broken up* knowledge (ours, assembled object by object). Christ has the infused mode and *also* the acquired mode; He really advances. The problem: what is the relation between knowledge possessed-as-a-whole and knowledge built-piecemeal — and why would the second be preserved in a soul that has the first? In contemporary terms: why is the default mode of any finite mind a *piecemeal, resource-bounded* construction of its world, never the single sweep — and what does it mean that this limited pathway is honored even where a fuller knowledge is given? This is the locus the bridges file maps (I Q.84-89, "how the soul knows") to Hoffman, Hawkins, and Friston.

## Resource

The leading move is Tom's PRS-form record of Friston. The free-energy principle (PRS-01) casts every living system as minimizing prediction error — *variational free energy* — to maintain its order against entropy; PRS-04 (the organism as active modeler) recasts the knower as "a continuous hypothesis-testing system" that "generates predictions, tests them against sensory input, and updates models." This is precisely *acquired, empirical, piecemeal* knowing: a finite agent does not receive the world whole but builds a serviceable model of it, object by object, under bounded resources — "a broken up knowledge of the world" assembled by error-correction. "Advancing in wisdom" (Luke 2:52) is, in this register, the very signature of a real limited perspective: a mind that *updates*. The bridges file (I Q.84-89) routes the same locus through Hawkins (the cortex builds reference frames / generative models — "broken up" knowledge constructed frame by frame) and Hoffman (the senses are a fitness-tuned interface; the phantasm-pathway constructs a serviceable world-model, not the totality, PRS-03). Wright's critical realism (PRS-01) keeps this piecemeal knowing from sliding into mere subjectivism: knowledge built under perspective and revised over time is still *knowledge*, tested against reality — the revisability is a feature of finitude, not a failure of contact.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the "broken up knowledge of the world," acquired piecemeal under the finite resources of sense, is the default mode of any limited perspective — bounded-rational active inference, a mind building and updating a world-model by minimizing prediction error, never the single sweep in which knowing oneself is knowing all. Christ's real acquired knowledge, and His genuine advancing in wisdom, honor that limited pathway from within: the assumed humanity does not bypass the finite mode of knowing but inhabits it, even while also possessing knowledge given from above.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps the two modes distinct — the sweeping (God's) and the piecemeal (ours) — and keeps Christ's acquired knowledge *real*: He learned through sense and truly advanced (Luke 2:52). It keeps the infused mode as a genuine second mode, not collapsed into the acquired.

*Where it reframes.* Aquinas distinguishes the modes by their metaphysical *habits*; the contemporary reframe identifies the acquired mode with the *default agent-under-scarcity mode* (Friston): the piecemeal pathway is what knowing *is* for any finite system maintaining itself under limited resources. "Broken up knowledge" becomes bounded-rational model-building; "advancing in wisdom" becomes the update signature of a real limited perspective. The infused mode, on this reading, is the limit-case of knowledge *given* rather than *earned* — but the striking datum is that even its possessor still really learns.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* Active inference as an account of empirical learning is empirically and formally firm (strong). The identification of "broken up knowledge" with bounded-rational inference is a strong analogy. That Christ possessed infused knowledge *and* truly acquired knowledge and advanced is theological. The two-habits metaphysics (Q.11 a.6) is theological/metaphysical. The central-theme fit is strong: piecemeal knowing under scarcity *is* perspective-limitation as an epistemology.

## The central-theme thread

The project's claim is that limited agents compute toward the whole "from our own perspective," "limited by the resources we perceive." Q.11 a.6 states that limitation as an epistemology in a single sentence: we have no "universal sweeping knowledge" but "a broken up knowledge of the world." To know what a dog is is *not* thereby to know what an angel is; each object is won separately, under the cost of attention and sense. Friston gives this a mechanism — a finite mind minimizes prediction error, building its model in pieces, never holding the whole at once — and shows that this piecemeal mode is not a malfunction but the very form of finite knowing under scarcity. The arresting move in Q.12 is that *Christ inhabits this mode too*: He "advanced in wisdom," really learning through sense, even though He also possessed infused knowledge and the beatific vision (Day 217). The one who beholds the whole still walks the road of piecemeal learning. That is the same structure the project keeps finding in these Christological questions: the comprehensor who does not refuse the wayfarer's limited pathway but takes it on. Our "fear and trembling" includes the slow, error-correcting, object-by-object character of finite knowing — and the assumed humanity does not skip it.
