# Day 140 — Contemporary Parallel: Knowing the Whole by Becoming Like It

## Frame

The batch closes where it has been heading. After the disorders of the social field — envy, discord, contention, schism, war, strife, sedition, scandal — Aquinas turns to the *precepts* of charity (Q.44) and to the *gift of wisdom* (Q.45), the highest of the Holy Spirit's gifts. Two of his moves here are, for this project, the most important in the whole charity treatise. First, in Q.44 a.2, he argues that one who truly understood the love of God would *see* that it entails love of neighbor, "as one who really understood the principle of sufficient reason would see that it entails an uncaused, perfect being." Second, in Q.45, he defines the gift of wisdom as judging divine things *by connaturality* — "by a kind of similarity, by being like God himself" — and crowns it with the seventh beatitude: "it belongs to charity to be at peace, but to wisdom to make peace by setting things in order," for peace is "the tranquility of order." Under mind / conscious-realist monism, these are not pious flourishes; they are, almost verbatim, the central claim this project has been exploring since Day 1.

## Problem

Two inquiry-problems, tightly linked. First, *how is the highest knowledge of God possible at all?* Faith assents to divine truth; but wisdom, Aquinas says, *judges by* it — and judges rightly about "the highest cause," God, "who transcends category altogether." How can a finite intellect judge rightly about a whole it cannot survey? Aquinas's answer is connaturality: not inference from premises but a *likeness* to the object, such that the knower judges "as the chaste man judges rightly about chastity" — from the inside, by being what he judges. The problem is to say what kind of knowing *that* is, and how a limited perspective could ever come to have it.

Second, *what is the relation between love of God and love of neighbor, and why do both reduce to a single order?* Aquinas's striking claim (a.2) is that they are not two unrelated commands but one, related as principle to consequence: "love unites affections," so to love God is already, implicitly, to love what God loves — the neighbor. The two precepts are spelled out separately only "for the less intelligent, who do not easily understand that one is included in the other." And the order of charity (a.8) — one's own soul, then the neighbor's soul, then one's own body, then the nearer and holier neighbors — is "included in the precept": love is structured, ranked, finite. The problem is to say why love has *this* shape: unifying at the top, ordered and bounded below.

## Resource

For wisdom-by-connaturality, the leading resources are Levin, Hoffman, and Kastrup, because this is the most metaphysical locus in the treatise. Begin with the sufficient-reason argument (a.2). Aquinas reasons from "everything that exists must have an explanation" to "an uncaused, perfect existence itself." Under mind-monism the conclusion is reframed rather than rejected: the "highest cause" that "transcends category" is the ontological primitive, and on Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup's work (PRS-01 in the Kastrup-tradition wiki) that primitive is *mind* — consciousness as fundamental, matter as what experience looks like from outside. On Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman's work (PRS-01/02), the question "what is fundamental *beneath* the interface" is the same question, and the answer is not spacetime but the network of conscious agents the interface hides. So the "fundamental cause" wisdom knows is, on this frame, mind-at-large.

And now connaturality becomes intelligible without remainder. Aquinas says wisdom judges divine things "by being like God himself," an effect "of grace and charity." On Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup's account of identity within monism (PRS-02), the finite agent is a *dissociated alter* of the one mind — a perspective that is, at bottom, *of* the very whole it is trying to know. To be "made like" God through grace is then to *recover, partially, one's participation* in the whole one already belongs to; and a perspective that participates in the whole can judge *by* it, not merely *about* it. This is knowing-by-being rather than knowing-by-representation — the move from dualistic to non-dual knowing in Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-01 in the Rohr-tradition wiki, Confidence High). It is worth being exact about where that leaves the argument, because the wiki records a live disagreement at just this point rather than a settled result. PRS-13 in the McGilchrist-tradition wiki is not a shared doctrine of participatory knowing; it *is* the dispute — McGilchrist insisting perception is genuine encounter with a field of consciousness, Kastrup retaining a representational model, the dashboard read from behind the dissociative boundary. This commentary has leaned on both: Kastrup's dissociated alter supplies the ontology that makes connaturality possible at all, and McGilchrist's participatory side supplies the *kind* of knowing connaturality is. That combination is a position, not a citation, and the register does not license treating the two as agreeing. Aquinas himself is unambiguously on the participatory side — the chaste man judges chastity from the inside, not from a dial.

Rohr's embodied-love epistemology (PRS-04, Confidence Medium) names the *organ* of connatural knowing, though the record is more specific than "love": love as a biophysiological event, with **the body** as the site of knowing, explicitly linked there to Fredrickson's positivity resonance. That is the incarnational form of the claim, and it is why Aquinas makes wisdom *presuppose charity* (a.4: no gift of wisdom without grace). One knows the whole by loving it, because love is the coupling that makes the knower like the known — and on this record the coupling is enacted in bodies, not only in intentions.

For peace as "tranquility of order," the resource is the formal account of the ordered state. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work treats the agent's well-functioning as the minimization of free energy — the settling of an agent (and, in the multi-agent case, PRS-06, a collective) into its low-surprise, well-ordered attractor. "Tranquility of order" is a remarkably exact gloss: peace is the state of an agent and a world *set right*, where prediction and reality, part and whole, are reconciled. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work gives the same picture in morphogenetic terms: the target form (PRS-01) is the order toward which the collective of cells computes, the attractor (PRS-03) that makes a heap of cells one ordered body. Aquinas's claim — "it belongs to charity to be at peace, but to wisdom to *make* peace by setting things in order" — distinguishes the bond (charity) from the *ordering computation* (wisdom) in just the terms the project has used: charity couples the agents; wisdom orders the whole they form toward its target. "Blessed are the peacemakers" names the agent that runs that computation.

For the unity of the two precepts, the resource is the non-zero-sum coupling already established. "Love unites affections" (a.2) is, on Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-06), the synchronization of models by which agents "become a coherent collective agent"; to love God (the whole) is already to weight the neighbor's good inside one's own model, because the whole contains them both. The C2A2 cross-index treats love/union as a *fundamental category* across traditions (CROSS-005), which is why the two precepts are one. And the *order* of charity (a.8) — ranked, bounded, beginning with what is nearest — is the resource-bounded structure the central claim insists on: love computed "from each perspective," "limited by the resources we perceive." Wright's reading of love of God and neighbor as the *fulfilment* of the whole law (by canonical work) anchors the unity scripturally.

Four further articles carry weight the headline moves can obscure. **Q.44 a.1** asks whether precepts of charity should be given at all — Aquinas's answer, that God as goodness itself is *due* love as our ultimate end, is what makes the rest a matter of order rather than of sentiment. **Q.44 a.4** specifies loving God "with the whole heart": nothing else loved as an *end* save God. On the frame used here that is a single-attractor condition, and it is stronger than it looks — not that other goods go unloved, but that none of them terminates the computation. **Q.44 a.5** unpacks the additions — whole soul, mind, strength — as the will's intention, the intellect's submission, the appetite's regulation, and external obedience: not four faculties issued four commands but one agent aligned at every level of its own hierarchy on a single prior. Reading that alignment as precision-weighting across a generative hierarchy is this commentary's extension of the Friston picture, not a claim any record makes. **Q.44 a.7** grounds love of neighbor in the neighbor as *imago Dei* called to the same beatitude — which, on the monist reading, is the strongest available warrant that the other's perspective is a perspective of the same whole.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads the gift of wisdom as *the partial removal of perspective-limitation by connatural union with the whole*, and peace as *the ordered, reconciled state that union produces* — "tranquility of order," loving unity named from the medieval side. With Aquinas: wisdom judges by connaturality, presupposes charity, is both speculative and practical, and crowns itself in peacemaking; the two precepts are one because love unites affections; and love is ordered. Where it reframes: connaturality, which Aquinas grounds in grace elevating nature to "partake the divine nature," becomes intelligible as the agent's *recovery of participation* in the one mind of which it is a dissociated perspective (Kastrup) — knowing-by-being made possible because knower and known are, at bottom, the same mind. This keeps Aquinas's gratuity intact: the union remains *gift*, not achievement (a.4: no wisdom without grace), and "to one is given the word of wisdom" in unequal measure (a.5). It also keeps his sobriety: the precept is fulfilled in this life "in habit, not act" (Q.44 a.6) — we cannot yet hold the whole steadily in view, only orient toward it habitually, "unwilling to do anything contrary."

This is the clearest point in the treatise where Aquinas's response and the project's central claim *coincide rather than merely parallel*. The central claim says that when all limitations are removed, the simple whole is seen and every limited perspective is contained in it; Aquinas says wisdom judges by being made like the whole, and that this is given by degrees in via and perfected only in patria. The claim says loving unity is the goal each limited perspective computes toward; Aquinas says wisdom *makes peace by setting things in order* — names the computation, and its telos, in his own words. The reframe does not need to bend Aquinas here; it needs only to read his metaphysics monistically and let his theology stand.

Evidence strength, named with care, because this locus is where the project is at once richest and most exposed. The connaturality-as-recovered-participation reading is *speculative metaphysics* (Kastrup/Hoffman, by theme) — the project's deepest leg and its least empirical, and it is marked as such, not smuggled. Peace-as-tranquility-of-order mapped to free-energy minimization and target-form is a *formal analogy* (Friston, Levin), suggestive and medium, not a demonstration. "Love unites affections" is empirically anchored on the non-zero-sum character of coupling (Fredrickson, CROSS-005), medium. The sufficient-reason argument is philosophical and contested. The theological core — wisdom presupposing grace, the gift's unequal measure, fulfilment in habit not act — is high in field and carries the weight the science cannot.

## The central-theme thread

The whole arc of this project has been the claim that conscious agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective, that loving unity is the goal each computes toward from inside those limits, and that when the limits are removed the simple whole is seen with every perspective contained in it. Day 140 is where Aquinas says the same thing in his own categories. Wisdom is the *partial removal of the limit*: not by acquiring more representations but by becoming connatural to the whole — and on the monist reading, connaturality is possible because the agent is already a perspective *of* the one mind, so union is recovery, not acquisition. Peace is the *name of the goal*: "tranquility of order," the reconciled state in which charity's bond and wisdom's ordering have set the many into one — which is what "loving unity" has meant all along. The asymmetry of Day 139 returns, redeemed: the gift is given to all who have charity but in unequal degrees, so some genuinely perceive more of the whole — and their task is not to scandalize the lesser-seeing but to *make peace by setting things in order*, scaffolding the others toward the union they themselves more fully enjoy. And the sobriety is exact: in via the precept is kept "in habit, not act," because we cannot yet hold the whole steadily before us. That is the "fear and trembling" precisely — not anxiety, but the working-out of a union we possess habitually and see only in part, by the wisdom that is given, in measure, to all who love.

## Where this leaves us

The charity treatise reaches its summit: wisdom as the connatural knowing of the whole, and peace as the tranquility of order that charity and wisdom together make — the loving unity this project has tracked from the start, named now in Aquinas's own words and given, in measure, to all who love. Aquinas continues in the Secunda Secundae toward the remaining gifts and then the cardinal virtues — prudence next, the practical wisdom that orders particular acts. The through-line this batch has sharpened carries forward intact: perspectives are individuated by their limits (envy's misread, discord's divergence); they fail by seceding (schism, sedition) or by tripping one another (scandal); and they are healed by the wisdom that, made connatural to the whole, makes peace by setting all things in order — each limited perspective working out, in fear and trembling and in habit more than in act, the union it already partly is.
