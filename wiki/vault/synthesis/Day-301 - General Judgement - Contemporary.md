# Day 301 — General Judgement (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas asks two questions about the end: whether each risen person will know the full moral character of all his own deeds, and whether each will read everything written in everyone else's conscience (Suppl. Q.87), and then whether there is a *general* judgment over and above the particular judgment at death (Q.88). His answer is striking in its emphasis on disclosure: nothing stays hidden. The "books" of Revelation are read as the book of each person's conscience; "the one divine power will cause all to recall their deeds"; and the interior of every soul becomes legible to every other, "unto God's glory."

Under mind / conscious-realist monism the question becomes one about *the boundary of a perspective*. Every finite agent is individuated partly by what only it can see — its own history, its own conscience, the inner states that no one else has access to. What would it mean for that boundary to become transparent?

## Problem

The underlying inquiry-problem, medieval and contemporary alike, is **the hiddenness of the interior**. A perspective is not just a viewpoint onto the world; it is also a screen that conceals its own inside. Aquinas treats the end of that concealment as the very form of judgment: justice can only be *manifestly* just if the merits behind each sentence are open to all. The contemporary form of the same problem is the question of how an agent's internal states relate to what is observable from outside it — and whether that screen is permanent or, in principle, removable.

## Resource

The emerging paradigm supplies a precise object here: the boundary that individuates an agent is a **statistical** one. In the active-inference program (the C2A2 network records the move in PRS-04 of the Friston-tradition wiki, the organism as active modeler; the Markov-blanket boundary condition itself is PRS-07 in the same wiki), an agent just *is* a set of internal states screened from external states by a blanket of sensory and active states. The blanket is what makes the interior inferable only indirectly — you model another agent, you do not inhabit it. Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman's trace-logic work (PRS-10 in the Hoffman-tradition wiki) generalizes this: trace blankets subsume Markov blankets, so the same screening structure runs from the cell to the community. CROSS-027 in the network's cross-program index pairs this with Kastrup's dissociation: the blanket is the seam along which one mind appears as many.

So *individuation-by-perspective-limitation* has, in this paradigm, a mechanism. To "read another's conscience" is to dissolve the blanket — to hold another agent's generative model not as an inference but as a presence.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary parallel reads the general judgment as the **limit case of mutual legibility**: every blanket made transparent at once, every perspective contained in and visible to every other. With Aquinas, it keeps the claim that this disclosure is *for the manifestation of a whole* — the public vindication that makes each sentence intelligibly just, and so redounds to the glory of the whole rather than to anyone's shaming. Where it reframes: instead of a forensic courtroom, the parallel sees the asymptote of exactly the thing this project is built to study — what happens when agents become *richly informed about one another's perspectives*. Judgment, on this reading, is total mutual information.

Honesty about evidence: the blanket formalism is genuinely well-developed (formal, and empirically anchored in Friston's neuroscience). That it could scale to *complete* transparency among all agents is not something the formalism delivers — that is a theological and frankly speculative extrapolation, and should be marked as such.

## The central-theme thread

The most suggestive detail is Aquinas's asymmetry. The blessed see all merits and demerits "at the same time in an instant... in the Word"; the damned grasp them only successively, "in a very short time, the divine power coming to their aid" — but *from the outside*, not from any intrinsic gift. Bandwidth tracks union. The agent nearest to the simple whole sees all the limited perspectives at once because it sees them *in* the whole; the agent still bound to its own limitation must grind through them serially, on borrowed power.

That is precisely the project's central claim. Conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective; when the limitations are removed the simple whole is seen, and every limited perspective is contained in it. Judgment is where that containment becomes manifest. And the reason the synthesis can still speak of "fear and trembling" (Phil 2:12) is Q.88's insistence that the hour is unknown: the computation toward loving unity runs under genuine resource-scarcity and genuine vigilance, "as a thief in the night."

## Bridges

- **PRS-07 in the Friston-tradition wiki + PRS-10 in the Hoffman-tradition wiki** — *the blanket as the unit of individuation* (formal/empirical). The mechanism by which a perspective conceals its interior; "reading the conscience" is dissolving it.
- **CROSS-027 (Kastrup × Friston, cross-program index)** — *one mind appearing as many along blanket-seams* (formal/speculative). Makes the "everyone reads everyone" article a claim about porous boundaries, not magic.
- **PRS-05 in the Wright-tradition wiki** — *salvation as incorporation into a people, not relocation of souls* (theological, High). Underwrites Q.88's point that man is judged "as a member of the race," not only as an individual.
- **PRS-07 in the Rohr-tradition wiki** — *relation as ontologically basal* (theological, Medium). If the unit of reality is the exchange, mutual legibility is the natural end-state, not an intrusion.
