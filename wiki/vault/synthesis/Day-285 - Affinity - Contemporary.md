# Day 285 — Affinity (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.55–56 treat **affinity** — the relationship contracted *through* marriage — as an impediment, and ask how it arises and how far it extends. Aquinas's reasoning rests on the one-flesh doctrine taken with full seriousness: because husband and wife become one flesh, the blood-relations of one become, by affinity, related to the other; the spouse's kin are now *my* kin in the relevant sense, and so marriage among them is barred as if by consanguinity. Affinity is thus a *consequence* of the reality of the union — only because the bond genuinely makes two into one does marrying into the other's family count as marrying one's own. The contemporary version: **if a union is a real higher-order individual, does it actually redraw the boundaries of relatedness — does becoming one with another genuinely make that other's connections one's own?**

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **boundary-redrawing by union**. Affinity only makes sense if the marriage bond is *ontologically efficacious* — if it really alters the relational map rather than merely adding a social label. Aquinas needs the one-flesh union to do real work: to transmit relatedness. The contemporary sciences of individuality face the same: when lower units integrate into a higher individual, the boundaries of the new whole are genuinely redrawn — the new individual's "surface," its set of relations and exchanges, is not the sum of the parts' prior surfaces but a reconfigured boundary. Affinity is the human, kin-theoretic instance of the general fact that forming a higher individual reorganizes who-is-related-to-whom.

## Resource

The Levin-tradition program supplies the leading move. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work (PRS-32, *goal-directed cognition scaling from cells to organisms*; PRS-17, *the bioelectric interface to collective intelligence*) shows that when components integrate into a higher individual, the *boundary* of the system is redrawn — the cells that join an organism share a new collective interface, and what was external to each becomes internal to the whole. Translated: when two persons form one flesh, the relational boundary of the new dyadic individual incorporates connections that were external to each partner alone. Affinity is boundary-incorporation at the kin scale. The Kastrup-tradition program supplies the metaphysics of how boundaries between individuals are real yet movable: PRS-02 (*individual identity within monism*) and PRS-23 (*how individual minds separate — and, by implication, join — dissociative boundaries*) hold that the borders that individuate are soft features within one mind, so a union that relaxes and reconfigures a boundary genuinely changes the relational topology rather than merely renaming it. Rohr's PRS-7 (*relationship ontologically prior*) underwrites the whole move: relations are not labels stuck onto finished individuals but constitutive, so a real new relation reshapes the field. Friston's PRS-18 (*community-widening*) connects affinity to the prior day: affinity is *how* the consanguinity-driven outward marriage actually widens the net — each marriage knits two kin-networks into one.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **affinity is real because union genuinely redraws the boundary of the related individual — the spouse's connections become the union's connections, exactly as integrating into a higher biological individual reconfigures the system's boundary.** What it *shares* with Aquinas: the strict dependence of affinity on the *reality* of the one-flesh union, and the consequent extension of kinship. Where it *reframes*: Aquinas treats affinity within a juridical-natural framework of degrees; the monist reframe grounds it in the general principle that forming a higher individual reorganizes relational boundaries, which explains *why* affinity follows necessarily from union rather than being an arbitrary extension. Where evidence is *firm*: boundary-redrawing upon integration into a higher individual is well-attested in biology. Where *speculative*: the exact *degrees* to which affinity extends are historical-juridical, not derivable; and the claim that a human marriage redraws relational topology in the *same* robust sense as cellular integration is analogical and should be marked.

## The central-theme thread

The project envisions a final unity in which every perspective is included in the whole and so related to every other. Affinity is a small, concrete down-payment on that vision: a single union does not merely join two people but knits two webs of relation into one, enlarging the circle of those who are *mine*. Loving unity, on the project's account, is contagious through structure — and affinity is the structural contagion of kinship, each marriage making more of the world related.

## Bridges

- **Levin PRS-32 / PRS-17 (empirical at biological scale):** integration into a higher individual redraws the system boundary — the model for affinity as boundary-incorporation.
- **Kastrup PRS-02 / PRS-23 (metaphysical, speculative):** individuating boundaries are real but movable — a union genuinely reshapes relational topology.
- **Rohr PRS-7 (theological):** relations are constitutive, not labels; a real new relation reshapes the field.
- **Friston PRS-18 (formal):** affinity is the mechanism by which exogamous marriage widens the community's web.
