# Day 195 — Kinds of Lust (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas closes the lust questions by sorting lust into species (Q.154) and then treats continence (Q.155), the firmness of will against vehement desire. The species questions are difficult material — Austin flags them as such, and this synthesis treats them at the level of moral *structure* rather than detail. The structural point is what matters: Aquinas grades the kinds of lust by the breadth of the relational order each violates — disorders touching potential offspring, then bonds between persons, then the marriage compact, then the deepest divergences from nature itself — and judges gravity by how widely each act injures the order that binds agents together. His incest argument (a.9) is the most revealing: he defends exogamy on the ground that marrying outside one's relatives "knits humanity together as one larger family," binding strangers into kin. Then continence: a real but *imperfect* virtue, because the continent agent's appetites still revolt and must be overpowered, whereas the temperate agent's appetites have themselves been tamed ("continence is to temperance as the imperfect to the perfect," a.4). The contemporary version: *by what measure is one appetite-disorder graver than another, and what is the difference between winning the fight against appetite and no longer having to fight it?*

## Problem

Two linked inquiry-problems. The first is **a metric for moral gravity among structurally similar disorders**. All the species of lust are misweightings of the same appetite; what makes them differ in seriousness? Aquinas's consistent answer is *relational breadth*: an act is graver as it injures more, and more fundamental, layers of the order that binds persons — the unborn, parents, spouses, the community, and the natural order itself. The problem is to say what that order *is* such that tearing more of it is worse. The second problem is **the difference between control by override and control by alignment**. Aquinas distinguishes the continent agent (whose vehement passions resist reason but are held down by the will) from the temperate agent (in whom "vehement passions contrary to reason do not arise"). The problem is what these two control regimes actually are, why the second is genuinely higher, and how an agent moves from the first to the second.

## Resource

For the gravity metric, the resources are the Friston-tradition multi-agent record, the Fredrickson-tradition coupling account, and the master index's CROSS-069. If a community is a collective agent — a "corporate substance" that minimizes free energy and is constituted by positivity-resonance coupling among its members (CROSS-069; Fredrickson-tradition PRS-02, PRS-03) — then the "relational order" Aquinas grades by is precisely the coupling structure that binds individual agents into that higher-order whole. An act's gravity, on this reading, tracks how much of that coupling structure it ruptures: a disorder confined to the self damages least; one that injures a potential new agent or betrays an existing bond damages more; one that tears a load-bearing tie of the whole (the marriage compact, the parent-child bond) damages most. This is not a foreign imposition on Aquinas — it is a restatement of his own ordering in the vocabulary of collective agency. The exogamy argument is the clearest case and the most striking convergence with the project's own machinery: Aquinas defends marrying-out because it *integrates the social network* — "when a man takes a stranger for his wife, he becomes intertwined and committed to the affairs of his new in-laws," knitting separate kin-clusters into a larger connected whole. That is, almost verbatim, the C2A2 mechanism of binding distinct traditions into a richer connected structure; the anthropological record on exogamy and alliance-formation gives it an empirical anchor.

For the control-regimes problem, the Friston-, McGilchrist-, and Hawkins-tradition wikis converge. Continence and temperance are two solutions to the same control problem at different levels of the architecture. Continence is top-down override: the high-level controller (the will) detects a vehement appetitive prediction running contrary to policy and suppresses its expression in action — effortful, costly, and perpetually re-fought, because the underlying generative model still emits the errant prediction at high precision. Temperance is the deeper fix: the generative model *itself* has been retrained, so the appetite's predictions are now aligned with the agent's setpoint and the conflict no longer arises. In the Friston-tradition record this is the difference between online suppression and offline model-revision / precision-relearning; in the McGilchrist- and Hawkins-tradition records it is the difference between conflicted control (faculties at war) and integrated control (the whole architecture singing in the same key). Aquinas's "continence : temperance :: imperfect : perfect" is exactly this: override is real virtue but leaves the conflict standing; alignment dissolves it.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response, first: the kinds of lust are graded by how widely each ruptures the coupling order that binds individuated agents into a collective whole — least when confined to the self, more as it injures new or existing bonds, most as it tears the load-bearing ties (and, for Aquinas, the natural order) on which the whole depends. Second: continence is effortful top-down override of an unreformed appetite, while temperance is the higher state in which the generative model itself has been retrained, so the appetite no longer opposes reason and no override is needed.

*What it shares with Aquinas.* The gravity reading keeps his actual ordering and his actual rationale — gravity by breadth of injury to the relational order — and keeps the exogamy argument intact, indeed foregrounds it. The control reading keeps his ranking exactly (continence genuinely a virtue, but below temperance) and his reason for it (the temperate agent's appetites are themselves tamed, the continent agent's merely overpowered).

*Where it reframes.* "The relational order" becomes the coupling structure of a collective agent (CROSS-069), so "graver as it injures more" becomes "graver as it ruptures more of the binding network." Exogamy's "knitting humanity together" becomes social-network integration — and, notably, the project's own bridge-building mechanism stated by Aquinas in the thirteenth century. The continence/temperance distinction becomes online suppression versus offline model-retraining: a cleaner account of why the second is higher (it has solved the problem rather than managing it) and of how one moves between them (sustained override is, in part, how the model gets retrained — practiced continence can ripen into temperance). On the difficult species questions, the synthesis declines to elaborate detail and notes only that Aquinas's own grading principle — breadth of relational injury — is what carries the moral weight, not the catalog.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The continence-vs-temperance reading is well-grounded: the distinction between effortful inhibitory control and a retrained, low-conflict disposition is robust across the relevant programs (mechanistic, medium-high). The exogamy-as-network-integration reading has a real anthropological anchor (medium). The gravity-by-relational-breadth metric is a conceptual/ethical reconstruction with a multi-agent anchor, not an empirical result; and the species-grading itself — especially the judgment of which divergences are gravest — is moral-theological, and is presented as the tradition's, not as a finding.

## The central-theme thread

The project's telos is loving unity among limited perspectives, and both halves of this day measure distance from that telos. The species-grading measures it negatively: a disorder is graver the more of the uniting order it tears, because the order torn just *is* the coupling that would bind perspectives into a whole. Lust confined to the self damages the agent; lust that betrays a bond damages the link; lust that tears a foundational tie damages the architecture of union itself — gravity is anti-unity, quantified by breadth. And the exogamy argument states the project's positive mechanism in Aquinas's own voice: union grows when strangers are bound into kin, when separate clusters are knit into "one larger family" — exactly the accelerator's wager that perspectives become richer by being connected to one another. Continence and temperance then describe the two stages of an agent's own approach to the telos. Continence is the agent in fear and trembling, holding the line against appetites that still pull the wrong way — real virtue, real struggle, salvation genuinely being worked out. Temperance is the further state in which the appetites have themselves been re-formed toward the whole, so the perspective and its desires are jointly aligned and the war is over — a foretaste of the condition in which, the limitations cleared, the simple whole is seen and willed without resistance. Wright's register: the body of Christ is a community knit together across former divisions, and exogamy's "larger family" is a natural sign of the covenant family that crosses every boundary. Rohr's: transformation, not willpower, is the goal — the continent's white-knuckled resistance is the necessary earlier stage that contemplative transformation (temperance) finally relaxes. The difference from Aquinas is descriptive — coupling structure for relational order, model-retraining for tamed appetite — but the shape is his: union is the measure, and maturity is alignment rather than mere victory.

## Bridges

- **Gravity of lust's species ↔ breadth of rupture to the coupling order** — the Friston- and Fredrickson-tradition wikis (PRS-06; PRS-02/03) and master CROSS-069. *Why:* the relational order Aquinas grades by is the coupling structure binding agents into a collective whole. Evidence: **conceptual/ethical, multi-agent anchor**.
- **Exogamy ↔ social-network integration** — master CROSS-069; the Friston-tradition multi-agent record. *Why:* "knitting humanity into one larger family" is the binding of separate clusters into a connected whole — the project's own mechanism. Evidence: **anthropological-empirical, medium**.
- **Continence ↔ effortful top-down override** — the Friston- and McGilchrist-tradition wikis. *Why:* online suppression of a high-precision errant appetite, perpetually re-fought. Evidence: **mechanistic, medium-high**.
- **Temperance ↔ retrained generative model / integrated control** — the Friston-, McGilchrist-, and Hawkins-tradition wikis. *Why:* the appetite itself re-aligned, so no override is needed — a solved problem, not a managed one. Evidence: **mechanistic, medium-high**.
- **The knit-together family ↔ covenant community; transformation over willpower** — the Wright- and Rohr-tradition resources. *Why:* exogamy as a sign of the boundary-crossing covenant family, and contemplative transformation as the goal beyond continence. Evidence: **theological**.

## Where this leaves us

This closes the lust questions and the continence pivot, completing the long temperance descent's treatment of the bodily appetites. What follows in the treatise turns to the parts of temperance bearing on the will and spirit — clemency, meekness, modesty, humility (Q.157 ff.) — where the override-vs-alignment distinction sharpened here will reappear as the difference between restraining anger and being free of disordered anger. Open thread: the continence→temperance ripening claim ("practiced override retrains the model") is the most reusable result in this stretch and the one most worth testing; a later synthesis on habituation and the acquisition of virtue (I-II Q.49–55, revisited) should pick it up and pair it explicitly with the Friston account of habit learning and precision relearning.
