# Day 240 — Contemporary Parallel: Received According to the Mode of the Receiver

## Frame

The risen Christ appears — but selectively, and selectively understood. Q.55 asks why the resurrection was manifested to the few rather than to all, and answers with a hierarchy: "the loftier mysteries reach the lower ranks through the higher," so Christ chose witnesses who would testify to the many rather than broadcasting the rising to everyone. The angels see first; the disciples receive it from them; the world receives it from the disciples. Then, explaining the Emmaus road — where the risen Christ walked with two disciples who did not recognize him — Aquinas reaches for the axiom that is the whole project in one line: "whatever is received is received according to the manner, or the mode, of the receiver." To the two "wavering in faith or in unbelief," Christ appeared "in another form"; what they could receive was set by what they brought. Q.56 then gives the causality: the resurrection causes ours both as efficient cause (through Christ's humanity as instrument) and as exemplar cause (the pattern our rising conforms to), operating as every cause does — "first upon what is nearest to it, and through it upon others which are more remote."

The contemporary question: **if even the risen Christ is received only as far as each perceiver's mode allows, then perception is not passive reception of a given but active construction under a perspective — and what does that make of disclosure, evidence, and the spread of a new reality from its first instance?**

## Problem

The inquiry-problem, medieval and contemporary, is **perception as perspective-bound construction**. The naive picture treats perception as a window: the object is simply there, and to see it is to receive it as it is. Aquinas's axiom denies this — the same risen Christ is veiled to the Emmaus pair and disclosed to the believing, because reception is conditioned by the receiver's mode (knowledge, faith, charity, the Latin one happens to know). The contemporary version is identical in structure: perception is inference, never a bare given, and what an agent perceives is shaped by the generative model it brings. The problem both frames pose: if all reception is mode-relative, how is shared truth possible, and how does a new reality (the resurrection) propagate through a population of perspectivally-limited receivers?

## Resource

**Perception as inference under a model.** Tom's PRS-form record of the Friston program is the resource that fits Aquinas's axiom almost word for word. Active inference holds that perception is the brain's best inference about its causes under a generative model, with precision-weighting determining what evidence is allowed to update belief. "Received according to the mode of the receiver" is, in this vocabulary, the claim that the posterior is shaped by the prior — that what the Emmaus disciples could perceive was bounded by the model they carried, and that recognition came (at the breaking of bread) when the model updated. This is empirical and formal in its own domain, and the convergence with the Summa's axiom is close enough to be startling: the master index already carries the Friston precision-reallocation reading of contemplative reframing, and this is the same mechanism applied to disclosure. The interface program (Hoffman-tradition wiki) gives the complementary ontological reading: perception is interface-relative, so even a basal reality is rendered to each perceiver according to that perceiver's interface — the form Christ takes is the form the receiver's interface can render.

**Propagation from the nearest node.** Q.56's causal principle — every cause "operates first upon what is nearest" — pairs with the new-attractor reading carried from Day 239 (Levin P3/P1). The resurrection's new mode of life is realized first in "that body which is naturally united with himself" and propagates outward to the remote; the risen body is the first node, and our resurrection is the attractor spreading through the population it is the exemplar of. Structural analogy, marked; but it makes Q.56's efficient-and-exemplar pairing intelligible — efficient cause as propagation, exemplar cause as the target-form each instance conforms to.

**The honest confidence level.** Q.55 a.5 is the text grading its own evidence: the resurrection "cannot be proved demonstrably," is "an article of faith," supported by "persuasive, sufficient proofs" (wounds, eating, the witness of scripture) but not coercive demonstration. The synthesis adopts this directly, and it dovetails with Wright's Medium-confidence historical argument (PRS-04) flagged yesterday: the appearances are offered as the best explanation to those whose mode can receive them, not as compelling proof to every mode — which is itself an instance of the reception-by-mode axiom operating on the resurrection's own evidence.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

**(a) Shared with Aquinas:** the resurrection is manifested hierarchically, to chosen witnesses for the many, not broadcast; what each receiver perceives is set by the receiver's mode, so the same risen reality is veiled or disclosed accordingly; the proofs are persuasive, not demonstrative; the resurrection causes ours as both efficient and exemplar cause, propagating from the nearest node outward.

**(b) Reframed:** where Aquinas states the reception-by-mode axiom and applies it to the appearances, the monist reframe proposes that this axiom *is* the project's central claim, and that active inference and interface theory give it a contemporary mechanism: perception is inference under a generative model / rendering at an interface, so individuation-by-perspective-limitation is not only a metaphysical thesis but an account of why disclosure is always mode-relative. The Emmaus veiling becomes the paradigm case — recognition withheld until the model updates — and the hierarchical manifestation becomes an information cascade through perspectivally-limited nodes, each receiving as far as its mode allows and passing on what it can. Q.56's "first on what is nearest" then reads as the new attractor (Day 239) propagating locally before remotely: the loving-unity telos, realized first in one perspective, spreading to others by both efficient contact and exemplary conformity.

**(c) Evidence strength:** the active-inference account of perception is empirical and formal in its domain, and its convergence with Aquinas's axiom is one of the tightest in this series — but the *application* to the risen Christ's appearances is structural, not a claim that resurrection-recognition is literally Bayesian updating. The interface reading is speculative metaphysics with a serious published case. The attractor-propagation reading of Q.56 is structural analogy, marked. The historical appearances rest on Wright's Medium-confidence argument; a.5's own admission ("an article of faith," not demonstrable) is the text's confidence level and the synthesis holds it. No naturalistic mechanism is smuggled in as proof of the supernatural claim; the convergence is offered at the level of *structure of disclosure*, not occurrence.

Difference from Aquinas made explicit: Aquinas states reception-by-mode as a metaphysical axiom and uses it to explain the appearances; the reframe proposes that this axiom is the empirical-and-formal core of the whole project — perception as perspective-bound inference — and that the resurrection narratives are, among other things, a sustained exhibit of it: the same reality, received differently by each mode, propagating through a hierarchy of limited receivers from a single first node.

## The central-theme thread

This is the day the Summa says the project's thesis in its own words. "Whatever is received is received according to the mode of the receiver" is Aquinas's axiom, but it is also the central claim verbatim: conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective, and what each can receive of the whole is bounded by the perspective each brings. Even the risen Christ — the fullest disclosure the project knows — is veiled to the wavering and shown to the believing, appears "in another form" to those whose mode cannot yet receive the true one. This is why the unity must still be worked out "in fear and trembling, from within each perspective's reach," and why we need "guidance from those who perceive more of the whole than we do": the angels who see first and tell the disciples, the disciples who tell the world, are the hierarchy of receivers passing the whole down through modes that can bear it. The loving unity is real and already risen; its reception is mode-bound, and that is the entire human situation in one Thomistic sentence.
