# Day 249 — The Eucharist (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (III Q.73-74) opens the treatise on the Eucharist, arguing that Christ is present *in very truth* and not merely as figure or sign, and beginning to specify the matter of the sacrament. This is the metaphysical centerpiece of the Tertia Pars: the question "what is *substance*, such that it can be present where its sensible accidents are not?" The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: if mind is the ontological primitive and spacetime a species-specific interface, what *is* the deep reality of a thing as against its perceptual appearance, and how can the former be present without the latter?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **what is the relation between the deep reality of a thing (its "substance") and its sensible appearance (its "accidents"), such that the reality can be wholly present where the appearance is not?** Aquinas inherits an Aristotelian substance/accident metaphysics straining under the doctrine of Real Presence. The contemporary frame asks the same question precisely because mind-monism *also* drives a wedge between appearance and reality — but locates the wedge differently.

## Resource

The metaphysical work here falls to Kastrup, Hoffman, and Levin. In Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup's program, PRS-02 (individual identity within monism) and PRS-09 (representation versus participatory encounter) make the deep reality of a thing a *mental* reality, and distinguish merely representing an object from participatively encountering it. On this reading "substance" is the thing's reality in mind-at-large, and "accidents" are how it is represented to a perceiver — so a change at the level of reality (substance) need not register as a change in the representation (accidents). That is exactly the structure Real Presence requires, and Kastrup's idealism makes it *ontologically legible* rather than a sheer paradox.

Hoffman's interface theory sharpens the appearance/reality gap. In Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman's program, PRS-01 (hard-problem transformation), PRS-03 (perception as fitness-tracking, not truth-tracking), and PRS-04 (observer-dependent spacetime) jointly hold that sensible appearances are an interface that does not resemble the underlying reality — so "the accidents of bread remain while the substance is Christ's body" is the general interface claim (appearance decoupled from reality) applied to one case.

Levin grounds identity-through-change empirically: PRS-01 (morphogenetic control) and his demonstrations of form/identity preserved through total material turnover give a this-worldly case of "what a thing really is" coming apart from "what material currently appears."

On Rohr's account of sacramental presence, the Universal Christ already indwelling matter makes the Eucharist the focal intensification of a presence that is everywhere latent. Wright guards the realism — the presence is of the crucified-and-risen body, a new-creation reality, not a symbol.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the Real Presence is the case where a thing's deep reality (its substance, read as its reality in mind) is wholly other than its sensible appearance (its accidents, read as interface representation) — so Christ's reality can be present while the bread's appearance persists, because under mind-monism reality and appearance are *already* decoupled.** This *shares* with Aquinas the strong realism (truly present, not figure) and the substance/accident distinction's work. It *reframes* "substance" as mental-reality (Kastrup) and "accidents" as interface (Hoffman), which dissolves the Aristotelian strain — the appearance was never the reality to begin with. It *marks honestly* that this is a metaphysical reinterpretation, contested and non-empirical; that Kastrup's idealism is a live but minority position; and that the *content* — that the reality present is Christ's body — is theological (Rohr/Wright), not delivered by the metaphysics.

## The central-theme thread

The Eucharist is the maximal case of the project's recurring motif: the whole present under a limited perspective's signs. Limited agents perceive only the interface (bread); the reality (Christ, the whole) is present to them precisely *under* the limitation of their perception. PRS-09 (participatory encounter vs. representation) in Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup's program makes communion more than seeing — it is participative inclusion in the reality, which is just what "salvation as inclusion in the people united with God" (Wright) names. Here perspective-limitation is not an obstacle to presence but the very mode under which the whole gives itself to be received.

## Bridges

- **Kastrup PRS-02 + PRS-09 (identity within monism; participatory encounter)** — *metaphysical, contested.* "Substance" as mental reality; communion as participation, not mere representation.
- **Hoffman PRS-01 + PRS-03 + PRS-04 (interface theory)** — *formal-speculative.* Accidents-as-interface decoupled from substance-as-reality.
- **Levin PRS-01 (morphogenetic control; identity through turnover)** — *empirical-in-domain.* This-worldly case of reality coming apart from current matter.
- **Rohr (sacramental presence; Universal Christ in matter)** — *theological / contemplative.* The Eucharist as focal intensification of a latent universal presence.
- **Wright (bodily, new-creation realism)** — *theological, high within method.* Guards against reducing presence to symbol.
