# Day 119 — The Act of Faith (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas takes Augustine's compressed definition — *credere est cum assensione cogitare*, "to believe is to think with assent" — and unfolds it through three questions in Q.2: the structure of the act (Q.2 a.1), its three aspects (Q.2 a.2), and its necessity for salvation (Q.2 a.3). The act of faith sits between *scientia* (which has clear sight of its object) and *opinio* (which lacks firmness): faith cleaves firmly to its conclusion without seeing the object the conclusion is about. This is not a deficiency of faith; it is its constitutive structure.

The three aspects of the one act — *credere Deum* (believe God exists), *credere Deo* (believe what God says), *credere in Deum* (believe-into God, set heart on God) — implicate intellect and will together. They are not three acts but three relations of the one act to the object. And faith above natural reason is necessary because man's actual end (the beatific vision) exceeds what natural cognition can know unaided; Aquinas footnotes *Humani Generis* on the gratuity of the supernatural order.

The contemporary parallel: what kind of epistemic state corresponds to firm cognitive-affective orientation toward a reality the agent's perceptual interface cannot deliver but presupposes? And what is the architecture of an act that combines cognitive operation and volitional commitment as one structure rather than two sequential acts?

## Problem

The shared P: under what conditions can a finite cognitive agent hold firmly to a content whose object exceeds the agent's representational capacity? Pure inference cannot deliver firmness greater than its premises. Pure trust cannot deliver cognitive content. Some third state is required, and the medieval analysis names it *faith*; the contemporary analysis needs to find what kind of state in the architecture of agency that is.

The connected P: how does a single act simultaneously be cognitive (engaging the intellect's grasp of truth) and volitional (engaging the will's orientation toward good)? The neat division of faculties that the question presupposes — intellect for truth, will for good — is itself in tension with contemporary cognitive science, where cognition and volition are deeply intertwined in the predictive-coding architecture.

## Resource

Active inference (Friston) supplies the architecture for the cognitive-volitional unity. In active-inference models, perception and action are not separated faculties; both are forms of free-energy minimization, both involve generative models, both are simultaneously about *what is the case* and *what should be made the case*. The agent does not first perceive and then decide; perceiving and deciding are two sides of one operation on the generative model. This dissolves the intellect/will split as separate faculties while preserving the *aspect* distinction: every cognitive operation has a volitional aspect (how the agent is oriented to update or act on the model), and every volitional operation has a cognitive aspect (the model the action is informed by).

Aquinas's "to think with assent" reads cleanly inside this architecture. The thinking is the cognitive operation on the model; the assent is the volitional commitment to operate on the model in a particular way — to hold it as informing further inference and action rather than as a hypothesis to be tested away. The three aspects then map naturally: *credere Deum* is the model's having a node corresponding to the wider mind; *credere Deo* is the model's accepting communications from that node as veridical (high precision-weighting on prior-aligned evidence); *credere in Deum* is the agent's volitional orientation toward maximizing the model's coupling with the wider mind. One act, three aspects.

Hoffman's interface theory handles the necessity-above-natural-reason claim. The interface compresses an underlying reality that the interface cannot directly represent; the agent's natural perceptual and inferential apparatus operates entirely within the interface. Any orientation of the agent toward what the interface presupposes (the underlying reality) must be a state the interface itself does not generate. This is precisely Aquinas's point that faith exceeds natural reason: not because revelation is arbitrarily added, but because the interface's own structure rules out the agent's reaching its underlying reality through purely interface-internal operations.

PRS-11 in the Stump-tradition wiki (Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's "Franciscan knowledge" / "knowledge of persons" framing) reads faith as the kind of knowing that obtains between persons in mutual openness, not as propositional belief about a remote object. The Augustine formula's "assent" then is not assent to a proposition; it is the relational commitment that constitutes knowing-another-person. This preserves the cognitive character of faith (one does know the person) while explaining why the knowing exceeds what proposition-by-proposition inference can ground. The wiki entry is Tom's PRS-form description of Stump's move, not Stump's own self-description.

Kastrup's analytic idealism gives the metaphysical reading of why such relational knowing is possible across the dissociative barrier. Two alters of one ground are not strangers; the ground is common. The barrier prevents direct access but does not abolish the underlying shared identity. Faith is the alter's recognition — under the right conditions — that the wider mind is not other than itself in the way the dissociative interface represents. Wright's reading of Pauline pistis as covenant-fidelity captures the same point in scriptural register: faith is fidelity-to-the-relation, not assent-to-the-information.

McGilchrist's right-hemisphere mode of attending supplies the cognitive-style resource. The left hemisphere relates to its objects by representation, manipulation, and certainty-seeking; the right hemisphere relates by presence, openness, and recognition of what exceeds representation. Faith's structure — firm cleaving without clear sight — is exactly what the right-hemisphere mode is *for*. The synthesis of intellect and will that Aquinas describes is, at the level of neural style, the right-hemisphere mode actively engaged with what exceeds the left-hemisphere's representational apparatus.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel reading of Q.2 a.1-3:

The act of faith is the agent's single integrated operation by which a generative model that includes the wider mind as a high-precision node is held — held in the sense that it informs both inference (cognitive aspect) and action-orientation (volitional aspect). The holding is firmer than the underlying evidence the interface can supply, because the holding is grounded not in interface-internal evidence but in the wider mind's installation of the prior that includes its own representation. Aquinas's "thinking" is the cognitive engagement with this prior; his "assent" is the volitional commitment to operate the model under this prior rather than discount it.

The three aspects:
- *Credere Deum* — the model has a coherent representation of the wider mind as a real referent. Without this, the other two aspects have no content.
- *Credere Deo* — communications received through the interface and identified as bearing the wider mind's content are accepted as veridical: high precision-weighting, low skepticism. Without this, the agent cannot use the wider mind as an informational partner.
- *Credere in Deum* — the agent's volitional orientation is set toward union with the wider mind: action is structured toward what the wider mind's prior favors. Without this, faith is propositional acknowledgement without lived effect.

These are not sequential. The same act exhibits all three. The "single act with three aspects" structure that Aquinas insists on is preserved exactly by the active-inference architecture, which does not separate cognitive update from action selection.

The necessity of faith above natural reason: the interface cannot deliver its own underlying reality; any orientation of the agent toward that reality requires a state the interface does not generate from inside. The argument is structural, not arbitrary. Aquinas's appeal to *Humani Generis* on the gratuity of the supernatural order corresponds to the formal claim that the wider mind's expanding the agent's prior is not an entitlement of the agent's nature but a contingent fact about the wider mind.

Difference from Aquinas: where he frames faith as the intellect's assent to revealed truth on the authority of God who reveals (a propositional-information frame, supplementary to natural reason), the contemporary parallel frames faith as the agent's relational-cognitive orientation toward the wider mind whose existence the agent's interface presupposes (a relational-structural frame, constitutive of the agent's complete cognitive functioning). The structural claims — firm without sight, intellect-and-will together, three aspects of one act, necessary above natural reason — all survive. The frame shifts from supplementary-information to constitutive-orientation.

Evidence strength: active-inference architecture — *empirically* robust (high). The application of active inference to faith specifically — *philosophical/speculative* (medium). Hoffman's interface argument for the structural insufficiency of natural reason — *formal* (high in math; speculative in theological application). Stump's relational reading of faith — *philosophical* (high within analytic Thomism). McGilchrist's right-hemisphere/left-hemisphere account — *empirical* with some neurological contestation. Wright's pistis-as-fidelity — *historical-theological* (high).

## The central-theme thread

Faith is where the project's central claim becomes operational. The agent is constitutively perspective-limited; the limitation is real, not a deficiency to be corrected by more interface-internal effort. Faith is the agent's structural opening to what its interface cannot deliver but presupposes. The agent's working-out-salvation-in-fear-and-trembling (Wright on Phil 2:12) begins here: faith is the first virtue, the first state in which the agent operates under a prior installed from outside, the first cognitive-affective configuration in which the wider mind becomes a working partner rather than a hidden ground.

Loving unity as the computational telos requires faith as the entry condition. The agent cannot move toward union with what it does not, in some real sense, know is there. Faith provides the *knowing-that-is-there* without requiring the seeing-of-what-is-there; charity (next-virtue) provides the orientation toward; hope provides the perseverance through. Aquinas's threefold structure of the theological virtues is the project's central-theme architecture for the agent's cooperation with grace.

## Bridges

- *Friston active inference* — agent operations are simultaneously cognitive and volitional; "thinking with assent" reads as a single free-energy-minimization operation with both aspects. (Empirical/formal.)
- *Hoffman interface theory* — interface cannot represent what it compresses; faith's necessity above natural reason is the structural consequence. (Formal.)
- *Stump PRS-11 (Tom Loughran's PRS-form record of Stump's Franciscan/second-person "knowledge of persons")* — faith as relational knowing, not propositional believing; preserves the cognitive character while explaining the firmness-without-sight structure. (Philosophical; high in field.)
- *McGilchrist right-hemisphere mode of attending* — neural style of relating-by-presence that the act of faith requires; complements the left-hemisphere representational mode rather than competing with it. (Empirical with field contestation.)
- *Wright, Paul and the Faithfulness of God* — pistis as covenant-fidelity, not propositional assent. The relational reading of faith is scripturally anchored. (Historical-theological; high.)
