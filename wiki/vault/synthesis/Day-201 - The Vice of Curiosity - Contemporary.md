# Day 201 — The Vice of Curiosity (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas asks two questions about the appetite for knowledge. Q.166 establishes the virtue: *studiousness*, the "keen application of the mind" that both restrains an immoderate desire to know and spurs the body's sloth toward the knowing it avoids — a part of temperance, because knowing, like eating, is a natural appetite that must be moderated by reason. Q.167 names the opposed vice: *curiosity*, the desire for knowledge "not directed in due manner to the knowledge of the sovereign truth." Crucially, Aquinas does not condemn knowledge; he condemns its *misdirection* — knowing for vain glory, knowing lesser things while neglecting greater, reaching for what is futile or beyond one's reach, or knowing through illicit means. The contemporary version of his question: in a cognitive system that can only attend to a little at a time, what makes the *allocation* of attention virtuous or vicious — and toward what is well-ordered knowing finally aimed?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is that **knowing is costly and directional, so the appetite for it needs governance**. Aquinas frames this as temperance over a rational appetite: the desire to know is good, but unbounded it pulls the agent toward the trivial, the vain, and the merely stimulating, and away from the truth that orders a life. His diagnostic is teleological — knowledge becomes vicious precisely when its end is wrong: "there may be sin in the knowledge of certain truths, in so far as the desire of such knowledge is not directed in due manner to the knowledge of the sovereign truth wherein supreme happiness consists." The problem, stated generally, is to explain *why* an appetite for truth — apparently the most innocent of appetites — can be disordered at all, and what fixes its right measure. That a faculty oriented to the true could nevertheless go wrong is not obvious; it requires that knowing be not free intake but a *spending* of something finite, and that the value of what is known depend on its relation to an end.

## Resource

The leading resource is the active-inference record of the agent as a prediction-error-minimizing system under resource scarcity. On Tom's PRS-form record of the Friston program (PRS-04, active inference as goal-directed agency within scientific naturalism), an agent does not passively receive information; it *samples* the world to reduce uncertainty, and it must weight which prediction errors to attend to — *precision-weighting* — because attention and inference are metabolically and computationally expensive. This gives Aquinas's "temperance in knowing" an exact contemporary form: studiousness is well-calibrated precision allocation. The virtue's two directions map precisely — restraining "immoderate desire to know" is down-weighting low-value, high-salience queries (the system that chases every novel signal never converges), while spurring the sloth that "avoids the trouble of seeking knowledge" is up-weighting neglected but important inference (the system that under-explores never learns). Curiosity-the-vice is *epistemic foraging gone wrong*: attention captured by salience, novelty, or social payoff rather than directed by the structure of what is worth knowing.

Hoffman's interface program supplies the deeper diagnosis of *why* an appetite for truth can be disordered. Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman at PRS-03 carries the formal result that perception evolved to track *fitness-payoff*, not truth — natural selection "drives true perception to swift extinction." Our native cognitive appetite is therefore tuned by default to the fitness-relevant and the salient, not to the true-as-such. This is the naturalized core of Aquinas's worry: the spontaneous desire to know is not spontaneously ordered to the sovereign truth; left to its evolved defaults it pursues the vivid, the status-conferring, the immediately useful — exactly the four failure modes Q.167 lists. Studiousness is the work of *re-directing* an appetite whose factory settings aim below the truth. And the McGilchrist record (PRS-05, attention as an ontological act; with CROSS-003 gathering McGilchrist, Friston, Hoffman, and Fredrickson on attention as world-constituting) adds that this is not a side issue: the *mode and direction* of attending does not merely select facts, it determines what kind of world shows up. To attend curiously — graspingly, for possession or display — discloses a world of objects to be used; to attend studiously — receptively, toward the truth of the thing — discloses a world that can be known as it is. Direction of attention is already a way of being related to the whole.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: the appetite for knowledge is the activity of a bounded inference system that must spend finite precision on what it attends to; **studiousness is the virtuous calibration of that spending — attending to the truth that matters, at the right intensity, against both the pull of salient trivia and the inertia of neglect — and curiosity is its misallocation**, attention spent for fitness-payoff, vanity, or stimulation rather than toward the truth that orders the agent's life. The "sovereign truth" to which all knowing is finally ordered is, in the project's frame, the whole seen when perspective-limitation is removed.

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps his central claim intact: knowledge is good, and the disorder lies in the *direction and measure* of the desire, not in knowing itself. It keeps the two-directional structure of the virtue (restraint *and* spur). It keeps the teleological criterion — the value of an act of knowing depends on its ordering to the highest end. And it keeps his list of vicious modes as recognizable failure cases of misallocated attention.

*Where it reframes.* "Temperance over a rational appetite" becomes precision-control in a resource-bounded predictive system; "the desire to know is not spontaneously well-ordered" becomes the empirical claim that perception and attention are tuned by default to fitness and salience, not truth (Hoffman); and "rightly directed knowing" becomes a *mode of attention* that constitutes a truthful rather than a merely usable world (McGilchrist). Aquinas grounds the right measure in a fixed hierarchy of goods culminating in God; the contemporary frame grounds the *mechanism* of measure in the economics of inference, while leaving the *terminus* — the sovereign truth — to the metaphysics and theology.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* That cognition is resource-bounded and governed by precision-weighted attention is well-supported (medium-strong). That our perceptual-cognitive defaults track fitness rather than truth is a formal result within interface theory (strong as mathematics, contested as metaphysics). That the mode of attention is world-constituting is a phenomenological-neurological claim (medium). The identification of the terminus of all right knowing with the vision of God is theological, presented as such.

## The central-theme thread

The project's claim is that conscious agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective, and that learning loving union with the whole is a computation run under the resources each perspective perceives. Today's two questions show that even the *appetite for truth* is run on that same scarce budget. Curiosity is the perspective spending its limited attention badly — curving knowing back toward the self (vain glory), toward the merely salient, toward the trivial-in-place-of-the-great — and so deepening its own enclosure. Studiousness is the perspective spending its attention toward the truth that contains and orders the others: it is the cognitive form of opening toward the whole. Aquinas's "sovereign truth, wherein supreme happiness consists" is just the limit-case of the central claim — the state in which all perspective-limitation is removed and the simple whole is seen, every partial truth contained in it. Between here and there, knowing rightly is not neutral; each well-directed act of attention is a small step in the computation toward union, and each grasping one a step back into the self. The treatise is about to turn to prophecy — knowing given from beyond the natural interface — and today quietly sets the stakes: how a limited knower spends its attention is already a way of moving toward, or away from, the truth that would make it whole.
