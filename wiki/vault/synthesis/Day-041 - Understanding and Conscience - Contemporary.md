# Day 41 — Contemporary Parallel: *Powers of the Soul as Modes of One Inferential System*

Day 40 closed with the soul's faculties in general — vegetative, sensitive, intellectual, motive — staged as distinct *powers* of one substantial form. Day 41 presses into the intellectual side. Q.79 walks the medieval architecture of the cognitive faculties: the intellect is *not* identical to the soul's essence (only in God are intellect and essence one); we are passive-and-active in our knowing; agent intellect abstracts essence from sensed image; *synderesis* names the natural starting-point of practical reason; *conscience* names the act of applying that knowledge to a particular case. Q.80 then opens the appetitive side with the bare claim that there is an *appetitive* power at all, distinct from but tightly coupled to the apprehensive powers, and that intellectual and sensitive appetites must be distinct powers because their objects are. The mind-monist frame holds the load: each "power" Aquinas distinguishes is a *mode of one inferential system*, individuated by the kind of generative model it runs and the object that model targets, not by being a separate metaphysical organ.

## Q.79 a.1 — The intellect is not the soul's essence

Aquinas's first move on Q.79 is the same move Day 7 named at the substrate scale: *only in God are intellect and essence and existence identical*. In creatures, existence is given, and from existence flows particular potentials — the *power* to understand, distinct from the soul's *being-a-soul*. The medieval frame protects the asymmetry: we are not God, we are not pure act, our cognition is a power-among-powers given existence by the substrate.

Mind-monism inherits the asymmetry directly. Kastrup's mind-at-large — PRS-01 in the Kastrup-tradition wiki, whose record states that consciousness is primary and matter is its expression (Confidence *High*) — is the substrate whose self-knowing and self-being are identical (the analytic idealist's analog of *actus purus*); the alter is a dissociated locus whose cognitive capacities are *partial expressions* of the substrate's structure, not the substrate itself. Friston's formal apparatus gives the contemporary version: an alter has a *generative model*, a *Markov blanket*, and a *posterior over its own structure*; none of these is identical with the alter's own being. The medieval distinction between soul-essence and intellectual power is, in Friston's language, the distinction between *the agent that exists* and *the inferential machinery that agent runs*. *Honest evidence calibration*: the formal distinction is mature in active-inference research; the metaphysical reading (the alter as a Kastrupian dissociation rather than a stand-alone substance) is philosophical.

## Q.79 aa.2–4 — Passive intellect, active intellect, abstraction

The medieval architecture is precise. Knowing is a *passing from potency to act* — and what is moved from potency to act is *passive* in the technical Aristotelian sense, even when the result is achievement. The intellect comes into the world a *blank slate* (Aquinas quotes Aristotle directly). To understand *what something is*, the alter must be *exposed* to instances, and from those instances the *active* intellect *abstracts* the essence, which then *informs* the potential intellect. Aquinas reaches for the metaphor: agent intellect is to potential intellect what visible light is to the eye — light makes color *actually* visible; agent intellect makes the essence-in-the-image *actually* intelligible.

The contemporary cortical architecture this names cleanly is Hawkins's HTM and the broader predictive-coding tradition. The Karpathy-wiki Hawkins-tradition register (PRS-01, *Hierarchical Temporal Memory and the Thousand Brains Theory: neocortex-inspired architectures with reference frames, temporal prediction, and distributed voting*, Confidence *High*; PRS-02, *intelligence as the emergent product of thousands of parallel, independent models voting — radical distribution with no central homunculus*, *High*; CROSS-040 on the Cortical Messaging Protocol as variational message-passing) describes a *uniform* cortical algorithm — hierarchical temporal memory operating in every cortical region — differentiated only by what each region is *connected to*. The "blank slate" is the cortex's initial state of weights; *exposure* is the sensory stream; *abstraction* is the upward flow through the hierarchy that compresses invariants from particulars; *the active making-actually-intelligible* is the mechanism by which lower-level features are bound into higher-level categorical representations. The medieval *agent intellect / passive intellect* split maps onto the *generative pathway / inference pathway* split in Friston's formalism, named in the C2A2 master wiki as CROSS-011 (*Is Hawkins's HTM a specific cortical implementation of Friston's FEP?*) — a structural analogy the cross-program index poses as an open question, `Status: Active`, not a settled result. PRS-04 in the Friston-tradition wiki on the organism as active modeler (Confidence *Medium*, and marked `(Implicit)` — a re-description rather than a claim Friston states in this form) and Hawkins PRS-01/PRS-02 converge on the same shape: the cortex is one algorithm differentiated regionally; agent and passive intellect are two functional aspects of that one algorithm.

One further Hawkins record deserves naming here rather than in the footer, because it is the sharpest single match in the register to this commentary's whole frame. **PRS-15** — *Cortical columns as Kastrup's alters* — reports the Thousand Brains claim that each of ~150,000 cortical columns builds its own *complete* model of the world, not a fragment, and concludes that consciousness at the brain level is the *integration* of these column-level alters via the voting mechanism. If that record is right, Aquinas's question about whether the intellect is one power or many is being answered at a scale he had no access to: many complete models, one integrated knower, and the integration rather than the multiplicity is what makes the intellect *this* alter's.

McGilchrist enters here as a third refinement. PRS-01 in the McGilchrist-tradition wiki (Confidence *High*, sourced to *The Master and His Emissary*) states the asymmetry as *the right hemisphere as primary, the left as parasitic on what the right first discloses* — *two whole modes of attention*, left-hemispheric narrow-focus categorical abstraction and right-hemispheric broad-context whole-grasping, that are not separate organs but *two modes of one cortex* configured differently. Aquinas's *agent intellect abstracting essence* is structurally a left-hemisphere-mode operation (decomposing into universals); the *informing of the potential intellect* is integration that pulls toward right-hemispheric whole-holding. Both modes are needed; both are part of how we know. CROSS-014 (*Are Hoffman's Conscious Realism and Kastrup's analytic idealism the same fundamental theory approached from different directions?*) and CROSS-013 (*Does McGilchrist's corpus callosotomy data provide empirical confirmation of Hoffman's conscious agent integration claim?*) together place this distinction inside the consciousness-first paradigm: the modes are aspects of one conscious agent's inferential architecture, not separate substances. Both index entries are *questions under investigation* — `Status: Active — Forwarded to Pattern Detector` — not findings, and the commentary uses them as framing rather than as evidence.

*Honest evidence calibration, split by record rather than stamped across the cluster*: Hawkins PRS-01 and PRS-02 are graded *High* and rest on mammalian cortical neuroscience. Hawkins PRS-15 (columns as alters) is a proposal, not a result. Friston PRS-04 is *Medium* and `(Implicit)`. The Friston × Hawkins isomorphism is posed as an open question in the index (CROSS-011), not recorded as established. The metaphysical reading — that these are modes of one substrate's self-experiencing — is the additional philosophical move mind-monism makes, and carries none of the above records' warrant.

## Q.79 aa.5–10 — One intellect per soul; memory; reason; intelligence; speculative-and-practical

a.5's *as many active intellects as there are souls* closes the door on Averroist monopsychism — the medieval temptation to make *one* agent intellect shared by all rational creatures. Aquinas's argument: a power belongs to the substance whose power it is; many human bodies, many souls, many active intellects. The contemporary cognitive-science translation lands on the same shape: *cognition is agent-relative*. Each alter runs *its own* generative model on its own substrate, and the model is individuated by the alter's perspective and history. Mind-monism does not collapse alters into the substrate; the substrate is *one*, the alters are *many in the perspective-limited sense*, and each alter's intellect is genuinely *its* intellect. CROSS-013 asks whether split-brain data — two apparently independent conscious agents when callosal integration fails — confirms conscious-agent individuation at the cognitive level. The index holds it as an open question forwarded for pattern detection, so it is a candidate empirical handle on the medieval claim rather than a confirmation of it.

a.6's distinction between *sensitive memory* (storage of past particulars indexed to time) and *intellectual memory* (retention of essences-as-such) is Hawkins-territory again. Sensory and episodic memory map onto the medial-temporal-lobe systems that store time-indexed particulars; semantic memory — the storage of *what dogs are* divorced from *the dog I saw on Tuesday* — is the cortical-column-level retention of invariants. The medieval distinction is structurally what contemporary cognitive neuroscience separates as episodic-vs-semantic. The Hawkins-tradition wiki frames this as the cortex's *temporal* dimension (sequence learning) vs. its *invariant* dimension (object identity); both are HTM operations; they are distinguishable by the kind of object-of-storage. Aquinas anticipates the distinction structurally.

aa.8–10's three-fold *understanding / reason / intelligence* gradient — *to understand is simply to apprehend intelligible truth; to reason is to advance from one thing understood to another; intelligence is the very act of understanding* — names the contemporary cognitive distinction between *fast* gestalt-recognition and *slow* propositional inference. Friston's hierarchical predictive coding gives the formal: high-level categorical recognition operates near-instantaneously when the generative model fits; *reasoning* is the slower, sequential composition-of-judgments that operates when the model does not yet fit and must search. Kahneman's System 1 / System 2 is the popular adjacent; McGilchrist's right-mode-gestalt / left-mode-sequential is the hemispheric refinement. The medieval triad is recoverable as a single inferential system operating at different levels of completion under different precision-weightings.

a.11's *speculative vs. practical intellect* is the final faculty-distinction. Aquinas: *the speculative intellect directs what it apprehends to consideration of truth; the practical intellect directs what it apprehends to operation*. They are not two powers but *one* differentiated by *end* — truth-for-its-own-sake or truth-for-the-sake-of-action. Active inference names this directly: *epistemic* free-energy minimization (model-improvement for its own sake, exploration) and *pragmatic* free-energy minimization (action-selection toward goal-states, exploitation) are two policies of the *same* inferential system, balanced by precision-weighting. The vehicle for both is **expected free energy**, and the Friston register does carry that: PRS-16 (Confidence *High*, sourced to Friston's 2026 OODA-loop treatment) records expected free energy as *a single formalism collapsing risk and ambiguity into one optimization target*. **A scope disclosure is owed here.** PRS-16's own Problem is a decision-theoretic one — practitioners in finance, conflict and strategy lacking a unified account of risk versus ambiguity — and a search of the register by Problem text turns up no record whose subject is the epistemic/pragmatic (exploration/exploitation) decomposition as such. The formalism is the register's; the reading of Aquinas's speculative/practical distinction *onto* that decomposition is this commentary's extension, and should be weighed as such. PRS-04 (*Medium*, `(Implicit)`) frames the practical-intellect side more loosely, as the organism-as-active-modeler claim. The medieval distinction-by-end and the contemporary distinction-by-policy converge in structure; the convergence is an argument being made here, not a result being cited.

## Q.79 aa.12–13 — Synderesis and conscience

These two articles are the day's central-theme node. *Synderesis* is the *natural habit of the first principles of practical reason* — the firm starting-point from which any moral reasoning proceeds, the foundational *the good is to be done and pursued, evil avoided* together with the principles of the natural law. Aquinas calls it *natural* because we are *naturally inclined* to apprehend it, even though our intellect comes blank-slate; we discover the principle through experience but the *capacity* to recognize it as binding is innate. *Conscience* is then the *act* of applying that habitual knowledge to a particular case — a judgment that *this* thing should or should not be done, *was* well or ill done — and Aquinas observes that this judgment, because it is an act of fallible reason, *can* be wrong. What cannot be wrong is the underlying first principle.

The mind-monist translation runs in two registers. *Synderesis* — the natural inclination toward apprehending the basic structure of practical reason — is Levin-territory: morphogenetic and behavioral *attractor states* are real, embodied, and present from the alter's earliest formation; the agent's fundamental orientation toward *its own good and away from harm* is built into the very structure of free-energy minimization. Two Levin records do distinguishable work here and should not be run together. PRS-03 (*Morphological attractor formalism*, Confidence *High*) offers *formal modeling of morphogenetic goal-seeking as free energy minimization* — but its stated Problem is that **no formal mathematical language yet exists** for basins of morphological attraction, so it supplies a program and a vocabulary rather than a finished result. PRS-04 (*Cognition substrate*, *Medium*) is where the *bioelectric* claim actually lives: *bioelectric computation as substrate-independent cognition; xenobots as empirical proof of concept*. Together with the broader *agency-at-every-scale* claim they ground the medieval *natural habit* doctrine in empirically-tractable biology, at the strength each record carries and no more. The first principle Aquinas names — *the good is to be done and pursued* — is, in the Levin/Friston frame, the substrate-level fact that any agent operating under FEP is *constitutively* oriented toward its own homeostatic goal-state; *the evil avoided* is the active-inference imperative to minimize prediction error and avoid trajectories that destabilize the Markov blanket. The medieval claim that this orientation is *natural-not-learned* survives the translation: the orientation is a *condition of being an agent at all*, not a contingent acquisition.

McGilchrist sharpens *conscience* — the act-of-applying — into hemispheric specificity. The *value-perceiving* side of conscience (the felt sense that *this* action carries moral weight) is heavily right-hemispheric: contextual, relational, gestalt, attuned to the unrepeatable particular. The *judging-and-articulating* side (this should or should not be done) is left-hemispheric: propositional, categorical, capable of articulation. Healthy conscience requires both modes coordinated. Pathologies of conscience — moral scrupulosity over-articulating, moral numbness under-perceiving — map onto hemispheric imbalance. Wright's scriptural framing of conscience (Romans 2:14–15, *what the law requires is written on their hearts, while their conscience also bears witness*) is preserved without strain: the *law-on-the-heart* is the natural orientation Levin and Friston ground in agency-as-such, and the *witness-of-conscience* is the McGilchristian act of bringing right-mode value-perception into left-mode articulate judgment.

Rohr adds the contemplative register, and the register holds a record written for precisely this move. **PRS-9 in the Rohr-tradition wiki** (Confidence *High*) states its Problem as the need for a contemplative-mystical articulation of this project's own central theme — *loving unity is the computational telos; perspective-limitation individuates conscious agents* — that is **operational rather than purely metaphysical**, and its Solution is contemplative practice as the pathway by which agents move from individuated perspective-limitation toward inhabited loving unity. Conscience matures, on that reading, by *increasing fidelity to the always-already pattern*, and the maturing is a practice, not an insight. **PRS-01** (*Dualistic knowing*, *High*) supplies the epistemology the McGilchrist pairing needs from the other side: contemplative non-dual awareness as *a complement to analytical reason*, mature consciousness perceiving unity-in-distinction rather than collapsing into false dichotomies. *Salvation in fear and trembling* is the alter's progressive calibration of conscience toward the loving-unity attractor — the substrate's pattern recognized in the particular case. *Honest evidence calibration*: the agency-orientation claim is empirically grounded; the moral-natural-law identification is philosophical-theological; the cosmic-pattern reading is contemplative-mystical. Each register is doing its own work; they should not be confused.

## Q.80 — Appetitive powers in general

Q.80's two articles are short but pivot the day. a.1 establishes that there *is* an appetitive power, distinct from the apprehensive — every existing thing tends toward its own perfection-preservation-propagation; in things-with-cognition this tending is *elicited through cognition* (you cannot desire the donut you do not see, nor God you do not know); the appetitive power is therefore real and is *intellectual* in the rational creature. a.2 then distinguishes *sensitive* from *intellectual* appetite — the donut versus the good itself — by their objects.

The contemporary handle on Q.80 runs through Friston's active-inference framework. Active inference frames the apprehensive-and-appetitive distinction as *perception* and *action* — two policies of the same inferential system. Perception updates the generative model under sensory evidence; action selects trajectories that fulfill the model's prior preferences (its *target distribution*). The two are not separate organs but the *two directions* of the inferential loop. The Karpathy-wiki Friston-tradition node (PRS-04 on the organism as active modeler — active inference, perception-action coupling) is direct: *appetite* in the medieval sense is the inferential system's *preference-loaded* term — the prior on goal-states that drives action-selection. *Apprehension* is the model-update term that brings sensory evidence into the agent's posterior. Aquinas's claim that appetition is *elicited through* apprehension — *we cannot desire what we do not first know* — is structurally the active-inference claim that action-selection requires a posterior belief about the world to act upon.

The intellectual-vs-sensitive split in a.2 maps cleanly onto the precision-weighting and timescale structure of active inference. *Sensitive appetite* runs at fast timescales, on bodily-affective generative models with high-precision priors over physiological homeostasis (Fredrickson's positive-affect literature gives the empirical refinement of the upbeat side; Friston's interoceptive-inference literature gives the formal). *Intellectual appetite* (the *will*, treated tomorrow in Q.82) runs at slow timescales, on conceptual-categorical models with priors over higher-order goods. Both are the *same inferential system* operating under different generative-model assumptions and different precision-weightings. The medieval *distinction by object* is preserved by the *distinction by model* — what each policy regards as *its* object is what its generative model targets.

This is mind-monism's home move on the appetites. They are not *lower-than* a separate intellectual will; they are *aspects of* the one inferential system at the timescale and abstraction-level the alter's body operates at. The hierarchy Aquinas marks — *the will is the superior appetite* — survives in the structural sense that the slower, model-richer, more abstract policy *can* dominate the faster appetites by precision-weighting (top-down attentional control suppressing prepotent responses); but the metaphysical reading flips. Hierarchy is not substance-stacking but timescale-stacking, and the *one* agent runs *one* inferential loop at *many* nested scales.

## The central-theme thread

Q.79–80 frames perspective-limitation cognitively. Each alter's intellectual powers are individuated by *what its generative model can reach* — the agent intellect abstracts essences from *its* sensory stream; the passive intellect retains *what it has been informed by*; synderesis carries *the natural attractor toward its own good*; conscience applies that attractor to *the particular case in front of it*. The alter's cognitive limits are constitutive of *being this alter rather than another*; expansion of the cognitive light-cone — Levin's larger-scope-of-pattern-recognition, Friston's deeper hierarchical generative model, McGilchrist's better-balanced bi-hemispheric integration — is the alter's progressive movement toward integration with the substrate's pattern. *Salvation in fear and trembling* on the cognitive register is the disciplined cultivation of these powers under the goal of loving union: the speculative intellect oriented toward truth, the practical intellect toward the good, the synderesis trusted as the natural attractor, the conscience refined by both right-mode value-perception and left-mode articulate judgment.

## Where this leaves us

Day 41 maps the medieval architecture of the intellectual and appetitive powers onto a single inferential system whose modes are individuated by generative model, precision-weighting, timescale, and hemispheric configuration. The medieval distinctions — agent vs. passive intellect, sensitive vs. intellectual memory, understanding vs. reason vs. intelligence, speculative vs. practical, sensitive vs. intellectual appetite — survive as functional-architectural distinctions inside one inferential loop, rather than as separate metaphysical organs. *Synderesis* and *conscience* take their place as the agent's natural-attractor-toward-the-good (constitutive of being an agent) and the McGilchristian act of bringing that attractor into bear on the particular case.
