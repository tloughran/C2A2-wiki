# Day 156 — Contemporary Parallel: Cursing, the Just Price, and the Real Worth of Exchange

## Frame

Q.76 wraps the verbal-injustice arc with *cursing* (*maledictio*): calling down evil on another. Q.77 opens the economic-justice arc with *cheating in buying and selling*: is it lawful to sell a thing for more than its worth (a.1), must the seller disclose a defect (a.3), is selling for more than the just price always sin (a.4). Together they pivot the treatise from injustices of word to injustices of exchange — and Q.77 directly sets up Day 157's question of usury.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem on cursing: *what is the moral structure of will-policies whose target is another's evil as such?* Aquinas's answer turns on the distinction between cursing as lawful (judicial sentence, prophetic warning — calling down evil for a legitimate end) and cursing as wishing evil simply *qua* evil (a malformed will whose target is itself wrong). The inquiry-problem on the just price: *exchange requires parties to converge on a price, yet each party sees only their corner of the value-field — so on what basis is the price *just*, and what constitutes cheating?* Aquinas's answer is the *just price* doctrine: the price should answer to the *worth* of the thing — its real value in the economic life of the community — not to the asymmetric needs of the parties or to the seller's exploitation of advantage. To sell a thing for more than it is worth because the buyer needs it is, in Aquinas's striking phrase, to sell something that doesn't exist (the *premium* answers to nothing in the underlying reality).

## Resource

Tom's PRS-form record of Carroll's work supplies the metaphysics of the just price. PRS-01 (poetic naturalism: particles, organisms, and persons are all legitimate levels of description of one substrate) is the formal claim that higher-level patterns can be *real* without being fundamental physics: temperature is a real pattern thermodynamics tracks; price, on this reading, is a real pattern the economic ecology tracks. PRS-28 (moral facts as real at the level of description appropriate to agents and their wellbeing, without non-natural properties at the fundamental layer) is the discipline: the worth is a real pattern, the moral norm of just exchange follows from the polity's actual telos (mutual flourishing through reliable exchange), and the wrong of cheating can be specified without sentimentality or special pleading.

Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman's work supplies the interface reading. PRS-03 (interface theory) names the metaphysical structure: what agents perceive in any domain is an interface, not the underlying reality. Money is an interface to real worth — labor expended, materials used, use-value created, scarcity in the polity's ecology. The interface is real (in the sense that economic action takes place through it) but partial; it can be exploited by parties whose access to the underlying value is asymmetric. Cheating is exactly that: pricing on the buyer's need (which is at the interface) rather than the thing's worth (which is in the underlying reality the interface is meant to track).

Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work supplies the collective-cognition reading of the market. PRS-17 (bioelectric pattern memory as *cognitive glue* — a mechanism constituting a collective knower above the level of its individual components) makes the market a distributed-cognition apparatus by which the polity discovers prices that track worth — analogous to the truth-finding apparatus of the courtroom (Day 152). PRS-02 (defection) names cheating as exploitation of asymmetric access to corrupt the discovery: the seller's privileged knowledge of the thing's true worth, used against the buyer's need, biases the apparatus's price-discovery against its own target.

Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work formalizes both halves of the day. Q.76 cursing: PRS-04 (the organism as an active modeler that generates predictions and acts to confirm them, rather than a passive responder) names the wrong directly — a will-policy whose target is another's evil as such is a malformed prior, not a means to any legitimate end. Q.77 cheating: PRS-06 (multi-agent coherence through mutual model-building) makes exchange a moment of mutual prior-updating where each party comes away with a revised model of the goods, the counterparty, and the polity's reliability; cheating injects bias into that updating to the seller's advantage and the buyer's cost, with downstream corruption of the apparatus's calibration.

Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's work provides the scriptural ground. PRS-01 (critical realism on economic value): the prophetic literature treats false weights and measures (Lev 19:35-36; Deut 25:13-16; Prov 11:1; Amos 8:5) as among the gravest economic wrongs — the wrong of *cheating the apparatus* by which exchange is supposed to track real worth. Aquinas's medieval just-price doctrine inherits this tradition; the contemporary frame restates the underlying claim — that worth is real, price is supposed to track it, and exploitation of the interface gap is wrong because it corrupts the apparatus.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed contemporary parallel preserves Q.76-77 substantially intact, with the metaphysics of value made functional. *Preserved*: cursing as wishing evil simply *qua* evil is wrong (Q.76 a.1) — the will-policy is malformed at the level of the target; judicial sentence and prophetic warning are lawful because the target is a legitimate end and the evil called down is instrumental to it. The just price stands (Q.77 a.1): selling for more than the thing is worth because the buyer needs it is wrong; concealing a defect is wrong (a.3); the doctrine bears on the everyday transaction, not just the merchant's profession. *Reframed*: worth is a real higher-level pattern (Carroll PRS-01) that money interfaces to (Hoffman PRS-03), discovered by the polity's distributed market-cognition (Levin PRS-17); cheating is signal-injection that biases the apparatus against its own target. *Extended*: the same apparatus reading that ran from accusation (Day 152) through fama (Day 154) extends to the economic interface — the market, like the courtroom and the reputation system, is a polity's distributed inference into a domain no single perspective can see whole.

Measure against Aquinas: clean convergence on substance, with the metaphysics of "worth" given a contemporary scaffolding (emergent realism; interface theory; collective cognition). The contemporary frame may *expand* the just-price discussion to recognize legitimate sources of price variance Aquinas did not foreground (risk-bearing, time-value, scarcity in the right sense — the underlying ecological realities, not whim-relative valuation), while preserving his bright line against pricing on need rather than worth. This expansion is the substantive setup for Day 157's careful handling of usury.

## The central-theme thread

Exchange is the central claim in economic form. Each agent sees only their corner of the value-field; mutual flourishing requires that exchange converge on prices that track real worth. The asymmetry of perspective — the seller knows what the thing actually is, the buyer knows what they need — is a *feature* of the structure that requires perspectival honesty to function. Cheating is the local-optimum-globally-defective exploitation of that asymmetry: the seller absolutizing their privileged access against the apparatus that exists to compose partial perspectives into reliable exchange. Cursing is the same defection at the will-policy level: the agent's intention targeting another's evil as such, rather than any legitimate end. Both are perspective-limitation weaponized against the substrate the central claim names as the goal.
