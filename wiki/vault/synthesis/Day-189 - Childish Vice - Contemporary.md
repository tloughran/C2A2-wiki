# Day 189 — Childish Vice (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas now treats what goes wrong with temperance. Q.142 names two opposed vices: *insensibility* by defect — rejecting even the pleasures nature needs — and *intemperance* by excess. Q.143 then lays out the whole architecture of temperance in one article: its integral parts (shamefacedness and onestum), its species (abstinence, sobriety, chastity, purity), and its annexed parts (continence, humility, meekness, modesty, and the rest). The episode's hinge is Aquinas's claim that intemperance is a *childish* sin. The contemporary version of the question, under mind / conscious-realist monism: *why does unregulated appetite become self-reinforcing, and what makes that failure more culpable than failures driven by fear?*

## Problem

The inquiry-problem is **maladaptive habit-formation** — how a regulatory system trains itself into its own dysfunction. Aquinas's account is remarkably mechanical. Intemperance is childish, he says, not because children struggle with adult appetites, but because, "just like we see with children, the desires of the flesh are unruly and reach out towards base petty things," and because — the crucial clause — "if they be not immediately and regularly disciplined, [they] grow increasingly hardened against the rule of their parents; so too concupiscence, if left unchecked, grows increasingly difficult to later subdue." He seals it with Augustine's confession: "lust served became a custom, and custom not resisted became a necessity." That is a closed feedback loop with a hardening time-constant: each unresisted indulgence lowers the threshold for the next and raises the cost of correction, until what began as a choice presents as a necessity. The opposite vice, insensibility, is the under-regulation failure — suppressing even necessary appetite "counter to the order of nature."

## Resource

The leading resource is the active-inference framework recorded in the Friston-tradition wiki. Augustine's "custom became necessity" is, in the contemporary register, the *hardening of a prior*: as the agent repeatedly acts on an appetitive prediction, that prior accrues precision and the policy that satisfies it becomes increasingly automatic and resistant to update. What Aquinas calls a habit that has become a necessity is a prior whose precision has run so high that competing evidence — reason's "this is petty, set it down" — can no longer move the posterior. The Fredrickson-tradition wiki supplies the affective dynamics: this is a downward spiral, the inverse of broaden-and-build, in which narrowed appetitive states erode the broadened attention and durable resources that would otherwise allow self-correction.

The Levin-tradition program adds why the *childish* framing is apt rather than merely rhetorical. Developmental systems have plastic windows that close: set-points laid down early and reinforced become progressively harder to repattern. Aquinas's intuition — discipline the unruly appetite *early, immediately, regularly*, before the hardening — is a recognizably developmental claim about plasticity and its narrowing. The synthesis should resist over-claiming here (this is analogy, not established neuroscience of virtue), but the structural fit is real.

On culpability, the resource is the Stump-tradition wiki. Aquinas argues intemperance is *more* blameworthy than cowardice (a.3): the coward is partly excused because the fear of death "can stun a man," reducing voluntariness, whereas the intemperate man "does really want specifically, voluntarily, this particular donut or this particular woman." Translated: cowardice is committed under overwhelming exogenous precision (an aversive signal so strong it swamps deliberation), while intemperance is committed under endogenous, self-cultivated precision the agent had time and daily opportunity to retune. Greater voluntariness, greater fault.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: intemperance is a *runaway endogenous prior* — a self-reinforcing appetitive set-point that the agent trained and could have retrained — and insensibility is its mirror, a pathological *suppression* of necessary set-points. The "childish" label marks the developmental fact that the loop hardens with time.

*What it shares with Aquinas.* The two-sided structure (defect and excess flanking the mean), the diagnosis of habit as the mechanism, and the culpability ranking are all preserved. The synthesis should explicitly endorse Aquinas's counter-intuitive verdict — that the *quiet* daily vice is more blameworthy than the dramatic battlefield failure — because the contemporary frame independently supports it: the failure with more deliberative slack and more correction opportunities is the more authored one.

*Where it reframes.* "Concupiscence hardened into necessity" becomes a high-precision prior resistant to update; "childish" becomes a claim about closing plastic windows; "voluntariness" becomes the degree to which a policy was endogenously cultivated rather than exogenously forced. The reframe also gently corrects a modern reflex: where contemporary culture tends to treat appetitive compulsion as purely pathology (and so as *exculpating*), Aquinas's analysis — backed by the active-inference reading — locates real authorship in the slow self-training, without denying that the hardened state genuinely constrains. Both the constraint and the culpability are true at once, at different points on the same trajectory.

*Evidence strength, honestly.* The prior-hardening reading is formally motivated and has suggestive support from developmental plasticity, but its application to virtue is speculative (medium). The culpability-by-voluntariness argument is philosophical. The insensibility-as-against-nature claim is philosophical-theological.

## The central-theme thread

This article is about how a limited perspective can *narrow itself further* — how an agent, by its own repeated acts, contracts its field until the petty fills it and the whole is lost. The central claim that agents are individuated by perspective-limitation gets a darker corollary here: limitation is not static; it can be self-deepened. Working out salvation in fear and trembling includes the sober recognition that today's small unresisted indulgence sets tomorrow's set-point — that formation runs whether or not we attend to it. Wright's register: formation happens *in* a community whose practices either discipline the unruly appetite early or let it harden; salvation as inclusion in the covenant people is partly a matter of being formed by the right practices before the windows close. Rohr's: the first-half-of-life appetite, left unexamined, becomes the prison of the second half; the childishness Aquinas names is the ego that never undergoes the necessary disorder.
