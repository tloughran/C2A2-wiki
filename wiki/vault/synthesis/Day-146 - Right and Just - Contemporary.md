# Day 146 — Contemporary Parallel: The Right as a Real Relation

## Frame

Two treatises meet in this episode. Q.56 closes the long treatment of prudence by asking why the Decalogue contains no positive precept of prudence, and answers that right reason about the means is too variable — "from person to person and from one circumstance to another" — to be legislated, though the Old Law can and does forbid the *vices* opposed to prudence. Then Q.57 opens the great treatise on justice with the most basic question of all: what is justice *about*? Aquinas's answer is *ius*, the right. The object of sight is color; the object of understanding is first principles; "so what is the object of justice?" The Latin gives the answer — *iustitia* is built on *ius*, the right — and justice is "the stable quality in a man's will which disposes him to will what is right." Under the mind / conscious-realist monism this project writes from, the live question is whether that "right" names something real — a mind-independent feature of the relation between persons — or only a useful convention we project onto our dealings.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem runs underneath both the medieval and the contemporary frame: *is there an objective standard for what is owed between persons, and if so, what kind of thing is it?* Aquinas's answer is striking precisely because of its objectivity. Justice is unique among the virtues, he says, because "provided equality is found in the act, we don't need to ask any more questions" — we cannot call an act temperate or courageous without inquiring into how and why it was done, but "no matter how or why this man gave $10 for the $10 thing, we can say that this exchange was just." Justice has an object that is settled *in the external act itself*, by an equality between thing and person. The contemporary problem is whether that equality is real — a structure the world actually has — or a projection; and if real, what grounds it once the medieval hierarchy of natures is no longer assumed.

## Resource

The leading resource is Wright's critical realism. Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's work (PRS-01) names the position exactly: reality "exists independently of the knower, but all knowing is perspectival, tradition-embedded, and revisable" — an epistemology "that takes evidence seriously without pretending to a view from nowhere." That is the precise shape *ius* needs. The right is real — there genuinely is an equality the relation demands — but it is known only from within a standpoint, never from nowhere. This dissolves the false choice between a naive objectivism (the right is simply readable off the facts) and a relativism (the right is whatever we agree). Aquinas's two-fold division in a.2 maps onto it cleanly: *natural right* is the equality "determined by the nature of the thing" (the $10 item is worth $10), and *positive right* is the equality "altered by positive law" (the state lets the poor pay $2). Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's *faithful improvisation* hermeneutic (PRS-02) gives the relation between them: positive right and the *ius gentium* (the law of nations, which the host, following Crean and Fimister, illustrates with the judicial branch) are not arbitrary additions but *faithful improvisations* on natural right — reasoned extensions into circumstances the bare nature of the case underdetermines, exactly as the host describes the right to private property being "discerned only by a judge."

For the ontology of the right itself, the monist reading carries the weight. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work holds that forms and target-states are empirically real and that goal-directedness scales (PRS-16's Platonic space of structured, real patterns; PRS-09/10's diverse-intelligence framework making teleology testable). If a morphogenetic target — the form a tissue computes toward — is a real causal structure and not a metaphor, then the *right* a community computes toward can be modeled as a real relational target rather than a mere convention. Against this stands the deflationary foil: Tom's PRS-form record of Carroll's poetic naturalism (PRS-01) would grant that justice-talk is a "legitimate story told about the same physical substrate" but deny it any reality beyond the useful — and PRS-06 in the Carroll-tradition wiki marks the "explanatory limit" where that deflation runs out and Thomistic (and monist) engagement begins. Finally, Aquinas's truth-parallel — justice and truth are both "a kind of equality" / conformity — is where the idealist reading enters: truth is the conformity of a model to what is really there; justice is the conformity of an act to the right. Both are the limited perspective's act of answering to something that exceeds it — which is, in Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup's work (PRS-02), just what a dissociated point of view is: a bounded perspective within an undivided whole it does not exhaust.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel: *ius*, the right, is a **real relation** — the equality a relation between persons actually demands — known perspectivally (critical realism) and best modeled, on the monist reading, as a real relational target rather than a projection. Natural right is the equality fixed by the nature of the case; positive right and the law of nations are faithful improvisations upon it, reasoned extensions where nature underdetermines. With Aquinas: the right is the genuine object of justice, settled in the external act, objective in a way no other virtue's object is. Where it reframes: Aquinas grounds *ius* in a fixed hierarchy of natures; the contemporary frame grounds it in real-but-perspectival relational structure (Wright) and in the reality of forms/targets that scales across agents (Levin), without needing the medieval hierarchy to carry the weight. Where the evidence is firm: critical realism is philosophically robust, and the truth–justice conformity-parallel is a clean conceptual identity. Where it stays speculative: that value/right is *robustly* real (not Carroll's deflationary "useful story") is the live contest, and the claim that social rights are real targets in the strong sense Levin's biological forms are remains an analogy doing philosophical, not empirical, work — marked as such.

The measure against Aquinas: he needs no correction on the objectivity of the right; if anything the contemporary frame *vindicates* his boldest move — that justice alone has an object settled in the act — by giving it a realist epistemology (perspectival but answerable) and a candidate ontology (real relational targets). What it removes is the load-bearing role of the fixed natural hierarchy, replaced by a structure of real relations among perspectives.

## The central-theme thread

The treatise on justice is, for this project, the treatise on how limited perspectives are to stand toward one another. *Ius* is the first measure of that standing: the equality one perspective owes another. The central claim says conscious agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective and that loving unity is the telos toward which each computes; justice is the discipline by which incomplete perspectives, under scarce resources, render each other their due — a first, external approximation of the unity in which every perspective is finally held. This is why Aquinas's truth-parallel is the hinge of the whole episode: to *know* truly and to *act* justly are two forms of one act — conforming oneself to something that is really there and exceeds one's own standpoint. Truth is the equality of my thought to the world; justice is the equality of my act to the right owed another. Both are the limited knower answering to the whole. And both are perspectival all the way down: I render the right and grasp the true always from where I stand, "in fear and trembling," needing the correction of those — human or divine — who see more of the relation than I do.

## Where this leaves us

Justice has been given its object — the right, a real equality owed between persons, known from within a standpoint and (on the monist reading) modeled as a real relational target rather than a convention. Tomorrow (Day 147, Q.58–59) Aquinas defines justice itself — "the perpetual and constant will to render to each one his right" — and makes the claim that will carry the next several episodes: that justice has a *real mean*, an objective standard unlike the agent-relative mean of the passions, and that it scales from one-to-one dealings to the common good. The through-line opens here: if the right is a real relation, then the virtue of rendering it is the virtue of standing rightly toward the whole one is part of — at every scale.
