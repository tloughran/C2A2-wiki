# Day 132 — Contemporary Parallel: The Signature of the Bond and the Drive Toward Rest

## Frame

Having defined charity (Day 129), its object and order (Day 130-131), Aquinas turns to its *interior effects*. Q.28: joy is caused in us by charity (a.1), both from possessing God by grace and from the love of benevolence by which we rejoice in a friend's good as in our own; this joy carries an admixture of sorrow in this life (a.2), since we possess God imperfectly, as wayfarers and not comprehensors; it cannot be complete until heaven (a.3); and it is a fruit of charity, not itself a virtue (a.4). Q.29: peace is more than concord (a.1) — Augustine's "tranquility of order," requiring union within the one man, so that thieves may have concord but never peace; all things desire peace (a.2), even by natural appetite; peace is the proper effect of charity (a.3), which integrates the soul's desires and unites it to the neighbor; and peace, like joy, is a fruit and not a distinct virtue (a.4).

Read under mind / conscious-realist monism, these two effects do something the project needs. Joy gives the *felt signature* of the coupling charity establishes; peace gives its *settled state*. And a.2 of the peace question — "all things desire peace" — opens onto the one place in this stretch where the contemporary paradigm finds itself agreeing with Aquinas *against* the modern consensus.

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem has two parts. First, *why does a bond of love produce joy at all, and why is that joy mixed with sorrow rather than pure?* If charity simply unites the lover to the good, one might expect either contentment or longing, not both at once. Aquinas insists on both: real joy in the possession, real sorrow in its incompleteness. Second, *what is peace, that thieves can have concord without it, and that "all things" — even a falling stone — can be said to desire it?* Aquinas answers with Augustine's "tranquility of order" and with a teleological claim of startling breadth: everything that moves toward an end and rests in its attainment desires peace. The contemporary task is to see whether the paradigm can ground both the mixed joy and the universal drive — and what it costs.

## Resource

For joy, the resource is Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson's work. Charity produces joy, Aquinas says, in two ways: by the presence of the good loved, and "in the love of benevolence whereby a man rejoices in the well-being of his friend." The second is the telling one — joy at *another's* good, because we see the friend "as an extension of us, as included in us." That is positivity resonance: shared positive affect with a measurable biophysiological substrate (PRS-02 in the Fredrickson-tradition wiki), the felt readout of a coupling event. On the mutual-modeling account (PRS-06 in the Friston-tradition wiki), the friend's good registers as my joy because the friend's preferred-states are now weighted inside my own model — the perspective-boundary has thinned enough that their flourishing reduces *my* free energy. Joy is what successful coupling feels like from inside.

The admixture of sorrow (a.2) is then not a defect but a structural feature of the in-via state, and here Wright's program supplies the frame. We possess God "as wayfarers, not comprehensors" — really, but partially. That is inaugurated eschatology exactly: the decisive good has already arrived and is genuinely held, yet its full implications are still outstanding (PRS-03 in the Wright-tradition wiki). The Song of Songs line Habash quotes — "I sought him whom my soul loves, but found him not" — is the affective signature of an agent that has truly coupled to the whole and is not yet fully within it. Joy and sorrow co-occur because possession and incompleteness co-occur. Rohr's non-dual reading (PRS-01 in the Rohr-tradition wiki) names the same thing: the mature perspective holds both/and — real union and real not-yet — without collapsing either.

For peace, the leading move is Friston's. Augustine's "tranquility of order" — desires unified under one love, the man no longer "at war with himself" — is, in active-inference terms, the integrated low-free-energy state of an agent whose generative model has resolved its internal conflicts. The man torn between money, pleasure, honor, and power is a system whose sub-models pull toward incompatible attractors: high internal prediction-error, metastability under strain (PRS-13 in the Friston-tradition wiki, on the collapse and restoration of flexible state-switching). Charity integrates by subordinating the many desires to one — installing a single dominant prior that the others can settle under — and the felt result is peace. That peace is "more than concord" (a.1) follows immediately: concord is alignment *between* agents (a shared external attractor), which a gang of thieves can have; peace adds alignment *within* the agent. You can be coordinated with others and still be a house divided.

Then a.2: "all things desire peace," even the stone, by "natural appetite," because everything moves toward an end and rests in its attainment. This is the Free Energy Principle stated as teleology. The FEP claims that every self-organizing system — cell, organism, brain, collective — acts to minimize variational free energy, which is to say, moves toward and rests in its preferred states (PRS-05 and PRS-07 in the Friston-tradition wiki, on distributed cognition across scales and substrate-independence). Levin's morphogenetic targets give the biological case: tissues navigate toward anatomical set-points as toward goals. Aquinas's claim and the FEP claim have the same shape — *everything tends toward its rest-state* — and this is the rare place where the contemporary paradigm does not merely reframe Aquinas but *agrees* with him against the dominant alternative. Mechanistic naturalism since the seventeenth century has tried to expel final causes from nature; the FEP and Levin's program quietly reinstate goal-directedness as a real, formal, measurable feature of self-organizing matter. Aquinas's "all things desire peace" and the paradigm's "all systems minimize free energy" are allies.

Finally, joy and peace are *fruits*, not virtues (a.4 of each). Charity is the virtue — the disposition, the coupling capacity; joy and peace are its effects, the phenomenology and the settled state. This matters for the project's larger claim: if loving unity has felt signatures (joy) and a dynamical signature (peace = low internal free energy), then the goal the whole synthesis computes toward is not merely posited but, in principle, detectable.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads joy as the felt signature of successful coupling — including, centrally, the benevolent joy by which another's good becomes mine — necessarily mixed with sorrow in via because the coupling to the whole is real but partial (the already/not-yet); and peace as the integrated, low-internal-free-energy state of an agent whose desires have been unified under one love, distinct from mere concord because it is alignment within and not only between. And it reads "all things desire peace" as the teleological face of the Free Energy Principle: every self-organizing system is drawn toward rest in its end.

This shares Aquinas's structure closely, and at a.2 it shares his *conclusion against the moderns*: nature really is goal-directed; rest-in-an-end is a real feature of things, not a projection. Where it reframes: Aquinas grounds the universal drive in Aristotelian natures and the principle of sufficient reason written into creation by an intelligent author; the parallel grounds it in the formal dynamics of free-energy minimization and morphogenetic set-points — which makes the teleology mathematically tractable without explaining it away, and which lets "the stone's natural appetite" be a literal limit case of the same dynamics that govern cells and minds.

Evidence strength, named honestly. Benevolent joy as measurable resonance is empirically supported (Fredrickson PRS-02: medium-high). Joy-with-sorrow as inaugurated eschatology is theological (Wright: high in field) and phenomenologically apt. Peace as the integrated low-free-energy agent is formally coherent and clinically suggestive (Friston PRS-13: medium-high). "All things desire peace" as the FEP is formally serious but contested as a universal *law* rather than a useful principle (Friston PRS-05/07: formal, medium); its agreement with Aquinas against mechanism is a real and underappreciated convergence, offered as such.

## The central-theme thread

Joy-with-sorrow is the precise affective signature of the perspective-limited agent working out its salvation. The central theme says each of us computes toward loving unity "from our own perspective," with the whole genuinely glimpsed but not yet possessed; a.2 of the joy question says exactly what that feels like — joy in the real coupling, sorrow in the real incompleteness, both at once, "in fear and trembling." Peace is the same agent's interior achievement: not the removal of limitation but the *integration* of a limited perspective's many desires under the one love that orients it to the whole — the local, in-via image of the unity in which, when all limitations are removed, every perspective is contained. And "all things desire peace" extends the claim to the whole of nature: every perspective, down to the falling stone, is already drawn toward rest in its end. The orientation toward the whole is not an achievement reserved for saints; it is written into the appetite of everything that is. What charity adds is not the drive but its *integration* and its *object* — gathering the many appetites into one and pointing them at the whole itself.

## Where this leaves us

The interior effects are in place: joy as the bond's felt signature, peace as its settled, integrated state, and a surprising alliance with Aquinas over the reality of nature's drive toward rest. Aquinas turns next from charity's interior effects to its *outward* ones — mercy and beneficence (Q.30-31), the bond turning toward the neighbor's misery and acting on it. Tomorrow's synthesis takes up mercy: what coupling looks like under the sign of the other's suffering, and why being *affectable* — the very thing the impassible God lacks until the Incarnation — turns out to be the condition of compassion.
