# Day 191 — Fasting (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas descends from temperance-in-general into its species. Abstinence (Q.146) is the virtue moderating food and drink under the command of reason; fasting (Q.147) is its chief disciplinary act. The argument is functional throughout: fasting is virtuous not as deprivation for its own sake but because reason directs it to three goods — it "bestows chastity" by bridling concupiscence, it frees the mind to consider the things of God, and it atones for past sin (Q.147 a.1). The contemporary version of the question, under mind / conscious-realist monism: *what is a self-imposed appetite-suppression for, in an agent whose attention and metabolic budget are finite and whose deepest setpoint is not bodily equilibrium?*

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **self-regulation under resource scarcity**. Aquinas frames appetite as a pull that "withdraws man from the good of reason" precisely because food is "necessary to man who needs it for the maintenance of life which he desires above all other things" (Q.146 a.2). The pull is not evil; it is a lower good correctly weighted for survival but liable to crowd out a higher ordering. So the problem is one of *allocation*: a finite agent cannot give full weight to the maintenance of the body and full attention to the contemplation of the whole at the same time. How does an agent deliberately re-weight its own appetites — turn down a signal that is doing its proper job — in service of an end the appetite itself cannot see? And how is voluntary discomfort intelligible as anything but loss?

## Resource

For the temperance treatise (II-II Q.141–170), the productive contemporary contact runs through the Friston- and Fredrickson-tradition records, summed in the phrase "precision-weighting as temperance." That phrase is exactly the resource. In the Friston-tradition record, the organism is an active modeler that minimizes long-run prediction error by acting on the world and updating its models (Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's free-energy work, PRS-01 and PRS-04). Appetite, in this frame, is a high-precision interoceptive prediction — "I will be fed" — and ordinary behavior services it almost automatically. Fasting is the deliberate *de-weighting* of that prediction: the agent withholds the expected input and tolerates the resulting prediction-error (hunger) on purpose, because a higher-level policy values an outcome the appetitive level cannot represent. This is precisely "precision-weighting as temperance" — temperance is the standing capacity to set how much a given appetite gets to drive action, and fasting is that capacity exercised acutely. Aquinas's first good, the bridling of concupiscence, is the re-training of the precision itself; his second good, "freedom of mind to consider the things of God," is the predicted consequence of releasing attentional and metabolic load from the lower loop so the higher one can run.

The Fredrickson-tradition record at `traditions/fredrickson/prs_triplets.md` supplies the embodied half. Self-regulation there is not bloodless cognition but a vagally-mediated, measurable bodily capacity (PRS-01, vagal tone and the physiology of regulation; PRS-05, the bodily signature of higher commitments). Aquinas's insistence that fasting be calibrated to the body — one meal, suspended for the sick (Q.147 a.4) — and Austin's chemotherapy example land here: regulation is an embodied control system with real limits, not a war on the flesh.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: fasting is the deliberate down-regulation of a lower predictive appetite so that a finite agent can re-weight its scarce attention and energy toward modeling a larger whole — a self-imposed prediction-error undertaken because the agent's true setpoint lies beyond bodily homeostasis.

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps his core move exactly: deprivation is not the point; the *re-ordering of appetite to reason* is. It preserves the functional triad — bridling, freeing, satisfying — and the strong principle that the discipline serves the agent and bends to reason ("fasts are made for man, not man for the fasts"). It even keeps his calibration instinct: the regulation is tuned to where the pull is strongest (the most delectable, costly foods, Q.147 a.8), which is just good control-theory — spend regulatory effort where prediction-error would otherwise be largest.

*Where it reframes.* Aquinas's faculty psychology (concupiscible appetite vs. reason) becomes a hierarchy of predictive models with adjustable precision; "freeing the mind" becomes a concrete claim about attentional and metabolic load-shedding rather than a metaphor; and the third good, atonement, is reframed (without being reduced) as the agent's costly signal of re-alignment — a deliberate acceptance of error to re-mark the setpoint it had let drift. The reframe's payoff is that voluntary discomfort stops being paradoxical: it is the only currency in which an agent can *purchase* a change in its own weighting from the inside.

*Evidence strength, honestly.* The resource-reallocation and allostatic-regulation reading rests on real work in interoception and self-regulation (empirical, medium); the precise "freeing the mind = load-shedding" mechanism is plausible but underdetermined (mechanistic-speculative); and the orientation of the whole exercise to the kingdom and to satisfaction for sin is theological, offered as such.

## The central-theme thread

The project's claim is that conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective, and that learning loving unity from a limited perspective is a computational task bounded by the resources we perceive. Fasting is a near-perfect in-via instance. The agent cannot attend to everything; its budget is finite; and its appetites, correctly weighted for survival, will by default consume the budget. Fasting is the agent reaching down into its own weighting to free resources for the wider perspective — not yet seeing the whole, but clearing room so the whole can begin to show. In Wright's register, bodily discipline is new-creation rehearsal: the resurrection body is the promise, and fasting trains the present body toward the life of the age to come rather than despising it. In Rohr's, the deliberate "falling" — chosen deprivation — is how the false self loosens its grip so the larger Christ-pattern can be seen. The difference from Aquinas is not in the destination but in the description of the road: where he names faculties subordinated to reason and reason to God, the synthesis names a resource-bounded modeler turning down a lower loop so a higher one can compute toward a unity it does not yet hold.
