# Day 265 — The Means of Satisfaction (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (Suppl. Q.15–16) treats the *means* of satisfaction and the *subjects* of penance. Satisfaction is made by penal works that restore the "equality of justice" — the traditional triad of prayer, fasting, and almsgiving — and restitution must restore not only the thing taken but more, since the malice of will also disturbed justice. Then a limiting case: even those with no actual sin to expiate (the sinless) can *possess* penance as a virtue, though they have no occasion to exercise it. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: what are the concrete actions by which a disturbed equality is restored, and how can the corrective *disposition* exist where there is no deviation to correct?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **what specific actions restore a disturbed equality, and can the corrective disposition exist without an occasion for the corrective act?** The first asks how the action-stage of the repair loop is concretely instantiated; the second presses the act/habit distinction to its limit — a virtue present without any act to perform.

## Resource

Levin and Friston supply the means half. In Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's program, PRS-03 (restoration toward a set-point) gives the penal works concrete sense: prayer, fasting, and almsgiving are targeted actions that restore a disturbed configuration toward balance — and restitution *exceeding* the thing taken matches the intuition that the perturbation included the will's malice, so the corrective action must address more than the surface quantity. Friston's account of action reducing divergence is the formal complement: the means of satisfaction are the actual error-reducing moves that close the repair loop (Days 254, 263).

For the subjects half, Friston's program frames the act/habit distinction (developed on Day 255). In Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's program, a virtue is a *prior* — a settled disposition of the generative model — and a prior can be present and well-formed even when no occasion calls it into act: the sinless agent holds the corrective disposition (the readiness to restore equality) without ever needing to deploy it, just as a robust controller can possess a correction policy it never has to fire. McGilchrist (PRS-11/PRS-06) reads the penal works as *embodied practices* — ritual, communal worship and contemplative discipline cultivating a mode of attention that propositional content cannot replace (PRS-11), transmitted as embodied skill and tacit habit (PRS-06) — and supports the disposition/act distinction: the virtue is a formed mode of being, not merely a tally of acts performed.

Rohr keeps the means from becoming transactional: prayer, fasting, and almsgiving are contemplative reorientations of desire (toward God, away from self, toward neighbor), not payments. The *meritorious content* of the works remains theological.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the means of satisfaction are targeted corrective actions that restore a disturbed equality (addressing the will's malice, not just the surface harm), and the corrective virtue can exist as a settled prior even where no deviation gives it occasion.** This *shares* with Aquinas the penal-works triad as restoration of justice's equality, restitution exceeding the thing taken, and penance-as-virtue present in the sinless. It *reframes* the means as set-point-restoring, divergence-reducing actions (Levin, Friston) and as embodied reorienting practices (McGilchrist), and the occasion-less virtue as a well-formed prior held without firing (Friston). It *firms up* the act/habit distinction and the idea that correction must address the disposition behind the deviation, not only its surface. It *marks* that the works' *merit* and what is owed to God are theological; that "restoring equality" must be read as contemplative reorientation, not transaction (Rohr); and that the resource explains the *form* of the means and the disposition, not their *grace*.

## The central-theme thread

The motif appears in two registers. The means of satisfaction are the concrete, resource-bounded actions by which a perspective-limited agent restores a relation its deviation disturbed — finitude working out its re-inclusion through prayer, fasting, and care for neighbor, addressing not just the surface harm but the disordered will beneath. And the sinless-possessing-penance case states the project's act/habit theme at its purest: the orientation toward restoring loving unity is a feature of a well-formed perspective as such, present even where no sin gives it occasion — the disposition toward the whole need not wait for a fall to be real.
