# Day 241 — Ascension (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (III Q.57) asks how it was fitting for Christ to ascend bodily, where he ascended, and how a body can be "at the right hand of the Father." The medieval question is spatial and metaphysical at once: where does a glorified body *go*, and what does it mean for an embodied person to be locally absent yet causally and royally present to the whole created order? The contemporary version, posed under mind / conscious-realist monism, is this: if spacetime is a species-specific interface rather than the fundamental container of reality, then "place" and "local withdrawal" are features of a perceptual dashboard, not of the deep order. What does it mean for an agent to recede from *one* interface-position while its ordering influence over the whole increases?

## Problem

The inquiry-problem shared across both frames: **how can a determinate, located perspective remain the organizing center of a whole from which it has visibly withdrawn?** Aquinas needs Christ to be genuinely *gone* (so the Spirit can come, so faith rather than sight is exercised) and genuinely *governing* (seated at the right hand, exercising universal causal influence through his humanity). The contemporary frame inherits the same tension in naturalized dress: a system's controlling variable is often not locally present in the state it governs — it acts as a boundary condition, a field, an attractor toward which the system moves without the attractor sitting "inside" any one location.

## Resource

The leading move comes from Hoffman's interface theory of perception. In Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman's program, PRS-04 (observer-dependent spacetime) treats spatial location as a data structure of the perceiving agent rather than a property of mind-independent reality. If "place" is interface, then "local withdrawal" is a change in how the whole is *presented* to limited perceivers, not a subtraction from reality. Christ's ascension, read through this resource, is not a departure from being but a transition out of the shared sensory interface — exactly the structure Aquinas needs to motivate faith over sight.

A second resource comes from Levin. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work (PRS-01, morphogenetic control; PRS-03, the morphological-attractor formalism) describes how a target form governs a developing system non-locally: cells are pulled toward an anatomical set-point that is not itself a cell, not located "in" any tissue, yet decisively ordering all of them. This gives a naturalized analogy — strictly an analogy, flagged as such — for a governing center that orders a whole without occupying a position within it.

The theological ground truth is Wright. His inaugurated eschatology (the decisive event has happened; its full implications are still unfolding) reframes the Ascension not as Jesus going "up and away" but as the enthronement that inaugurates a reign now being worked out within history. Rohr's Universal Christ supplies the cosmic scope: the ascended Christ is the pattern present in all reality, not a body relocated to a far compartment of the sky.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the Ascension is a change of interface, not a change of presence.** It *shares* with Aquinas the insistence that Christ is bodily real and genuinely exalted to universal governance. It *reframes* the spatial worry — "to the right hand of the Father" need not name a location in the perceptual manifold; under interface theory it names a mode of ordering the whole that no longer registers as a local object to creaturely perception. It *firms up* one thing Aquinas could only assert: that local absence and total governance are compatible, because controlling centers routinely act as boundary conditions rather than interior occupants.

Where is this firm and where speculative? The Christology and eschatology are theological claims, strong *within* Wright's critical-realist historical method but not empirical. The interface read of "place" is formal and philosophically live but not established physics. The Levin analogy is empirically grounded in *its own domain* (bioelectric morphogenesis) and only analogically transferred here — the transfer is speculative and must be marked so.

## The central-theme thread

The project's claim is that conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective, and that loving unity is the goal each limited agent computes toward. The Ascension is a limit case from the other direction: a perspective that *recedes* from the shared interface so that the many limited perspectives must now seek the whole through faith, guidance, and one another rather than through direct sight. Wright's reading makes this concrete — the withdrawal is precisely what constitutes the community as agents who must "improvise faithfully," computing toward unity under conditions of partial information, "working out salvation in fear and trembling" (Phil 2:12) because the governing center is no longer locally visible. Absence, here, is the condition of the community's agency.

## Bridges

- **Hoffman PRS-04 (observer-dependent spacetime)** — *interface / formal-speculative.* Lets "local withdrawal" be a fact about presentation, not subtraction from being; the cleanest resource for Aquinas's spatial worry.
- **Levin PRS-01 + PRS-03 (morphogenetic control; attractor formalism)** — *empirical-in-domain, speculative-in-transfer.* A non-local governing set-point that orders a whole without occupying it.
- **Wright (inaugurated eschatology)** — *theological, high confidence within method.* Ascension as enthronement inaugurating a reign worked out in history; primary voice on III Q.1-59.
- **Rohr (Universal Christ)** — *theological / contemplative.* Cosmic scope: the ascended Christ as pattern in all reality, not a relocated body.
- **Stump (atonement as union)** — *theological-philosophical.* Receives the monist reframe as Thomistic interlocutor: governance-by-union rather than governance-by-location.
