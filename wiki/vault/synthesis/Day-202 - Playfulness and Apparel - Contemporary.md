# Day 202 — Playfulness and Apparel (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Both of today's questions are about *self-presentation* and its right measure. Q.168 treats the outward movements of the body, and within it the surprising virtue of *play* — eutrapelia, the well-tuned capacity for mirth. Aquinas's argument is functional: the mind, like the body, wearies (especially in contemplating "the abstract or the immaterial"), and "rest in the good, technically speaking, is what joy is"; so play is virtuous "in so far as it is a virtuous way to rest the mind for the sake of later activity." His image, borrowed via St. Bruno, is the bow that snaps if never unstrung. Q.169 treats *apparel*: dress is virtuous when it represents the interior truthfully and fits one's state and the custom of the community, vicious when it serves vain glory or — the load-bearing case — when it is worn "to provoke a lust of those who happen to see them," which is "contrary to charity... to destroy the soul of others." The contemporary version: *how should an agent regulate its own restoration and its own signals, given that it is part of a larger whole whose coherence its behavior helps or harms?*

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is that **a limited agent's self-management is never merely private — it is coupling to a whole**. Two facets. First, the *economics of rest*: a system that does sustained, costly cognitive work degrades and must restore, and Aquinas insists the restoration is not waste but ordered to operation ("pleasure and rest are not in quest for their own sake but for the sake of operation"). The problem is to say what kind of system has this property — that it must periodically *stop optimizing* in order to keep functioning, and that the right amount of stopping is a calibrated middle (excess mirth and total mirthlessness both vices). Second, the *semiotics of self-presentation*: Aquinas treats movement and dress as *signals* matched to the integrity of the interior and to communal norms, and makes the strong claim that mis-signaling can harm others ("destroy the soul"), grounding it in the Pauline scandal-principle — Paul "would willingly never eat meat" rather than scandalize a weak believer (1 Cor 8). The problem is why an individual's signal should be answerable to others at all, and what makes a community the kind of thing whose members' self-presentations are mutually binding.

## Resource

For the economics of rest, the leading resource is the Fredrickson program's *broaden-and-build* account of positive affect: positive states are not idle pleasure but a distinct functional mode: they broaden the agent's attentional and behavioral repertoire and *build* durable resources (cognitive, social, physiological) that are spent down under sustained effortful, narrowing work. (This claim is carried on Fredrickson's published theory rather than on a numbered record: the Fredrickson tradition record contains no broaden-and-build triplet — it names the framework only in the Problem line of PRS-07, as the frame within which multi-level conducive conditions remain untheorized. The gap is a known one and is flagged rather than papered over with a nearby id.) This is Aquinas's bow, formalized: the agent that never enters the broadening, restorative mode — never "rests in the good" — depletes the very resources its operation requires, while the agent that lives in it neglects operation. Eutrapelia, the calibrated middle, is the well-tuned alternation between narrowing work and broadening rest. (The same point reads from the active-inference side as precision-relaxation: a system clamped at high precision on a hard task must periodically relax to re-explore and avoid runaway error — rest as re-calibration.)

For the semiotics of self-presentation, the leading resource is the Friston program's account of how agents become a coherent collective. Tom's PRS-form record at PRS-06 (multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling) holds that individual agents "couple through mutual informational alignment — reducing prediction error in each other's behavior," and names this the informational substrate of love: agents synchronize their models and thereby "become a coherent collective agent." This is exactly the ontology Aquinas's scandal-principle presupposes. A community is one agent to the degree that its members can model one another reliably; *signals* — how one moves, how one dresses — are the public surface over which that mutual modeling runs. Truthful, fitting self-presentation (Aquinas's honestum matched to interior integrity, plus affability toward others) *reduces* the others' prediction error and strengthens the coupling; vain, deceptive, or deliberately lust-provoking presentation *injects* error or exploits the others' models to their harm — "destroying the soul" is, in this register, degrading another agent's capacity to model and govern itself well. And the master index gathers the deeper claim that what is being built here is not incidental: CROSS-005 records love/union as a candidate *fundamental* category (Fredrickson measuring it as positivity resonance, Kastrup reading it as re-integration, Stump as ontological union), and CROSS-018 frames Fredrickson's measurable coupling and the metaphysical account of love-as-union as complementary levels of one description. The "shared whole" Habash invokes is, on this reading, a real higher-order agent constituted by loving alignment.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: an agent's self-management — its rest and its signals — is the means by which a limited part couples to the whole it belongs to. **Play is the restorative, capacity-broadening mode that keeps the agent able to do the work of union (Aquinas's "rest in the good is joy"), calibrated between depletion and dissipation; dress and comportment are legible signals over which a community's mutual modeling runs, virtuous when truthful and upbuilding, vicious when they inject error or exploit others' models to their harm.** Modesty, in both, is the temperance of one's own signal for the good of the shared whole.

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps rest as ordered to operation, not an end in itself, and keeps eutrapelia as a calibrated middle. It keeps dress as a *signal* answerable to interior truth and communal norms. And it keeps the strong relational claim — that self-presentation can build up or harm others — together with its Pauline grounding in love of neighbor.

*Where it reframes.* "Rest in the good" becomes the broaden-and-build mode that restores and builds the agent's resources; "the mind wearies" becomes the depletion of a precision-clamped inference system. "Scandal" becomes the injection of modeling-error or the exploitation of another's generative model; "a community whose members are bound to one another" becomes a higher-order agent constituted by mutual model-alignment (Friston PRS-06). Aquinas grounds the obligation in charity and the order of creation; the contemporary frame supplies the *mechanism* of coupling while leaving the *why-it-matters-ultimately* — that this whole is the people being drawn into union with God — to the theology.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* That positive affect restores and broadens functional resources is well-supported empirically (medium-strong). That self-presentation functions as costly signaling shaping others' models is well-grounded behaviorally (medium). That mutual modeling constitutes higher-order collective agency is a formal-conceptual proposal (medium). That love/union is a fundamental category, and the scandal-principle's grounding in charity, are theological/metaphysical, presented as such.

## The central-theme thread

This day states the project's thesis in its own voice: "we are responsible for one another. Our fates are intertwined with one another as parts of a shared whole." The central claim is that agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective and that loving union is the telos toward which each limited perspective computes — and here that telos becomes concrete and bodily. The limited agent is real and individual, but its rest and its self-presentation are not its own private business: it must restore itself (play) precisely so that it can keep contributing to the whole, and it must signal truthfully and considerately (modest movement, fitting dress) so that the others can model it and the shared agent can cohere. Aquinas's scandal-principle is the central theme made interpersonal: because I am a part of a whole that includes the weak believer, my freedom is exercised *under obligation to* their good — I will "never eat meat again" rather than degrade their capacity to walk toward union. Vice here is the perspective treating its own restoration or its own display as ends, injecting error into the shared model (vain glory) or exploiting it (provoking lust); virtue is the perspective ordering even its play and its clothing to the building-up of the whole. The treatise will next ascend to prophecy and rapture — knowing that overflows the individual interface — but today keeps the feet on the ground: union is computed not only in contemplation but in how a body rests, moves, and presents itself among others.
