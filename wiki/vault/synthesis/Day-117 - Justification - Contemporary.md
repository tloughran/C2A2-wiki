# Day 117 — Justification (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas asks three questions in tight sequence: Who causes grace? Can the creature prepare for grace? Is grace given according to merit? His answers form a closed argument. (a) God alone causes grace, because grace exceeds every created nature. (b) The creature must prepare — but the preparation itself is moved by prevenient (actual) grace, so the preparation is itself caused by God. (c) Grace is given according to the measure God wills, not earned, and it varies in degree between persons; no created agent merits the first grace, though once given, increase in grace can be merited through cooperation animated by charity.

The contemporary version: in a world where agents are partly self-causing and the causes of their cognitive states are partly internal, what does it mean to say that some structural enlargement of the agent comes from outside the agent and yet operates through the agent's own activity? How does the work-out-your-salvation-with-fear-and-trembling structure — Aquinas quotes Phil 2:12 directly in the transcript — get cashed out in mechanistic terms? And does the empirical evidence support or undercut Aquinas's "God alone" claim once we re-read it through interface theory and active inference?

## Problem

The P shared across the medieval and contemporary frames: free will and divine providence appear to compete for causal credit. If the agent's preparation for grace is itself grace, then either the agent is reduced to a passive substrate (and "free will" becomes empty) or grace is reduced to an external label on what is really natural causation (and "from God alone" becomes empty). Either horn destroys the structure Aquinas wants to preserve.

The contemporary equivalent: if active-inference models capture the causal structure of agency — and they capture an enormous amount of it — then the agent's actions are determined by its priors, its sensory inputs, and the gradient between them. What is left for "from outside" to do? Either the prior-shift is itself fully internal (no room for grace), or the prior-shift comes from outside in a way that bypasses the agent's processing (no room for free will). Hoffman, Levin, Kastrup, and Friston each press the problem from a different angle, and none of them dissolves it by fiat.

## Resource

Friston's active inference is the most direct technical resource. An agent acting under active inference has a generative model — a prior over what states the world should be in, given the agent's identity. The agent minimizes free energy by moving its perceptions and actions to match its priors. Crucial fact: the prior is not chosen by the agent. The prior is given by the agent's history, embodiment, and the larger system within which the agent is constituted. An agent cannot freely re-prior itself any more than a cell can freely re-specify its fate in a morphogenetic field. The prior is the agent's *participation* in a structure that exceeds it.

Aquinas's "God alone is the cause of grace" can be read inside this framework not as a theological imposition but as a statement about the architecture of priors. Any expansion of an agent's prior toward a target the agent's current prior cannot represent must come from a level above the agent's prior — from the system in which the agent's prior is embedded. The agent can prepare itself (by tuning its sensory channels, by acting in ways that allow new evidence in, by lowering its precision-weighting on familiar attractors), but it cannot, from inside its own prior, install a new prior. That move belongs to the embedding system. Whether we call the embedding system "God" or "the wider mind" or "the broader morphogenetic field" depends on the metaphysics we accept; the *causal structure* is preserved across all three readings.

Hoffman's interface theory reaches the same conclusion from a different direction. The interface compresses an underlying mathematical structure; the interface cannot represent its own underlying structure. Any expansion of the interface — any move toward seeing more of what is — must come from operations the interface cannot perform on itself.

Kastrup's analytic idealism gives the cleanest metaphysical reading. The agent is a dissociated alter of a wider mind. The dissociative barrier *is* the agent's perspective-limitation. The alter cannot dissolve its own barrier, because the barrier is constitutive of its being an alter. Any thinning of the barrier is, from the wider-mind side, the wider mind permitting more of itself to be available to the alter. From the alter's side this looks exactly like Aquinas's prevenient grace: the alter finds itself disposed toward what was previously unavailable, without having moved itself there.

Levin's morphogenetic teleology supplies the empirical analogue. Cells in a developing tissue do not earn their fate; the morphogenetic field assigns it. But the cells are not passive — they perform the work of differentiation, division, and migration that the field directs. Field and cell are not in causal competition; they are different levels of one causal structure. Aquinas's account of how prevenient grace and free will cooperate without canceling either has the same shape as this field/cell relationship. PRS-04 in the Stump-tradition wiki explicitly recovers this point.

The Stump-tradition wiki (PRS-02, PRS-04) supplies the philosophical bridgework that Tom Loughran's PRS-form was built to expose: the contemporary cognitive turn lacks the metaphysical framework Aquinas already developed for exactly this kind of dual-level causation. The wiki's recording is Tom's PRS-form re-description of Stump's move, not Stump's own self-description.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel reading of Q.112-113:

Justification is a state transition. The agent moves from a configuration in which its prior systematically excludes certain goal-states (call this the state of being closed to the wider mind) to a configuration in which the prior begins to include them (the state of grace). The transition is structurally a re-priorization, but the re-priorization cannot be performed by the agent on itself. The agent's contribution is to act in ways that admit new evidence — to remain open, to expose itself to what does not fit its current prior, to weaken the precision on attractors that exclude God. This is the *preparation*. The transition itself — the actual installation of the wider prior — is the work of the embedding system: prevenient grace.

After the transition, the agent's cooperation with the new prior is real cooperation. The free will is not destroyed; it now operates in a prior-space it could not have constructed itself. Acts done in this state can be merited, because the agent is now a co-actor in the system that gave it the new prior. The asymmetry Aquinas insists on — first grace not merited, subsequent increase merited — falls out of the architecture: the agent cannot earn the prior it does not have, but once it has a prior, its actions in that prior have consequences within the prior's economy.

The variation in grace between persons (Q.112 a.4 — Mary and the humanity of Christ as the limit cases) reads as variation in bandwidth: how much of the wider mind a given alter is structurally able to hold. This is not a moral judgment on the agents who hold less; it is a structural feature of finite embodiment. The hierarchy Aquinas notes ("a clear hierarchy in creation") corresponds to differences in interface capacity, not differences in worth.

Where this differs from Aquinas: he treats the agent as a substance receiving an accidental form from a numerically distinct divine substance, with the cooperation between them being a metaphysically delicate but ultimately two-substance picture. The contemporary parallel treats agent and wider mind as one ground, dissociatively expressed; the cooperation is internal to the ground rather than between separated substances. The structural claims — God alone causes grace, preparation requires prevenient grace, first grace not merited, hierarchy real — all survive the reframe. What changes is the underlying ontology of how two terms can be both one and distinguishable.

Evidence strength: the active-inference framework is *empirically* robust in cognitive neuroscience (high). The architectural claim that priors cannot self-bootstrap to representations outside their own range is *formal* (the math is tight) but the application to grace specifically is *philosophical*. Levin's field/cell analogy is *empirical* (high) for biology but *analogical* when extended to grace. Kastrup's idealism remains *speculative metaphysics*. Wright's reading of Pauline salvation as inclusion in the covenant people is *historical-theological* (high within Pauline scholarship). The synthesis claim that all of these line up to render Aquinas's three articles intelligible without modification of structure is *the speculative move under test*.

## The central-theme thread

Phil 2:12 — Wright reads *salvation* as inclusion in the people gathered around the risen Christ. We work this out in fear and trembling because the computation is real and the limitations of our perspective are real. Aquinas reads the same verse to make the cooperation-without-competition point: God is at work in us *both to will and to work*, and we work. The contemporary parallel reads it as: the prior is being installed and re-installed in us by the embedding system, and our acting in fear and trembling is the agent-side work without which the installation does not become operative cooperation.

This is the project's central claim, restated for grace: conscious agents are individuated by their perspective-limitation; the limitation is real; the resources for transcending it are not internal to the agent; the transcending nonetheless requires the agent's work; loving unity is the target; the computation runs across a substrate that includes both the agent and the embedding system. Aquinas builds the cooperation architecture with hylomorphism and analogical predication. Friston and Levin and Hoffman build it with priors and morphogenetic fields and interfaces. The architecture is the same shape.

## Where this leaves us

The cause of grace and the structure of preparation are clarified. Tomorrow's volume-transition episode covers merit at the end of Prima Secundae and opens the long Secunda Secundae treatise on faith. The natural next question: if grace installs a wider prior and the resulting acts can be merited, what is the *first* of the infused virtues that the wider prior makes possible? Aquinas's answer is faith, and the contemporary parallel will need to read faith not as cognitive assent to propositions but as the agent's openness to the prior-shift that grace effects. The Augustine-via-Aquinas formula — "to believe is to think with assent" — has its own contemporary reading waiting.
