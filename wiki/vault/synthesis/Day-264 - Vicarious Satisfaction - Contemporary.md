# Day 264 — Vicarious Satisfaction (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (Suppl. Q.13–14) asks whether satisfaction is *possible* and treats its *quality*, including whether one can satisfy *for another*. A finite creature cannot render God an infinite recompense by *quantity* — no finite act matches infinite goodness — but can satisfy by a *proportion* or orientation, as the command to love God "with all your heart" is met not by infinite love but by whole-hearted love. And satisfaction can be *vicarious*: one member can satisfy for another within the bonds of charity. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: how does a finite agent's corrective action count before an infinite whole, and how can one agent's corrective work be reckoned for another?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **how is a finite corrective act sufficient before an infinite standard, and how can corrective work be shared across agents?** A quantitative model makes finite satisfaction hopeless and vicarious satisfaction unintelligible. Aquinas's answer turns on *proportion* (genuineness, not magnitude) and on *solidarity* (corrective work distributed within a charity-bound community).

## Resource

Friston's program is the structural resource for the vicarious half. In Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's program, PRS-06 (how individual agents form coherent higher-order wholes without losing autonomy) makes a charity-bound community a genuine higher-order agent in which corrective work can be *shared*: one member's free-energy-reducing action contributes to the whole's state, so that satisfaction performed by one can count for another within the bonds that compose them into a single body. Vicarious satisfaction is not a transfer of tokens but the real interdependence of nested agents whose corrective work is, to a degree, collective.

For the finite/infinite half, the project's recurring move applies: what the perspective-limited agent contributes is genuineness, not magnitude — the *proportion* (whole-hearted orientation) rather than a quantity it could never reach. This echoes Day 260's threshold logic raised to the relation with the infinite: sufficiency is about the orientation of the whole limited self, not an impossible amount.

Wright grounds the *content* decisively. The corporate solidarity of the covenant people — bearing one another's burdens, the one for the many, the body in which members' actions are reckoned to the whole — is the scriptural ground truth for vicarious satisfaction, culminating in the representative work of Christ for the body. Rohr (PRS-7, relationship ontologically prior) frames the shared Body as more fundamental than the isolated individual, which is why one's corrective work can be genuinely *for* another. Fredrickson's broaden-and-build resonates with the closing point that *positive* good deeds void punishment more than mere omission of evil — corrective action, not just avoidance.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **finite satisfaction suffices by proportion (whole-hearted orientation), not quantity; and vicarious satisfaction is real because charity-bound agents compose a higher-order body within which corrective work is genuinely shared.** This *shares* with Aquinas the proportion-not-quantity answer and the possibility of satisfying for another within charity. It *reframes* proportion as the genuineness of the whole limited self's orientation, and vicarious satisfaction as distributed corrective work in a multi-agent body (Friston PRS-06), grounded in covenant solidarity (Wright) and the ontological priority of relationship (Rohr). It *firms up* the structural premise that genuine corrective work can be shared across agents composing a higher-order whole. It *marks* that the *infinite recompense* and Christ's representative satisfaction are theological — the formal sharing-of-work explains member-to-member solidarity, not the unique sufficiency of the one for all; and that "proportion" must not become a discount but names the wholehearted turning of a finite self.

## The central-theme thread

The motif reaches one of its deepest statements. The perspective-limited agent cannot match the infinite whole by quantity — finitude is precisely the inability to render the whole's measure — yet it is included by the *proportionate* turning of all it is, and, more strikingly, by being a *member* whose corrective work is shared with and borne by others. Loving unity is not the sum of isolated sufficiencies but the interdependence of limited agents in one body, where what one cannot do alone is done *for* and *with* the rest. "Working out salvation in fear and trembling" is here explicitly corporate: the finite perspective is saved into, and partly through, the body it belongs to.

## Bridges

- **Friston PRS-06 (agents forming a higher-order body; shared corrective work)** — *formal, developing.* Vicarious satisfaction as distributed free-energy reduction within a charity-bound whole.
- **Wright (corporate solidarity; the one for the many)** — *scriptural, high within method.* The ground truth for satisfying for another.
- **Rohr PRS-7 (relationship ontologically prior; shared Body)** — *theological / contemplative.* Why one's work can be genuinely for another.
- **Fredrickson (positive corrective action)** — *empirical (affective science).* Good deeds void punishment more than mere omission.

## Where this leaves us
