# Day 209 — States of Life (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas first finishes comparing the two lives, then opens the architecture of the Christian community. Q.182 gives nine reasons the contemplative life "is better than the active" — it engages the highest faculty; furnishes greater delight; is "in a way self-sufficient, for man has what he needs in himself to contemplate God while the active life requires those who are to be served"; continues into eternity; is "the goal of man… not as a means"; is the rest that activity exists to obtain; has the greatest object (God); is proper to man; and is Mary's "better part." Yet the Dominican adds a disclaimer: "in a restricted sense, and in a particular case, one should prefer the active life on account of the needs of the present life" — "better to be wise than rich; yet for one in need, it is better to be rich." On *merit* (a.2), the principle is "friendship with God on account of being made adopted sons by grace"; generically the contemplative (love of God) exceeds the active (love of neighbor, which is "for the sake of" love of God), but one who leaves contemplation "out of an overflowing love of God… to advance the salvation of others" may merit more — so that, in summary, merit "comes down to the degree of charity in the heart… regardless of what state" one is in. Contemplation itself he then defines, with John of the Cross, as "a secret, peaceful and loving infusion from God which… enkindles the soul with the spirit of love" — what the angels have without phantasms and we, in this life, normally do not. Q.183 opens the *states*: a state is "a condition of freedom or servitude," an obligation "firmly established" binding the person. A diversity of states is fitting because God, "utterly simple… requires a multitude of various and splendid things to manifest his goodness as perfectly as can be done in the created realm" (and because, with Paul, "if the whole body were an eye, where would be the hearing?"), and for the sake of order. Offices differ for *perfection*, *action*, and *beauty* ("due proportion, harmony, right order"). And states track *beginning, middle, and term* — beginners, the proficient, and the perfect. The contemporary version: *how does one simple whole come to be manifested and loved through a multitude of limited, differently-bound perspectives — and what, across all of them, is the thing that counts?*

## Problem

Three inquiry-problems. (1) **Why a multitude at all?** Aquinas's most striking claim is that divine *simplicity* is the very reason for created *multiplicity*: a simple goodness can be manifested "as perfectly as possible" only through "a multitude of various things." The problem is why a single whole should require *many limited* expressions to be displayed, and how those many compose into *one* (the body of many members) rather than merely scattering. (2) **What makes a knowing "infused" rather than "abstracted"?** Aquinas, with John of the Cross, defines contemplation as an *infusion* — truth received directly, "without phantasms," as the angels know — over against the human default of thinking only through images (the triangle). The problem is to say what distinguishes received, immediate knowing from constructed, mediated knowing. (3) **What is the invariant across all states and stages?** Aquinas orders the community by state (freedom/servitude), by office (perfection/action/beauty), and by stage (beginner/proficient/perfect) — and then says merit tracks *none of these* but only "the degree of charity in the heart." The problem is to identify the one thing that is measured the same way across every role and rank.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **one simple whole is manifested — and lovable from every side — only through a multitude of limited perspectives, each bound differently (its "state") and each contributing what its position can, composed by loving resonance into a single body rather than a scattering. Within that body, knowing ranges from constructed, interface-mediated abstraction up to received, near-unmediated infusion; roles range across perfection, action, and beauty; and growth runs beginner → proficient → perfect toward a target form. But the one invariant measured identically across every state, office, and stage is the degree of loving union (charity) — because that, not the role or the rank, is what the whole arrangement is for.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps simplicity-grounds-multitude; keeps the Pauline body of differentiated members; keeps the state as a firm condition of freedom/servitude; keeps the perfection/action/beauty distinction and beauty-as-order; keeps the beginner/proficient/perfect stages; keeps contemplation as infused; keeps the contemplative life as the *goal* and the active as ordered to it; and keeps the decisive verdict that merit tracks charity, not state.

*Where it reframes.* "Simplicity requires a multitude to manifest goodness" becomes *a whole is displayed and loved from every angle only through many limited perspectives, composed into one body*; "infusion vs phantasms" becomes *participatory unmediated vs constructed interface-mediated knowing*; "beauty as due proportion" becomes *the structural beauty of an integrated whole*; the stages become *developmental ascent toward a target form*. The theology — adoptive sonship, grace, merit, divine simplicity — is carried, not reduced.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* Scale-free composition of limited agents into a more-manifest whole is well-instantiated in Levin's biology (empirical for biology; analogical for "states of the Church" — medium). The infusion/abstraction contrast is structurally clean on the interface picture (medium). Beauty-as-order has a real companion in positive-geometry aesthetics (analogical, medium). The beginner/proficient/perfect ascent toward an attractor is structurally supported (medium). "Merit tracks charity regardless of state" is the project's strong central-theme claim (strong) and theological. Divine simplicity, grace, and adoptive sonship are theological.

## The central-theme thread

This day states the project's metaphysics of individuation in Aquinas's own theological key, and then states its telos as a correction. The project holds that conscious agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective, and that "when all limitations are removed, the simple whole is seen and every limited perspective is contained in it." Q.183 a.2 is the constructive half of that claim: a *simple* goodness "requires a multitude of various things to manifest" it — the one whole is shown, and lovable, from every angle only because there are many limited angles. The diversity of states is not a falling-away from unity but the very *means* by which the simple whole is displayed; and the Pauline body is the project's loving communal union made concrete — many limited members, differently bound, composed by resonance into one. So the multiplicity the project starts from (perspective-limitation) is here given its positive role: limitation is the condition under which the whole can be manifested perspectivally and then *re-integrated* in love. And then comes the discipline. Across all this beautiful order — states, offices, hierarchies, stages — Aquinas says merit "comes down to the degree of charity in the heart, regardless of state." The project's central thesis is exactly this: the goal is *loving unity computed from each perspective*, and the measure of any perspective's progress is not its role, rank, or even its contemplative leisure, but its charity. The contemplative life is still the *goal* (a.1) — the vision of the whole — and the active life still the resource-bounded work toward it; but the *currency* is love, the same in the bishop and the layperson, the monk and the married. Beginner, proficient, perfect names the computation's progress toward the target form; "slave of righteousness" names habituation deepening toward the attractor; the whole ascent is "worked out in fear and trembling," each member dependent on the order and the guidance of those who perceive more of the whole. Rohr's register holds the posture (growth through tested passages, the reorder deeper than the order); Wright's holds the horizon (the body's final form is the resurrected, communal people, not a hierarchy frozen in this age).
