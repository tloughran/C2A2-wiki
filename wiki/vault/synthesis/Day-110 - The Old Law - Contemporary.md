# Day 110 — The Old Law (Contemporary)

## Frame

Q.98-99 take up the divine law in its Old-Covenant phase. Two of Aquinas's claims are central:

1. The Old Law was good and from God, given at a specific historical moment for two purposes: prophetically pointing to Christ and forming Israel interiorly out of idolatry (Q.98 a.2, a.6).
2. The whole law is summed in *friendship* — both with neighbor and with God — and "likeness is the reason of love," so the moral precepts are about *becoming* what one is to love (Q.99 a.2).

The contemporary question: *what does the convergent paradigm say about (a) the developmental sequencing of moral formation across communities and individuals, and (b) the role of likeness-as-love in agent-relations?*

## Problem

The shared problem: how does an external rule-system *form* an agent into something other than a rule-follower? Aquinas's answer is the pedagogical staging: rule-following under temporal sanctions develops the capacity for higher-order self-regulation, which is then fulfilled in the New Law's interior charity. Without the staging, the higher state cannot be reached.

The contemporary version: how does any agent come to *intrinsically* value what it initially had to be *extrinsically* trained to value? This is a deep problem in moral psychology and machine learning alike: extrinsic reward shaping (carrot-and-stick) is necessary at the start, but agents whose learning *only* depends on extrinsic shaping never develop robust intrinsic motivation. The Old-Law-to-New-Law transition is precisely this transition.

## Resource: developmental staging + broaden-and-build + likeness-as-love

**Friston's active inference under developmental staging** (FLAG-05): generative models are built up by stages. Each stage's priors must be sufficient for the next stage to be learnable. A child cannot bootstrap into abstract moral reasoning without first having concrete-rules priors that the abstract reasoning can refine. This is precisely the developmental ordering Aquinas describes in Q.98 a.6: "the law was given to them as a help… when the natural law began to be obscured on account of the exuberance of sin… it was fitting that this help should be bestowed on men in an orderly manner, so that they might be led from imperfection to perfection. Wherefore it was becoming that the Old Law should be given between the law of nature and the law of grace."

**Fredrickson's broaden-and-build** (FLAG-11 in cross-program index, from traditions/fredrickson/wiki.md): positive affect *broadens* the agent's behavioral repertoire and *builds* enduring personal resources. Negative affect (fear, threat) narrows the repertoire to fight-flight-freeze. Aquinas in Q.99 a.6's "the Old Law conducts men to God by means of temporal goods for which the imperfect have an affection" is exactly this: meeting the agent at its current motivational state, using positive-affect handles (the carrot) and only secondarily negative-affect threats (the stick). This is *not* the modern caricature of the Old Testament as purely punitive; Aquinas reads it as a graduated pedagogy.

**McGilchrist's hemispheric asymmetry** (FLAG-09): the Old Law's external rules are largely left-hemispheric scaffolding; the heart-level transformation Aquinas points toward (Q.99 a.2's "you shall be holy for I the Lord your God am holy") is right-hemispheric relational integration. Both are needed; the integration cannot be bootstrapped without the scaffolding.

**Wright's covenantal reading** (*Paul and the Faithfulness of God*; *The Resurrection of the Son of God*): Israel is the *norming norm* — the community in whom the covenant story unfolds, whose specific vocation is to be the people through whom God's purposes for the world come about. The Old Law is the *constitution* of that community in its imperfect-but-prepared state, structured to bring it to recognize the Messiah when he comes. Q.98 a.2's two purposes are precisely Wright's emphasis: prophetic preparation and interior formation.

**Rohr's developmental-stages reading** (*Falling Upward*): the "first half of life" is characterized by acquiring the rules, the law, the structure, the tribal identity — and this is necessary. The "second half" is characterized by recognizing the rules as scaffolding for love. Q.99 a.6's "as a father issues different commands to children and adults… one king God and one Kingdom gave one law to men while they were imperfect, and another more perfect law when they had been led to greater capacity for divine things" maps onto this exactly.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The Old Law is the *developmental curriculum* through which an agent-community (Israel) and through it humanity is brought from natural-law-only-access (impaired by *fomes*) toward grace-enabled charity. Its mechanisms:

1. *External rules* train the recognition of moral structure that natural-law access alone could not reliably deliver under post-Fall conditions (Friston: priors that are not yet sufficient to learn the higher pattern need explicit scaffolding).
2. *Temporal goods as motivation* meet the agent at its current motivational state (Fredrickson: broaden-and-build operates from the agent's actual affect).
3. *Prophetic typology* (ceremonial precepts as figures) trains the agent to *recognize the pattern* when the pattern is fulfilled (Hoffman: the figure is the species-specific interface pointing at the deeper reality).
4. *Friendship as the telos* (Q.99 a.2): the law's end is not rule-compliance but *becoming-like-God* so that friendship-with-God is possible.

### What this shares with Aquinas

- The Old Law was good, from God, and necessary for the developmental sequence.
- The point of the law is friendship — likeness producing love.
- The law's pedagogy uses temporal goods because the imperfect have affection for them.
- The law foreshadows what is to come; it is not the whole pattern but a stage in the unveiling.

### Where this reframes

- **The "stages" model is now broader.** Aquinas describes the stages as a historical sequence (natural law → Old Law → New Law); the contemporary reading sees this *also* as a personal-developmental sequence: every agent recapitulates something like this trajectory in moral maturation, with grace operating throughout.
- **The "likeness is the reason of love" claim (Q.99 a.2) is empirically rich.** Modern attachment theory, parasocial bonding work, and broader social-psychology all show that *perceived similarity* is a robust predictor of relational warmth. The friendship-with-God claim is the limit case: to be a friend of God you must become like God, and Aquinas locates this in *moral* likeness, not metaphysical equality.
- **Q.98 a.4's gratuitous election is theologically essential and difficult.** Wright reads it as *vocational*: Israel is chosen *for* the sake of the nations, not *over* them. The contemporary reading must hold this carefully without flattening the difficulty.

### Where the evidence is firm vs. speculative

*Empirical*: Developmental psychology on stage-dependent learning. Fredrickson's broaden-and-build is well-replicated. Attachment / similarity-bonding effects are robust.

*Empirical*: Predictive-processing accounts of curriculum-shaped learning.

*Theological*: Wright's covenant-vocation reading of Israel's election. Rohr's developmental-stages spirituality.

*Speculative*: The full identification of Old-Law pedagogy with curriculum-staged generative-model formation is a translation between vocabularies; the Old-Law content is not exhausted by the developmental analogy.

*Theological/hard*: The herem texts and similar difficulties in the Old Law deserve careful theological treatment beyond what this entry can supply (and beyond the host's compressed harmonization).

## The Central-Theme Thread

This entry sharpens one face of the central theme. *Perspective-limitation* is not just an obstacle — it is the *condition* under which developmental staging makes sense. An agent whose perspective is already at the New-Law level does not need the Old Law. An agent whose perspective is still at the *fomes*-dominated post-Fall level *cannot* receive the New Law directly. The developmental sequence is the path by which limited perspectives are brought into closer alignment with the loving-unity telos.

The Wright-Rohr-monist synthesis here is unusually clean: Wright provides the *covenantal-historical* shape (Israel as the locus of the developmental sequence for humanity); Rohr provides the *spiritual-developmental* shape (each agent recapitulating the sequence inwardly); the monist paradigm (Friston, Fredrickson, McGilchrist) provides the *computational* shape (priors-updating through staged affect-positive scaffolding). All three converge on the central-theme claim: the loving-unity goal is computational, perspective-limited, and necessarily staged.

"We work out our salvation in fear and trembling" is precisely the agent's recognition that they are *in the middle of* the developmental sequence. Neither at the start (pure *fomes*) nor at the end (perfect charity). The work is the steady alignment-improvement under the resources available.
