# Day 92 — Theological Virtue (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas asks, at I-II Q.62 a.1–2: *are there virtues whose formal object is God himself, distinct from natural moral and intellectual virtue?* The contemporary version, under mind/conscious-realist monism, asks: **when the agent's perspective-limitation is taken seriously as an ontological condition (not merely an epistemic shortfall), what dispositions are required for the limited agent to compute toward loving unity at all?** Aquinas's answer is the three theological virtues — faith, hope, charity (Q.62 a.3) — and their distinguishing mark is **infusion**, which is the burden of the following question, Q.63 a.1–3 (whether virtue is in us by nature, by habituation, or by divine infusion): they cannot be self-produced. The contemporary reframe asks what we mean by *infused* under a metaphysics in which mind is fundamental.

## Problem

A purely natural-virtue account of the moral agent confronts a hard limit: the agent's natural capacities, however well-formed, do not generate the dispositions required to relate to *what underlies all interface-experience*. In Aquinas: the natural intellect cannot know the triune God on its own. In Kastrup's language: the dissociated alter cannot, from inside its dissociation, generate a stable disposition toward Mind-at-Large; that disposition must arrive from beyond the dissociative boundary. In Friston's language: certain *hyperpriors* cannot be inferred from the agent's own evidence stream; they are scaffolded by the developmental context. The problem is general: **the agent needs dispositions whose origin cannot be the agent itself.**

## Resource

PRS-33 and PRS-35 in the Wright-tradition wiki (`traditions/wright/prs_triplets.md`; PRS-35 High, PRS-33 **Medium** — the register notes it is Wright's answer to a listener question, stated as a contrast-class argument rather than developed in a book) read what faith, hope, and charity actually *are* in first-century Christian usage. PRS-33 states the day's hope-claim directly: resurrection-grounded hope is "participatory but not self-generated," and differs from a secular progress narrative in *source* (gift vs. extrapolation), *warrant* (a past event vs. a trend line), and *shape* (cruciform vs. optimizing). PRS-35 is the register's non-competitive-agency record — building *for* the kingdom rather than building *the* kingdom — and is the anchor this commentary's Phil 2:12 thread needs. Critically: Wright reads πίστις (*pistis*) not as private-mental-assent but as **covenant-loyalty toward the God of Israel made present in the risen Jesus**, ἐλπίς (*hope*) as confident expectation of **new creation** (resurrection-of-the-body, renewed earth), and ἀγάπη (*charity*) as the cruciform, other-directed love that **embodies** that loyalty and hope in concrete community. This is not the medieval scholastic decomposition; it is the first-century covenant-renewal grammar.

Tom's PRS-form re-description of Stump on faith (PRS-11 in the Stump-tradition wiki) reads faith as **second-personal knowledge of a person** — knowledge-of (Francesca) rather than knowledge-that (a list of propositions about Francesca). This explains why Aquinas can say, at Q.62 a.4, that faith *precedes* love in the order of generation (you cannot love whom you do not know) while also placing charity ahead of faith in the order of perfection (the relationship deepens past mere acquaintance into union). Theological-philosophical, not empirical.

"Infused" then means: not derivable from the alter's evidence stream; supplied at the boundary.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

Aquinas's three theological virtues, reframed under conscious-realist monism, are the **scaffolded priors** required for the limited agent's loving-unity computation to be tractable at all:

- **Faith** = the agent's stable second-personal disposition toward the conscious whole. Computationally: a prior on "the relationship-network I am embedded in is real and trustworthy." PRS-11 in the Stump-tradition wiki is the philosophical engine; Friston PRS-08's *target-morphology-as-prior-belief* gives the formal shape it can actually be given from the register.
- **Hope** = the agent's stable disposition that loving unity is *achievable* — that the trajectory is real, not arbitrary. **Wright PRS-33** is the exact anchor: hope grounded in a past event rather than extrapolated from a trend line, and *participatory but not self-generated*. Levin PRS-08 shows that intentional trajectories at scale are empirically intelligible.
- **Charity** = the agent's stable disposition to *act in concert* with the conscious whole. Wright's cruciform agape and **PRS-04 in the Rohr-tradition wiki** (*embodied love as epistemology*; Medium) converge here: charity is not benevolent feeling, it is the lived computation of unity from within limitation. PRS-04 is the record that actually carries this claim — its Solution states love as a biophysiological event *and* a contemplative practice rather than merely a theological concept, with the body as a site of knowing, and it names the connection to Fredrickson's positivity resonance in its own text. **PRS-04 replaces PRS-03 as the anchor for this bullet, by content** (repointed 2026-09-14, not withdrawn — see the scope note in Bridges).

**What this shares with Aquinas**: the categorical distinction between virtues with God as formal object and virtues with finite objects (Q.62 a.1–2). The order of generation (faith → hope/charity) and the order of perfection (charity ranks first), both at Q.62 a.4. The claim that these virtues cannot be self-produced — **Q.62 a.1** (they are "infused in us by God alone") together with **Q.63 a.2** (habituation causes acquired virtue, but the infused virtues only God can give and only God can increase).

*Citation corrected 2026-09-14.* The three sentences above previously anchored the cannot-be-self-produced claim on **Q.63 a.3**, which is a different article making a different and stronger claim: not that the theological virtues are infused, but that God infuses **moral** virtues — a supernatural prudence, justice, temperance and fortitude — *in addition* to the theological three, because the theological virtues alone do not equip the agent to judge and will the proximate means to the supernatural end. The claim is Aquinas's and the id was well-formed, which is exactly why it passed: it was attached to the wrong article.

**Q.63 a.3 is in fact a gain for this commentary's thesis, and it was being left on the table.** If the scaffolding account were confined to the three theological virtues, it would describe a narrow supernatural channel bolted onto an otherwise self-sufficient natural agent. Q.63 a.3 denies that: the scaffolding reaches all the way down into the ordinary practical virtues, so the agent whose end has been reset needs its *means-level* dispositions re-formed too. In the contemporary register: re-anchoring an agent's terminal prior does not leave its policy repertoire intact — a goal-prior it did not infer propagates downward into the whole control hierarchy, and the cardinal capacities of Day 91 are re-specified rather than merely supplemented.

**Where this reframes**: the *infused* claim of Q.62 a.1 and Q.63 a.2 does not require a supernatural-vs-natural ontological dualism. Under analytic idealism (Kastrup) the alter receives dispositions at the dissociative boundary; under Levin's morphogenetic framework, cross-scale scaffolding is the empirical rule, not the exception; under Friston's active inference, target morphologies are priors the agent optimizes under but did not infer (PRS-08) — with the further step to developmentally and culturally scaffolded hyperpriors being this commentary's own extension, declined above. *Infused* becomes a name for **boundary-supplied or scale-supplied disposition** rather than a supernatural-vs-natural marker.

**What is preserved but relocated — and it corrects the paragraph above.** Read on its own, "boundary-supplied disposition" makes infusion a claim about *provenance*: same disposition, different causal history. **Q.63 a.4 explicitly refuses that.** Aquinas asks whether virtue acquired by habituation is of the same species as infused virtue and answers no — and his reason is not that they come from different sources but that they answer to different **rules**, so the mean itself falls in a different place. His example is food: by the rule of human reason the mean is that food not harm health nor impede the use of reason; by the divine rule it belongs to a man to chastise his body and bring it into subjection. Infused and acquired temperance therefore differ *in species*, and the same holds for the rest. Habash's closing illustration is the same point in biography — the ascetic whose diet the classical philosophers would have judged contrary to reason, and which a different rule judges sanctity.

The contemporary frame can hold this, and holding it makes the account sharper rather than looser: a prior the agent did not infer is not a differently-sourced copy of one it could have inferred, because installing it **changes the objective function the agent's whole policy is optimized against**. The mean moves because the target moved. Two agents can display the same surface behaviour — the same temperate act — while optimizing different quantities, and on Aquinas's account that difference is not a nuance but a difference in *kind*. So Q.63 a.4 survives the reframe intact in substance and is relocated from a metaphysics of two orders of being into a claim about **which objective a disposition is answerable to**. This is also the limb that keeps the day's thesis from flattening into "grace is just unusual input."

**What is superseded**: the two-orders-of-being ontology itself — the natural order and a supernatural order *added on top of it*, as distinct grades of reality. Under conscious-realist monism there are not two orders; there is one, differentiated by dissociative boundary and by scale (Kastrup PRS-02, Levin PRS-08). The file's earlier phrasing said the reframe "does not require" the dualism, which understates the case: the dualism is not merely dispensable here, it is unavailable, and every claim above has to be cashed without it. What the tradition secured *by means of* that dualism — that the agent cannot bootstrap its own terminal ends — is kept, on other grounds.

**Where evidence is firm vs. speculative**: Levin's cross-scale scaffolding — empirically firm. Friston's morphogenetic prior architecture (PRS-08) — formal, a modelling result graded Medium; the cultural-hyperprior extension is unanchored. Kastrup's analytic-idealism reading of "infusion" — coherent metaphysics, not decisively empirically falsifiable. Wright's covenant-loyalty reading of *pistis* — strong-but-disputed historiographic consensus in NT studies. The integration into a single contemporary-parallel account of theological virtue — speculative.

## The central-theme thread

The deep central-theme question this article opens is: **whose perspective does the limited agent need to receive in order to begin computing toward loving unity?** Aquinas's answer: God's, mediated by the revealed faith, the hoped-for new creation, and the in-pouring of love. The contemporary reframe under mind-monism: the perspective of *those who perceive more of the whole than we do* — divine or human, scaled-up or in-the-tradition. This is exactly Phil 2:12 (*work out your salvation in fear and trembling*) read computationally: the agent cannot derive the relevant priors from inside its own perspective; the priors must be scaffolded. **The theological virtues name the scaffolding.**

**Rohr PRS-27** anchors the step this thread had been taking without a record. The obvious worry about a scaffolded-prior account is that it makes perspective-limitation a *deficit* — a gap to be closed by better inputs, with the limited agent's own standpoint contributing nothing. PRS-27 denies exactly that: the creature cannot love back the way it is loved, and that asymmetry is generative rather than defective, sustaining desire, humility and ongoing transformation. Read computationally, the scaffolded prior does not dissolve the limitation it arrives into; it makes the limitation *productive*, which is why hope can be a stable disposition rather than a placeholder for information the agent still lacks. This matters for the day's argument because it blocks the reading on which infusion would eventually make the theological virtues unnecessary — on PRS-27's account the asymmetry does not close, so the virtues do not expire.
