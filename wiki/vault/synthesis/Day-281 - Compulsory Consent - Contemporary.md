# Day 281 — Compulsory Consent (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.47–48 take up the consent that *fails*: consent extracted by force or grave fear, and consent given under conditions. Aquinas's rule is firm — coerced consent is no consent; "grave fear that would move a constant man" voids the marriage, because the will, though it moves, is not freely determining itself toward this union. Conditional consent likewise binds only as its condition allows. The contemporary version: **what distinguishes a freely self-determining act of coupling from a state-change merely compelled by external pressure — and why does only the former constitute a real union?**

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is the **freedom-condition on a constituting act**. Aquinas needs a principled line between a will that is *moved by reasons it makes its own* (valid consent) and a will *overridden by fear* (void consent), even though in both cases the person outwardly assents. The contemporary sciences of agency face the same line: an agent that selects a policy by minimizing its *own* expected free energy is acting; a system whose state is simply forced by an external clamp is being acted *upon*. The difference is not in the outward configuration reached but in whether the agent's own generative model selected it.

## Resource

The Friston-tradition program supplies the leading move. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-04 in the Friston-tradition wiki, *the organism as active modeler*; PRS-16, *expected free energy as the unified objective*) treats genuine agency as *free policy selection*: the agent chooses among possible actions by their expected free energy under its own model and preferences. A clamp — a state imposed from outside that bypasses policy selection — is precisely *not* an action of the agent; it is the agent being moved as an object. This gives Aquinas's "the will moves but does not freely determine itself" an operational reading: coerced consent is a clamped state, not a selected policy, and so cannot be the act that constitutes a union *of agents*. The Stump-tradition program enters here: Stump's analyses of the will's free responsiveness to reasons, and of the difference between surrender and capitulation, articulate philosophically what active inference models formally — and the two should be triangulated, not blended. PRS-04 in the Rohr-tradition wiki (*embodied love as epistemology*) and Wright's covenant theology converge on the same point from the theological side: covenant love is *won*, never extracted; a God who coerces assent would void the very relation sought.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **coerced consent fails to constitute a marriage because a clamped state is not an act of the agent's own model — only freely selected policies couple agents into a real shared unit; a forced configuration couples nothing.** What it *shares* with Aquinas: that freedom is a necessary condition of valid consent, and that grave fear voids the bond. Where it *reframes*: Aquinas frames the defect in faculty-psychological terms (the will not freely determined); the monist reframe frames it in agency-theoretic terms (a clamp bypassing policy selection), which has the advantage of explaining *why* coercion voids union specifically — because a union is a coupling of *agents*, and a coerced party is not, in that act, functioning as an agent. Where evidence is *firm*: the formal distinction between policy selection and externally imposed state is well-defined in active inference. Where *speculative*: the mapping of grave fear onto "clamping" is a model, and real human coercion lies on a graded spectrum the binary clamp/select distinction only approximates.

## The central-theme thread

The project's signature phrase is "fear and trembling" (Phil 2:12) — and this article marks the crucial difference between *that* fear and the fear that voids consent. The "fear and trembling" of working out salvation is the reverent self-determination of a limited agent who knows it does not see the whole; coercive fear is the opposite — an external clamp that replaces the agent's own computation. Loving unity must be *chosen* from within a perspective, never imposed on it from without; a coerced union is a contradiction in the project's terms, because it produces configuration without the self-determining act that alone makes two into a real one.

## Bridges

- **Friston PRS-04 / PRS-16 (formal; juridical application speculative):** freely selected policy vs. externally clamped state — the operational meaning of "coerced consent is no consent."
- **Stump (Thomistic, philosophical; triangulated):** the will's free responsiveness to reasons and the difference between surrender and capitulation; articulate, do not fuse with, the formal account.
- **Rohr PRS-04 (theological) / Wright (scriptural):** covenant love is won, not extracted; coercion would void the relation sought.

## Where this leaves us

Q.49–50 (tomorrow) turn from the conditions of valid consent to the *goods* the consent is for — offspring, fidelity, and sacrament. Having secured that the constituting act must be free, we ask next what that free act commits the partners *toward*. Open thread: the clamp/select binary needs refinement for the graded, partly-internalized pressures (family, culture) that real consent navigates — a place where MacIntyre's tradition-formed agency may be the better tool than a clean agency/clamp split.
