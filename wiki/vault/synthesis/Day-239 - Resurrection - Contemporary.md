# Day 239 — Contemporary Parallel: The First Instance of a New Mode of Being

## Frame

The rising. Q.53 argues the resurrection's necessity with a phrase that reorganizes the whole soteriology: the passion "wrought our salvation properly speaking by removing evils, but the resurrection did so as the beginning and exemplar of all good things." The passion cleared the barriers (Days 236-238); the resurrection inaugurates the good. Then the precise distinction the episode flags: Christ is "the first to rise" by a *perfect* resurrection — "the first to attain life utterly immortal" — whereas Lazarus and the others rose by an *imperfect* resurrection, revived to a mortal life they would lose again. Q.54 settles the identity question the prior days left open: a true body, the same body united to the same soul, "entire" with flesh and bones and blood, yet differing in glory — and, astonishingly, retaining the scars, kept "as an everlasting trophy of his victory."

The contemporary questions: **what is it for the same individual to persist through death into a genuinely new mode of being? And what makes the resurrection a beginning — the first instance of something — rather than a return?**

## Problem

The inquiry-problem, medieval and contemporary, is **identity-through-radical-transformation**. Days 237-238 established that what persists through death is the form and its subject, not the matter. Now the form is re-embodied — but into a mode that did not exist before: a body that eats and is handled, yet passes through doors and is no longer subject to death. So the problem sharpens: how is this the *same* individual (continuity) while being a *new kind* of embodied thing (transformation)? Too much continuity and it is mere resuscitation (Lazarus); too much novelty and it is a different individual, not a resurrection at all. Aquinas's answer — same body, same soul, entire, but glorified, and bearing the very scars — holds both horns at once, and the contemporary frame must do the same.

## Resource

**The new attractor.** Tom's PRS-form record of Levin (P3, the morphological attractor formalism; P1, morphogenetic control) supplies the structural resource. A morphogenetic system converges toward a target form stored as an attractor; what Q.53-54 describe is the establishment of a *new* attractor — "life utterly immortal" — instantiated first in one body and then, per tomorrow's Q.56, propagated to others. The perfect/imperfect distinction maps cleanly: Lazarus was restored to the *old* attractor (mortal life, which decays again); Christ is the first instance of a new one (a glorified mode that does not decay). The biology is empirical and in-domain; the transfer is structural analogy, marked — but it gives "the first fruits of them that sleep" a precise shape: the first node at which a new target-form is realized.

**Form across transformation.** PRS-04 in the Stump-tradition wiki (formal/final causality recovered for biology) is the resource for continuity-with-novelty: if identity rides the form, then the same form re-expressed in a transformed mode is the *same individual* differently realized — which is exactly identity-through-transformation rather than replacement. Kastrup's P2 (individuation within one mind) adds that the persisting subject, established as the bearer of identity through death (Day 238), is the same subject now re-embodied. The scars, on this reading, are not residual damage but retained *features of the form* — which is why Aquinas can call them glory, not defect.

**Inauguration, and the historical claim.** This is Wright's territory, and it carries the run's most important evidence-strength flag. PRS-03 (inaugurated eschatology — the decisive event has occurred, its implications still unfolding) is the scholarly frame for "the beginning and exemplar of all good things": new creation has begun, not been postponed to a distant end. But PRS-04 (the historical argument for the bodily resurrection) is, by Wright's own confidence rating, *Medium* — a historical argument that the resurrection is the best explanation of the evidence, mounted against the methodological naturalism that would exclude it in principle. The master index flags this as the live tension with Carroll's Core Theory completeness, and the honest reframe must hold it as contested, not settled. Rohr's PRS-02 (order-disorder-reorder) gives the pattern its contemplative register: the resurrection as the reorder that follows the cross's disorder, "deeper than the original order because it has been tested and survived" — and the scars are that survival made visible.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

**(a) Shared with Aquinas:** the resurrection is the inauguration of the good, not merely the reversal of the passion's evils; it is a genuinely new mode of embodiment ("life utterly immortal"), distinct from resuscitation; the risen Christ is numerically the same individual — same body, same soul, entire — yet transformed in glory; the scars are retained as honor.

**(b) Reframed:** where Aquinas distinguishes perfect from imperfect resurrection and asserts sameness-with-glory, the monist reframe proposes that this is the installation of a new attractor — a target-form that did not previously exist in the morphospace of living things — realized first in one node and (tomorrow) propagated. Identity holds because the form and its subject carry it (Days 237-238); novelty holds because the form is re-expressed in a mode where limitation no longer entails corruption. The scars become legible: in attractor terms they are retained features of the new pattern, not failures of repair; in Rohr's terms they are the reorder bearing the marks of the disorder it passed through — the *particular* wounds making this *particular* glory, which is the central theme's signature (limitation as the condition of a distinct contribution).

**(c) Evidence strength — stated carefully:** the attractor framework is empirical biology; its application to the resurrection is structural analogy, explicitly not a claim that resurrection is a morphogenetic process. The formal-cause account of identity-through-transformation is live analytic-Thomist metaphysics. The inaugurated-eschatology frame is strong New Testament scholarship *as an account of what the texts claim*; the historical claim that the bodily resurrection actually occurred is, on Wright's own rating, a Medium-confidence historical argument, and it stands in genuine tension with the naturalistic closure Carroll's program defends — that tension is named, not dissolved. The episode's own a.5 admission (next day) that the resurrection "cannot be proved demonstrably" and is "an article of faith" with persuasive but non-coercive proofs is the text supplying its own confidence level, and the synthesis adopts it.

Difference from Aquinas made explicit: Aquinas establishes sameness-and-glory by the metaphysics of body and soul and defends the resurrection's necessity by its salvific fittingness; the reframe proposes a new-attractor model for "the first to attain life utterly immortal" and a formal-cause model for identity-through-transformation — while flagging, more sharply than Aquinas needs to, that the historical occurrence is a contested claim resting on a Medium-confidence argument against methodological naturalism.

## The central-theme thread

The resurrection is what the whole computation is *for*. The central theme holds that loving unity is the telos toward which limited perspectives compute, and the standing worry is whether that telos requires escaping limited embodied perspective altogether. Q.53-54 answers no: the goal is not flight from the body but its transformation — the same form, the same person, re-embodied in a mode where limitation no longer means decay. The risen body is the first instance of a perspective still particular (these hands, these feet, these scars) yet no longer corruptible — limitation transfigured rather than abolished. That is the project's wager about persons confirmed in a body: the loving unity does not dissolve the many into the simple whole; it glorifies each limited perspective into an incorruptible particular within it.
