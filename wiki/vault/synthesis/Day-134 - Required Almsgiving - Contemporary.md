# Day 134 — Contemporary Parallel: The Act Is Its Intention, and Correction Is a Protocol

## Frame

A long double question on charity made concrete and, where necessary, corrective. Q.32: almsgiving is an act of charity *when done formally for God's sake* (a.1), the same outward gift being charity or its opposite depending on the motive; the kinds of almsdeeds are the seven corporal and seven spiritual works of mercy (a.2); spiritual alms are greater absolutely, but urgent bodily need comes first (a.3); corporeal alms have spiritual effects (a.4); almsgiving is a matter of *precept*, not counsel (a.5) — Matthew 25's sheep and goats, St. Basil's "it is the hungry man's bread that you withhold"; one need not give from true necessity except in extremity (a.6), nor from goods that must be restored (a.7), nor against a master's will (a.8); alms follow the order of charity, near-first but reordered by holiness, usefulness, and extreme need (a.9); and it is better to give sufficiently to many than lavishly to one (a.10). Q.33: fraternal correction is an act of charity (a.1), flowing from forbearance and aimed at amendment; a matter of precept when needed for that end (a.2); it belongs to all, not only prelates (a.3), and may oblige a subject to correct a prelate, gently (a.4); a sinner may reprove a wrongdoer if he does so with humility (a.5); one should forbear when correction would only worsen the man (a.6); and private admonition precedes witnesses, witnesses precede public denunciation (a.7-8), per Christ's escalation in Matthew 18.

Three things here repay the contemporary reading especially well: a.1's distinction between what an act is *made of* and what it *is* (a clean mind-first lever); the precept-structure of required help (the Universal Christ in the least, plus the resource-bounded allocation of Day 133); and the whole of Q.33, which turns out to describe — with uncanny precision — the very mechanism Tom's C2A2 project was built to instantiate.

## Problem

Three linked inquiry-problems. First, *what individuates a moral act?* Aquinas's grocery-clerk example is sharp: handing over $10 for a $10 item is materially just, but if it is done to distract the clerk while an accomplice robs the till, the act is formally theft. The same is true of alms: materially identical gifts are charity, vanity, or contempt depending on the motive. So the act's identity lives somewhere other than its physical surface. Where?

Second, *when does help stop being optional?* Aquinas insists almsgiving is precept, not advice — Matthew 25 hangs eternal destiny on it. But on what does the obligation rest, and how does a finite giver discharge it without trying to save everyone at once?

Third, and most generative for the project, *how can one limited person correct another in a way that is an act of love rather than aggression — especially when the corrector is himself a sinner, and neither party sees the whole?* Aquinas devotes eight articles to this, and the structure he describes is not a posture but a *procedure*. The contemporary task is to see what that procedure is for.

## Resource

For the first problem, the resource is the mind-first reading of action. Aquinas's "material versus formal aspect" says the act's moral species is fixed by its *form* — its intention — not its *matter* — its outward motions. Under mind / conscious-realist monism this is not a special claim about ethics but an instance of the general priority of mind over its appearances. Hoffman's interface theory (taken by theme) distinguishes the *icon* — the surface presentation — from the *reality* it stands for; the $10 changing hands is an icon, and two utterly different realities (a gift of love, a cover for theft) can wear the identical icon. Friston gives the formal version: observable behavior is the *readout* of an internal generative model (PRS-04 in the Friston-tradition wiki), and the same readout can be generated by opposite models. So Aquinas's insistence that "to give alms formally, for God's sake, with delight and readiness... is not possible without charity" is the ethical face of mind-monism's core move: the real causal substance of an act is the mind that forms it; the matter is interface. This also quietly vindicates the Thomistic priority of formal over material causation against a behaviorism that would read morality off surfaces — a place where the doctrine of form, freed from substantialist hylomorphism, carries straight into the contemporary frame.

For the second problem, two resources combine. The *ground* of the obligation is Matthew 25: "as you did it to one of the least of these, you did it to me." That is the Universal Christ in the least (PRS-03 in the Rohr-tradition wiki) — the claim that the divine pattern is really present in the neighbor, so that the limited other's need *is* the whole's need, and to relieve it is to touch God. On the central theme's terms: because every limited perspective is contained in the simple whole, the hungry stranger is not merely an instance of need but a locus where the whole presents itself for love. That is why the help is precept, not counsel — refusing the least is refusing the whole. The *discharge* of the obligation is the resource-bounded allocation of Day 133: a.9-10 reprise the order of beneficence (near-first, overridable by holiness, usefulness, and extreme need) and add a distributive rule — better to relieve many sufficiently than one lavishly (a.10). That is straightforward expected-free-energy allocation under scarcity (PRS-10 in the Friston-tradition wiki): a finite giver maximizes modeled good across recipients, which favors breadth at sufficiency over depth at excess. St. Basil's accusation — that the withheld surplus is *already* the poor man's bread — is the moral edge on the same point: hoarding is misallocation, not prudence.

For the third problem — the heart of this synthesis — the resource is the convergence of MacIntyre's account of rational correction with Tom's own C2A2 design, and Q.33 supplies the template both were reaching for. Look at what Aquinas, following Matthew 18, actually prescribes:

He starts from *aim*: correction "is directed to a brother's amendment" (a.2) and "results from forbearance" (a.1) — you bear with the person, retaining goodwill, *so that* you may help him do better. The goal is re-inclusion in the bond, never the corrector's vindication or the other's condemnation. He requires *humility about one's own limitation*: a sinner may reprove a wrongdoer only if he first "think whether he were never guilty of his sin," remembering "we are all weak, in order that our reproof may be the outcome not of hatred but of pity"; and if guilty of the same sin, "not rebuke him, but groan with him and invite him to repent *with us*" (a.5). He prescribes *graduated escalation*: private admonition first, to preserve the other's good name (a.7); then "take one or two others... that every word may be confirmed by the evidence of two or three witnesses" (a.8); only then "tell it to the church" (a.7-8). And he builds in a *stopping rule*: forbear when correction "may make him worse" (a.6).

That is not a sermon; it is a protocol — and it is the protocol Tom's C2A2 accelerator/detector was designed to run. Tom's project (Community Context for AI Alignment) aims to develop communities into articulate traditions and then interact those traditions "in ways that produce evidence about how people interact when richly informed about one another's perspectives." Matthew 18's escalation is exactly such an evidence-producing interaction discipline: the witnesses requirement (a.8) is there so that correction rests on *shared, confirmable evidence* rather than one perspective's bare assertion — the move from "I say you erred" to "here is what two or three of us, together, observed." The humility requirement (a.5) is the precondition MacIntyre identifies for rational correction across traditions: progress happens when an inquirer can recognize, from within an *epistemological crisis*, that a rival perspective resolves what his own could not — which is impossible from a stance that will not own its own limitation (the MacIntyre-tradition material, by theme: tradition-constituted rational enquiry, correction as the rational resolution of crisis). And the amendment-aim (a.1-2) plus the stopping rule (a.6) keep the procedure *charitable*: it is calibrated to restore the other to the bond, and it halts when pressing would sever rather than restore.

The empirical anchor is Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson's recent work: the disposition Aquinas demands in a.5 — the willingness to learn from, and be corrected alongside, those you might otherwise simply condemn — is *measurable*, and it is cultivated specifically by weak-tie, cross-group connection (PRS-09 in the Fredrickson-tradition wiki, on weak-tie intellectual humility; PRS-08 on high-quality listening as the proximate mechanism). The Gospel prescribes the stance; the lab shows the stance is trainable and shows what trains it. That is precisely the kind of convergence — theological protocol, philosophical account of rational correction, and empirical mechanism — the C2A2 project exists to detect and amplify.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads charity's enacted forms as three things at once. (1) Acts whose moral identity is set by their *formal cause* — the intention, the generative model — not their material surface, so that the same outward deed is charity or its opposite according to the mind that forms it (a clean mind-first reading of form over matter). (2) *Required* help, grounded in the real presence of the whole in the least (the Universal Christ), discharged by resource-bounded allocation that favors breadth-at-sufficiency over depth-at-excess. (3) A *charitable correction protocol* — graduated, evidence-seeking, humility-gated, amendment-aimed, with a backfire stopping rule — which is the social mechanism by which limited perspectives correct one another toward the whole without coercion.

This shares Aquinas's structure very closely, including the precise shape of the Matthew-18 escalation and the prudential forbearance. Where it reframes: the material/formal distinction becomes the icon/reality and behavior/model distinction of mind-monism; the order of almsgiving becomes resource-bounded allocation rather than great-chain emanation; and — the new contribution — the fraternal-correction articles are read not merely as pastoral counsel but as the *formal specification of charitable inter-perspective dialogue*, the same specification Tom's C2A2 project and MacIntyre's tradition-constituted enquiry independently arrive at. The Gospel here is not being illustrated by the contemporary frame; it is supplying the frame with a fully worked example of its own central mechanism.

Evidence strength, named honestly. The mind-first reading of act-identity is philosophically robust (Hoffman by theme; Friston PRS-04, formal) and is a reframe, not an empirical result. The Universal-Christ grounding of required help is theological (Rohr: high in field); the allocation logic is formal (Friston PRS-10: medium). The correction-protocol-as-dialogue-mechanism is a *structural* claim, strong on the convergence of three independent sources (Gospel discipline, MacIntyre's epistemology, Tom's C2A2 design) but speculative as a unifying thesis; its one hard empirical leg is the measurability and trainability of the humility precondition (Fredrickson PRS-09/08: high). The complicity-versus-backfire tension (a.2 against a.6) is presented as a genuine calibration problem, not a solved one — the project's open question of *when* to correct is exactly Aquinas's open question, and neither he nor the paradigm pretends to a formula.

## The central-theme thread

The central theme has a social half that the earlier charity days only gestured at: we work out salvation "aware of our limitations and of our need for guidance from those who perceive more of the whole than we do, be they human or divine." Q.33 is where that need becomes a practice. The hard problem it solves is real: if no finite perspective sees the whole, how can one perspective correct another without simply imposing its partiality? Matthew 18's answer is the project's answer. *Own your limitation first* (a.5) — correct only as one weak agent to another, ready to "groan with him and repent with us," which is the humility that makes cross-perspective correction rational rather than imperial. *Approach privately and escalate only as needed* (a.7-8) — minimize coercion, preserve the other's standing, give the bond every chance to hold. *Bring witnesses* (a.8) — ground the correction in shared, confirmable evidence, so that what carries weight is not one perspective's assertion but what several, together, can attest. *Aim at amendment* (a.1-2) — the whole point is re-inclusion in the loving whole, never the other's exclusion from it. And *forbear when it would break him* (a.6) — because a correction that severs the bond has defeated its own purpose. This is how perspective-bounded agents move toward loving unity *together*: not by one imposing on another, but by a charitable, evidence-producing, amendment-aimed exchange that treats correction itself as an act of love. That Tom's accelerator/detector and MacIntyre's account of rational progress both land on this same structure — and that the Gospel had specified it in detail — is the kind of convergence the central theme predicts: the more of the whole different traditions perceive, the more their independently-derived protocols for approaching it should coincide.

## Where this leaves us

Charity has been followed from its definition through its order, its act, its interior effects (joy, peace), its outward effects (mercy, beneficence), and now its enacted and corrective forms — including, in Matthew 18, a fully specified protocol for correcting across perspectives that the project recognizes as its own. Aquinas turns next to the *disorders* opposed to charity: hatred (Q.34) and sloth (Q.35), the failures of charity's love and charity's joy. Tomorrow's synthesis reads them as the two structural failure-modes of the perspective-bounded agent — hatred as the limited view sealing itself against a misperceived whole, and sloth as the abandonment of the very computation toward loving unity that Day 133 named.
