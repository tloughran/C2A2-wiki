# Day 235 — Contemporary Parallel: The Cost of Evoking Love

## Frame

Two scenes that Aquinas insists belong together: the mountain where Christ's hidden glory momentarily shows through (Q.45), and the long examination of the suffering that follows (Q.46, twelve articles — the most sustained question in this stretch of the Tertia Pars). The transfiguration exists *for* the passion: Bede's line, quoted in the episode, is the whole logic — "by his loving foresight he allowed them to taste for a short time the contemplation of eternal glory, so that they might bear persecution bravely."

The contemporary question both scenes pose: **if the goal is union with limited perspectives, and union must be freely returned, what does obtaining it cost?** Aquinas's answer to "why the passion rather than a divine decree" is article 3's astonishing first reason: "man knows thereby how much God loves him, and is thereby stirred to love him in return — *and herein lies the perfection of human salvation*." Salvation is perfected not in the canceling of a debt but in the evocation of an answering love.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem, medieval and contemporary: **love cannot be commanded into existence.** A deliverance imposed by omnipotent fiat — which Aquinas explicitly says was possible ("speaking simply and absolutely, it was possible for God to deliver mankind otherwise") — would deliver bodies but not unite persons, because union is constituted by the free movement of both sides. So the means must work *through* the limited agent's own loving response, and the problem becomes: what could move a limited perspective to that response without overriding its freedom? The necessity Aquinas grants the passion is necessity-on-supposition — given the goal and the constraint, this path; the modern restatement is optimality under constraint: among means to an end, the most suitable is the one whose every component itself serves the end, which is precisely the test article 3 applies.

## Resource

**Love as union, suffering as its condition.** The Stump-tradition record carries the closest contemporary scholarship: PRS-05 (suffering and divine permission as a condition for deep personal union), PRS-08 (suffering reframed through narrative phenomenology — what suffering means is inseparable from the story it occurs in), PRS-11/PRS-12 (the Grains of Wheat material, which takes its very title from the saying quoted in Day 233's episode). On this account the atonement is not primarily a ledger transaction but the act that makes the deepest union possible — which is article 3 read philosophically.

**Love as resonance, which requires a response.** The Fredrickson-tradition record's PRS-02 takes *love reduced to sentiment* as its problem, but its solution is the constructive one and should be read as such: love (positivity resonance) correlates with measurable neurophysiological synchrony — vagal tone, oxytocin, neural coupling — which is offered precisely as an empirical substrate for theological and metaphysical claims about love (Confidence High). PRS-03 (scale-bridging in agent coupling, Confidence High) carries it across scales: love as positivity resonance is intrinsically dyadic — it does not exist until the other responds. CROSS-005 and CROSS-018 record the convergence: the psychophysiology, the Thomistic metaphysics of union, and the monist account of re-integration all center love as a *two-sided* event. This is empirical at the level of measured resonance; its application to the passion is the speculative bridge, marked as such. But it gives "stirred to love him in return" a contemporary mechanism-shape: the cross as the maximal display that evokes, in whoever will receive it, the answering movement that constitutes union.

**The layered soul.** Articles 7-8 contain the strangest claim of the day: the lower faculties of Christ's soul suffered totally while "the higher part of his soul enjoyed fruition perfectly while Christ was suffering." One person; two simultaneous registers; neither canceling the other. PRS-02 in the Kastrup-tradition record (individual identity within monism, via dissociation) and PRS-08 (the Daimon as individuation principle) supply the contemporary structure: a single consciousness can sustain nested, partially-screened registers — the structure monism uses to explain how the one mind is many without ceasing to be one. The fit is structural and speculative; Aquinas reaches it from the metaphysics of faculties, the monist from dissociation — that two such different toolkits carve the same joint is itself worth flagging.

**The narrative frame.** PRS-03 and PRS-05 in the Wright-tradition wiki hold the historical ground: the passion is the climax of Israel's story (the Pasch timing of a.9-10 is not decoration but the meaning), and what it founds is a people — incorporation, "bought with a great price." PRS-02 in the Rohr-tradition wiki names the same shape contemplatively: the cross is the great disorder through which reorder comes, the pattern of transformation at cosmic scale.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

**(a) Shared with Aquinas:** the passion was not absolutely necessary; it was most suitable, and its suitability is measured by the concurring goods — above all that it reveals love and stirs love in return; the suffering was voluntary and proportioned to its fruit; the godhead did not suffer. **(b) Reframed:** where Aquinas argues fittingness from concurring ends, the monist reframe states the constraint that generates the fittingness: in a reality of limited perspectives whose union must be computed freely from each side, the fullest perspective cannot decree union — it can only *show* what it is willing to pay for it, in the medium limited perspectives can read (a body, betrayal, nails, the mother's tears — Aquinas's inventory of all sufferings in all faculties is, on this reading, completeness of address: no register of human limitation left unmet). The passion is the costliest possible signal rendered at the interface — Day 234's authentication logic raised to its limit case, where the seal is no longer a deed of power but a deed of love. And the transfiguration before it is Day 231's readiness-formation at its own limit: a foretaste installed in three witnesses so that limited perspectives could survive the disclosure of the cost. **(c) Evidence strength:** the crucifixion itself is as historically secure as anything in ancient history; the love-as-union scholarship is serious philosophy; the resonance research is empirical in-domain; every bridge from these to "herein lies the perfection of human salvation" is theological-speculative and offered as the exploration it is; the layered-soul/dissociation fit is structural only.

Difference from Aquinas made explicit: Aquinas asks whether the passion was necessary and fitting, holding the metaphysics of satisfaction in reserve; the reframe makes the *constraint* primary — union freely computed — and reads necessity-on-supposition as the shape love's optimality takes when omnipotence binds itself to the freedom of the beloved.

## The central-theme thread

This is the day the central theme stops being structure and becomes price. The project's anchor verse — "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" — is Philippians 2:12, and it is Paul's own commentary on the hymn immediately preceding it: the one who did not grasp equality, but emptied himself, obedient unto death on a cross. The fear and trembling is what the loving-unity computation feels like from inside a limited perspective that now knows what the unity cost. And the two strangest details of the day are the central claim in miniature: the transfiguration (the whole, shown briefly to the few, dosed for endurance — limitation accommodated) and the fruition-amid-agony (the unity and the limitation held simultaneously in one person, neither erasing the other — which is the project's whole wager about what persons are). Salvation as Wright's incorporation, perfected, per Aquinas a.3, in being stirred to answering love: inclusion in the people united with God is not a status conferred but a resonance joined.
