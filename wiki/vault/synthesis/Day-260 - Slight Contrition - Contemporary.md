# Day 260 — Slight Contrition (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (Suppl. Q.3–5) takes up degrees of contrition: whether contrition can be *slight* or remiss, whether a lesser sorrow suffices, and how the intensity of contrition relates to the remission obtained. The medieval intuition holds together two things: contrition has degrees (it can be slight), yet even a *true* minimal contrition, informed by charity, is met by a remission that exceeds its measure — grace is not rationed strictly to the magnitude of the act. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: how can a *minimal* corrective movement be *sufficient* — and what makes the response to it exceed its size?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **when is a small corrective movement sufficient, and why might the response exceed the movement's magnitude?** A purely proportional model would tie outcome strictly to effort; Aquinas needs an account on which a genuine-but-slight turn crosses a threshold into sufficiency, and on which the remission outruns the act.

## Resource

Friston's program gives the threshold structure. In Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's program, active inference accumulates evidence until a *threshold* is crossed, at which point a state-change (a decision, a commitment, a shift of the generative model) occurs — the change is not strictly proportional to the accumulated quantity but triggered when sufficiency is reached. A *slight* but genuine contrition can be exactly the minimal true evidence that crosses the threshold into the changed disposition: small in magnitude, sufficient in effect. This explains how lesser contrition can suffice without making contrition's degrees illusory.

Rohr supplies the disproportion. In Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr's program, the *Universal Christ* — grace as the always-prior, latent reality that meets the agent — frames remission as *exceeding* the measure of the act: the minimal true turn is met by a grace already present and larger than the turn, so the response is disproportionate by design. This is the theological content the formal threshold cannot supply: what the agent contributes is the small sufficient turn; what exceeds it is given.

Wright grounds the *content*: the gospel pattern in which a minimal true turning — the tax collector's "be merciful to me," the thief's word on the cross — suffices for inclusion, because inclusion is grace's act met by the smallest real assent, not a wage earned by sufficient sorrow.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **slight contrition suffices when the genuine minimal turn crosses the threshold into a changed disposition, and the remission exceeds the act because grace, already present, meets the smallest true turn with more than its measure.** This *shares* with Aquinas both the reality of degrees and the disproportion of remission to act. It *reframes* "sufficiency of slight contrition" as threshold-crossing in evidence accumulation (Friston) and the disproportion as grace exceeding the measure (Rohr). It *firms up* the structural premise that a small input can be sufficient via threshold dynamics — a formal feature, not special pleading. It *marks* that *what* crosses the threshold (charity-informed contrition) and *what* exceeds the act (grace) are theological; that the model gives the *form* of sufficiency, never the forgiveness; and that the disproportion is the gospel's, not the mathematics'.

## The central-theme thread

The motif appears at its most consoling. The whole accepts even a maximally limited perspective's smallest true turn. Loving unity is a computation under limited resources — and for a perspective with almost none, the requirement is not a large output but a genuine one: the slightest real turning toward the whole crosses into inclusion, because the whole is already reaching toward the part with more than the part can muster. "Salvation as inclusion" (Wright) is shown to be gracious at its lower bound: the finite agent contributes the minimal true assent; the whole supplies the rest.

## Bridges

- **Friston (threshold-crossing in evidence accumulation)** — *formal, well-developed.* Minimal genuine input crossing into a changed state; sharpest resource.
- **Rohr (Universal Christ; grace exceeding the act)** — *theological / contemplative.* Why remission outruns contrition's measure.
- **Wright (the minimal true turn suffices for inclusion)** — *scriptural, high within method.* The gospel pattern of disproportionate mercy.
- **Stump (Thomistic interlocutor)** — receives the threshold-and-disproportion reframe of degrees of contrition.
