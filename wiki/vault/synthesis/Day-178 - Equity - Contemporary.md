# Day 178 — Equity (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas treats two annexed parts of justice. Q.120 is equity (*epikeia*): the higher justice that corrects the letter of the law by the legislator's intent when literal application would defeat the law's own purpose — the classic case being that one need not return a deposited sword to an owner who has since gone mad and means to kill. Equity is not lawlessness but a *deeper* fidelity to law: it keeps faith with what the law is *for* against a mechanical reading that would betray it. Q.121 is piety (*pietas*): the reverence and service owed to parents and country, the near-in agents from whom one's being and nurture derive.

Under mind / conscious-realist monism, equity raises a question about *modes of attention* — how an agent attends to a rule — and piety raises one about *nested agency* — how an agent honors the larger self it is part of.

## Problem

Any community of limited agents must encode shared expectations as rules, because no rule-maker can foresee every case. This guarantees a recurring gap: situations the letter of the rule covers but its purpose excludes, and vice versa. An agent that attends only to the letter will, sooner or later, do real harm in the law's name; an agent that ignores the letter dissolves the shared coordination the rule exists to provide. The problem of equity is *how to attend to a rule so as to serve its intent without abandoning its function*. The problem of piety is adjacent: how an agent rightly owes itself to the larger wholes — family, polity — that constituted it.

## Resource

The leading move comes from **McGilchrist**. In Tom's PRS-form record of his work (P5 / attention as ontological act), the mode of attention is not a neutral spotlight but constitutes the kind of world that shows up: the left hemisphere abstracts, fixes, and manipulates explicit tokens — the letter, the rule as a closed object — while the right hemisphere attends to context, the whole situation, the living intent that the token was meant to carry. Equity is, almost exactly, the right hemisphere's correction of left-hemisphere literalism: a re-attending to the rule *in its context and for its purpose* rather than as a free-standing string to be executed. McGilchrist's diagnosis of modernity — the emissary (left) usurping the master (right) — is the civilizational form of the very failure equity guards against: rule-execution detached from the whole it was meant to serve.

**Carroll** secures why this is fidelity and not evasion. His poetic naturalism (PRS-01 in the Carroll-tradition wiki) treats laws, like all higher-level descriptions, as useful *stories* told for the sake of an underlying good — coordination, protection, the flourishing of persons. A story is honored by serving its point, not by being recited when its point has lapsed. Equity is thus the disciplined recognition that the rule-level description is in service to a good that the letter only approximately tracks; correcting the letter by the intent is keeping faith with the description's actual job.

For piety, **Levin's** nested-agency model supplies the resource. In Tom's PRS-form record of his work, selves are nested: cells compose tissues compose organisms, each larger agent real and owed something by its parts. Piety names the human-scale recognition of this — that the agent is constituted by larger wholes (parents, people, place) which have a genuine claim on it precisely because the agent's very capacities were grown within them. Reverence here is not sentiment but the accurate registration of one's own dependent, nested constitution.

**Stump**'s work on the second-personal character of Thomistic justice keeps *epikeia* pointed at the persons the law is for, so that justice is never reduced to the impersonal matching of case to clause.

**Wright** grounds the scriptural form: "the Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath" (Mark 2:27) is equity itself on Jesus's lips, and Paul's "the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life" (2 Cor 3:6) names the whole dynamic. **Rohr** gives the contemplative form: law as servant of love, never its master — the contemplative reads every rule back to the love it was meant to protect.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: equity is the right mode of attention to a rule — holding it in its context and for its purpose (McGilchrist) rather than as a detached token — which is the disciplined fidelity that a higher-level rule-description owes to the good it serves (Carroll). Piety is the accurate registration of one's nested constitution by larger agents and the reverence that registration calls for (Levin). This preserves Aquinas's insight that equity is *more*, not less, faithful to law, and that piety is a real debt of justice rather than mere feeling.

What it shares with Aquinas: equity corrects the letter by the intent and is a virtue, not a license; piety owes reverence to the near-constituting wholes. Where it reframes: equity becomes a claim about *attention* and *levels of description* rather than only about legal interpretation, and piety becomes a claim about *nested agency*. Evidence strength: the hemispheric account is empirical-plus-interpretive (the neuroscience is empirical, its cultural reading interpretive), the laws-as-stories account is formal, the nested-self account formal-analogical, and the spirit-over-letter reading theological.

## Central-theme thread

Each agent sees only part of the whole, and rules are how a community shares the parts it has already seen. Equity is the humility built into rule-following: the acknowledgment that the rule-maker, too, was perspective-limited, that the letter cannot contain the whole intent, and that fidelity sometimes requires attending past the token to the good it gestured at. Piety is the complementary acknowledgment — that the agent did not make itself, that it is held within larger selves that perceive more of the whole than it does, and owes them reverence on exactly that account. Both are the central theme in miniature: loving unity computed by limited agents who know their limits and look past the letter, and past themselves, toward the whole the law was always trying to serve.
