# Day 181 — Fearlessness (Contemporary)

## Frame

Having taken fortitude to its lower collapse yesterday (timidity, too much fear), Aquinas now treats the two vices on the opposite side. Q.126 asks whether *fearlessness* (*intrepiditas*) is a sin, and answers that it is — when it is a defect of fear: the failure to fear what one ought, whether real bodily danger, or the loss of higher goods, or God. His scriptural emblem is the unjust judge of Luke 18 "who neither feared God nor regarded man." Q.127 treats *foolhardiness* or audacity (*audacia*), the excess of daring: rushing into danger beyond what reason warrants. Fortitude sits as the calibrated mean between three failures now fully mapped — too much fear (timidity), too little fear (fearlessness), and too much daring (foolhardiness).

Under mind / conscious-realist monism the contemporary version is precise. If fear is the agent's weighting of a threat signal, and fortitude is holding a good against a rightly-weighted threat, then fearlessness is the threat term set *too low* — danger that is real goes untracked — and foolhardiness is the approach term set *too high* — the agent advances on a gradient it has not actually measured. Both are mis-calibrations of the very parameter timidity set too high.

## Problem

The threat-tracking that protects an agent can fail in three directions, not one. Yesterday's vice was over-weighting: the threat signal governs even small dangers. But the signal can equally be *under-weighted* — clamped below the level real danger warrants — so that the agent fails to flee what it should, neglects goods whose loss it should dread, and (in Aquinas's strongest case) does not fear the One before whom it stands. And the approach side has its own failure: an agent can be accurately afraid yet still rush forward, the drive to act over-riding the appraisal. The deep problem is that "no fear" looks like courage and is often praised as such, while being in fact a perceptual defect: the agent that feels no fear of death has not transcended its finitude, it has stopped perceiving it.

## Resource

**Friston** gives the formal anatomy. In Tom's PRS-form record of his work, emotions are precision-weightings on prediction errors, and action follows from minimizing expected free energy across candidate policies. Fear is high precision on a threat prediction; fortitude is that precision set *accurately*, so the threat informs the policy without capturing it. On this account fearlessness is a determinate error — the threat precision clamped below the level the modeled danger warrants — so the agent's policy fails to register real costs. Foolhardiness is the complementary error on the approach side: the expected-value term of a forward policy over-weighted relative to its risk term, so the agent selects the advance before the danger has been adequately inferred. Crucially, the formalism makes Aquinas's judgment exact: calibrated fear is *not* a defect to be eliminated but information to be weighted rightly, and "no fear" is a mis-estimate, not a maximum.

**Fredrickson** supplies the systems-level picture. In Tom's PRS-form record of her work, the broaden-and-build dynamic widens an agent's action-repertoire under positive affect; the threat response narrows it for protection. Real resilience, on her data, is not the absence of the threat response but the capacity to recover breadth *after* an accurate appraisal — breadth held alongside, not instead of, a working alarm. The fearless agent that never narrows has not achieved resilience; it has disabled the appraisal that resilience presupposes. Foolhardiness is the approach/positivity system running without its calibrating brake.

**McGilchrist** locates the cognitive signature. In his key, fearlessness of the disordered kind often wears the left hemisphere's overconfidence — the mode that has reduced a situation to a manageable token and so cannot see the danger it has abstracted away. The right hemisphere's standing-open to the whole situation includes registering the parts of reality that *should* alarm. Courage is not the suppression of that registering; it is acting well in its full light.

**Stump** enters as Thomistic interlocutor: fortitude as a mean set by reason between defect and excess, with fearlessness and daring as its two measurable failures — the metaphysical frame in which "the right amount of fear" is a real, normative quantity, not merely a feeling-state.

**Wright** grounds the premise the formalism cannot supply. The biblical "fear of the Lord" is not anxiety but accurate orientation: to stand before the One who holds life and death and *not* fear is, on this account, to mis-perceive one's actual situation in the deepest way. In *Surprised by Hope* the proper relativizing of death comes not from feeling no fear of it but from the resurrection that re-ranks it as penultimate — which leaves the fear of God, and of the loss of the highest good, exactly where Aquinas puts it. **Rohr** gives the contemplative form: false fearlessness is the False Self's denial of its own contingency, the bravado that cannot admit it is small; true freedom from fear comes only *through* admitting finitude, not around it. In Tom's PRS-form record of his work (PRS-53, Confidence High) the denial is given a structure the day needs: the confusion of the two selves is *structural rather than culpable*, since both are experienced from inside as one's own selfhood. The disordered fearless agent is therefore not primarily posturing — it cannot tell from within that the self doing the not-fearing is the constructed one, which is why the remedy is the triangulation Rohr's method insists on (inner report against text and community) rather than more introspection.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: fearlessness, as a vice, is the threat signal under-weighted until real danger and real loss go untracked, and foolhardiness is the approach term over-weighted until the agent advances on an unmeasured gradient (Friston) — both calibration errors, exactly as Aquinas holds, on the same parameter timidity over-weights. Fortitude is the accurate setting: threat weighted truly, approach braked by appraisal, breadth retained alongside a working alarm (Fredrickson), the whole situation seen rather than abstracted away (McGilchrist).

What it shares with Aquinas: fearlessness and daring as real vices flanking courage; fear as information that ought to be measured rightly, not abolished. Where it reframes: the three vices become three settings of two precision parameters, with fortitude as their joint calibration — a mechanism, not just a list. Evidence strength, marked honestly: the precision and approach/avoidance accounts are formal and empirical; the claim that one *ought* to fear God and the loss of higher goods is a normative-theological premise (Wright, Rohr), firm within those programs and openly so beyond them. The formalism can say fear should track real costs; it cannot, on its own, rank which goods are highest.

## Central-theme thread

Conscious agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective — and fear, rightly calibrated, is part of how an agent *perceives* its own limits. To fear real danger is to perceive truly that one is finite and can be harmed; to fear the loss of the highest good is to perceive truly that one's good lies partly outside one's own control; to fear God is to perceive truly one's place in a whole one does not contain. Disordered fearlessness is therefore a perceptual failure dressed as strength: the perspective that has under-counted its own finitude and so feels large. This is why "fear and trembling" is not the opposite of working out salvation but its accurate affective register — the right amount of trembling is *true*, the agent feeling its real smallness before a whole it is being drawn into but does not yet see. Foolhardiness, by contrast, is the perspective that perceives the danger but lets the drive to act outrun the seeing. Between them, fortitude is the perspective that sees its limits clearly and acts well anyway.
