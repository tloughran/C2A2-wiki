# Day 184 — Magnificence (Contemporary)

> Sequencing note: Habash flags that this episode (Q.134–135) is out of order and should follow Day 185's vainglory/pusillanimity (Q.132–133). The synthesis is written to the locus, not the broadcast order.

## Frame

Q.134 treats magnificence (*magnificentia*): the virtue of conceiving and carrying out *great works* that involve great expenditure, done fittingly and for a worthy end. Aquinas is careful to distinguish it from liberality: liberality concerns the right *use of money* in general, whereas magnificence concerns the *great work* itself — the cathedral, the public benefaction, the thing whose scale is part of its point. Q.135 treats its opposed defect, meanness or *parvificence* (stinginess): falling short on a great work, stinting and counting pennies where the occasion and the means call for greatness. Magnificence is annexed to fortitude because doing the great work requires a kind of courage — the firmness to commit large resource to a large end without flinching at the cost.

Under mind / conscious-realist monism the contemporary version is about the *direction of resource flow*. If broaden-and-build describes an agent accumulating durable resources through upward spirals, magnificence is that process turned *outward*: the resourced agent letting its surplus flow into great, generative works that build the shared world. Meanness is the inverse — resource dammed by an over-applied scarcity model, hoarded against a threat that is no longer real.

## Problem

Surplus has to go somewhere, and an agent's disposition toward its own surplus is a real fork. A resourced agent can let surplus flow outward into works whose scale benefits a whole community — and doing so, on the upward-spiral dynamic, tends to generate still more resource and connection. Or it can dam the surplus behind a protective prior: treat resource as perpetually scarce, hoard against danger, and stint even on works it could easily fund. The problem is that the hoarding disposition is *adaptive under genuine scarcity* and becomes a vice only when carried into conditions of real plenty — meanness is a scarcity model that has outlived the scarcity. And the magnificent disposition carries its own risk of counterfeit: the great work done for display rather than for the good of the work (which is why magnificence sits so close to vainglory). The problem is to get great resource to flow toward great *real* goods, neither dammed nor diverted into spectacle.

## Resource

**Fredrickson** supplies the leading move. In Tom's PRS-form record of her work, the broaden-and-build dynamic accumulates durable personal and social resources through repeated micro-moments of positive coupling and broadened action. Magnificence is the *externalization* of that accumulation: an agent far enough along the spiral that it has surplus to commit, spending it on works whose scale serves the community and, in doing so, building further shared resource. Her framework also explains meanness as more than miserliness — the hoarding disposition is the broaden-and-build dynamic blocked, surplus retained rather than spent, the upward spiral stalled at the point of outflow.

**Friston** gives the formal anatomy of the block. In Tom's PRS-form record of his work, an agent acts to minimize expected free energy over a horizon; magnificence is an agent allocating resource to long-horizon, *collective-scale* projects whose payoff is the reduction of free energy across a community, not just itself. Meanness is the case where a scarcity-or-threat prior is over-weighted: the agent's generative model treats future resource as more precarious than it is, so policies that release resource score poorly and the agent stints. The vice is a *mis-set prior*, not a mere preference — exactly the kind of calibration error this bloc keeps surfacing.

**McGilchrist** names the cognitive signature. In his key, the great work — the building, the made thing of scale and beauty — is the right hemisphere's mode: a creation answered to the whole, valued as living reality. Meanness is the left hemisphere's penny-counting, the grasp that measures and retains and cannot let resource flow toward something larger than its own ledger.

**Stump** enters as Thomistic interlocutor, holding the distinction the reframe needs: magnificence is about the great *work*, not money-management. This matters for the contemporary version — it keeps the virtue from collapsing into "philanthropy" and locates it in the *making of great real goods*, which is what the broaden-and-build externalization is actually describing.

**Rohr** grounds the theology. In *The Universal Christ*, creation itself is God's magnificent work — abundance overflowing into form, grace that gives without counting. Human magnificence, on this reading, participates in a divine generosity that is the deep structure of reality; meanness is the refusal of that pattern, the dammed imitation of a God who pours out. **Wright** adds the eschatological warrant: in *Surprised by Hope*, works done for the kingdom are not lost but *last into the new creation* — which makes the great work a genuinely long-horizon investment in the most literal sense, not spent into the void but built into what endures.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: magnificence is broaden-and-build externalized — a resourced agent committing surplus to great, generative works for the shared world (Fredrickson), allocating resource to long-horizon collective-scale projects (Friston), creating the great real good rather than counting the ledger (McGilchrist). Meanness is the inverse: an over-weighted scarcity prior that dams the outflow and stints on great works the agent has the means and occasion to do (Friston) — the upward spiral stalled at the point of release.

What it shares with Aquinas: magnificence as the virtue of great works requiring great expenditure, distinct from liberality and annexed to fortitude; meanness as the defect of stinting. Where it reframes: the virtue becomes the outward direction of accumulated resource and the vice a mis-set scarcity prior — a mechanism for *why* meanness is a vice (a scarcity model outliving the scarcity) rather than a bare condemnation. Where it goes beyond the formalism: the great work is grounded in a divine abundance it participates in (Rohr) and given a real long horizon by new-creation (Wright). Evidence strength, marked honestly: broaden-and-build and the scarcity-prior account are empirical and formal; the claim that great works participate in divine generosity and persist into new creation is theological, firm within Rohr's and Wright's programs and openly so beyond them.

## Central-theme thread

A perspective rich in resources, on the project's central claim, is most itself when its surplus flows *outward* into the building of the shared whole — and that overflow is a movement toward loving unity, not away from the self but through it. Magnificence is therefore not the opposite of the agent's good but its fullest expression: the limited perspective, given more than it needs, building goods that bind a community and last beyond the builder. Meanness is the perspective curved protectively around its own holdings — surplus dammed by a scarcity signal that has outlived its truth, the upward spiral refused at the moment it would have turned generative. The deepest version of the claim is Rohr's: the magnificent agent is imitating the structure of reality itself, a whole that overflows into limited forms and draws them, through their own overflowing, back toward unity.

## Bridges

- **Fredrickson (broaden-and-build externalized)** — surplus committed to great works; meanness as the spiral stalled at outflow. *Empirical.*
- **Friston (resource on collective-scale horizons; scarcity prior)** — meanness as an over-weighted scarcity prior damming release. *Formal.*
- **McGilchrist (great work vs penny-counting)** — the made thing of scale answered to the whole vs the retaining ledger. *Empirical / interpretive.*
- **Stump (magnificence ≠ liberality)** — the virtue located in the great *work*, not money-management. *Theological / metaphysical.*
- **Rohr + Wright (divine abundance; works that last)** — human magnificence as participation in overflow; the great work built into new creation. *Theological.*
