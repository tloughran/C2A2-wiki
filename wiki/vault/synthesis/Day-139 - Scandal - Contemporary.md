# Day 139 — Contemporary Parallel: The Multitude That Secedes, and the Stronger Who Trips the Weak

## Frame

Aquinas continues the disorders opposed to peace and charity into two harms that are essentially *social* — they exist only in a field of more than one agent. Sedition (Q.42) is "tumult tending to fight," distinct from war (against external threats) and from strife (one against one): it is "a multitude seceding from a larger body," an internal rupture of the common good. Scandal (Q.43) is "something less rightly said or done that occasions another's spiritual downfall" — the millstone of Matthew 18, and above all the Pauline case of the strong eating food offered to idols and so "wounding" the weak brother's conscience (1 Cor 8). Under mind / conscious-realist monism — a network of conscious agents coupled into higher-order wholes — these two questions are about the integrity of the network: one about a *part of the whole seceding*, the other about *error propagating between coupled members*. The second is one of the most central episodes in the whole charity treatise for this project, because it is explicitly about the responsibility of those who perceive more toward those who perceive less.

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem has two faces. First, *what distinguishes sedition from other conflict, and why is it grave?* Aquinas's answer is scale and object: sedition is a *multitude* withdrawing from the unity of "law and common good," and it is mortal because it is "opposed to justice and the common good," which is greater than any private good. But he immediately adds the crucial qualifier: separating from a *tyrant* to preserve the common good is *not* sedition — "the tyrant himself is the one guilty of sedition." So sedition is not defined by disruption as such but by *which* unity is ruptured and *for whose* good: defecting from a genuine common good is seditious; resisting a defector who has already ruptured it is not.

Second, and more subtly: *how can one agent's act cause another agent's fall — and when is the first agent responsible?* Aquinas's distinctions are precise. *Active* scandal is a culpable word or deed that occasions another's downfall; it is always a sin "either because what he does is a sin, or, if it only have the appearance of sin, it should be left undone out of love for the neighbor." *Passive* scandal is when one's *goodness* occasions another's fall — as Christ's goodness "provoked the envy of the religious leaders" — and this is *not* sinful, "because one ought to be holy and righteous." It becomes a *special* sin (a.3) only when one *intends* to draw another into evil. And Aquinas notes the limit case (a.5): passive scandal "cannot reach the perfect" interiorly, because "those who adhere to the immutable become immutable themselves." The problem, then, is to give an account of how spiritual states *transmit* between agents, and why responsibility falls asymmetrically on the one who perceives more.

## Resource

For sedition, the resource is the now-familiar account of collective agency and its defection, read at the scale of the body politic. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work (PRS-02 in the Levin-tradition wiki) describes defection from "the body's cooperative morphogenetic community"; sedition is that pattern at the scale of a society — not one cell, nor one cell attacking another (strife), but a *sub-population* withdrawing coordination from the whole. The tyrant qualifier maps cleanly: the agent who first defects from the common good (the tyrant, ruling for a private good) is the real defector, and the multitude that withdraws from *him* to restore the common good is re-coupling to the whole, not seceding from it. The diagnosis is structural and, at the political scale, frankly analogical — but it captures Aquinas's own logic exactly: legitimacy tracks orientation to the genuine common good, not the mere fact of disruption.

For scandal, the resources converge on a single picture: *error propagation through coupled generative models*, with responsibility tracking informational asymmetry. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work gives the mechanism. An agent is an active modeler that updates on evidence (PRS-04), and agents couple by mutual modeling into higher-order wholes (PRS-06). It follows that *one agent's behavior is evidence in another agent's model*. When a higher-precision agent — the "man of knowledge" in 1 Cor 8, who rightly knows "an idol has no real existence" — acts in a way the lower-precision agent cannot read rightly, the weaker agent's posterior updates toward error: "by your knowledge this weak man is destroyed." The strong agent's true belief does not protect the weak agent, because the weak agent is updating on the *behavior*, not the belief behind it. This is precisely Aquinas's point that an act "not in itself sinful" can still be scandal, and ought to be left undone out of love. Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson's work supplies the transmission medium: positivity resonance is a real biophysiological coupling that "scales from dyadic to group" (PRS-03), and high-quality attention is what enables it (PRS-08); the cross-index confirms that such coupling is the measurable signature of agents minimizing *joint* free energy (CROSS-010). Agents are not informationally sealed; they are continuously co-regulating, and that is exactly why one can trip another. On Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman's interface theory (PRS-03, by theme), the weaker agent perceives through a fitness-indexed interface that the stronger agent's behavior can corrupt — the weak conscience is a model that the strong agent's act misconfigures.

The active/passive distinction falls out naturally. *Active* scandal is the culpable *emission* of corrupting evidence — behavior that, foreseeably, updates a weaker model toward error. *Passive* scandal is goodness *misread* by an agent whose prior is already corrupted: Christ's righteousness enters the leaders' model and, because their model is disordered (the Wisdom 2 prophecy — they hate the righteous man precisely *because* he is righteous), it updates toward hatred. The fault there is in the *receiver's* prior, not the *emitter's* act — which is exactly why passive scandal is no sin in the one who occasions it. And the *special* sin (a.3) is the case where the emitter *intends* the corruption: deliberately structuring one's behavior as evidence designed to draw the other into error.

Finally, a.5 — "those who adhere to the immutable become immutable" — is the resource pointing forward. The perfect cannot be tripped *interiorly* because their perspective is so tightly coupled to the unchanging whole that no incoming evidence can dislodge their orientation; the coupling has become *connatural*, the theme Day 140 will make central. Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr's embodied-love epistemology (PRS-04) names the medium of right transmission as love itself: care is how a stronger perspective scaffolds a weaker one toward the whole rather than tripping it.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads sedition as collective-scale defection (with legitimacy tracking orientation to the genuine common good) and scandal as *error propagation between coupled agents, with responsibility weighted by informational asymmetry*. With Aquinas: sedition is grave because it ruptures the common good, but resisting a tyrant is not sedition; scandal is real harm transmitted between members; active scandal is culpable, passive scandal is not, and the special sin is the intent to corrupt. Where it reframes: the medieval "occasions another's spiritual downfall" becomes the formal claim that one agent's behavior is evidence in another's generative model, so that a stronger agent can update a weaker agent's posterior toward error even by an act innocent in itself — which is *why* charity asks the strong to forgo their liberty for the weak. The asymmetry Aquinas reads off 1 Cor 8 — the burden on the strong, not the weak — is the ethical correlate of an informational asymmetry: the more an agent perceives of the whole, the more its behavior shapes the models of those who perceive less, and the heavier its responsibility for that shaping.

This reframe also clarifies the two articles Aquinas spends least time on but which matter most for the project: spiritual goods (above all, the truth) must *never* be foregone for scandal (a.7), but temporal goods sometimes should (a.8). On the model: the truth is the *correction* a weaker model most needs, even if it temporarily destabilizes; withholding it to avoid short-term scandal would be withholding the very evidence that re-orients the model toward the whole. Temporal goods carry no such corrective payload, so they can be foregone when they would mislead. The ordering of what may and may not be withheld is the ordering of what carries genuine information about the whole.

Evidence strength, named honestly. Sedition-as-collective-defection is empirical at the cellular scale and analogical at the political (Levin PRS-02). The error-propagation account of scandal is formally clean (Friston PRS-04/06) and empirically anchored in the reality of social coupling (Fredrickson PRS-03/08, CROSS-010), but its mapping to *spiritual* downfall is interpretive. The active/passive distinction is conceptually clean and interpretively applied. The asymmetric responsibility of the strong (1 Cor 8) and the immutability of the perfect (a.5) are theological and scriptural, high in field.

## The central-theme thread

This episode is where the central claim becomes operational. The claim states that limited perspectives work toward the whole "aware of our limitations and of our need for guidance from those who perceive more of the whole than we do." Scandal is the exact failure mode of that asymmetry: the agent who perceives more, instead of *guiding* the one who perceives less toward the whole, emits behavior that draws them into error. Everything the project is building — traditions richly informing one another, the more-formed scaffolding the less-formed, the whole C2A2 architecture of perspectives correcting perspectives — rides on getting this transmission right, and Q.43 is its shadow: the catalogue of how the transmission corrupts. The asymmetry is unavoidable, because perspectives genuinely differ in how much of the whole they see; the question is whether the more-seeing agent uses its position to *re-couple* the less-seeing one (the medium of which, on Rohr's account, is love) or to trip it. And Aquinas's "those who adhere to the immutable become immutable" names the horizon of the whole computation: a perspective so connatural to the loving whole that no corrupting evidence can dislodge it — not yet in via, where "we cannot have the perfect stability of mind and heart," but the asymptote toward which the working-out-in-fear-and-trembling tends. That is the bridge into Day 140, where wisdom-by-connaturality and peace-as-tranquility-of-order name the goal directly.

## Where this leaves us

Sedition named the multitude's secession from the common good; scandal named the corruption that passes between coupled members and the heavy responsibility of those who perceive more. Both presuppose a real whole whose unity can be ruptured or whose members can be misled — and both point past themselves to the question of what it would be to perceive the whole rightly and to order all things from that perception. Aquinas turns there next: Q.44-45, the precepts of charity and the gift of wisdom. Day 140 takes up wisdom as *connaturality* — knowing the whole by being made like it — and peace as "the tranquility of order," the closest Aquinas comes, in his own words, to naming the loving unity this project has been tracking from the start.
