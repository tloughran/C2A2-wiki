# Day 272 — Extreme Unction (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas treats Extreme Unction under two heads: its essence and institution (Q.29), and its effect (Q.30). The sacrament is a physical anointing — blessed oil applied to the body — administered to one in danger of death, and its declared effect is the healing of the remnants of sin, the strengthening of the soul, and, conditionally, the restoration of bodily health. The medieval question is sacramental-theological: how can a material sign, oil on skin, carry a spiritual effect, and what is that effect at the threshold of death? The contemporary version under mind/conscious-realist monism is a question about *form-bearing matter*: in what sense does a material intervention on a body re-orient that body toward the form it is meant to hold — and what happens to that orientation as the body reaches its limit?

The sacrament turns on hylomorphism — matter ordered toward form — so the contemporary engagement leans on the form-and-matter programs, Levin above all, read alongside the Thomistic recovery of hylomorphism.

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **the efficacy of a material sign upon form-bearing matter.** Aquinas inherits the Aristotelian commitment that matter is potency ordered toward form; a body is not inert stuff but stuff *informed* — held in a living shape by something more than its chemistry. Extreme Unction presupposes exactly this: that acting on the matter (anointing) can touch the form (the person, body-and-soul) and re-point it toward health or toward its final good. The difficulty for any naturalism that takes matter as fundamental and inert is that there is no place for a "target form" upon which a material sign could act. The problem is therefore not first about grace; it is about whether matter is the kind of thing that *has* a form to be restored to.

## Resource

Here the emerging paradigm offers its strongest empirical move. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's program, PRS-01 (high confidence), holds that bioelectric circuits encode *anatomical target states* — membrane voltage functions as a morphogenetic memory of the shape the organism is meant to hold. PRS-07 turns this into a therapeutic paradigm: disease and damage can be addressed by *updating the bioelectric instructions* that coordinate cells toward their target form, rather than by editing genes — demonstrated in planaria, frogs, and early mammalian models. PRS-08 frames cells as cognitive agents navigating an "anatomical morphospace," using bioelectric signals as a proto-cognitive medium that stores and processes information about body form.

This is, in contemporary scientific dress, *form-bearing matter*. The master wiki's CROSS-008 makes the connection explicit: Levin's cells acting toward forms not yet realized is a description of final causality in Aristotle's sense, and the Thomistic recovery of hylomorphism (in Stump's program) supplies the philosophical framework within which Levin's data is "not metaphorical but metaphysically accurate." the Friston-tradition wiki's PRS-03 formalizes the same fact — morphogenesis as free-energy minimization toward a target anatomy, the body continually inferring and acting toward the shape it predicts it should hold.

Against this background the anointing becomes legible. A material sign applied to the body is not magic acting on inert matter; it meets a body that is *already* a form-seeking system, continually re-pointing its matter toward a target state. Rohr's resources name the theological surplus: PRS-03 (the Universal Christ — the divine pattern present in all creation, including matter) gives a warrant for taking sacramental matter seriously rather than as bare symbol, and PRS-04 (embodied love as epistemology; the body as a site of knowing, explicitly linked to Fredrickson's biophysiology of positivity resonance) refuses any spirit/matter dualism that would make oil-on-skin theologically idle.

And Wright supplies the eschatological vector. Extreme Unction is the threshold sacrament — administered toward death — and Wright's program insists the Christian hope is not escape from the body but resurrection and new creation, "a historical event with cosmological significance." The anointing therefore points beyond restoration-in-this-life toward the body's ultimate target form: the resurrection body.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel reading: **Extreme Unction is a material sign acting on form-bearing matter at the body's limit.** What it *shares* with Aquinas is the entire hylomorphic structure — matter is ordered toward form, and acting on the matter can re-orient the whole. Indeed the monist reading *strengthens* Aquinas here, because Levin's bioelectric target-states give empirical content to the medieval intuition that a body is held in a shape by an information-bearing principle, not merely by its molecules.

Where it *reframes*: Aquinas treats the sacrament's bodily effect as conditional and secondary to its spiritual effect. The monist reading lets us see a continuous natural substrate for the bodily side — the body's own re-pointing toward its target form — while marking clearly that the sacrament claims something the substrate does not: a re-orientation of the *whole person*, body-and-soul, toward a final good that exceeds biological homeostasis. Where the evidence is *firm*: target-state restoration is empirically demonstrated (Levin PRS-01/07) and formally modeled (Friston PRS-03). Where it is *metaphysical*: the hylomorphic reading of *matter as such* (CROSS-008) is a philosophical interpretation of that data, contested but coherent. Where it is *theological*: that the anointing heals the "remnants of sin," strengthens the soul, and orients the dying toward resurrection (Wright) are claims of faith.

The honest seam runs exactly at the body's limit. Levin's resource explains restoration *toward* a target state when the system can still be re-pointed; it says nothing about a body that has reached the end of its capacity to hold any form at all. This is precisely where the sacrament makes its boldest claim — that at the limit where the natural re-pointing fails, the person is re-oriented toward a form that lies beyond death. The science illuminates the slope; the theology speaks to the cliff.

## The central-theme thread

This project reads agents as individuated by the limitations of their perspective, computing toward loving unity under scarce resources. The dying person is the limiting case: perceptual and bodily resources most depleted, the capacity to act for one's own good nearly exhausted. Extreme Unction is therefore the central claim in its starkest form — the sacrament supplies orientation and guidance from the whole exactly where the limited perspective can do least for itself. "Work out your salvation in fear and trembling" reaches its sharpest point here, where the working-out is no longer something the agent can drive and becomes almost wholly a matter of being held and re-pointed by those who perceive more of the whole — the Church, the saints, and God. The anointing is the whole's hand on the part at the moment the part can no longer find its own form.

## Where this leaves us

If unction acts on the body's form, the next question is *which* parts of the body, and why — Day 273 turns to the minister, the recipient, and the anointing of particular members, the senses. The open thread to carry: the senses are the body's *interface* with the world, which invites the interface-theoretic resource (Hoffman) alongside the morphogenetic one — the loci of perception become the loci of repair.
