# Day 112 — Worship (Contemporary)

## Frame

Q.102 explains the *causes* of the ceremonial precepts — both literal and figurative — and unpacks why the specific forms of Old-Covenant worship (sacrifices, sacred things, sacraments, observances) take the shape they do. Q.103 takes up the *duration*: pre-existence before Moses (some forms of worship), justification (no, except through faith in the Mediator), cessation at Christ (yes), and the strong claim that the legal ceremonies cannot now be observed *as religious acts* without committing mortal sin.

The contemporary question: *what makes sacrificial-ritual worship a near-universal feature of human cultures, and what does the convergent paradigm say about the sign-signified relationship the ceremonial precepts exhibit?*

## Problem

The shared problem: worship is *neither* arbitrary cultural decoration *nor* purely propositional belief. It is a structured practice that does *something* — to the worshipper, to the community, and according to the worship's own self-understanding, to the relation between worshipper and the worshipped. Aquinas's account locates this in the order-of-creation/recognition logic (Q.102 a.3) and in the figurative/typological function (Q.102 a.2). The contemporary version must account for the cross-cultural ubiquity of sacrifice and ritual without flattening it into a single function.

## Resource: sign-signified, embodied formation, costly-signaling, sacramental ontology

**Hoffman's interface theory** (FLAG-02): signs operate at the interface level; they point at realities the agent does not directly see. Q.102 a.2's "ceremonial precepts had also literal besides their figurative causes" is precisely the multi-layer interface logic: a sign can have an immediate referent (the historical Passover) and a deeper referent (the paschal Christ) at the same time. The same sign-token operates at multiple levels of the agent's representational system.

**Friston's active inference** (FLAG-05): embodied practice updates generative-model priors more durably than propositional learning. Q.102 a.3's account of sacrifice as *recognition that what one has is from God and is directed back to God* is precisely the kind of cognitive-affective update that embodied practice can produce. The act of *giving back* re-installs the prior that *what is given came from elsewhere*. This is upstream of any propositional belief; it is a generative-model-level recognition.

**McGilchrist's hemispheric asymmetry** (FLAG-09): ritual is right-hemispheric — embodied, contextual, relational. Q.102 a.4's "man's tendency is to reverence less those things which are common… whereas he admires and reveres those things which are distinct" is the precisely the attention-economics McGilchrist describes. Markedness is what right-hemispheric attention tracks; setting things apart *for* God is what makes them visible *as* sacred.

**CROSS-09 (master wiki on costly-signaling) and CROSS-13 (markedness in attention-economics)**: cross-cultural anthropology has converged on the function of costly ritual: it produces in-group commitment, distinguishes the committed from the merely-claiming, and trains specific cognitive-affective dispositions. The Old-Law ceremonial precepts on food, dress, time, and bodily practice are paradigm cases. They are not arbitrary tribal markers; they are structured to train particular generative-model dispositions in the worshipping community.

**Sacramental ontology (Rohr's contemplative reading)**: in Catholic and Eastern Christian traditions, the sacraments of the New Covenant are *efficacious* — they do what they signify, not merely point at it. Q.103 a.2's distinction between Old-Law ceremonies that cleanse ritually-but-not-spiritually and the New-Covenant sacraments which justify is the heart of this. Rohr's *Universal Christ* extends sacramental ontology to a cosmic frame: the world itself is the first sacrament; every act of recognition-and-return is participation in the cosmic Christ pattern.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

Worship is *the practice through which limited-perspective agents re-cognize the deeper structure their interface obscures*. Sacrifice is the grammar of giving-back, which trains the recognition that what we have is not self-grounded. Ritual is the embodied right-hemispheric formation that installs durable generative-model priors. Costly-signaling is the social mechanism that makes these practices group-stabilizing. Sacramental ontology is the metaphysical claim that the practice *participates in* the reality it signifies — not merely indicates it.

### What this shares with Aquinas

- Worship has real causes, traceable to the structure of created-creature relation.
- Sacrifice expresses the recognition that all is from God and directed to God.
- Ceremonial practices set the sacred apart, producing reverence and attention.
- The Old-Law ceremonies foreshadowed Christ; the cessation is real but does not erase the figures' meaning.
- The sacraments of the New Covenant are efficacious in a way the Old-Law ceremonies were not.

### Where this reframes

- **The cross-cultural ubiquity of sacrifice and ritual** is now explainable from below: human cognitive architecture has dispositions that make sacrificial-ritual worship natural. This *strengthens* rather than weakens the natural-law claim. Q.102 a.6's "fittingness" arguments are now empirically grounded.
- **The literal/figurative double reading** of Old Testament narrative and law is methodologically defensible without an a priori commitment to medieval allegory. Hoffman's multi-layer interface logic supplies the cognitive infrastructure.
- **The cessation claim (Q.103 a.3-4) is theologically sharp** but the modern Catholic position significantly refines how Christians relate to ongoing Jewish observance. *Nostra Aetate* (1965) and subsequent magisterial documents read the abiding covenant relationship between God and Israel in a way that complicates the simpler supersessionist reading. The synthesis must hold the *internal* theological logic of Aquinas's claim (the figure has been realized) without flattening it into the polemic that has historically attached.

### Where this is firm vs. speculative

*Empirical*: Anthropological ubiquity of sacrifice. Costly-signaling effects on group cohesion. Ritual effects on individual self-regulation (some replication issues but generally robust).

*Empirical*: Predictive-processing accounts of embodied learning.

*Theological*: Sacramental ontology — the claim that the New-Covenant sacraments are efficacious — is a theological position with specific evidential structure (Christ's institution, the practice of the apostolic church, the formal teaching of the magisterium for Catholics).

*Theological*: Wright's covenant-fulfillment reading.

*Hard*: The supersession question requires careful theological handling. The synthesis should note that Aquinas's specific medieval position (Q.103 a.4 on mortal sin) has been substantially refined by subsequent Catholic teaching.

## The Central-Theme Thread

This entry attaches the central theme through the *sign-signified* relation. The eternal pattern (Day 107) is what all worship ultimately points at; the species-specific interface (Day 108) is how creatures access it; the developmental staging (Day 110) is how individual and community capacity is brought to that access; and worship is *the embodied practice* in which the recognition happens.

*Perspective-limitation* means the agent cannot directly see the eternal pattern. Worship is the practice through which the agent acts in a way that *would be appropriate* if the pattern were seen — and through which, gradually, more of the pattern *is* seen. The ceremonial precepts of the Old Law trained the recognition; the sacraments of the New Covenant *enact* the relation; both are necessary phases of the central-theme dynamic.

"We work out our salvation in fear and trembling" attaches here to the worshipper's posture: not knowing fully what is being signified, performing the practice anyway, in trust that the practice participates in the reality. Wright's covenantal reading: the community formed through these practices is the people in whom God is bringing about the loving-unity end. Rohr's reading: sacramental life is the path of seeing-the-Christ-in-everything that contemplative practice produces.
