# Day 91 — Cardinal Virtue (Contemporary)

## Frame

Aquinas asks: *how do we count the moral virtues, and which among them are principal?* The contemporary parallel under mind/conscious-realist monism asks: **what are the persisting dispositions of a limited agent that make its computations toward loving unity tractable, and which of these dispositions are load-bearing for the rest?**

Aquinas individuates virtues along **two** axes, not one, and the distinction matters for everything below. At Q.60 a.1 the appetitive faculty has diverse objects (God, neighbour, parents), so the habits perfecting it are correspondingly diverse — individuation by **formal object**. But at Q.60 a.4 he individuates by the **power that is the virtue's subject**: the concupiscible passions take one virtue (temperance), while the irascible passions, not being of one order, take three (fortitude for fear and daring, magnanimity for hope and despair, meekness for anger). Q.61 a.4 states both axes together — the cardinal virtues are distinct "precisely because of their subject and object." The ranking at Q.61 a.3 is then by generality of object and importance of subject matter. The contemporary frame holds the object-axis individuation and reads the ranking as a claim about *network position* rather than metaphysical hierarchy; what it does to the **power-axis** is the supersession marked below.

## Problem

The agent has multiple appetitive and rational faculties, each tracking different sorts of objects (money, honor, fear, danger, others' due). What unifies these faculties into a single *agent* whose actions are coherent — not just a bundle of context-specific responses? Aquinas's answer is **virtue**: stable dispositions individuated by their objects *and* by the powers they perfect. He is willing to let the count run high — Q.60 a.3 multiplies the virtues of operation (religion, piety, gratitude, each answering a different kind of debt), and Q.60 a.5 arrives at **eleven** moral virtues before justice is even added back in. Q.61 a.1–2 then asks which of that population is *principal*, and returns four.

The deeper problem, surfaced by Gregory the Great in the opening objection of Q.61 a.4, is that those four are *not* independent — they imply and presuppose one another. Aquinas grants the mutual implication and still denies that it collapses the distinction. So we need an account of virtue that explains both *differentiation* and *coupling across the network*.

## Resource

PRS-09 in the Stump-tradition wiki (*virtue as cooperative causality*, drawn from Stump's exposition of Aquinas on the seven cardinal virtues): Tom's PRS-form re-description of Stump on Thomistic virtue keeps Aquinas's formal-object individuation but reads the virtues as stable second-personal capacities — what one brings to relationship with another agent. Stump's contribution is that virtue is not merely intrapersonal habituation but a *relational competence*, especially for the cardinal virtues (justice with neighbor, prudence with one's situation, temperance and fortitude with oneself as a tractable interlocutor).

PRS-02 and PRS-06 in the Fredrickson-tradition `prs_triplets.md` (positivity resonance as measurable neurophysiological coupling, graded High; the capacity for love as learnable and scaling from dyad to community, an Implicit record graded Medium), together with the PRS-05 row of `fredrickson/wiki.md` (broadened attention), supply an empirically backed account of how *coupled* virtue-like capacities cohere. Broaden-and-build predicts that positive emotional states under the right conditions widen the action repertoire (broaden) and lay down enduring resources (build) — a mechanism for *how* habituated dispositions accumulate and couple. The empirical literature on broaden-and-build is mixed-but-meaningful: meta-analyses support the broadening effect; the long-term build effect is real but smaller than initial estimates.

PRS-01 in the McGilchrist-tradition wiki (*hemispheric asymmetry*, from *The Master and His Emissary*): hemispheric balance as the substrate of attentional character. McGilchrist's claim that the two attentional modes (left-hemisphere narrow/manipulative, right-hemisphere broad/integrative) generate complementary cognitive styles bridges Aquinas's faculties-of-the-soul carving to a neuroscientifically grounded one. McGilchrist is contested in detail but his core attentional-asymmetry claim is well-supported.

CROSS-007 in `master/cross_program_index.md` (*is basal cognition, in Levin's sense, a concrete instantiation of active inference in Friston's?* — logged there as an explanatory bridge, Friston supplying the mechanism Levin leaves open) is the cross-program entry this day leans on: Friston's hierarchical active inference predicts that high-level traits emerge from coupling across lower-level generative models; Levin on biological agency emphasizes that competence at one scale presupposes competence at adjacent scales. Extending that bridge from cells to *dispositions* is this commentary's own move, not something the index records. So read the **virtue-as-coupled-network** claim as a proposed extension of CROSS-007 rather than a finding it already contains. Both traditions yield a formal prediction that *single-virtue exemplars* should be rare; *coupled-virtue networks* should be the modal pattern. This is the contemporary formalization of Gregory the Great's chiasmus.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

Aquinas's four cardinal virtues map cleanly onto four functional capacities a perspective-limited agent needs to compute coherently toward loving unity:

- **Prudence** = the agent's capacity for accurate generative-model maintenance and policy selection under uncertainty. Friston's framework gives this a *formal* shape rather than empirical teeth: PRS-04 in the Friston-tradition wiki is an **(Implicit) / Medium-confidence** record — predictive coding and the Bayesian brain as a reconceptualization of the organism, not a measurement — so prudence-as-good-active-inference is a modelling claim awaiting empirical discrimination, not an established finding.
- **Justice** = the agent's stable disposition to render correctly to other agents in the network. Stump's second-personal reframe and the broaden-and-build mechanism converge on this: justice is what makes relationship-graphs computationally sustainable.
- **Temperance and fortitude** = the regulatory disposition of the agent's own affective state space: temperance attenuates pull-toward stimuli; fortitude attenuates push-away stimuli. McGilchrist's hemispheric framework is suggestive but not necessary; Fredrickson's broaden-and-build is the better empirical anchor. **Note what this bullet has done**: it has replaced Aquinas's two-faculty individuation with a single regulatory system carrying two valence directions. That is a supersession, not a translation, and it is marked as such below.

**What this shares with Aquinas**: individuation by **formal object** — that the agent's dispositions are differentiated by what they are *about*, so that there are *kinds* of virtue rather than a single undifferentiated substance. Q.60 a.1 and a.3 survive intact under the reframe: diverse objects still yield diverse stable dispositions, and the reason is still that the objects impose different demands on the agent.

**Where this reframes**: the *ontology* of virtue. Aquinas treats virtue as a quality of the soul. The contemporary reframe under mind/conscious-realist monism reads virtue as a *stable computational invariant* in the limited agent's generative model — a recurring pattern in how perspective-limited consciousness organizes its inference and action. The reframe does *not* require an Aristotelian-substance metaphysics; it does require some kind of stable disposition tracking.

**What is superseded**: the **power-axis** individuation of Q.60 a.4 — the claim that temperance and fortitude are distinct *because they perfect two really distinct appetitive faculties*, the concupiscible and the irascible. This is a faculty-psychology claim, and the contemporary frame does not preserve-and-relocate it; it drops it. There is no evidence for two distinct appetitive powers. What replaces it is a single valuation-and-regulation system whose states differ by **valence and expected-value direction** rather than by residing in different faculties — approach-regulation and avoidance-regulation as two directions within one loop, not two loops.

Two consequences the commentary should own rather than pass over. First, Aquinas's derivation of the number **four** at Q.61 a.2 draws two of its four from the concupiscible/irascible split; remove that split and the four is no longer *derived*, it is merely *retained* as a functional taxonomy — which is why the empirical non-recovery noted below is unsurprising rather than embarrassing. Second, Q.60 a.4 does not stop at temperance and fortitude: the irascible order's three object-classes yield **magnanimity** (hope and despair) and **meekness** (anger) alongside fortitude. The contemporary four-capacity reading silently absorbs magnanimity and meekness into general affect-regulation. That absorption may be right, but it is a loss of resolution, and Aquinas's finer carving is a standing empirical question — whether hope/despair regulation and anger regulation dissociate from fear/daring regulation — rather than a settled supersession. Marked here as **superseded-with-a-remainder**.

**Where evidence is firm vs. speculative**: that humans have stable trait-like dispositions tracking object domains — firm (psychometric replications across cultures). That those traits are *coupled* into a network — firm (Big Five intercorrelations; meta-analyses of character-strength interrelations). That the coupling has the specific cardinal structure Aquinas names — speculative; the empirical character-strength taxonomies (VIA, Big Five) do not cleanly recover prudence/justice/temperance/fortitude as the four hubs. The Thomistic carving may be wisdom about *functional roles* rather than wisdom about *empirical clusters*.

## The central-theme thread

Perspective-limitation individuates agents: each of us computes from incomplete information, with finite affective and cognitive resources, toward loving unity. The cardinal virtues are exactly the *minimum stable dispositions* a perspective-limited agent needs so its computations don't collapse:

- Prudence keeps the agent's generative model accurate under limited evidence.
- Justice keeps the agent's relational graph stable.
- Temperance and fortitude keep the agent's affective regulation tractable.

Cut any one and the others degrade. This is Gregory the Great's chiasmus, and it is also a structural prediction from active-inference networks: hubs that are too sparsely connected lose their coherence-providing function. *We compute our salvation in fear and trembling* — the cardinal virtues are the workable scaffolding for that computation.

## Where this leaves us

Q.62 tomorrow turns to the **theological virtues** — faith, hope, charity — which take God himself as their formal object. The contemporary version of that question is: what *non-natural* dispositions are added when the agent's loving-unity computation gets re-anchored under conscious-realist monism (mind/consciousness as ontological primitive, not as one feature among many)? Aquinas says the theological virtues are *infused*; the contemporary frame will ask whether some computational priors are scaffolded from outside the agent's own optimization loop — and if so, by what.
