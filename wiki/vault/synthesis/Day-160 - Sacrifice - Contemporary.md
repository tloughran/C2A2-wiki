# Day 160 — Contemporary Parallel: Adoration, Sacrifice, and the Body's Alignment

## Frame

Q.84 takes up *adoration*: adoration as an act of *latria* (the reverence due God alone) vs *dulia* (the reverence due to creatures in their proper degree); whether adoration involves bodily acts (a.2); whether a determinate place is required (a.3). Q.85 takes up *sacrifice*: whether sacrifice is of the natural law (a.1, with Aquinas's answer that it is — every people in every age has offered sacrifice); whether sacrifice is due to God alone (a.2); whether sacrifice is a special act of the virtue of religion (a.3); whether all are bound to offer sacrifice (a.4). Together they form the embodied complement to yesterday's interior acts: devotion and prayer named the alignment in will and address; adoration and sacrifice name it in body and offered substance.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem, in both frames: *the religious virtue requires the agent's interior alignment with the whole — but the agent is bodily and material, and the polity it belongs to is constituted by shared bodily and material practices — so what is the embodied and material form of the alignment, and why must it be embodied at all?* Aquinas's answer turns on the human person as ensouled body: the rightly-oriented will *manifests* in bodily reverence and material offering, both because the body is part of what the will orders and because the polity's shared religious life is constituted by shared embodied practices. The contemporary problem, under mind-monism, is to recover the same answer without the substance-ontology while keeping the seriousness of the body and matter.

## Resource

Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work supplies the formal hylomorphism the question requires, in three steps rather than the two this commentary previously took. PRS-01 (Confidence *High* — bioelectric circuits encoding anatomical target states) establishes that form is *empirically* real and causally load-bearing, but at the tissue scale: it makes Aquinas's "outer sign manifests the inner sacrifice" more than metaphor by supplying a demonstrated case in which a pattern rather than a substance does the organizing, and the extension to ritual form is structural isomorphism, not a further empirical result. PRS-17 (*High*) names bioelectric pattern memory as *cognitive glue*, constituting a collective knower **above the level of individual cells** — a claim about tissue, which on its own does not license the jump to a human community that this commentary needs when it calls ritual the polity's coordination mechanism. **PRS-45** (*High*) is what licenses it, and was missing here: it generalizes cognitive glue to a cross-substrate principle on which *any* glue — bioelectric, neural, or explicitly **social** — solves the same coordination-under-scarcity problem, a shared model letting subunits form compatible plans without central control. With PRS-45 in place, ritual as the polity's shared coordinating model is an instance of a named principle rather than an analogy borrowed from tissue.

Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work formalizes ritual, with one anchor corrected. PRS-06 (Confidence *Medium*, label *Implicit*) names multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling — agents couple by reducing prediction error in each other's behavior — which genuinely underwrites ritual as collective inference. Two caveats: Medium/Implicit means the entry reconstructs Friston's program rather than reporting a result he states, and it supplies no anthropological field literature, so the empirical support this commentary earlier claimed is asserted here, not carried by the register. The substance of sacrifice — the offered will as what makes the outer act more than performance — had been anchored to PRS-04 under the gloss "intention as model-target." **That gloss is not PRS-04's content**: PRS-04 (Medium, Implicit) is "organism as active modeler," predictive coding and continuous hypothesis-testing, with no notion of a preferred outcome against which a model is targeted — and the offered will is precisely such a notion. The register carries it at **PRS-16** (Confidence *High*), where risk is the divergence between **prior preferences** and predicted outcomes. On that anchor the inner sacrifice is the agent's prior preference being offered and re-set, a well-defined operation — with the disclosure that PRS-16's own domain is decision under risk and ambiguity in finance and conflict, so its application here is this commentary's transfer.

Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work supplies the second-personal scaffolding. PRS-11 (Confidence *High*) supplies the second-personal category itself; its own material is Franciscan, non-propositional knowledge as applied to biblical **suffering narratives**, so the transfer to bodily adoration is this commentary's step, and what the entry licenses is the category, not the application. On that category, prostration before the divine is the body's enactment of what the will recognizes — the asymmetric relation made bodily. PRS-14 (Confidence *High*, the hylomorphic metaphysics of corporate substance) and PRS-15 (Confidence *Medium*, the non-metaphorical reading of "body of Christ" as a real hylomorphic composite) extend to sacrifice: the polity's sacrifice is the corporate body's enactment of its acknowledgment, with each member's participation joining the corporate body's continuous act.

Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr's work supplies the contemporary theological reframe, and the two entries this commentary had been leaning on need separating and supplementing. PRS-03 (Confidence *Medium*) is "The Universal Christ," and its **Resource** line does say the divine pattern is present in all of creation — which is what is needed here, so the anchor holds. Its **Solution**, however, is a warrant for genuine multi-tradition inquiry, not a doctrine about matter; so PRS-03 licenses the presence-in-creation premise and nothing beyond it. On that premise, the offered material gift is not the transfer of something the divine lacks (the medieval frame had to work to clarify this; Aquinas's answer is that sacrifice is for the honor of God, not God's needs) but the conscious *acknowledgment* of the divine pattern's prior presence in the material.

PRS-01 (Confidence *High*) had been carrying the further claim that sacrifice is participatory rather than propitiatory, and it cannot carry that alone: PRS-01 concerns **dualistic knowing**, and its solution is contemplative epistemology — perceiving unity-in-distinction rather than collapsing into false dichotomies. That licenses the *mode* in which non-separateness is apprehended, not the non-separateness itself. The ontological claim belongs to **PRS-21** (Confidence *Medium*), the contemplative-Christian articulation of the network's mind-monist metaphysics — Universal-Christ-in-creation offered as the lived-practice side of *mind as primitive*, held inside the Franciscan tradition rather than as freelance pantheism. With PRS-21 supplying the ontology the reframe stands as stated: the agent is not transacting with a separate deity but enacting alignment with the field of which both agent and offered material are expressions — resting on PRS-21's Medium, not PRS-01's High.

Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's work supplies the scriptural and Christological scaffolding. PRS-29 (Confidence *High*) is verbatim on the point that matters — suffering as participation in the crucified pattern, not as transactional debt, with meaning emerging from cruciformity rather than from rational closure. Its own setting is pastoral, a late-Wright voice for chronic illness and affliction, so its extension from suffering to sacrifice as such is this commentary's move; what makes the move a short one is that the participation-not-transaction structure is exactly what the sacrifice question turns on. On that reading, the temple-sacrifice tradition is read in second-temple Judaism and Pauline letters as climaxing in the cross — the singular sacrifice that fulfills and ends the sacrificial system (Hebrews 7-10) — so Christian sacrifice is participation in *that* sacrifice, not the perpetuation of pre-Christian offering patterns. "I beseech you, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice" (Romans 12:1) is the Pauline form: the Christian's continuing sacrifice is the *body offered*, the life-policy oriented in alignment with the cross-and-resurrection pattern.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed contemporary parallel preserves Q.84-85 substantially. *Preserved*: adoration involves bodily acts (Q.84 a.2) because the human person is ensouled body and the orientation must extend through the body; *latria* and *dulia* properly distinguish reverence due to God alone from reverence due to creatures in their proper degree (a.1) — the formal mark of the central claim's asymmetric structure (the perspective owes the whole what it owes no part). Sacrifice is of the natural law (Q.85 a.1) — the universal human practice of offering material gifts in religious acknowledgment reflects something real about the agent-whole relation; sacrifice is due to God alone (a.2); the inner sacrifice (the offered will) is the substance, the outer sign is the manifestation (a.2 ad finem). *Reframed*: under mind-monism, sacrifice is not propitiation of a separate deity but participatory enactment of the agent's alignment with the field — the offered material is the acknowledgment of the divine pattern's prior presence in the material (Rohr Universal Christ); the offered will is the alter's deliberate alignment (Kastrup's analytic idealism); the corporate act is the polity's shared coordination through ritual (Friston PRS-06, multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling; Levin PRS-17 with PRS-45, cognitive glue generalized to the social case). *Extended*: the Christological reframe (Wright PRS-29 — cruciformity, suffering and offering as participation in the crucified pattern rather than transactional debt) makes Christian sacrifice's specifically Christological shape explicit — the cross as the definitive sacrifice into which all subsequent Christian sacrifice is participation, and the "living sacrifice" of Romans 12 as the day-to-day form of that participation in the embodied life of the believer.

Measure against Aquinas: deep convergence on substance — Aquinas's account of sacrifice as natural-law universal whose inner substance is the offered will and whose outer sign manifests the inner is one of the cleanest sections of the religion treatise for contemporary translation. Augustine's "every work done that we may cling to God in holy fellowship is a true sacrifice" (Q.85 a.3) is the central claim stated almost verbatim, and the contemporary frame extends it: every action of the limited perspective that effects its alignment with the whole is, by that fact, sacrifice. Where the contemporary frame extends Aquinas: the explicit Christological reframe (the cross as the definitive sacrifice, Romans 12's living sacrifice as its day-to-day form) and the mind-monist clarification of why sacrifice is participatory rather than transactional.

## The central-theme thread

Adoration and sacrifice are the embodied form of the alignment the central claim names. The perspective is bodily; the polity is constituted by shared bodily and material practices; alignment with the whole therefore takes bodily and material form. Bodily prostration in adoration is the asymmetric relation made bodily — the perspective's acknowledgment that what it bows to is what it owes everything. Material offering in sacrifice is the perspective's extension of the alignment into the matter that is also the field's. The cross, in Wright's and Rohr's readings, is the central claim's deepest case: the whole entering the part's suffering to draw it into the alignment by *absorbing* its defection rather than excising it — the theme that Day 150's death-penalty question reached. Christian life, on Romans 12's reading, is the day-to-day form of that participation: the body presented as living sacrifice, the perspective's life-policy oriented in alignment with the cross-and-resurrection pattern of the whole.
