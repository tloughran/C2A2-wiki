# Day 160 — Contemporary Parallel: Adoration, Sacrifice, and the Body's Alignment

## Frame

Q.84 takes up *adoration*: adoration as an act of *latria* (the reverence due God alone) vs *dulia* (the reverence due to creatures in their proper degree); whether adoration involves bodily acts (a.2); whether a determinate place is required (a.3). Q.85 takes up *sacrifice*: whether sacrifice is of the natural law (a.1, with Aquinas's answer that it is — every people in every age has offered sacrifice); whether sacrifice is due to God alone (a.2); whether sacrifice is a special act of the virtue of religion (a.3); whether all are bound to offer sacrifice (a.4). Together they form the embodied complement to yesterday's interior acts: devotion and prayer named the alignment in will and address; adoration and sacrifice name it in body and offered substance.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem, in both frames: *the religious virtue requires the agent's interior alignment with the whole — but the agent is bodily and material, and the polity it belongs to is constituted by shared bodily and material practices — so what is the embodied and material form of the alignment, and why must it be embodied at all?* Aquinas's answer turns on the human person as ensouled body: the rightly-oriented will *manifests* in bodily reverence and material offering, both because the body is part of what the will orders and because the polity's shared religious life is constituted by shared embodied practices. The contemporary problem, under mind-monism, is to recover the same answer without the substance-ontology while keeping the seriousness of the body and matter.

PRS-01 (form-as-real, with bioelectric pattern as a literal causal substrate) makes Aquinas's "outer sign manifests the inner sacrifice" more than metaphor: form is real, and the outer ritual form is the carrier of the inner orientation, with real causal weight in the polity's shared meaning-making. PRS-30 (collective cognition) makes ritual the polity's shared coordination mechanism — the embodied practice by which many limited perspectives align together.

Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work formalizes ritual. PRS-06 (shared generative models) names ritual as collective active inference: the participants' priors are coordinately updated through the shared bodily and material act; the ritual is doing real epistemic and motivational work on the polity. PRS-04 (intention as model-target) names the substance: the offered will is the rationale of the act — without it the outer act is empty performance, with it the outer act is the sacrament of the inner reality.

Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work supplies the second-personal scaffolding. PRS-09 (second-personal knowledge) makes adoration second-personal address embodied: prostration before the divine is the body's enactment of what the will recognizes — the asymmetric relation made bodily. PRS-14/15 (corporate substance) extends to sacrifice: the polity's sacrifice is the corporate body's enactment of its acknowledgment, with each member's participation joining the corporate body's continuous act.

Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr's work supplies the contemporary theological reframe. PRS-03 (Universal Christ in matter) makes sacrifice's material side intelligible under mind-monism: the divine pattern is present in matter from the beginning, so the offered material gift is not the transfer of something the divine lacks (the medieval frame had to work to clarify this; Aquinas's answer is that sacrifice is for the honor of God, not God's needs) but the conscious *acknowledgment* of the divine pattern's prior presence in the material. PRS-01 (non-dual) clarifies the mode: sacrifice is participatory, not propitiatory — the agent is not transacting with a separate deity but enacting alignment with the field of which both the agent and the offered material are expressions.

Tom's PRS-form record of Wright's work supplies the scriptural and Christological scaffolding. PRS-04 (resurrection-shape, with the cross as the definitive sacrifice): the temple-sacrifice tradition is read in second-temple Judaism and Pauline letters as climaxing in the cross — the singular sacrifice that fulfills and ends the sacrificial system (Hebrews 7-10) — so Christian sacrifice is participation in *that* sacrifice, not the perpetuation of pre-Christian offering patterns. "I beseech you, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice" (Romans 12:1) is the Pauline form: the Christian's continuing sacrifice is the *body offered*, the life-policy oriented in alignment with the cross-and-resurrection pattern.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed contemporary parallel preserves Q.84-85 substantially. *Preserved*: adoration involves bodily acts (Q.84 a.2) because the human person is ensouled body and the orientation must extend through the body; *latria* and *dulia* properly distinguish reverence due to God alone from reverence due to creatures in their proper degree (a.1) — the formal mark of the central claim's asymmetric structure (the perspective owes the whole what it owes no part). Sacrifice is of the natural law (Q.85 a.1) — the universal human practice of offering material gifts in religious acknowledgment reflects something real about the agent-whole relation; sacrifice is due to God alone (a.2); the inner sacrifice (the offered will) is the substance, the outer sign is the manifestation (a.2 ad finem). *Reframed*: under mind-monism, sacrifice is not propitiation of a separate deity but participatory enactment of the agent's alignment with the field — the offered material is the acknowledgment of the divine pattern's prior presence in the material (Rohr Universal Christ); the offered will is the alter's deliberate alignment (Kastrup's analytic idealism); the corporate act is the polity's shared coordination through ritual (Friston shared generative models; Levin collective cognition). *Extended*: the Christological reframe (Wright PRS-04) makes Christian sacrifice's specifically Christological shape explicit — the cross as the definitive sacrifice into which all subsequent Christian sacrifice is participation, and the "living sacrifice" of Romans 12 as the day-to-day form of that participation in the embodied life of the believer.

Measure against Aquinas: deep convergence on substance — Aquinas's account of sacrifice as natural-law universal whose inner substance is the offered will and whose outer sign manifests the inner is one of the cleanest sections of the religion treatise for contemporary translation. Augustine's "every work done that we may cling to God in holy fellowship is a true sacrifice" (Q.85 a.3) is the central claim stated almost verbatim, and the contemporary frame extends it: every action of the limited perspective that effects its alignment with the whole is, by that fact, sacrifice. Where the contemporary frame extends Aquinas: the explicit Christological reframe (the cross as the definitive sacrifice, Romans 12's living sacrifice as its day-to-day form) and the mind-monist clarification of why sacrifice is participatory rather than transactional.

## The central-theme thread

Adoration and sacrifice are the embodied form of the alignment the central claim names. The perspective is bodily; the polity is constituted by shared bodily and material practices; alignment with the whole therefore takes bodily and material form. Bodily prostration in adoration is the asymmetric relation made bodily — the perspective's acknowledgment that what it bows to is what it owes everything. Material offering in sacrifice is the perspective's extension of the alignment into the matter that is also the field's. The cross, in Wright's and Rohr's readings, is the central claim's deepest case: the whole entering the part's suffering to draw it into the alignment by *absorbing* its defection rather than excising it — the theme that Day 150's death-penalty question reached. Christian life, on Romans 12's reading, is the day-to-day form of that participation: the body presented as living sacrifice, the perspective's life-policy oriented in alignment with the cross-and-resurrection pattern of the whole.
