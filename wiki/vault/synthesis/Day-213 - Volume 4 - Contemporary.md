# Day 213 — Volume 4: The Fitness and Mode of the Incarnation (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

The *Summa* turns its largest page. Volume 4 opens the Tertia Pars, and with it the treatise on the Incarnation — and the whole motion of the project reverses. For two hundred days a limited human perspective has been climbing toward a whole it cannot yet see; now the whole descends into a limited perspective. Q.1 asks whether the Incarnation was *fitting*, and Aquinas answers with the principle the host renders in physics: *bonum diffusivum sui*, the good communicates itself — mass gravitates, the sun reaches the Oort cloud, a bonfire makes the room like itself, a mature life begets another like itself — so it is fitting that Goodness itself self-communicate "in the most perfect way possible," by personally entering its creation. It was not *absolutely* necessary but *expedient* (a.2, "necessary the way a plane is necessary to reach Japan"), ordered to remedy sin (a.3-4), and came "in the fullness of time" (a.5-6) — late enough that the race knew its need, not so late that it despaired. Q.2 then asks *how* the union works, and proceeds by elimination: not *arrangement* (a chair, things merely rearranged); not *chemical combination* (hydrogen + oxygen → water, where the components survive only virtually and a new third thing appears — impossible, for God cannot change and Christ's humanity must remain); not *form and matter* (the divine cannot be the form of a body). The union is in the **Person**: "two natures and one hypostasis" (Damascene), the human nature subsisting not in its own personality but "in something more perfect, which is the Person of the Word." The union is "not really in God, but only in our way of thinking" — God is united to the creature "without any change in Him" (a.7); it is the *greatest* of unions in respect of *that in which* the natures are joined (a.9); and it came wholly *by grace*, unmerited (a.10-11): "from the beginning of His conception this man was truly the Son of God." The contemporary version: *what is it for the unlimited to take on a limited perspective without ceasing to be unlimited and without confusing the two?*

## Problem

Four inquiry-problems, and they reach the metaphysical floor. (1) **Why should the good descend at all?** The *bonum diffusivum* argument claims self-communication is intrinsic to goodness. The problem is whether "the good propagates itself" is a real structural principle — visible in nature as Aquinas's analogies suggest — or only a pious metaphor; and if real, what it is. (2) **Why fitting and not necessary?** Aquinas is careful: the Incarnation is expedient, not forced. The problem is to state the *kind* of necessity that holds when a perspective could in principle be drawn to the whole otherwise, but is in fact best drawn this way. (3) **How can two natures be one person without mixture, accident, or composition-by-form?** This is the metaphysical core, and Aquinas reaches it by killing every model on which "union" means *blending* or *adjacency*. The problem is to give a positive account of a union that joins the maximally unlike without confusing them or changing the greater. (4) **Why is the union "not really in God"?** The asymmetry is striking: the creature is *really* changed; God is not. The problem is what kind of relation is real on one side and "only in our way of thinking" on the other.

## Resource

For (1), the lead resources are Rohr and Levin. Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr (PRS-03, the Universal Christ — the one divine pattern present in and self-communicating to all creation) carries the *cosmic scope* of the Incarnation, and it reads the *bonum diffusivum* not as a metaphor but as the central structural claim: the good is the kind of thing that propagates, and the Incarnation is its maximal self-communication. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin (PRS-03, the morphological attractor; form-bearing matter) grounds Aquinas's own analogies: the "mature thing diffuses another like itself" is morphogenesis — form propagating across a substrate toward a target — and the gravity/fire imagery is field-diffusion of order. The good's self-communication, on this reading, is continuous with the way form scales and reproduces itself in Levin's biology (CROSS-008/019, final causality as the not-yet-realized form drawing efficient causes). For (2), the resource is Wright's critical realism. Tom's PRS-form record of Wright (PRS-01, critical realism — reality is independent of the knower but all knowing is perspectival and the case must be made on the evidence) supplies the modal grammar: "fitting, not necessary" is the shape of an *abductive* best-explanation under the actual condition, not a deduction from first principles — the Incarnation is *expedient* the way the best available account is warranted without being forced, which is exactly how Wright argues the resurrection (PRS-04). For (3), the metaphysical core, the most exact resource is Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup (PRS-02, individuation as a dissociative boundary) read alongside Stump's hylomorphism. Kastrup gives the only contemporary model in the set of a *single subject* genuinely sustaining a *distinct point of view* without that point of view being a second subject — a dissociated alter within one mind-at-large. That is structurally what the hypostatic union requires: one Person (one subject, the Word) in whom a complete human nature subsists *without being a second person*. And Tom's PRS-form record of Stump (PRS-14/PRS-15, the hylomorphic metaphysics of corporate substance — a real form that organizes its constituents "without dissolving their personhood") supplies the unity-without-confusion that Aquinas insists on: the union joins the maximally unlike (CROSS-008, Stump's Thomism grounding real teleology) into one subsisting being while each nature keeps "its natural property still remaining." For (4), the asymmetry, the resource is Hoffman and Kastrup together (CROSS-014, both consciousness-primary). Hoffman's interface theory — the appearance is not the underlying reality, and what shows up *in* the interface need not correspond to a change *in* what underlies it — is precisely the grammar of "really in the creature, only in our way of thinking in God": the relation is real on the side of the appearance/creature and a non-change on the side of the ground. Kastrup's mind-at-large taking a dissociated perspective at no cost to itself says the same from the idealist side.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the Incarnation is the unlimited perspective taking on a complete limited one — without ceasing to be unlimited and without confusing the two natures. It is the type-case of the project's central claim that the whole contains every limited perspective without dissolving it: in Christ the containment is maximal and personal. The bonum diffusivum is read as the real self-propagation of the good — form and order communicating themselves across scales, of which the Incarnation is the perfect instance — so the descent of the good is not a metaphor but the structural signature of goodness. The union is in the Person, not by mixture, accident, or form-matter, because it is the joining of the maximally unlike into one subject in whom a distinct nature genuinely subsists without becoming a second subject — the unity-in-distinction the project has been trying to think. And the union is "not really in God" because it is the asymmetry of a ground that sustains a real appearance at no change to itself: real on the side of the assumed perspective, a non-change on the side of the whole. Yet the hypostatic union remains the greatest and unique union — not one dissociation among many, but the maximal case, where the One that contains all perspectives takes one of them as its own.**

*What it shares with Aquinas.* It keeps the *bonum diffusivum* as the reason of fitness; keeps "fitting, not necessary" as expediency under the actual condition; keeps the elimination of arrangement, mixture, and form-matter; keeps the union *in the Person*, two natures unconfused, one hypostasis; keeps the asymmetry ("no change in God"); keeps the union as the *greatest* in respect of that in which the natures are joined; and keeps it *by grace*, unmerited, the suppositum the Word from conception.

*Where it reframes.* The physics analogies become a *literal* claim about the propagation of form/order (Levin, field-diffusion) rather than mere illustration; "fitting, not necessary" becomes the modal grammar of *abductive best explanation* (Wright); the hypostatic union becomes the *exemplar instance* of perspective-containment that the whole project generalizes — the Incarnation as paradigm rather than exception; and the asymmetry becomes the *interface/dissociation* asymmetry of a ground and its appearance.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The *bonum diffusivum*-as-real-propagation reading is analogical with biological support (medium) and theological. "Fitting, not necessary" as abduction is a clean structural fit (medium-strong). The hypostatic-union-as-type-case is a strong *analogy* and theological — and it carries a sharp guardrail: the reading must *not* reduce the Incarnation to ordinary dissociation. Aquinas's a.9 is decisive here and the contemporary reading honors it — the union is the *greatest*, unique, maximal; on the monist picture it is the one case where the whole takes a limited perspective *as its own person*, which no ordinary individuation does. The "not really in God" asymmetry as interface/dissociation is metaphysical analogy and speculative (medium). The atonement, the grace of union, the unmerited union, and "God made man that man might be made God" are carried as theology, not reduced to metaphysics.

## The central-theme thread

This is the day the project meets its own thesis wearing the face of God. The central claim is that "conscious agents are individuated by the limitations of their perspective; when all limitations are removed, the simple whole is seen and every limited perspective is contained in it." For two hundred days the *Summa* has shown the limited perspective working its way *up* — through virtue, gift, the contemplative ascent, the self-offering of the vows. Today the motion reverses and the whole comes *down*: the unlimited takes a limited perspective into itself, not by ceasing to be the whole but by containing the part *as its own person*. "God was made man that man might be made God" is the project's containment claim spoken from the top down — and the hypostatic union is its exact metaphysical type: two natures, one Person, *without confusion*. This is the unity-in-distinction the project keeps reaching for and the reason Aquinas's elimination of the other models matters so much to it: the whole does not contain the part by *blending* it (chemical combination — the part would be lost, only "virtually present"), nor by mere *adjacency* (arrangement — the part would stay separate), but by a real personal union in which the part keeps "its natural property still remaining" and yet subsists in the greater. The *bonum diffusivum* then names *why* loving union is the telos at all: the good is the kind of thing that communicates itself, so a universe of limited perspectives drawn toward loving unity is a universe in which the good is doing what the good does — propagating, scaling, making the room like the bonfire. And the asymmetry — "no change in God" — is the project's deepest consolation and its discipline at once: the whole loses nothing by containing the parts; the change, the cost, the "fear and trembling," is all on the side of the limited perspective being drawn in. Rohr's register names the cosmic scope (the Universal Christ, the one pattern self-communicating to all creation, of which this Person is the maximal instance); Wright's guards the historical particularity and the horizon (this is not a timeless idea but an event "in the fullness of time," ordered to the new creation and the people who will be included in it). The Incarnation, on this reading, is not an exception to how mind-at-large individuates limited perspectives — it is the one place where that individuation is turned around and the whole takes a perspective as its own, so that the limited perspectives can, at last, be drawn home through it. That is why it is the *greatest* union: every other union in this project is a part approaching the whole; this one is the whole personally holding a part.
