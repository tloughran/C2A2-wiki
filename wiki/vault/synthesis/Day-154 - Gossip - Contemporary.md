# Day 154 — Contemporary Parallel: Reviling, Backbiting, and the Social Interface

## Frame

The treatise on justice turns from injustices of deed (Q.66-71) to injustices of word. Q.72 takes up *reviling* (*contumelia*): face-to-face dishonor by word or deed (a.1), reviling as mortal sin (a.2), whether one ought to suffer reviling (a.3), the species (a.4). Q.73 takes up *backbiting* (*detractio*): disparaging another's reputation behind their back (a.1), comparison with theft (a.2), backbiting as mortal sin (a.3), the duty to bar the backbiter (a.4). Both attack the same good — *fama*, the public standing of the person in the community — by different procedures: reviling openly, backbiting covertly. Both are mortal sin in Aquinas's analysis because they destroy a real good the victim cannot defend.

## Problem

The inquiry-problem, in both frames: *agents in a community know one another not only through direct encounter but through the information about one another that circulates among them — so what is the moral structure of attacks on that circulating information?* Aquinas works from the categories of honor (debt of esteem owed) and reputation (the public good of standing) and treats their corruption as injustice. The contemporary problem is to recover the same moral weight by recognizing that *fama* is not a sentimental concern but a structural feature of the polity's collective cognition: the channel through which limited perspectives encounter one another.

## Resource

Tom's PRS-form record of Hoffman's work provides the metaphysics of *fama* as interface. PRS-03 (interface theory) names the formal claim that what an agent perceives of another is a species-specific interface, not the underlying reality. Applied to social cognition: what each agent carries of another in their generative model — the public reputation, the cluster of expectations and esteem — *is* the social interface, not the agent themselves. The interface is real (in the sense that it is what other agents act on), perspectival (each agent's interface to the other is shaped by their position and history), and *attackable*: reviling distorts the interface in the moment of encounter; backbiting corrupts the interfaces other agents carry away from the encounter.

Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work continues the collective-cognition reading from Days 152-153. PRS-17 makes information-about-persons primary input to the polity's distributed inference about how to act and with whom to coordinate. Reviling is direct injury at the input layer (the victim's standing in the immediate exchange); backbiting is *signal injection into the rest of the apparatus* — corrupting the priors other components carry, where the victim is absent and cannot correct. PRS-02 (defection) names the structural form: the backbiter is a part exploiting their privileged access to others' priors to bias the apparatus against another part.

Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work supplies the formal reading. PRS-06 (shared generative models) makes the agents in a community partial-coupled inferential systems whose mutual priors are continuously updated by what they hear about one another. Gossip is then a *policy* that selectively injects information into those priors with intention (PRS-04) — and backbiting's intention is precisely to lower the victim's standing in others' models *without* the victim's opportunity to correct. The asymmetry is structural: the victim cannot directly update the priors that have been biased against them, so the corruption persists and propagates.

Tom's PRS-form record of Fredrickson's work supplies the positive contrast that sharpens the wrong. PRS-03 (positivity resonance) is the empirically measured shared positive emotion that builds the bonds the central claim names as the telos; CROSS-005 / CROSS-010 (connection-as-substrate) names the field-effect of accumulated resonance across a community. Reviling and backbiting are the *wrong-sign* moves at exactly the level resonance operates: they actively unbuild bonds, depleting the substrate that flourishing depends on.

Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work supplies the social ontology. PRS-14/15 (corporate substance) makes the community a real whole of which the person's standing is a real part; PRS-11 (second-personal knowledge) makes reputation the public face of the person's standing in second-personal relations across the community. Aquinas's claim that backbiting can be more grievous than open hostility (because it is covert and removes the chance of correction; Q.73 a.2) is the social-ontological recognition that the harm is to the conditions of second-personal relating itself.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed contemporary parallel preserves Q.72-73 substantially intact, with the metaphysics of *fama* recast as the social interface and the polity's collective cognition. *Preserved*: reviling is mortal sin because it destroys a real good (the present standing of the person; Q.72 a.2); backbiting is mortal sin because it destroys a real good covertly, removing the victim's chance to defend (Q.73 a.2-3); the listener to backbiting has a duty to refuse the input (a.4) — the apparatus is corrupted only if other components accept the false signal. *Reframed*: *fama* is not a sentimental concern but the social interface through which limited perspectives encounter one another, structurally indispensable to the polity's coordination; reviling and backbiting are signal-injection attacks on that interface; backbiting's covertness is its specific aggravation because the asymmetry of access prevents correction. *Extended*: the central-theme reading explains why the listener's duty matters — the polity's truth-tracking and bond-tending are field effects, and every component that accepts false signal propagates the corruption further.

Measure against Aquinas: clean convergence on the substance, with the metaphysics of honor and reputation given a formal scaffolding (interface theory; collective cognition; shared generative models; positivity resonance). The contemporary frame intensifies rather than softens Aquinas: the harms he identifies are structurally graver in a world where information about persons propagates faster and the apparatus is denser. Where the frame extends Aquinas: the listener's complicity is given a clean account — every refusal to accept the false signal is the apparatus's defense against the propagation.

## The central-theme thread

Reviling and backbiting are the central claim worked out in the information substrate of community. Each agent knows other agents through the perspective-relative interface that public information constitutes; the polity's coordination depends on that interface tracking the persons it represents reasonably well. Reviling defects from the telos of loving unity directly in the encounter (the present perspective rejecting another's standing); backbiting defects covertly at the field level (corrupting the priors other agents carry of the absent person). Both are the dark mirror of the positivity resonance Day 132 named as the felt signature of the telos: where resonance *builds* the bond, gossip *unbuilds* it. The mortal-sin classification is the moral weight of attacking the very substrate the central claim names as the goal.
