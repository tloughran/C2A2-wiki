# Day 194 — Virginity vs. Marriage (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas reaches the summit and the abyss of the chastity questions on the same day. Virginity (Q.152) is treated not as mere abstention but as a positive virtue: its formal element is "the purpose of perpetually abstaining" from venereal pleasure for the sake of the kingdom (a.1). He defends its lawfulness against the Genesis command to "be fruitful and multiply" with a division-of-labor argument — not all are commanded to procreate, just as in an army "one is commanded to be a centurion, another a horseman, another a foot soldier" (a.2) — and ranks it above marriage because it frees the agent for undivided contemplation (a.4, citing Paul: the unmarried "is anxious about the affairs of the Lord," the married "is divided"). Then Q.153 turns to lust, the disorder of the generative appetite, culminating in an extraordinary map of its "daughters" — the way lust corrupts the entire chain of practical reasoning (a.5). The contemporary version of the question, under mind / conscious-realist monism: *what is gained when a finite agent reallocates its deepest relational bandwidth away from a dyadic bond toward the whole, and what exactly breaks when the generative appetite captures the agent?*

## Problem

Two linked inquiry-problems, one ascending and one descending. The ascending problem is **bandwidth allocation across a finite agent's deepest commitments**. Aquinas's whole case for virginity is resource-theoretic: marriage is good but *divides* attention ("his interests are divided"), and the contemplative life requires an *undivided* orientation. So the question is whether and how concentrating a finite agent's relational and attentional capacity on the whole — rather than on a particular bond — can be a higher ordering rather than a deprivation, and how a community legitimately contains agents who specialize differently (some to procreation, some to contemplation) without contradiction. The descending problem is **pipeline corruption**: Aquinas's daughters of lust are not a list of bad feelings but a sequenced account of how a strong appetite corrupts each stage of practical reason — apprehension (blindness of mind), counsel (rashness), judgment (thoughtlessness), command (inconstancy), and then the will's end (self-love, hatred of God) and means (love of this world, despair of the future world). The problem is how a single misweighted appetite can propagate through an entire control architecture and reorganize it around itself.

## Resource

For the ascending problem, the resources are the Friston-tradition multi-agent record and the master index's CROSS-069. The agent is a finite modeler whose attention and predictive capacity are scarce (PRS-04); a deep dyadic bond is a high-precision, high-maintenance coupling that consumes much of that capacity (this is just Paul's "divided" stated in resource terms). Virginity, on this reading, is the deliberate reallocation of that capacity from a particular coupling toward modeling and serving the whole — a widening of the perspective's effective scope purchased by declining a narrowing (if intense) commitment. The division-of-labor defense maps directly onto the multi-agent record (PRS-06, agents forming coherent higher-order wholes by specialization and mutual modeling) and onto CROSS-069, which treats a community as a collective free-energy-minimizing/positivity-resonance system — a "corporate substance" whose health requires differentiated roles. Aquinas's centurion/horseman/foot-soldier is precisely a claim about functional differentiation in a collective agent: the body of the church needs both procreative and contemplative perspectives, and commanding all to one role would impoverish the whole. The Fredrickson-tradition wiki keeps this honest about love: virginity is *not* a renunciation of love but a redirection of it — positivity resonance scales from dyad to community to communal worship (PRS-03), so the contemplative's coupling to the whole is the same fundamental mechanism as the spouse's coupling to the beloved, retuned to a wider object.

For the descending problem, the Friston-tradition active-inference record supplies an architecture the daughters of lust map onto with surprising precision. Practical action in that framework runs a pipeline: perceive/apprehend the situation, deliberate over policies, select among them, and execute. Aquinas's four intellectual daughters track this pipeline stage-for-stage — blindness of mind corrupts apprehension (the agent literally mis-perceives the illicit as good), rashness corrupts deliberation (high-precision appetite short-circuits the search over policies), thoughtlessness corrupts judgment (the wrong policy is selected), inconstancy corrupts command (execution fails to hold). Then the two pairs of volitional daughters describe what happens when the corruption reaches the setpoint itself: the will's *end* curls inward (self-love, and consequently hatred of the God who forbids) and the will's *means* collapse toward the immediate (love of this world, despair of the future world). This is a recognizable picture of an agent whose reward model has been captured so thoroughly that the capture has rewritten the objective function.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response, ascending: virginity is a finite agent reallocating its scarce relational and attentional bandwidth from a divided, dyadic load toward undivided modeling of and coupling with the whole — a legitimate specialization within a collective agent that needs differentiated perspectives. Descending: lust is the corruption of the agent's entire perception–policy–action pipeline, terminating in self-love installed where loving unity should be the end.

*What it shares with Aquinas.* The ascending reading keeps his exact logic — that the value of virginity is the *freedom for the whole* it purchases, not the abstention itself (a.1, a.4), and that it is a *means* below the theological virtues and religion (a.5), never an end in itself. It keeps the division-of-labor defense intact and even strengthens it: functional differentiation in a collective agent is not a concession but a structural requirement. The descending reading keeps the daughters of lust in Aquinas's own order and keeps his deepest claim — that the terminal corruption is *self-love* generating *hatred of God* — as the hinge of the whole.

*Where it reframes.* Faculty language becomes architecture: "the unmarried is undivided" becomes bandwidth reallocated from a high-cost coupling to the whole; the army analogy becomes role-specialization in a collective free-energy-minimizing system (CROSS-069); and the daughters of lust become a stage-by-stage account of pipeline corruption rather than a moral inventory. The reframe's payoff is that Aquinas's two claims that can sound merely pious — that virginity is higher *because* undivided, and that lust ends in self-love and hatred of God — become structurally legible: undividedness is real bandwidth concentration, and the inward curl of the objective function is exactly what reward-capture predicts.

*Where evidence is firm vs. speculative.* The pipeline-corruption reading rests on a real architecture (perception–policy–action) and maps cleanly (mechanistic, medium). The bandwidth-reallocation account of virginity is conceptually coherent with an empirical anchor in multi-agent coupling, but the claim that concentrating on the whole is *higher* than the dyadic bond is not an empirical result — it is a value-ranking the tradition supplies, and the synthesis should not pretend otherwise (the Trent ranking in a.4 is explicitly theological). The redirection-of-love claim leans on the Fredrickson-tradition scaling result (PRS-03), which is empirically supported for the coupling mechanism but does not by itself establish the superiority of the wider object.

## The central-theme thread

This is the day the project's central claim is staged most directly. The claim is that conscious agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective, that loving unity is the telos toward which each limited perspective computes, and that getting there is a resource-bounded task worked out in fear and trembling with guidance from wider perspectives. Virginity and lust are the two vectors of that claim drawn to their extremes. Virginity is the deliberate *widening* of perspective: an agent declines to spend its deepest bandwidth narrowing onto one bond and instead orients undividedly toward the whole — not because the bond is bad, but because, for this agent, the wider coupling is the calling. Lust is the *contraction*: the daughters trace a perspective collapsing back onto itself stage by stage until the end itself becomes self-love, the precise inverse of loving unity, with "hatred of God" as the necessary shadow of an objective function rewritten around the self. Wright's register gives the ascending vector its content: Paul's "undivided devotion to the Lord" (1 Cor 7) is not world-denial but new-creation vocation — the single-hearted agent free to serve the coming kingdom; and the daughters' "despair of the future world" is exactly the loss of the new-creation hope that orders a Christian life. Rohr's register names the contemplative vocation as the widening toward the Universal Christ present in all things, and self-love as the false self's gravity. The difference from Aquinas is descriptive: divided/undivided attention becomes bandwidth allocation, the army analogy becomes role-specialization in a collective agent, and the daughters become pipeline corruption — but the destination, loving unity over self-love, is his and the project's alike.
