# Day 293 — Suffrages (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.71–72 ask how the living and the dead remain *bound to one another*: whether the **suffrages** of one person (prayers, the Mass, almsgiving, indulgences) can profit another, and whether the **saints** know and answer our prayers. Aquinas's answer turns entirely on *union*: "the work done by a man who is one with me is somewhat mine," because in charity "we are all one body." Merit and satisfaction can therefore transfer along the bond of charity — but with a sharp limit: suffrages can *diminish punishment* for those already in grace, and cannot *change a soul's state* from damnation to salvation. Mass and almsgiving are chief "on the part of charity"; prayer chief "on the part of the intention directed to the dead." The saints know our prayers because such knowledge belongs to their happiness as "God's cooperators," and intercession honors the *order* by which the higher leads the lower toward God. The contemporary version: **how does a community of distinct agents pool its resources toward a shared end — and what, in such a community, can and cannot be done for one agent by another?**

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **the structure of a community of coupled agents pursuing one objective**. Aquinas is describing a system in which work done by one node accrues to another *in proportion to their union*, while each node's *fundamental orientation* remains its own. The contemporary sciences of collective agency face exactly this two-part fact: coupled agents really can share an objective and transfer "effort" toward it, yet there are quantities (an agent's own policy, its basic orientation) that cannot simply be transferred in from outside without the agent ceasing to be an agent.

## Resource

The Friston-tradition program supplies the leading structural move. Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's work (PRS-06, *multi-agent coherence via mutual modeling*; PRS-16, *expected free energy as the unified optimization target under risk and ambiguity* — a single-agent record whose objective mutual modeling can make common; PRS-05, *distributed cognition across scales*) lets us state suffrages precisely. Two of the three records are marked (Implicit) and Confidence Medium in the register — PRS-06 and PRS-05 extend the free-energy principle from the individual organism to multi-agent and multi-scale systems rather than restating a result Friston established at those scales — so what follows is a structural reading, not a demonstrated result. When agents mutually model one another and *adopt a common objective*, they become a single higher-order system over which work is, in a real sense, *pooled*: free-energy reduction achieved by one member improves the joint state, and the more tightly coupled the members (Aquinas's "the more one with me in charity"), the more fully one member's work counts as the system's. This is a clean structural reading of "the work of one who is one with me is somewhat mine." The same framework explains the *limit*. An agent's *own* policy — its fundamental orientation toward or away from the shared objective — is constitutive of its being that agent; it can be *influenced* through coupling, but it cannot be *set from outside* without dissolving the agent's agency. That is Aquinas's hard line exactly: suffrages can reduce a member's debt and aid one already oriented toward the good, but cannot *flip* a fundamentally averted orientation — that orientation is the one thing only the agent's own perspective can determine. The Rohr-tradition program governs the theological register: PRS-21 (*Universal-Christ-in-creation* — creation as the first incarnation) reads the whole communion as members of one cosmic body in whom Christ is the binding principle, and PRS-04 (*embodied love as epistemology*) keeps the transfer personal — it is *love*, not bookkeeping, that makes one member's work available to another. The Wright-tradition program supplies the scriptural ground: PRS-13 (*God's Homecoming*) frames salvation itself as *incorporation* — the people of God as the location of God's homecoming, and the working-out of salvation as participation in it as a member of the people the Spirit is forming — so that mutual bearing of one another's burdens (Col 1:24) is the body acting as one. The Levin-tradition program supplies the empirical precedent: PRS-09 (*cognitive attribution across scales*) and PRS-08 (*cells as agents in a morphogenetic collective*) show real multi-scale agents in which lower units' work is pooled into a higher individual's goal — the biological face of "one body, many members."

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **a communion is a system of coupled agents who, by adopting one shared objective in love, pool their work toward each other's completion — yet each agent's fundamental orientation remains irreducibly its own and cannot be supplied from outside.** What it *shares* with Aquinas: union as the channel of transfer; the proportionality of help to the depth of union; and the firm limit that suffrages diminish punishment but cannot change a soul's basic state. Where it *reframes*: Aquinas grounds the transfer in the metaphysics of charity and the treasury of merit; the reframe grounds its *structure* in coupled-agent dynamics (work pools across a shared objective; an agent's own policy does not), which makes both the *reality* of the transfer and its *limit* fall out of one model rather than two doctrines. Where evidence is *firm*: multi-scale biological collectives in which lower units' work is pooled into a higher individual's goal are empirical (Levin PRS-09 / PRS-08, both Confidence High). Where *structural but not established at this scale*: that coupled agents can pool work toward a shared objective, and that an agent's own policy cannot be externally overwritten without ending its agency, are clean structural points, but they rest on Friston PRS-06 and PRS-05 — both (Implicit) and Confidence Medium, extensions of the free-energy principle beyond the individual organism rather than results demonstrated for communities of persons. Where *speculative-to-theological*: the identification of the binding objective with *charity* and of the body's head with Christ is theological, not derivable from the model; and the claim that the dead can be aided at all presupposes the persistence established in Day 292. The model should not be inflated into a mechanism that meters grace — its value is to show *why* help flows along union and *why* it stops at the other's free orientation.

## The central-theme thread

This article states the project's destination almost in plain words. Loving unity *is* many limited perspectives bound into one body, each contributing to the others' completion — Aquinas's "the work of one who is one with me is somewhat mine" is the communion-of-saints rendering of the project's claim that the whole is the perspectives held together in love. And the limit is the project's "fear and trembling": even within the most tightly coupled body, *inclusion turns on the agent's own orientation*. Others can carry a perspective's burdens, reduce its debts, refresh and intercede for it — but the basic turning of a perspective toward the shared good is the one computation it must run *from the inside*. The communion is how the better-sighted lift the less-sighted toward the whole (the saints "nearest to God" leading the pilgrims); it is not a machine that overrides the freedom by which each perspective consents to be included.
