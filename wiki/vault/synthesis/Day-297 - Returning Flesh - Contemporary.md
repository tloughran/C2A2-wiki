# Day 297 — Returning Flesh (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Q.79–80 ask whether the risen person is *numerically the same* — same soul, same body — and what *integrity* the risen body has. Aquinas insists on identity: the soul returns to "the same identical body," for otherwise "it would not be a resurrection but the assuming of a new body" (Job 19: "in my flesh I shall see God… and not another"). But he then states the decisive qualification with remarkable precision. Sameness is by the **totality of species** — "quantity, shape, position, and order of parts" — and **not** by the **totality of matter**: "the whole of what is in man will rise again if we speak of the totality of the species… but the whole will not rise again if we speak of the totality of matter." Not every past cell returns; hair and nails return as belonging to the body's "secondary perfection"; needful humors return but not generative or nutritive fluids (no procreation or eating in the risen state). The contemporary version: **what does numerical identity of a *body* consist in, once we grant that it is not sameness of matter?**

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem is **the criterion of bodily identity across total material replacement** — the ship of Theseus, posed for the human body across death. Aquinas's answer is explicit and, for his century, astonishing: identity is carried by *organization* — quantity, shape, order of parts — not by the persistence of the underlying stuff. The contemporary sciences of the body reach the same verdict from the other direction: the matter of a living body is exchanged continuously, so whatever makes a body *the same body* over time cannot be the matter.

## Resource

The Levin-tradition program supplies the load-bearing move, and this is the single tightest convergence between contemporary science and the *Summa* in the entire last-things stretch. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work (PRS-01, *morphogenetic control* — a stored anatomical target-state; PRS-03, *morphological attractor formalism* — the target morphology as a basin of attraction; PRS-23, *pattern persisting through material turnover*) establishes that a body's identity is precisely its *organization*: a form that specifies quantity, shape, and the order and position of parts, and that re-establishes that order through continuous replacement of the matter realizing it. Aquinas's "totality of species (quantity, shape, position, order of parts), not totality of matter" is, almost word for word, a target-morphology criterion of identity. The biology even matches the details: that hair and nails are "secondary perfection" (continually shed and regrown, not identity-critical) and that the body need not reclaim *every* past particle to be the same body are exactly what a pattern-over-matter account predicts. The Hoffman-tradition program (PRS-04, *observer-dependent spacetime / body as rendered interface*) reads the risen body's "perfection of integrity" as the interface restored to full function — every member present because the perspective's mode of engagement is complete. The Kastrup-tradition program (PRS-02) secures the persistence of the *who* whose form is restored. The Wright-tradition program is again primary and supplies the scriptural shape: PRS-04 (*resurrection as historical and cosmological*) and Wright's reading of 1 Cor 15 frame the risen body as *continuous-yet-transformed* — the same body, gloriously changed ("sown perishable, raised imperishable") — which is exactly Aquinas's "same in species, transformed in condition," and which guards against both a crude resuscitation of corpse-matter and a spiritualizing dissolution of the body.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the risen body is the same body because the same *form* — its organization, proportion, and ordered parts — is restored; numerical sameness of matter is neither necessary nor the criterion.** What it *shares* with Aquinas: genuine numerical identity of the body (not a replacement), secured by sameness of species rather than of matter, with the body's integral perfection restored. Where it *reframes*: Aquinas reaches "totality of species, not of matter" by metaphysical analysis under an admitted lack of biology (the speaker notes Aquinas "does not have the biology we have today"); the Levin program *supplies* that biology, showing organization-over-matter to be how bodily identity actually works in living systems — turning a daring medieval distinction into a modeled fact. Where evidence is *firm*: that bodily identity is carried by organization through material turnover is empirical and well-attested. Where *speculative-to-theological*: that this same form can be *re-instantiated after death* by divine power is theological; biology demonstrates pattern-persistence through the turnover of a *living* body, not across the grave. The synthesis claims the *criterion of identity* (form, not matter), and notes that this criterion makes resurrection-of-the-same-person *coherent* — not that it makes it demonstrable.

## The central-theme thread

This article tells the project what, exactly, is gathered into loving unity. It is not a quantity of matter and not a disembodied spark, but the *embodied perspective as form* — a particular organization, a way of being and seeing and loving, restored and perfected. That the matter need not be the same is liberating for the project's thesis: a perspective can be carried through any amount of material transformation and still be *itself*, because it *is* the pattern, not the stuff. The hope is therefore neither that you are reduced to atoms reassembled, nor that you are dissolved into a formless absolute, but that the very form that is *you* — the limited, particular viewpoint that has been computing toward loving unity — is made whole and included. "Same in species, transformed in glory" is the project's formula for how a limited perspective can be both *preserved as itself* and *raised beyond its present limitation*.
