# Day 263 — Making Satisfaction (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (Suppl. Q.11–12) joins two themes: the *seal* of confession and the *nature* of satisfaction. The seal is inviolable — "God does not reveal our sins, neither should the priest," and, practically, people would not disclose honestly if the seal could be broken. Satisfaction is then defined: "compensation for an inflicted injury according to the equality of justice," consisting in "giving God due honor." The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: what makes a channel safe enough for honest self-disclosure, and what is it to *restore a disturbed equality* once the deviation is disclosed?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **what condition makes truthful disclosure possible, and what is it to restore a relation that injury has unbalanced?** The seal addresses the first — disclosure requires a trustworthy, non-leaking channel. Satisfaction addresses the second — beyond acknowledging the deviation, the agent must act to restore the equality the injury disturbed.

## Resource

McGilchrist supplies the seal's rationale. In Tom's PRS-form record of McGilchrist's program, PRS-06 (knowledge and self-disclosure are transmitted within trusted, embodied relationships, not as context-free data) explains why the seal is constitutive rather than incidental: honest self-disclosure is only available inside a relationship the discloser can trust absolutely, and a leaking channel destroys the very condition that makes confession work. Friston's program names the same point formally — a safe, low-cost channel is what allows an agent to externalize its hidden error without defensive distortion; the seal is the guarantee that makes the error-sharing channel usable.

Levin supplies the satisfaction half. In Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's program, PRS-03 (target morphology) and the restoration of a perturbed system toward its set-point give "equality of justice" a concrete shape: satisfaction is the corrective action that returns a disturbed configuration to balance — the closing, action-stage of the repair loop (Day 254), not merely the registration (contrition) or representation (confession) of the deviation. Friston's account of action reducing divergence is the formal complement.

Rohr (PRS-7, relationship as ontologically prior) reframes "giving God due honor": the equality restored is not a ledger balanced but a *relation* set right — honor as the restoration of right relation, which is why satisfaction completes, rather than purchases, reconciliation. The *content* (what is owed to God, what honor means) remains theological.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **the seal is the trust-condition that makes honest error-disclosure possible, and satisfaction is the corrective action that restores a disturbed equality — the action-stage closing the repair loop, understood as the setting-right of a relation rather than the balancing of a ledger.** This *shares* with Aquinas the inviolability of the seal and satisfaction as restoration of justice's equality through due honor. It *reframes* the seal as the constitutive trust-condition of the disclosure channel (McGilchrist, Friston) and satisfaction as return-to-set-point corrective action (Levin, Friston), with "honor" as restored relation (Rohr). It *firms up* the premises that truthful disclosure requires a trusted channel and that restoration is active return to balance. It *marks* that *what* is owed to God and *what* honor consists in are theological; that "equality of justice" must be read relationally, not transactionally, lest satisfaction become a payment; and that the resource explains the *structure* of trust and restoration, not the *reconciliation* itself.

## The central-theme thread

The motif appears twice. The seal is the held space in which a perspective-limited agent can safely bring its hidden deviation into relationship — disclosure toward the whole requires the assurance of being received, not exposed. Satisfaction is the limited agent's active turning to restore the equality its deviation disturbed — the resource-bounded labor of re-aligning a relation toward loving unity. Both are conditions of a finite perspective being re-included: a trustworthy channel to disclose through, and a real action by which to set the relation right.

## Bridges

- **McGilchrist PRS-06 (relational trust as condition of disclosure)** — *humanistic, well-evidenced.* The seal is constitutive of honest confession.
- **Friston (safe error-sharing channel; action reducing divergence)** — *formal, well-developed.* Trust enables disclosure; satisfaction as corrective action.
- **Levin PRS-03 (restoration to a set-point)** — *empirical-in-domain.* Satisfaction as return of a disturbed equality to balance.
- **Rohr PRS-7 (relationship ontologically prior; honor as right relation)** — *theological / contemplative.* Equality restored is a relation set right.

## Where this leaves us

Day 264 takes up the *possibility* and *quality* of satisfaction — whether a finite creature can satisfy an infinite God (by proportion, not quantity), and whether one can satisfy *for another* (vicarious satisfaction). The resource opens toward collective active inference and Wright on corporate solidarity. Open thread: keep "equality of justice" relational, not transactional; satisfaction completes reconciliation, it does not buy it.
