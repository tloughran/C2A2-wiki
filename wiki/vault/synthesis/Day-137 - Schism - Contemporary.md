# Day 137 — Contemporary Parallel: Secession from the One Body

## Frame

Day 136 ended with discord — contrariety of wills — and noted that it can be sinless finitude or willed departure. Day 137 follows the escalation. Contention (Q.38, two articles) is discord that has reached *speech*: "just as discord denotes a contrariety of wills, so contention signifies contrariety of speech." Aquinas grades it rather than condemning it flatly — a.1 makes contention mortal only when it attacks the truth, venial when the intention is good, and *praiseworthy* when it is conducted virtuously out of charity against falsehood — and a.2 traces the vicious form to vainglory, the refusal to lose an argument for the sake of one's own excellence. Schism (Q.39, four articles) is discord that has reached *structure*: a willed separation from the unity of the body. Under mind / conscious-realist monism — where reality is a nested hierarchy of conscious agents coupling into higher-order wholes — both questions are about the same thing at two scales: what happens when a part stops cohering with the whole it belongs to, first in its words, then in its membership.

## Problem

The shared problem is the *metaphysics of corporate unity* and its failure. Aquinas treats the Church as a real unity — "the body of Christ on earth" — such that to secede from it is a distinct sin, schism, "willfully and intentionally" separating "from the unity of the church." But this only bites if the unity is *real* in some strong sense; if "the body of Christ" is mere metaphor, schism is no more than leaving a club. So the inquiry-problem is: in what sense is a community of persons one thing, such that secession from it is a wound and not just a departure? And, downstream: how does contention — the verbal defense of one's own opinion — function as the leading edge of that secession?

Aquinas adds two nuances the project must keep. Schism is *less* grave than unbelief (a.2), because it offends "ecclesiastical unity, which is a participated good and a lesser good than God himself" — the secondary object of charity (the neighbor) rather than the primary (God). And someone raised inside a separated body "may not himself be guilty of the sin of schism" (a.1) — the man on the island who takes his church for the one true church. Culpability tracks the will, not the mere fact of separation.

## Resource

For the metaphysics of unity, the leading resources are Levin and Stump. Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's work names *cancer as defection* (PRS-02 in the Levin-tradition wiki): cancer is a cell or cell-line withdrawing from "the body's cooperative morphogenetic community," dropping out of the bioelectric field that binds many cells into one organism's goal-directed whole. The remedy, in Levin's frame, is not to kill the defector but to *restore it to the collective field* — to re-include it. That is a remarkably exact structural image of schism: a part that was a member of a higher-order agent secedes, and the question is whether it can be re-coupled. And the unity it secedes *from* is, on Tom's PRS-form record of Stump's work, no metaphor: PRS-15 in the Stump-tradition wiki defends "a non-metaphorical reading of 'body of Christ'" as a real "hylomorphic composite" of persons, anchored by the interpersonal neural coupling Stump treats as the empirical signature of corporate substance (PRS-14). Put together, Levin gives the dynamics (cohesion vs. defection across scales) and Stump gives the ontology (the community as a real corporate person, not an aggregate). That ontology has a scriptural leg it can stand on, and it is worth naming precisely because the claim is doing so much work here: PRS-40 in the Wright-tradition wiki reads the "elect lady" of 2 John as a congregation addressed in the second person singular — a church written to in the grammatical register one uses for an individual. The point of the record is that the composite is not merely *describable* as one thing but *addressable* as one thing, with the epistolary form itself as the evidence. If that is right, then Aquinas's "body of Christ" is not a metaphor the tradition later hardened; it is the New Testament's own way of speaking to a community, and secession is a wound to something that can be addressed as a person. (Theological; Confidence Medium in the register, and reported rather than quoted — the record's own Evidence line says to verify it against the source audio *if the Wright-Stump corporate-substance pairing is pressed*, which is exactly what this commentary does. The caveat rides with the claim.) Friston's multi-agent account (PRS-06 in Tom's PRS-form record) supplies the binding mechanism the two share: agents become "a coherent collective agent" by mutual model-building, and schism is the failure — or refusal — of that coupling.

For contention, the resource is the account of the over-confident, non-updating mind. Aquinas says contention defends "his own opinion by words" for the sake of vainglory — holding the position to *look* right rather than to *be* right. In the active-inference frame this is a posterior held with pathologically high precision: the agent weights its own model so heavily that incoming evidence (the interlocutor) cannot move it, so it argues rather than updates. Tom's PRS-form record of McGilchrist's work names the cognitive style behind this — the left-hemisphere mode that "grasps" and fixes, mistaking its own representation for the world (PRS-01 in the McGilchrist-tradition wiki), together with the privileging of division over integration (PRS-07's division-and-union) and the refusal to let a tension stand (PRS-08's paradox-as-signal). Contention is the verbal symptom of a perspective that has absolutized itself.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The proposed parallel reads schism as *sub-collective defection from a real higher-order unity*, and contention as its verbal leading edge — the over-precise self-model defending itself by words. With Aquinas: the unity is real (Stump PRS-15 makes "body of Christ" non-metaphorical), secession from it is a genuine wound (Levin PRS-02 makes defection a real loss of function), and contention is its daughter through vainglory (the self-weighting that prefers its own glory to the shared good). Where it reframes: Aquinas specifies the unity canonically — communion with the successor of Peter — and applies it to name the Orthodox churches schismatic; the contemporary parallel does not adjudicate that specific ecclesial claim but takes seriously the *structure* it presupposes — that there is a real corporate whole from which a part can withdraw — and aligns it with the scientific picture of nested collective agents and the scriptural picture of the one body (PRS-08 in the Wright-tradition wiki, the "one new humanity" of Eph 2:11-22 read as reconciliation-*without-erasure* — a unity that does not dissolve the difference of its members, and one the register marks as already-inaugurated and not-yet-complete, which is the right tense for a body that can still be re-coupled; Rohr's Universal-Christ-in-creation, PRS-21). It also keeps Aquinas's two nuances as central, not marginal: schism ranks below unbelief because it offends the secondary object of charity, and invincible ignorance excuses — culpability is in the willed secession, not the inherited separation.

Two articles the earlier draft of this commentary passed over deserve their place, because both bear directly on the collective-agent reading. Q.39 a.3 asks whether schismatics retain any power, and Aquinas draws a distinction the contemporary frame can use almost unchanged: a schismatic bishop's ordinations remain *valid* while his jurisdiction does not. Translated, a seceded node keeps its intrinsic generative capacity — the thing it can do in itself — and loses only its authorization to act *for* the whole. That is the same split Levin's cancer case exhibits, where the defecting cell retains full metabolic and replicative competence and loses exactly its coupling to the collective's morphogenetic target; the capacity is intact, the participation is not. Q.39 a.4 then asks whether excommunication is the fitting punishment, and Aquinas answers by symmetry: one is punished by the thing in which one sinned, so the schismatic who cut himself off from communion is formally cut off. This sits in real tension with the re-inclusion telos this commentary has been pressing, and the tension should be named rather than smoothed. The most that can be said honestly is that Aquinas's excommunication is medicinal in intent — a declaration of an existing separation aimed at return — which narrows but does not close the gap with a frame whose whole instinct is to re-couple rather than excise. (Theological; the reading of excommunication as medicinal is standard in the tradition, but the alignment with the re-inclusion telos is this commentary's own and remains contestable.)

Evidence strength, named honestly. The cancer-as-defection structure is empirical at the cellular scale and *analogical* at the ecclesial scale (Levin PRS-02). The corporate-substance ontology is philosophical-theological and live, not settled (Stump PRS-15, medium). The contention-as-over-precision reading is formally suggestive (Friston PRS-06) and interpretive (McGilchrist). The theological ranking and the role of invincible ignorance are high in field. The specific Rome/Orthodox application is Aquinas's own and is reported, not endorsed or contested here.

## The central-theme thread

The central claim holds that agents are individuated by the limits of their perspective and are called to compute, together, toward a loving unity none of them yet sees whole. Schism is the temptation built into that condition: to prefer one's partial view to continued membership in the whole — to secede rather than to keep correcting and being corrected. And the project's reading is disciplined by Aquinas's own nuances. Partiality is not the sin; *every* finite agent is partial, and Day 136 already insisted that even good-faith agents diverge. The man on the island is not a schismatic. The sin is the *willed* withdrawal — the part that perceives the unity and chooses against it, defending its own frame by contention until the frame becomes a faction and the faction a secession. Re-inclusion, not excision, is the telos: Levin restores the defecting cell to the field; charity restores the separated member to the body; and the whole, on the project's terms, is the loving unity that loses nothing it can still re-couple.

## Where this leaves us

Discord became speech (contention) and then structure (schism): the partial perspective hardening into a faction and seceding from the real whole it belonged to. Aquinas now widens the lens from the body of the Church to the body politic and the use of force: Q.40-41, on war and on strife. Day 138 asks when coercion serves the common good of a whole and when it is mere private violence — the question of authority, and of which agent acts for the whole.
