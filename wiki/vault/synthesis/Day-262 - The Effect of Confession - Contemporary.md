# Day 262 — The Effect of Confession (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (Suppl. Q.9–10) treats the *quality* and *effect* of confession. Two claims stand out: imperfect contrition (attrition) suffices for the sacrament — the penitent need not bring perfect contrition, since the power of the keys completes what the disposition lacks; and confession should be *entire*, with the effect whole — "it is wicked to hope for half a pardon," and even sins genuinely forgotten (excused by ignorance) are loosed with the rest. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: how can a *minimal* disposition be sufficient, and why is the corrective effect *whole* rather than proportioned to the penitent's contribution?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **when is a minimal corrective movement sufficient, and why is the resulting repair total rather than partial?** A strictly proportional model would tie the effect to the quality of the contrition; Aquinas holds that attrition crosses into sufficiency and the effect is whole, even covering what the penitent could not represent.

## Resource

Friston's program supplies both halves. In Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's program, the *threshold* structure of evidence accumulation (developed on Day 260) explains attrition-as-sufficient: a genuine but imperfect contrition is the minimal evidence that crosses into the changed state; sufficiency is about crossing the threshold, not maximizing the input. The *entirety* requirement reads as the demand that the error-representation be complete: a partial disclosure leaves residual divergence unmodeled, so the corrective effect requires the whole to be brought forward — which is why confession should be entire and why the pardon, once given, is whole rather than fractional.

Rohr supplies the disproportion. In Tom's PRS-form record of Rohr's program, the *Universal Christ* — grace already present and exceeding the act — frames the whole pardon meeting even the imperfect disclosure: the penitent contributes the genuine minimal turn and the entire honest disclosure; the *wholeness* of the pardon is grace's, not earned by the quality of the contrition. The loosing of forgotten sins is the clearest sign: the effect covers what the agent could not even represent, because the pardon is given, not computed from the input.

Wright grounds the *content*: forgiveness as real, total re-inclusion — "no half-pardon" — a restored relation rather than a partial credit.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **attrition suffices because a genuine minimal contrition crosses the threshold into the changed state, and the pardon is whole because grace meets the entire honest disclosure with more than its measure — even loosing what the penitent could not represent.** This *shares* with Aquinas the sufficiency of attrition and the wholeness of the effect. It *reframes* sufficiency as threshold-crossing (Friston), entirety as complete error-representation (Friston), and the whole pardon as grace exceeding the act (Rohr). It *firms up* the structural premise that a minimal genuine input can be sufficient and that complete representation conditions complete repair. It *marks* that the *pardon itself*, and the loosing of forgotten sins, are theological — the effect outruns the formal account precisely where it covers the unrepresentable; and that the model gives the form of sufficiency and completeness, never the forgiveness.

## The central-theme thread

The motif appears at its most reassuring, paired with Day 260. The whole accepts even an imperfect, perspective-limited turn — attrition, not flawless contrition — and gives a whole pardon in return, reaching even what the limited agent cannot see in itself (the forgotten sins). The finite perspective contributes the genuine minimal turn and the honest, entire disclosure of what it *can* see; the whole supplies completeness, including over the agent's blind spots. "Salvation as inclusion" is shown to be gracious both at its lower bound (attrition suffices) and beyond the agent's self-knowledge (forgotten sins loosed).
