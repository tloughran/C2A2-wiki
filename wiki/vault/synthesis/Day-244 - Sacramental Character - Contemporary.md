# Day 244 — Sacramental Character (Contemporary Parallel)

## Frame

Aquinas (III Q.63) argues that baptism, confirmation, and orders imprint a *character* — an indelible spiritual mark deputing the recipient to divine worship, which persists even where grace is lost and is never repeated. The medieval question is about a stable, individuating configuration on the soul. The contemporary version under mind / conscious-realist monism: what is a durable, identity-fixing pattern that persists through change of state and substrate, and what individuates one agent's standing within a single underlying mind or field?

## Problem

The shared inquiry-problem: **how does a configuration become a stable, persisting individuator — a mark that outlasts the very dispositions it grounds?** Aquinas needs character to persist when grace does not, to be unrepeatable, and to orient the bearer permanently toward worship. The contemporary frame meets the same problem wherever identity is carried by a configuration that survives turnover and fluctuation of the underlying matter or state.

## Resource

Levin is the leading empirical resource. In Tom's PRS-form record of Levin's program, PRS-01 (morphogenetic control) and PRS-03 (the attractor formalism), together with the bioelectric pattern-memory documented across his planaria work, show configurations that *persist as set-points* through total cellular turnover — a stable target pattern stored bioelectrically, re-establishing the same form after disruption, and in the cut-and-altered planaria persisting as a durable, re-imposed identity. This is the strongest available structural parallel (empirical *in its own domain*) to an indelible, identity-fixing mark that outlasts the tissue carrying it.

Kastrup supplies the metaphysics of individuation. In Tom's PRS-form record of Kastrup's program, PRS-02 (individual identity within monism) and PRS-08 (the Daimon as principle of individuation) address exactly the question character raises in a monist setting: if there is one underlying mind/field, what makes *this* agent persistently *this* one? Kastrup's dissociative-boundary account gives a way to speak of a durable individuating standing within a single whole — which is the metaphysical shape "character" needs once substance dualism is set aside.

PRS-04 (organism as active modeler) in Tom's PRS-form record of Friston's program connects the mark to function: a stable configuration that deputes the bearer to a role is, formally, a persisting prior that shapes all subsequent action.

## Solution (proposed, speculative)

The contemporary-parallel response: **sacramental character is a durable individuating configuration — a stable set-point that fixes the agent's standing and orientation, persisting through loss of the dispositions (grace) it normally grounds, much as a bioelectric target-pattern persists through turnover of the cells it organizes.** This *shares* with Aquinas the realism about an indelible, unrepeatable mark and its orientation toward worship. It *reframes* "imprint on the soul" as a persisting configuration/individuating boundary rather than a quasi-substance added to the soul. It *firms up* the bare possibility — durable, identity-fixing patterns that outlast their substrate are empirically actual (Levin) — while *marking* that the specific claim (a worship-deputing mark conferred by God) is theological, and that the Kastrup individuation account is metaphysical, not empirical.

It also *supersedes* one thing, and the supersession should be named rather than absorbed into the reframe. Aquinas locates character as a quality inhering in the soul's intellective power — a spiritual accident in a spiritual substance, on the hylomorphic account of the soul that the mind-first frame does not retain. Under monism there is no substance-plus-accident structure available to imprint: what persists is the configuration of the individuating boundary itself, not a quality added to a thing standing behind it. The medieval answer's *content* survives — a real, durable, non-dispositional standing — while its *ontology* does not.

## Unrepeatability, and why only three sacraments

Aquinas's two sharpest structural claims about character are that it cannot be repeated and that only three sacraments confer it. Both fall out of the set-point reading rather than needing separate warrant, which is a small but real gain in economy for the contemporary frame.

*Unrepeatability.* On a set-point account, imprinting is the establishment of a target configuration, not the deposit of an object. Re-imposing a set-point already established is a null operation — not a second mark, and not a stronger one; the system already sits at the attractor, so the intervention has nothing left to do. Levin's planaria are the concrete case: once the bioelectric target pattern is set, re-applying the same signal does not yield a second head. Aquinas argues unrepeatability *from* the mark's indelibility; the configuration reading derives it from what a set-point structurally is. Note the direction — this is the contemporary frame recovering a medieval conclusion rather than correcting it, and the recovery is analogical, so it is a structural parallel and not evidence.

*Why only three.* Aquinas restricts character to the sacraments that depute the recipient to a standing in divine worship — baptism to receive, confirmation to profess, orders to minister — as against those that heal or restore. Under the reframe the distinction is between an intervention that changes an agent's *configuration* and one that changes its *state within a configuration already fixed*. Penance and anointing restore a disposition that the standing presupposes; they operate at the level of state. The three character-conferring rites fix which role the agent occupies within the whole — which is, in this project's own terms, a change to what individuates this perspective rather than to how well that perspective is currently faring. *Interpretive: the mapping is this commentary's, and Aquinas's own criterion (deputation to divine worship) is still doing the classifying work — the frame explains his criterion rather than replacing it.*

## The central-theme thread

This article is unusually close to the project's core. The central claim is that perspective-limitation *individuates* agents. Character is a theological instance of durable individuation: a permanent mark that fixes *which* limited perspective this agent is within the whole, orienting it (toward worship, i.e., toward loving unity) regardless of its current dispositional state. Kastrup's dissociative-boundary individuation (PRS-02/PRS-08) and Levin's persisting set-point give two registers — metaphysical and empirical — of the same structure the synthesis keeps returning to: a stable boundary makes a perspective *this* perspective, and the orientation of that boundary is toward the whole.
