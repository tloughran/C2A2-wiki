## Key Discussion Points
- **Walk 4 = "Is dialectic the science of ingression?"** (seeded from the 11-24 walk).
- **Formation before reasons (McGilchrist).** Tom: rhetoric and allure are holistic, right-hemisphere engagement; don't let the analytic "emissary" judge what the "master" is sovereign over. Formation by a tradition gives a stable stage, not a final one. Claude conceded it had treated allure as contamination and withdrew "shallow agreement means capture" (Aristotle: habituation first; Aquinas: connatural knowledge).
- **Time as the dose (Elmer Gantry and the grandma on the porch).** Tests are relational and take time. Claude's version: false and true teachers both attract on day one. The difference is whether a tradition's stability *grows with exposure to rivals* or *depends on insulation*, and mature members judge that. Tom put prophecy in this space: seeing before you can articulate it.
- **Who the dose-response design is for.** Claude: not for the tradition (the mature members' verdicts already suffice) but for rivals like Levin. By MacIntyre's test, you best a rival by solving its problem in a form its own standards accept. For Levin that form is a research program that can be wrong. Tom accepted the framing.
- **Tom's resituating.** Such studies have real efficiency value (Peter Gray's *Free to Learn*; Amish leave-at-18; "teaching ruins good human beings" by pushing them toward authoritarian efficiency), but they sit *inside* the life of a tradition. Tom also named **ineliminable submission** among mature members: "I won't argue for a position until she thinks it". Complementarity of temperaments may go all the way down, possibly genetic, but correlational only, given computational irreducibility. Claude tied this to Aquinas's docility as a part of prudence: even the wise stay teachable.
- **Causation and agency.** Claude pushed back that Levin gets causal knowledge by addressing agents (the axis of persuadability: rewire → train → give reasons), and that Wolfram allows pockets of reducibility. Tom agreed. In his view stimulus-response is how we describe things where we don't know how to judge agency. Reduce down to the computationally irreducible, but not past agency.
- **Wolfram: "computation with nothing running it."** Claude: for Wolfram the ruliad simply *is*, and only the observer (bounded, persisting) makes things "happen". The observer is assumed, not derived, so it's the Thomist objection again: form without an agent. Claude also flagged that Wolfram's rules are deterministic, so "agents computing non-deterministically" is Tom's addition.
- **Xenobot replication (Kriegman, Blackiston, Bongard, Levin 2021; cited from memory).** Tom wondered whether replication always needs something outside the self. Claude: the parent xenobots only *gather* loose cells, and the form arises from the cells' own competence, so the parent is an occasion, not the source (also von Neumann's separate description; "man generates man, and the sun as well").
- **Loop closed:** three systems that look self-sufficient and aren't (stimulus-response, Wolfram's observer, xenobots). Those entry points are what a science of ingression studies. And the "pin": **a tradition replicates the xenobot way**. It gathers people, they take up its form with competence they already have, so a tradition is an agent we're mind-blind to. The 40 steps are behavioural science at that level.
