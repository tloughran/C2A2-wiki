  Sources:
    1. Popper, K. (Logik der Forschung / Conjectures and Refutations; extracts via Internet Encyclopedia of
       Philosophy entry "Popper, Karl: Philosophy of Science" and theotodman.com PDF of C&R pp. 33-58).
       — Names four "conventionalist stratagems" by which a theory is immunised against refutation,
       the first being the introduction of ad hoc hypotheses and the second the modification of
       ostensive definitions to alter the content of a hypothesis. This is precisely
       "amend rather than adjudicate", and Popper treats it as a defect.
    2. Lakatos, I. Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes (via Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
       entry "Imre Lakatos"; qualityresearchinternational.com summary; Aeon essay "Imre Lakatos and the
       philosophy of bad science"). — Distinguishes progressive from degenerating problemshifts:
       modification of the protective belt is legitimate only if it is content-increasing and
       occasionally corroborated; otherwise the programme degenerates.
    3. "Moving the goalposts" (Wikipedia; Logically Fallacious; Snopes article 464308, "The Fallacy of
       'Moving the Goalposts', Explained"). — Snippet-level only. Describes the pattern where the
       claim or its success criterion is redefined after each challenge so that no rebuttal can land,
       classed as an informal fallacy related to special pleading.
    4. Anonymous/preprint, arXiv:2601.08850, "The Inconsistency Critique: Epistemic Practices and AI
       Testimony About Inner States." — Snippet-level only; not read beyond search summary. Describes an
       "epistemic immune system" that generates new objections as old ones are met, shifts ground when
       challenged, and never specifies in advance what would change the conclusion. Same structural
       pattern, in an AI-adjacent setting. Treat as low-weight given I saw only the snippet.

  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: The generalizable limb is well supported and, importantly, well NAMED. Philosophy of science
    has at least three independent vocabularies for exactly this move — Popper's conventionalist twist,
    Lakatos's degenerating problemshift, and the informal-logic "moving the goalposts" / special-pleading
    family. All three converge on the same verdict: absorbing a challenge by modifying the claim, rather
    than letting the challenge decide the claim, reduces the claim's testability and is a failure mode,
    not a neutral bookkeeping choice. The literature therefore supports (a) that amendment-on-challenge is
    a real and distinguishable pattern, and (b) that its unqualified form degrades claim quality. What the
    literature cannot supply is the in-house factual premise that this pipeline is *now routinely* doing it;
    that requires an audit of the pipeline's own decision log, not a search.

  Caveats: The literature's condemnation is conditional, not blanket. Popper explicitly permits auxiliary
    hypotheses that INCREASE the degree of falsifiability; Lakatos explicitly permits content-increasing
    belt modifications. So "amending rather than adjudicating" is only diagnostic of degeneration when the
    amendment is content-preserving or content-reducing. An amendment that adds a new, independently
    checkable commitment is progressive on both accounts. This means ASSUMPTION-1241 as stated is
    under-specified: it counts amendments without grading them. Transfer concern: all three sources come
    from adversarial-inquiry settings (scientific programmes, debate) where an opponent exists; a
    self-review pipeline amending its own claims has no opponent, which if anything worsens the risk.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED
