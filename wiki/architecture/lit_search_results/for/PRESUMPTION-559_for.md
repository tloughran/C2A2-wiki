  Sources:
    1. Liu, N.F., Zhang, T., & Liang, P. (2023). "Evaluating Verifiability in Generative Search Engines." Findings of EMNLP 2023. — Human audit of four generative search engines (Bing Chat, NeevaAI, perplexity.ai, YouChat) found that on average only 51.5% of generated sentences were fully supported by their cited sources, and only 74.5% of citations actually supported the sentence they were attached to. This is the most direct available support for the presumption's core distinction: in generated text, the presence of a citation is roughly a coin-flip indicator of the cited source actually supporting the claim. Citing is not checking.
    2. Walters, W.H., & Wilder, E.I. (2023). "Fabrication and Errors in the Bibliographic Citations Generated by ChatGPT." Scientific Reports 13. — Over 636 citations in 84 generated literature reviews across 42 topics: 55% of GPT-3.5 citations and 18% of GPT-4 citations were entirely fabricated, and among the non-fabricated citations, 43% (GPT-3.5) and 24% (GPT-4) contained substantive errors. Establishes that even the weaker property — the referent existing as described — fails at material rates without external checking.
    3. "Evaluating the Integrity of LLM-Generated Citations: Prevalence and Risks of Fabricated References in Scientific Literature." Data 11(5):122 (2026), MDPI. [Author names not retrieved in this search pass — cited by title/venue/DOI-locus; flagged as a verification gap rather than asserted.] — Reports for GPT-4o that 19.9% of citations across six simulated literature reviews were wholly fabricated and 45.4% of the seemingly real ones carried bibliographic errors (most often invalid DOIs). Corroborates (2) on a current-generation model.
    4. "Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge." arXiv:2410.21819 (2024); and "Do LLM Evaluators Prefer Themselves for a Reason?" arXiv:2504.03846 (2025). — LLM evaluators systematically assign higher scores to text they themselves are likely to have produced (lower-perplexity, more familiar text), a directional evaluative deviation independent of quality. The recommended mitigation in this literature is ensemble or cross-model evaluation, i.e. decorrelating the evaluator from the generator. Directly supports the second half of the presumption: an audit of the pipeline's own outputs, run by the pipeline, is a biased instrument by a measured mechanism.
    5. Huang, J., Chen, X., Mishra, S., Zheng, H.S., Yu, A.W., Song, X., & Zhou, D. (2023). "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet." arXiv:2310.01798 (ICLR 2024). — Without external feedback signals, models cannot reliably identify errors in their own outputs, and self-"correction" can degrade performance. Supports the claim that an internal audit does not substitute for an external check.
    6. GAO, Government Auditing Standards ("Yellow Book"), GAO-02-388G and successors; and IOSCO (2002), "Principles of Auditor Independence and the Role of Corporate Governance in Monitoring an Auditor's Independence." — The self-review threat: an audit organisation must not audit its own work, and independence is treated as a precondition of assurance rather than a quality of it. This is the institutional-design analogue of the presumption, developed over a century of practice in a domain with strong incentives to get it right.
    7. Raji, I.D., Xu, P., Honigsberg, C., & Ho, D.E. (2022). "Outsider Oversight: Designing a Third Party Audit Ecosystem for AI Governance." AIES 2022 / arXiv:2206.04737. — Argues, from the history of audit in other sectors, that first-party (internal) AI audits are structurally insufficient for assurance and that genuine third-party access is the binding requirement. Applies the auditor-independence principle specifically to AI systems.

On limb two — that the pipeline cannot audit its own external-referent property from inside — the LLM-as-judge literature documents a measured self-preference bias whose recommended remedy is exactly decorrelating evaluator from generator, and the self-correction literature finds that error detection fails without an external signal. The audit-profession and AI-audit literatures independently establish that self-review is not assurance and that independence is a precondition, not a refinement.
