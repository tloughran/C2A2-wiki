    1. Driffield, T., & Smith, P. C., 2007. "A Real Options Approach to Watchful Waiting: Theory and an Illustration." Medical Decision Making 27(2):178-188. — Models watchful waiting as holding an option. Under backward induction there are three regions: discharge at low expected net benefit, treat immediately at high, and watchful waiting at intermediate levels. "Monitor" is therefore a principled, informative decision state with its own region, not just a fallback. (fetched: abstract only; full text paywalled)
    2. GRADE-derived research-gap frameworks (AHRQ, "Frameworks for Determining Research Gaps During Systematic Reviews," NCBI Bookshelf NBK62478; USPSTF "Identifying Evidence Gaps"). — An "insufficient evidence" judgement is meant to be informative: it records the reason for the gap (imprecise, biased, inconsistent or indirect evidence) and feeds research priorities. (search-result level)
    3. Watchful-waiting trials in low-value care (e.g. study protocol "Watchful Waiting as a Strategy to Reduce Low-Value Spinal Imaging," PMC7910785). — Watchful waiting is used deliberately as an active management option with defined follow-up. (search-result level)

  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: Decision theory and evidence-grading practice both treat a monitor or insufficient-evidence category as informative when it is defined: it occupies a distinct region of expected value (Driffield & Smith), and it records why evidence falls short and what would resolve it (GRADE gap frameworks). Clinical watchful waiting is a structured option with scheduled reassessment and thresholds for escalation. This supports the view that a MONITOR disposition can carry information.

  Caveats: In every supporting source, informativeness depends on conditions: explicit thresholds for leaving the category, periodic reassessment, and a recorded reason for the classification. None of the sources supports a category that absorbs 7 of 7 items, or that is assigned without reason codes or re-review dates. The real-options result also implies that the category should shrink as evidence accumulates. Transferring clinical and evidence-grading practice to agent dispositions is an analogy.
