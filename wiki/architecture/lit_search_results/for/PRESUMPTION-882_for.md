  Summary: The formal literature gives the presumption a clean warrant. Under the closed-world
    assumption a complete database licenses negation-as-failure — absence *is* falsity, not ignorance —
    and this is the standard semantics for exactly the operation the nightly verification performed.
    The argument-from-silence literature, in its most careful treatment (McGrew 2013), gives explicit
    conditions under which absence supports inference, and a corpus with a declared back-citation
    convention plausibly satisfies them: the source was expected to carry the information, intended
    completeness, and the item was significant enough to warrant mention. The qualitative-methods
    literature supplies the corresponding methodological notion of *theoretical completeness* as a
    defensible stopping and judging criterion, distinct from mere saturation. Two limits are equally
    clear from the same sources. First, the closed-world assumption is a *choice*: the open-world
    reading of the identical data treats absence as unknown, and nothing in the data selects between
    them. Second, and more pointedly, the partial-closed-world literature exists precisely because
    real registers are complete in some regions and not others — which is the configuration 14b
    identified, where accession is complete but repair coverage stands at five days of 307. The
    corpus-linguistics position (a corpus can prove existence, never non-existence) is a direct
    counterweight.

  Caveats: (1) The distinction that actually drives the item — a corpus that has stopped *growing* but
    has not stopped being *edited* — is not addressed by any located source. Every supportive source
    assumes the register is static once closed. (2) The closed-world assumption is a modelling
    decision, and adopting it converts work-in-progress into findings by fiat; the presumption is
    exactly this adoption, made implicitly. (3) McGrew's conditions are conditions, and his paper
    stresses that rival explanations for silence often defeat the inference — "not yet repaired" is a
    live rival explanation here. (4) Saunders' completeness criterion and Tight's critique come from
    qualitative social science, where the object is a theory, not a citation register; transfer is
    assumed. (5) Several attributions (Reiter 1978, Saunders 2018, Partington & Duguid) are inferred
    from titles and secondary pages and should be verified before being cited in the wiki. (6) All
    sources read at snippet or abstract level.
