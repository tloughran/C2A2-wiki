Grounds the item's finding in a known and expected defect class rather than an idiosyncratic bug.

SAST precision measurements (Mobb, "Top SAST Tools Compared by False Positive Rate"; Corgea, "How to Reduce False Positives in SAST: The Complete Guide (With Data)"). — Reports 18-36% precision for commercial static analysis tools, i.e. 64-82% of flagged findings are false positives. The item's claimed rate — 11 of 13 false positives plus 2 artifacts, a precision of 0/13 — is below but adjacent to the documented band, which makes the finding unsurprising rather than extraordinary. Empirical precedent that a detector can be in production and be mostly or entirely wrong.
    3. "Reducing False Positives in Static Bug Detection with LLMs: An Empirical Study in Industry." arXiv:2601.18844; and ZeroFalse, arXiv:2510.02534. — Reports that state-of-the-art open-source analysers including CodeQL and Infer exhibit over 95% false-alarm rates for null-pointer-dereference detection at Linux-kernel scale. This is a directly comparable case of a mature, widely trusted detector operating at near-zero precision on a real corpus, which is the strongest available precedent for the item's 0-genuine finding.
    4. Alert-fatigue and disengagement literature (Contrast Security, "The Truth About AppSec False Positives"; The Hacker News, "The Truth About False Positives in Security"; Ancker, J.S. et al., "Effects of workload, work complexity, and repeated alerts on alert fatigue in a clinical decision support system," PMC5387195). — Establishes the operator-side consequence the item's second, open defect implies: repeated non-issues produce desensitisation, developers "begin to disregard even legitimate alerts," and the resulting cycle is more false positives leading to less trust leading to fewer fixes leading to more real defects reaching production. The clinical work quantifies the same effect under workload.
    5. Clinical decision support precision literature (PMC4052586, "Clinical Decision Support Alert Appropriateness: A Review and Proposal for Improvement"). — Cross-domain confirmation that low-precision automated alerting is a general pathology of rule-based detection, not a software-specific one, and that the remedy is detector-side precision improvement rather than recipient-side discipline.

  Strength of support: Strong
  Summary: Every element of the item's general form is well documented. Rule-based and static detectors routinely operate at precisions of 18-36%, and a specific, well-known case exists — CodeQL and Infer on null-pointer dereference in the Linux kernel — where the false-alarm rate exceeds 95%, which is within rounding distance of the item's claim of zero genuine findings among thirteen. The structural explanation is standard: over-approximation for soundness generates warnings on infeasible paths, and abstraction away from aliasing, external libraries, and conditional compilation produces mismatches with real behaviour, which is the same family as a locus regex that is insensitive to which part of the Summa a citation sits in. The consequence of leaving the second defect open is also documented rather than speculative: sustained low precision produces operator desensitisation and the ignoring of true positives, which converts a detector into a liability. The item's proposed action — tighten the locus regex, re-run, diff, then audit remaining detectors for the same defect class — matches the remediation pattern this literature prescribes, and the generalisation step (audit siblings for the same defect class) is specifically supported by the survey finding that these are systematic over-approximation errors rather than one-off bugs.
  Caveats: (a) The literature supports the *plausibility and expectedness* of the item's finding; it cannot confirm the specific audit of 13 violations, which is an internal record claim outside search scope. (b) There is a self-audit problem the sources sharpen rather than resolve: the reclassification of 11 findings as false positives was performed by the same pipeline that produced them, and no retrieved source endorses single-party adjudication of one's own detector output. Static-analysis FP studies typically use independent manual triage with inter-rater agreement. This connects the item to PRESUMPTION-508 and PRESUMPTION-511 and is the main threat to the finding. (c) The SAST precision figures come from vendor comparisons with commercial incentives to report competitors' false-positive rates as high; the arXiv sources are the more reliable anchor. (d) A precision of exactly zero is at the tail of the documented distribution and warrants the alternative hypothesis that the detector is measuring something real but mis-localised — which the open locus-regex defect would produce. Tightening the regex and diffing is precisely the test that separates these, and it has not been run. (e) arXiv:2601.18844 and arXiv:2510.02534 are preprints of unverified peer-review status.
