  Summary: The learning-progressions literature directly supports the workflow assumed here: curricular ladders encode theoretically ordered milestones, and standard practice treats those implied milestones as candidate elements of an assessment construct that must then pass a validation step. Because ASSUMPTION-296 claims only *candidate* status (with dyad ratification as the validation gate), it sits comfortably within established practice. The standing caveat in the same literature is that curriculum-derived milestones are hypotheses, not facts — empirical recovery studies regularly find learners do not traverse curriculum-implied orders — and that curriculum-linked progressions do not generalize across curricula.
  Caveats: Support is for milestones-as-candidates, not milestones-as-validated; dyad ratification is a much weaker validation step than the empirical-recovery studies the literature expects (it substitutes a 2-member panel for learner data). Curriculum-specificity warning applies: ladders from one tradition's curriculum may not transfer.
