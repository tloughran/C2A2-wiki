  Summary: The assumption has a genuine and well-established intellectual ancestor, and it is not
  Kuhn or Laudan — it is Swanson. Literature-based discovery is a fifty-year-old research programme
  built on exactly the move C2A2 is making: treat a structural difference between two bodies of work
  that do not talk to each other as a positive signal that something is there, then go and check. Its
  founding paper is verified, and its canonical case is a validated hit. Laudan supplies the missing
  half of the grounding by making "solved problem" a legitimate predicate over a research tradition
  rather than a rhetorical flourish, and the traditions-as-unit premise already ACTIVE in C2A2's
  register (lines 18-23) rests on the same authors. What I could NOT find is the thing the assumption
  actually asserts. Swanson's operator is disjointness of literatures; C2A2's operator is open-vs-closed
  status of a shared problem. These are different predicates, and I located no study measuring the
  precision of the second one — no base rate for how often a cross-programme status mismatch marks a
  real gap rather than a difference in what each programme counts as an answer. The word doing the work
  in ASSUMPTION-1284 is "strong", and nothing found licenses it.

  Caveats:
  (a) THE SOURCES SUPPORT A SEARCH HEURISTIC, NOT A STRENGTH CLAIM. Swanson's own framing is that the
      incompleteness of retrieval makes undiscovered public knowledge "possible, and plausible" — a
      claim about where to look. Literature-based discovery is a hypothesis-GENERATION method whose
      outputs require downstream validation; the fish-oil case is famous partly because most candidates
      generated by such methods are not. Transferred honestly, this supports "worth investigating" and
      not "strong candidate."
  (b) THE OPERATOR DOES NOT MATCH. Swanson's disjointness is a property of CITATION STRUCTURE and is
      measurable from outside either literature. Open-vs-closed status is a property of what each
      programme SAYS about itself, which is the routed question's worry exactly. The transfer from one
      to the other is my inference and is the weakest joint in this result.
  (c) GALISON CUTS BOTH WAYS AND I HAVE NOT READ HIM. The trading-zone literature's central observation
      — that groups coordinate while ascribing different significance to the same objects — is as
      readily an account of why status labels diverge WITHOUT a real gap as of why the divergence is
      informative.
  (d) THE HILBERT CASE IS A COUNTER-CASE IN THE PUREST INSTANCE. In the one domain that maintains
      explicit, curated, prestigious open-problem lists, "solved" is reported to be
      interpretation-dependent. If status is soft where it is most formalised, it is unlikely to be hard
      where it is merely conventional.
  (e) NO BIBLIOMETRIC LITERATURE ON "OPEN PROBLEM" RHETORIC WAS LOCATED. The routed question's first
      limb — whether status declarations track documentation and rhetorical convention — was searched
      and returned only generic "bibliometric review + future research agenda" methodology papers, which
      are about how REVIEWERS declare gaps, not about how PROGRAMMES declare problems closed. This is a
      real gap in my coverage and 15b may do better on it.
  (f) SEARCH SCOPE: preliminary — broader search recommended. Five queries. Not covered and likely
