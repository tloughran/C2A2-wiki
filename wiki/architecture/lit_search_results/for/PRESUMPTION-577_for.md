  Original statement: [inferred] Consolidating three HIGH systemic-risk flags into one is warranted by PREMISE-121 — more separately-tracked items lowers the probability any is acted on — which presumes the binding constraint on action is the reader's attention rather than each item's decidability. The consolidation note anticipates part of this ("each sub-flag retains its own DISPOSITION-NNN so nothing is lost"), which addresses retrievability but not salience — and salience is precisely what PREMISE-121 is a claim about.

  Sources:
    1. Fischhoff, B., Slovic, P. & Lichtenstein, S. (1978). "Fault Trees: Sensitivity of Estimated Failure Probabilities to Problem Representation." Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance 4(2):330-344. — This is the single most direct empirical support for the presumption. Using fault trees for the event "a car fails to start", the authors report three findings that map one-for-one onto 14b's concern: people were markedly insensitive to what had been *left out* of a tree; adding detail to the tree as a whole changed perceptions only slightly; and — decisively — "the perceived importance of a particular branch was increased by presenting it in pieces", i.e. as two separate component branches rather than one combined branch. Read in the direction of the consolidation at issue, presenting three findings as one branch with three sub-parts predictably *lowers* their perceived importance relative to three branches, at identical content. Retrievability of the sub-parts does not rescue this: the omission-insensitivity result shows readers do not spontaneously reconstruct what is not presented at the top level, which is exactly the gap between retrievability and salience that 14b identifies.
    2. Tversky, A. & Koehler, D.J. (1994). "Support Theory: A Nonextensional Representation of Subjective Probability." Psychological Review 101(4):547-567. — Support theory is the general mechanism behind the fault-tree result. Its central prediction is implicit subadditivity: the judged probability (and, in the theory's terms, the judged support) of an event described as a whole is systematically *less* than the sum of the judged probabilities of its explicitly unpacked components. Because the theory attaches support to *descriptions* rather than to events, it predicts that the same three findings will command less total weight under a packed description ("one HIGH flag with three sub-flags") than under an unpacked one ("three HIGH flags"), with no change in the underlying facts. This supplies the theoretical warrant the consolidation note lacks: the note asserts that nothing is lost because the DISPOSITION ids persist, whereas support theory says what is lost is precisely the weight attaching to the unpacked description.
    3. Libby, R. & Brown, T. (2013). "Financial Statement Disaggregation Decisions and Auditors' Tolerance for Misstatement." The Accounting Review 88(2):641-665. — An experiment with experienced auditors that measures the presumption's settling quantity in an adjacent domain. The authors find that auditors require correction of *smaller* errors when the numbers are disaggregated — that is, the same magnitude of defect crosses the action threshold more readily when presented in disaggregated form. They also find auditors expect greater external scrutiny of uncorrected errors in disaggregated numbers, and, importantly for the retrievability question, that the effects are substantially reduced when the disaggregated numbers are relegated to the notes rather than the face of the statement. That last result is the closest published analogue to "each sub-flag retains its own DISPOSITION-NNN so nothing is lost": moving detail to a retrievable-but-subordinate location measurably weakens its effect on the action threshold.
    4. Fox, C.R. & Rottenstreich, Y. (2003). "Partition Priming in Judgment Under Uncertainty." Psychological Science 14(3):195-200. (Replicated and extended as a Registered Report: Royal Society Open Science 12(7):250669, 2025.) — Four studies establish partition dependence: judgments shift systematically with how the outcome space is carved up, under both ignorance and partial knowledge, because the partition supplies the default against which judgment anchors. Applied to the consolidation, the choice to present the day's adverse findings as a one-item partition rather than a three-item partition is not a presentational nicety but an input to the reader's judgment of how much is wrong. The 2025 Registered Report replication matters here: it means the effect is not resting on a single 2003 finding in a literature with known replication problems. This source supports the specific structural claim that the aggregator's partition choice is consequential and therefore requires justification and a symmetry check.
    5. PCAOB AS 2201 (An Audit of Internal Control Over Financial Reporting) and AS 1305 / AU-C 265 (communicating internal control matters), as documented practice. — Where a profession has had to settle how to report multiple related deficiencies, the settled answer is *both* evaluations, not a substitution. AS 2201 requires the auditor to determine whether individual deficiencies affecting the same account, assertion or control component collectively constitute a material weakness — i.e. aggregation is mandated as an *additional* severity test — while the communication standards separately require each significant deficiency and material weakness to be communicated in writing. The profession thus explicitly declines the move at issue: it does not permit n findings to be replaced by one consolidated finding on reporting-hygiene grounds, precisely because the count and the aggregate carry different information for the recipient. This is documented practice support for 14b's claim that consolidation needs an affirmative salience argument rather than a hygiene presumption.
    6. Power, M. (1997). The Audit Society: Rituals of Verification. Oxford University Press. — Power's central analytical move is to separate audit's operational characteristics from its normative ones, and to show that reporting practices which look like neutral technique are in fact constitutive of what counts as a problem. His account of how audit's outputs become "rituals of verification" whose form shapes downstream response is the general sociological case for 14b's specific worry: a reporting convention adopted for hygiene reasons ends up determining the visibility, and therefore the consequence, of what is reported. Power supplies the framing for the second half of the presumption — that the layer doing the aggregating has an interest in the aggregate — since much of the book concerns audit practices whose form serves the auditing apparatus's needs rather than the recipient's.

  CORRELATION DISCLOSURE: Sources 1, 2 and 4 are drawn from the judgment-under-uncertainty tradition that also supplies the general grounding for PREMISE-121 itself (attention as a scarce resource shaping which items get acted on).   Summary: Supported, and EXTERNALLY ANCHORED (named external referents: Fischhoff, Slovic & Lichtenstein 1978 in JEP:HPP; Tversky & Koehler 1994 in Psychological Review; Libby & Brown 2013 in The Accounting Review; Fox & Rottenstreich 2003 in Psychological Science with a 2025 registered replication; PCAOB AS 2201 / AS 1305 as standards texts; Power 1997, Oxford University Press). The literature supports 14b's distinction between retrievability and salience directly and with unusual precision. Fischhoff et al. 1978 found both halves of the presumption in one experiment: splitting a branch raises its perceived importance, and readers are insensitive to what a representation omits — so preserved sub-flag ids do not restore the weight that packing removes. Tversky & Koehler 1994 gives the mechanism (implicit subadditivity: support attaches to descriptions, and packed descriptions command less support than the sum of their unpacked parts), and Fox & Rottenstreich 2003, now with a registered replication, establishes that the aggregator's partition choice is itself an input to the reader's judgment. Libby & Brown 2013 measures something very close to 14b's settling quantity in an audit setting: at matched defect magnitude, disaggregation lowers the threshold for requiring correction, and the effect shrinks when the disaggregated detail is moved to a subordinate location — the published analogue of "the DISPOSITION ids are still there." Finally, PCAOB practice shows a profession that faced exactly this reporting choice and mandated aggregation *in addition to*, never *in place of*, the individual findings.

  Caveats: (a) The direction of effect in sources 1, 2 and 4 is about *judged importance and probability*, not about *remediation rate*, which is the quantity 14b names. Only Libby & Brown 2013 measures anything like an action threshold, and it measures auditors' tolerance for misstatement rather than whether a finding gets fixed. The inferential step from "judged as more important" to "more likely to be remediated" is plausible and standard but is not established by these sources for this domain. (b) The support is therefore for the presumption's *negative* claim — consolidation is not salience-neutral, and the consolidation note's retrievability argument does not address salience — and not for the stronger claim that the consolidation was wrong. PREMISE-121's countervailing effect is real: if the reader's attention is genuinely the binding constraint, some consolidation raises total action even while lowering per-item salience. The literature here does not adjudicate the net effect at n=3, and n=3 is small enough that the attention saving may be negligible while the subadditivity penalty is not — but that is an inference, not a finding. (c) Domain transfer is nontrivial in two places. The fault-tree and support-theory experiments use naive participants judging probabilities of unfamiliar events; the recipient here is a single expert reader with prior context on the pipeline, for whom the packing effect may be attenuated. Libby & Brown's auditors are closer in expertise but are judging quantitative misstatements against a numeric materiality threshold, which is a more structured task than reading systemic-risk flags. (d) Publication bias: the unpacking/subadditivity literature is a canonical heuristics-and-biases result set, and such literatures are known to contain inflated effect sizes and to under-report null findings. This is why the 2025 registered replication of Fox & Rottenstreich is cited explicitly; no comparable pre-registered replication of the 1978 fault-tree study was located, so source 1 — the strongest single source — carries the higher replication risk. (e) The self-audit half of the presumption is the weakest-supported part. Nothing found here directly addresses *whether an aggregating layer systematically packs findings adverse to itself or its siblings*; Power 1997 supplies only the general argument that reporting form serves the reporting apparatus. The IESBA and IIA self-review-threat framing (see PRESUMPTION-586_for, source 3) is the nearer literature and it concerns independence of the reviewer, not aggregation choices. 14b's own observation that no symmetry check is recorded stands as the strongest available evidence on that half, and it is internal. (f) Search scope: six targeted verification searches. One literature was reached for and did not yield a usable result — empirical work on whether the *count* of internal audit findings versus their *severity* predicts management remediation. Searches returned practitioner guidance and one adjacent study on material-weakness severity and audit fees, but no peer-reviewed study measuring 14b's exact settling quantity (remediation rate for n findings separate vs. consolidated at matched severity).
