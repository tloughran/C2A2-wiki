(2012), "Computing Inter-Rater Reliability for Observational Data: An Overview and Tutorial," Tutorials in Quantitative Methods for Psychology 8(1) (PMC3402032); Statistics By Jim, "Inter-Rater Reliability"; Wikipedia, "Inter-rater reliability." — Supplies the decisive distinction the item is reaching for. The field's standing warning is that agreement between raters and accuracy against truth are different quantities: "the raters might give consistent ratings, [but] those ratings may be hitting the correct bullseye or the various raters might be collectively consistent — but consistently wrong." Correspondingly, "the best studies don't only compare raters to each other but also to a known standard." Reconciling two counters to each other is not the same operation as establishing which is right, and de-inflating a disagreement does neither.

  Summary: The item's core distinction — that a measurement disagreement may be a contradiction carrying information rather than a defect to be smoothed — is standard in measurement methodology and is stated there in stronger terms than the item uses. Inter-rater reliability practice warns explicitly that raters can be consistent and consistently wrong, and therefore that agreement between instruments is not evidence of correctness; the prescribed procedure is comparison against a designated reference standard, which C2A2 does not have. The Rasch literature adds the sharper point that when two counters are built on different constructs, reconciliation is not merely difficult but ill-defined — the numbers are answers to different questions, and de-inflating them discards precisely the signal that would have revealed the definitional split. The metric-governance literature makes the same diagnosis from the data side and supplies the remedy the item's OPEN-124 proposes: named owners and designated tie-breaking authority per metric, without which no technical layer resolves the divergence. Lineage tooling gives the concrete diagnostic move — compare derivation paths, not outputs.

  Caveats: (a) The support is for the *general* claim that disagreements should be diagnosed rather than de-inflated; the item's specific reading of the 2026-07-19 events (four numbers, two corrections, two ID-maximum disagreements) is an internal-record claim outside search scope and I make no finding on it. (b) The literature does not endorse treating every disagreement as substantive — measurement error is real, and IRR practice exists precisely because some disagreement is noise. The item risks the mirror error of the pattern it names: presuming contradiction where there is genuine measurement variance. The discriminating test is the one the item already proposes (run both resolvers over a frozen snapshot); if the difference is fully explained by the definitional delta, it was definitional, not contradictory. (c) Reference-standard designation assumes a standard can be established; for "how many files are in the vault" a frozen snapshot supplies one cheaply, but for derived quantities like knowledge-graph health it may not exist. (d) Sources 4 and 5 are practitioner material; sources 1-3 carry the methodological weight.
