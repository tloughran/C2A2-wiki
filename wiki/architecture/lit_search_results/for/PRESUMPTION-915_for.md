  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: Ontology engineering, philosophy of natural kinds, knowledge organisation, and the sociology of
  standards all converge on the position that the structure of a classification is stipulated by its
  designer according to purposes, not read off the classified material. Noy and McGuinness state it
  verbatim for class hierarchies; Dupré and Hjørland give the philosophical grounding; Bowker and Star give
  the empirical record for large standards taxonomies; and the levels-of-organisation literature itself
  (per SEP) treats levels as heuristic rather than uniquely ordered. Deferring the ladder's structure to
  the author's ruling is therefore normal practice in the relevant fields.

  Caveats: (a) Stipulative legitimacy is a claim about the domain; it does not by itself show that ruling is
  BETTER than reading the author's own tables against a decision rule when the tables already exist. (b)
  Grüninger and Fox partially cut the other way: their method checks the ontology against stated
  competency questions, which is a decision rule of the kind PRESUMPTION-915 says was not applied. (c) The
  SEP levels-of-organisation entry reports pluralism and heuristic use but does not directly address the
  total-order vs lattice vs per-tradition distinction; support is by inference. (d) Search scope:
  preliminary — 6 queries; the BFO / upper-ontology design literature (Smith, Arp), Ereshefsky on
  taxonomic pluralism, and the Wimsatt levels literature were not covered and would sharpen this.
