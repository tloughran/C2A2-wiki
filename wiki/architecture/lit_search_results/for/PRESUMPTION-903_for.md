  Summary: Searching in the supportive direction returned nothing. I found no source arguing, or reporting evidence
  consistent with, the proposition that retrospective extraction of reasoning is time-indifferent, and no source
  treating a delayed reconstruction as equivalent to a contemporaneous one. Every relevant literature located runs
  the other way, and does so consistently across three independent traditions. Eyewitness-memory research reports a
  measurable forgetting trajectory with delay and — the more damaging finding for "deferred, not lost" — that
  reminiscence on re-interview is *least* apparent at the longest delays, so the second pass yields less, not the
  same. Hindsight-bias research, robust across fifty years, reports that outcome knowledge causes reviewers to
  construct narrative explanations and to re-weight material they had previously passed over, which means a delayed
  extraction does not merely recover less but recovers something differently shaped. And the forensic literature
  adds that the substrate itself is perishable. The self-referential point in the intake note stands and is worth
  recording: this run's own extraction was performed on a daily digest rather than a transcript, i.e. on a record of
  what someone already noticed, which is precisely the filtered, outcome-aware material the hindsight literature
  warns about.

  Caveats: (i) All the memory evidence concerns human recall; assumption extraction from a *written* artefact is
  not memory retrieval, and to the extent the substrate is a durable text the decay limb transfers weakly. The
  hindsight-bias limb transfers much better, because it concerns a reviewer reading a record with the outcome
  already known — which is exactly the case here. (ii) The forensic "golden hour" sources are practitioner
  marketing and carry little independent weight; they are listed for completeness, not relied on. (iii) A NULL in
  the supportive direction is not proof the presumption is false — it is possible that a literature on deferred
