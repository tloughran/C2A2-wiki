    (Searching for support of the presumption returned, uniformly, sources that name the failure mode.
    Listed here because their existence is itself the finding, and because the conditional form of the
    presumption does draw partial support from them.)
    1. Lakatos, I. Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes (via Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy,
       "Imre Lakatos"; qualityresearchinternational.com summary). — Names the failure mode directly:
       a degenerating problemshift, where theoretical growth lags empirical growth and modifications give
       only post-hoc accommodation. Also supplies the CONDITIONAL under which amendment IS improvement:
       the modification must be theoretically progressive (excess empirical content, predicting novel
       facts) and those novel facts must be at least occasionally corroborated.
    2. Popper, K. (via Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Popper, Karl: Philosophy of Science";
       theotodman.com PDF of Conjectures and Refutations pp. 33-58). — States the rule explicitly:
       only those auxiliary hypotheses are acceptable whose introduction does NOT diminish the degree of
       falsifiability, but increases it. An amendment introduced to explain a particular difficulty and not
       independently testable is "ad hoc" and reduces falsifiability.
    3. "Moving the goalposts" / special pleading (Wikipedia; Logically Fallacious; Snopes 464308).
       — Snippet-level only. Names the accretive version of the failure mode: continual redefinition after
       each counter-argument, "effectively making it impossible to refute their claim."
    4. Aeon Essays, "Imre Lakatos and the philosophy of bad science." — Snippet-level only. Popular-level
       framing of the same distinction; useful as a plain-language statement of the criterion but not as
       primary evidence.

  Strength of support: None (for the presumption as stated); Moderate for a conditional reformulation.

  Summary: I could not find literature supporting the unqualified proposition that amendment is
    improvement. The search returned the opposite with unusual consistency: every relevant tradition
    names a failure mode for amendment, and names it centrally rather than as a footnote. Popper's rule
    is a bright line — an amendment is acceptable only if it raises falsifiability. Lakatos's criterion is
    the same line drawn dynamically — a belt modification counts as progress only if it is
    content-increasing and occasionally corroborated. Informal logic names the accreted version as a
    fallacy. What the literature does support is a CONDITIONAL: amendment is improvement when, and only
    when, it is content-increasing. The presumption as held by the pipeline drops the antecedent, and it
    is precisely the antecedent that does the epistemic work. The claim that "no failure mode is named"
    is therefore false about the literature — it is a description of the pipeline's methodology, not of
    the state of knowledge.

  Caveats: This is a search FOR support and it found none, so the finding is a negative one and should be
    read as such — it does not establish that every amendment in the pipeline is degenerative. Lakatos and
    Popper are both contested in detail (the ad hoc/non-ad hoc boundary is notoriously hard to operationalise;
    see the Higgs-mechanism debate at arXiv:1305.5110, snippet-level only), so the criterion is a
    direction of travel rather than a decision procedure. Transfer concern: the criterion assumes empirical
    content and novel-fact prediction, which maps awkwardly onto a wiki of conceptual proposals where many
    claims are interpretive rather than predictive. An analogue of "excess content" would need to be defined
    for this project before the criterion is usable — e.g. does the amendment create a new way the claim
    could be found wrong?

    Searched scope: philosophy of science (Popper, Lakatos), informal logic, unfalsifiability-by-accretion.
    Finding: NOT novel, and the 14b novelty flag should be withdrawn. The failure mode the pipeline has
      left unnamed is one of the most thoroughly named objects in the field. The novelty was an artefact
      of the pipeline not having looked.
    Implication: This is directly load-bearing for the project's MacIntyre commitment. MacIntyre's
      criterion for rational tradition-progress requires that a tradition be able to say, from inside
      itself, that an earlier position was defective — i.e. it requires adjudication capacity. A pipeline
      that absorbs every challenge into the statement cannot satisfy that criterion, because it never
      records a defeat. The recommended remedy is concrete: attach a content-grade
      (increasing / preserving / reducing) to every amendment, and require that a fixed proportion of
      challenges terminate in adjudication rather than amendment.
