  Summary: The re-check finds the premise in better shape than at validation. The most significant development since April is theoretical: the 2025 emergence work derives linear analogy structure from co-occurrence spectra, giving the displacement-vector mechanism a principled basis rather than a purely empirical one. In parallel, the Linear Representation Hypothesis program has continued to validate concept-directions in modern LLMs through causal interventions, and the Webb/Holyoak/Lu counterfactual-task results have blunted the strongest published objection to emergent analogical capability. The limitation literature has also matured (multi-dimensional features, manifold structure), which supports the premise's explicit claim that limitations are known and manageable via validation protocols rather than undermining it.

  Caveats: Support is for the mechanism's validity, not for any particular application's outputs — cross-tradition similarity claims still require the premise's validation protocols case by case. The strong (universal) form of linear representation is empirically refuted in low-capacity networks and for inherently multi-dimensional concepts; displacement-vector methods will systematically miss relations encoded on manifolds or in circular features. Cross-domain (cross-tradition) analogy via embeddings remains less directly benchmarked than within-vocabulary analogy; the second-order inference from "linear analogies exist" to "structural similarity across traditions is identifiable" retains a gap that only the project's own validation data can close.
