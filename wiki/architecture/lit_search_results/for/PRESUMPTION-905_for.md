  Summary: The ontology-evolution literature from the Leipzig group (Gross, Hartung, Rahm and colleagues, 2009–2016) is the closest precedent and is broadly favourable: annotations against an evolving classification scheme are mostly stable, instability is concentrated around the changed concepts, and the recommended practice is to identify the affected annotations from the change pattern and re-process only those. Change-impact analysis in software evolution supplies the same principle in general form. Practitioner guidance on annotation-guideline changes agrees that adding a label does not require restarting the project. The TAR literature supports keyword-seeded selection as an efficient way to find members of a newly-defined class. All of this supports the *form* of what was done — a targeted re-pass rather than full re-classification.

  Caveats: (a) Every supportive source makes the same condition explicit: the affected set must be *computed from the change* (which concepts are siblings/parents of the new one) and the repair *measured* (stability measures; held-out re-validation; recall sampling). The literature therefore supports the presumption's method but not its unmeasured status.
