  Sources:
    1. Pollock, J.L. (2008). "Defeasible Reasoning," in J.E. Adler & L.J. Rips (eds.), Reasoning: Studies of Human Inference and Its Foundations, Cambridge University Press; and Koons, R., "Defeasible Reasoning," Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. — Pollock's canonical distinction: a *rebutting* defeater supplies reason to believe the negation of the conclusion; an *undercutting* defeater "gives one a reason for doubting that the usual relationship between the premises and the conclusion holds in the given case" — it attacks the inference itself without asserting the conclusion is false. On Pollock's account a conclusion is warranted only if supported by an *ultimately undefeated* argument. This is the presumption's exact structure, named in external literature: defect (2) (inflated apparent convergence) and defect (5) (an is/ought correction to the inference structure) are undercutting defeaters of the stated ground. A verdict that survives an undercutting defeater is no longer a warranted verdict, even if it is still a true one — so "the disposition was not wrong" and "the grounds were only presentationally defective" are separate claims, and the second does not establish the first.
    2. Leamer, E.E. (1983). "Let's Take the Con Out of Econometrics." American Economic Review 73(1):31-43. — Leamer's central methodological claim is that inference rests on assumptions that must themselves be varied, and that "the professional audience consequently and properly withholds belief until an inference is shown to be adequately insensitive to the choice of assumptions," urging that fragility be studied systematically rather than assumed away. Directly supports the presumption's core: stability of a conclusion under revised premises is a result to be demonstrated, never a default. Classifying six grounds-level defects as non-substantive asserts insensitivity without running the fragility check that would establish it.
    3. Saltelli, A., et al. (2008). Global Sensitivity Analysis: The Primer. Wiley; and Saltelli, A. & Funtowicz, S., "Sensitivity auditing" (see Saltelli et al., "What do I make of your Latinorum? Sensitivity auditing of mathematical modelling," Int. J. Foresight and Innovation Policy, 2013; sensitivity auditing is recommended by the European Commission and SAPEA 2018). — Sensitivity analysis good practice prescribes that uncertainty in an inference be quantified by simultaneously activating *all* the assumptions' uncertainties, then identifying which assumptions are chiefly responsible for output uncertainty. The associated Leamer criterion — conclusions are "sturdy" only if a credibly wide neighbourhood of assumptions yields a usefully narrow interval of inferences — is the operational form of the test the presumption says was skipped. This is external, standards-adjacent support for the specific remedy: re-derive the disposition with defects (2) and (5) corrected and see whether it survives.
    4. IAASB, ISA 450, Evaluation of Misstatements Identified during the Audit (IFAC/IAASB Handbook); and Rice, S.C. & Weber, J.P. (2012). "How Effective Is Internal Control Reporting under SOX 404? Determinants of the (Non-)Disclosure of Existing Material Weaknesses." Journal of Accounting Research 50(3):811-843. — ISA 450 is explicit that a *classification* misstatement is evaluated on qualitative grounds and can be material irrespective of amount, and that an item numerically trivial may have qualitative significance: whether a defect is presentational is a judgement that must be argued from effect, not asserted from category. Rice & Weber supply the empirical base rate the presumption's risk profile needs: a large majority of restating firms had not reported the existing material weakness prior to the restatement announcement, and the disclosure rate declined over the period studied — i.e., under-classification of a real substantive defect as something lesser is the common direction of error in practice, not the rare one, and it is corrected only by an event external to the classifying party.
    5. Ross, L., Lepper, M.R. & Hubbard, M. (1975). "Perseverance in Self-Perception and Social Perception: Biased Attributional Processes in the Debriefing Paradigm." Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 32(5):880-892; with Anderson, C.A., Lepper, M.R. & Ross, L. (1980). "Perseverance of Social Theories: The Role of Explanation in the Persistence of Discredited Information." JPSP 39(6):1037-1049. — After the evidential basis of a judgement was *completely discredited* by thorough debriefing, subjects' judgements persisted at levels well above chance; the 1980 extension identifies the mechanism as the explanation the subject constructed while holding the belief, which survives the removal of the evidence that prompted it. This is the psychological base rate the presumption warns about: the default human response to a discredited ground is to retain the conclusion and re-narrate the grounds, which is precisely what a blanket "drafting, not disposition" classification looks like from the outside. Support here is analogical (lab judgement tasks, not verification verdicts) but it is the best-established finding on the specific behaviour at issue.

  Summary: Supported, and EXTERNALLY ANCHORED (Pollock/SEP on defeasible reasoning; Leamer 1983 in AER; Saltelli et al. on sensitivity analysis and sensitivity auditing; IAASB ISA 450 and Rice & Weber 2012 in JAR; Ross, Lepper & Hubbard 1975 in JPSP). The epistemology literature names the exact structure the presumption identifies: an inflation of apparent convergence and an is/ought correction are *undercutting* defeaters, which remove warrant from an inference without asserting the conclusion is false — so establishing that the disposition might still be right does nothing to establish that its stated ground survived. Leamer and the sensitivity-analysis tradition supply the positive requirement: insensitivity of a conclusion to revision of its premises is a result that must be demonstrated by re-deriving under the revised premises, and belief is properly withheld until it is. Audit standards make the same move in the applied register — a classification defect's materiality is a qualitative judgement argued from effect, and Rice & Weber show empirically that when parties classify their own defects the error runs toward under-classification, corrected only by an external event. Finally, the belief-perseverance literature establishes that retaining a conclusion after its basis has been discredited is the default rather than the exception, which is the specific failure mode the presumption flags. The literature does not show the disposition is wrong; it shows the classification does not license the inference that it is unaffected.

  Caveats: (a) Undercutting defeat is a claim about warrant, not truth: every source here supports "the stated ground no longer carries the conclusion," and none supports "the disposition is wrong." The presumption is correctly framed as being about propagation of defect, and its supporters should not be read past that. (b) The presumption's force depends entirely on a premise 15a cannot verify from literature — that convergence really is *the* stated ground of the disposition, and that defect (2) really does inflate it materially rather than marginally. If the disposition has independent sufficient grounds, defect (2) is an undercutter of one strand only and the "drafting" classification may be correct; this is an in-house recomputation, not a literature question. (c) Defect (5), the is/ought correction, is the stronger half: the Hume/Moore is-ought gap is a claim about deductive validity, so a correction at that level is by definition structural rather than presentational, and no literature was found that would license classifying an inference-structure repair as drafting. (d) Rice & Weber concerns financial-reporting controls in SOX-era US firms; the base rate transfers as a direction-of-error signal, not as a magnitude. (e) Ross et al. and Anderson et al. are laboratory studies on naive subjects; transfer to a structured verification process with an explicit defect list is by analogy and the process's own auditability cuts against the effect. (f) Moderate-to-comprehensive search scope: seven targeted searches plus two citation-verification searches; no search was run for domain-specific literature on multi-agent or LLM-pipeline self-assessment, which may exist.
