    1. Kuhn, T.S., taxonomic incommensurability and the NO-OVERLAP PRINCIPLE (1991, 1993, collected in
       *The Road Since Structure*, 2000); with Feyerabend, P. (1962), "Explanation, Reduction and
       Empiricism," on meaning variance. — **The direct support for clause (C1), and the strongest-
       verified source in this file.** Kuhn's later position is that "**taxonomic incommensurability
       results in translation failure between local subsets of inter-defined terms due to the
       CROSS-CLASSIFICATION of objects into mutually exclusive taxonomies**." The no-overlap principle
       states that no two kind terms may overlap in their referents unless related as species to genus;
       and kind terms "must be learned TOGETHER" — 'force' with 'mass' and 'weight' through Hooke's law
       and Newton's laws — so a term's content is fixed by its position in a whole contrast set, not by
       its spelling. Consequently "theories separated by a revolution **cross-classify the same things
       into mutually exclusive sets of kinds**," producing "conflicting expectations about the same
       objects, loss of logical relations between statements made with those concepts, and ultimately
       incoherence and miscommunication." Feyerabend's independent version: the Newtonian concepts
       represented by 'mass', 'length' and 'time' are incommensurable with their relativistic
       counterparts, and rival theories give **different meanings to the SAME observation sentences**.
       The canonical worked case is exactly 827's failure mode — 'mass' in Newtonian and in relativistic
       mechanics does not have the same referent, though a lexical matcher would score it as a perfect
       hit. Ptolemy's 'planet' includes the sun; Copernicus's does not. **Two traditions sharing a word
       is, on this account, WEAK EVIDENCE OF AGREEMENT AND STRONG EVIDENCE THAT THE WORD IS DOING
       DIFFERENT WORK.**

  Summary: The corrective proposition is strongly supported, and four independent disciplines converge
  on it. Kuhn's mature account makes shared vocabulary positively misleading rather than merely
  uninformative: kind terms are learned together inside a contrast set, so content is fixed by taxonomic
  position, and theories separated by a revolution cross-classify the same objects into mutually
  exclusive kinds while retaining the same words — 'mass', 'planet'. MacIntyre extends this to traditions
  of enquiry and adds the crucial bound: incommensurability is never absolute, but the evidence of a
  shared commitment is the ability to render a claim in the other tradition's own terms, not the
  recurrence of a word. Ontology matching supplies the most directly transferable form — label-based
  matchers are a recognised source of false positives between "semantically related but non-equivalent"
  concepts, and the standard remedy is **label penalties where structural signals are absent**, i.e. an
  uncorroborated lexical match is scored DOWN. False-friend detection supplies the only measured rate
  located: 73-81% accuracy for a purpose-built detector, so even dedicated machinery errs on about one
  pair in five. Comparative theology names conflation and the flattening of difference as its
  occupational hazard. **The most consequential finding of this run is not in the literature but in the
