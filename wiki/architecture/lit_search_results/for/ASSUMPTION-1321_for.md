  Caveats:
    (i) **The "all 20 cases" figure is not supported by anything in this file and I did not try to
      support it.** It is a self-measurement from inside the instrument. PREMISE-124 requires an
      external baseline, a seeded denominator, or an UNCALIBRATED tag; PREMISE-116 requires a
      pre-registered 4-8% expectation stated before the sweep. Neither exists. 20/20 must not be quoted
      as a rate, on the same ground PREMISE-174 records for 2/2.
    (ii) **Domain transfer.** Kawamoto measures human clinicians in real clinical settings in trials
      ending 2003. The mechanism it establishes — that knowledge must arrive in the actor's workflow at
      the decision moment — is plausibly stronger for an LLM agent, which has no persistent memory
      between runs and therefore cannot "know" a premise it was not shown. But that is my inference, not
      the review's finding, and no source I located tests point-of-decision knowledge delivery in
      multi-agent LLM systems.
    (iii) **Confidence-interval width.** The DARE abstract's own commentary flags that the regression
      sample was small; the time-and-location OR of 15.4 has a 95% CI of 1.3-300.6, and the workflow OR's
      upper bound is infinity. The direction is solid, the magnitude is not. Do not quote 112.1 as a
      magnitude.
    (iv) **Selection bias, stated by CRD.** The search was RCTs, English only, MEDLINE/CINAHL/CCTR to
      2003; CRD's commentary notes relevant studies may have been missed and that results may be biased
      toward significant findings for abstractable process outcomes. Publication bias in the supportive
      direction is exactly what a FOR search should expect here.
    (v) **PREMISE-173 binds any remedy.** A push mechanism passes 173's acceptance test — the final
      element is the originating run's context, and what changes when it fires is what the run reads
      before acting. A "premise-consulted: yes" field does not pass, and would reproduce PREMISE-108's
      acknowledgement-receipt defect exactly.
