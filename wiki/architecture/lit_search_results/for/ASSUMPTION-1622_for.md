  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: Epidemiological surveillance offers a well-documented precedent: when the counting rule
    changes (or is ambiguous) and the count is nonetheless carried forward as continuous, every derived
    rate and trend inherits the artifact until comparable definitions are restored. CDC's 1993 AIDS
    case-definition expansion is the textbook case and explicitly required re-derivation of trends.
    Construct-validity theory predicts the same: downstream precision cannot repair an upstream
    definitional error. The assumption is an instance of an established principle rather than a novel
    claim.

  Caveats: The precedent concerns a known, documented definition change; the assumption concerns an
    *unresolved* gap, where the size and direction of bias may be unknown (support for "propagates" is
    strong; support for "into every downstream ratio" is logical rather than empirical — ratios whose
    numerator and denominator share the same bias may partially cancel). Transfer from public-health
    surveillance to pipeline PRS counts is analogical.
