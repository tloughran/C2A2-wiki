Lost Update is the case in which a transaction's committed write is silently destroyed by a second transaction that read an earlier state and wrote based on it: "at the end of this sequence T2's update will be lost, even if T2 commits." That is the 2026-08-14 pattern exactly, with the escalation as T2's update. Two consequences transfer. (i) THE ANOMALY IS INVISIBLE FROM EITHER SIDE: neither writer errs, neither writer can detect the loss, and the final state is internally consistent and wrong — which is why 14b had to find this by comparing transcripts rather than by inspecting the artefact. (ii) THE FIX IS NOT DISCIPLINE, IT IS REPRESENTATION: the anomaly is precluded either by holding a read lock to commit or by making the write CONDITIONAL ON THE VERSION READ. Exhortation to "check whether someone escalated it" is the null remedy the literature specifically does not offer.   Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is strongly supported and, unusually, is supported convergently by four fields that do not read each other. Databases supply the formal diagnosis: a writer that clears a value it never read is executing a blind write, and the resulting LOST UPDATE is invisible from both sides because neither writer errs and the final state is internally consistent — which is precisely why 14b had to detect this by comparing transcripts rather than by inspecting the artefact. Incident management supplies the semantic answer to the item's actual question: acknowledge and resolve are architecturally distinct operations, an acknowledged claim on attention re-triggers rather than decays, and closure-without-a-human-act exists but as an OPT-IN setting with a declared timeout, never as the default reading of a fresh measurement. Process safety supplies the shape of legitimate suppression: attributed, credentialed, time-limited, separately displayed and automatically re-enabling, with the stated rationale that a suppressed condition must not be forgotten. Clinical handoff supplies the transfer protocol: an action list carrying an explicit owner and deadline, and a read-back performed by the RECEIVER — the only located practice in which a transfer is completed by the receiving party rather than declared by the sending one, which is PREMISE-108's instrumentation constraint expressed as a procedure. Taken together they converge on the item's own formulation: an escalation is a claim on an external actor and no measurement internal to the system can discharge it, because measurement and adjudication are different operations addressed to different parties. The cause 14b names is also confirmed as the operative one: because `needs_review` is a computed predicate over timestamps, escalation has no representable state, and a state that cannot be represented cannot survive a write. That is a data-model defect, and every remedy located is a change to the representation rather than an instruction to future runs.

  Caveats:
    (a) THE REGISTER HOLDS THE DELIVERY AXIS AND THIS FILE MUST NOT RE-MINT IT. PREMISE-108 already holds that a flag does not transfer responsibility and that the loop closes only on evidence the recipient acted; PREMISE-138 already holds that escalation changes the addressee, not the amplitude; PREMISE-133 already holds that a suspension must name its discharge condition, adjudicator and deadline. The disjoint contribution here is the CONCURRENCY AXIS — a third party, uninvolved in the escalation, destroying it by a blind write — plus the DATA-MODEL claim that a derived predicate cannot carry a disposition. A disposition that reads this as new support for "escalations must reach a named actor" is re-minting 108.
    (b) 108'S INSTRUMENTATION CONSTRAINT BINDS THE REMEDY AND EXCLUDES THE OBVIOUS ONE. The tempting fix is an acknowledgement field. PREMISE-108 explicitly forbids treating that as delivery: it "makes 'acknowledged' measurable and leaves 'acted on' exactly as unmeasurable as before, installing a green metric over the unchanged failure." What survives 108 is narrower and is what the sources actually prescribe: not an acknowledgement, but (i) a PERSISTED escalation state that is not derivable from timestamps, (ii) a CONDITIONAL WRITE so that a clearing run must have read the prior disposition, and (iii) an EXPIRY that returns the item to visibility rather than to silence. None of these claims the human acted; they only prevent the claim from being erased.
    (c) THE STRONGEST OBJECTION IS INTERNAL AND MUST BE STATED: PREMISE-145 CUTS THE OTHER WAY. 145 holds that withholding a discrepant own-measurement is a reporting bias rather than a null act, and that deference to an incumbent published figure is not the conservative move. The clearing runs took a fresh measurement and published it, which 145 endorses. The resolution is that the corrective proposition does NOT require the later run to suppress its measurement — it requires the two to occupy different fields. A fresh measurement may legitimately update the MEASURED STATE; it may not thereby discharge the CLAIM ON ATTENTION, because those are different objects with different discharge conditions. If the disposition collapses this into "later runs must defer," it contradicts 145 and is wrong.
    (d) EVERY ANALOGY IMPORTS A COORDINATOR C2A2 DOES NOT HAVE. The DBMS enforces the version check; PagerDuty enforces the lifecycle; the DCS enforces shelving expiry; the oncoming clinician is physically present for the read-back. C2A2 has scheduled tasks writing markdown with no arbiter. What transfers is the DATA MODEL — an escalation must be a persisted, attributed, expiring field with an owner, distinguishable from staleness — which is a file-format decision. What does NOT transfer without building something is ENFORCEMENT: nothing prevents a future run from overwriting the field anyway, and the version-conditional write is only as good as the runs that honour it. PREMISE-164 is the governing constraint on any such remedy: the record is durable only if it is written where the next executor's own procedure requires it to read.
    (e) THE CLINICAL AND INCIDENT-MANAGEMENT SOURCES CARRY INTERVENTION-POSITIVE PUBLICATION BIAS, and the handoff figures here are second-hand with a live conflation hazard between two different I-PASS studies. The 47% and the two-thirds-of-sentinel-events figures are cited as order-of-magnitude context and are NOT load-bearing for any claim in this file; the load-bearing content from source 5 is the PROTOCOL STRUCTURE (owner field, receiver read-back), which is not in dispute.
    (f) PER PREMISE-777, "two independent instances, four days affected" is an unmarked self-report by the run that counted it, though the item quotes both sides verbatim, which is checkable. The argument does not depend on the count: one escalation silently cleared by a run that never read it is sufficient to establish that the representation cannot hold the distinction.

  Recommendation: SUPPORTED (Strong) for the corrective proposition; equivalently NO-SUPPORT-FOUND for the presumption as worded. The disposition-worthy residual is the concurrency axis plus the data model, and it is concrete. An escalation must be a PERSISTED FIELD, not a predicate computed from timestamps: it should carry who escalated it, to whom, on what ground, and what would discharge it — and it must be excluded by construction from any predicate a re-measurement can flip. A run that clears an item must first read that field, and if the field is set, the run may update its own measurement but may not clear the escalation; only the named actor, or a declared expiry that returns the item to visibility, discharges it. That is the version-conditional write and the shelving expiry, expressed as a file convention. The in-house test is one query, answerable today: for every item currently NOT in `needs_review`, can the record distinguish "reviewed and passed" from "escalated and subsequently overwritten"? If it cannot, the item's Critical-adjacent risk clause holds and the size of the silent withdrawal is not merely unknown but unrecoverable. Caveat (c) must carry forward: this does not license later runs to withhold discrepant measurements, which PREMISE-145 forbids — it separates the measured state from the claim on attention.
