  Sources:
    1. "Agent role structure and operating characteristics in large language model clinical classification: A comparative study of specialist and deliberative multi-agent protocols." Informatics in Medicine Unlocked (ScienceDirect S2352914826000535); preprint medRxiv 2026.02.22.26346818. — The single most on-point source located, and the closest thing in the literature to the controlled experiment the disposition asked for. It is a controlled architectural study that isolates *internal role decomposition as the sole manipulated variable*, holding base weights, decoding settings, compute budget and adjudication logic fixed, comparing a Generic Deliberative protocol against a Feature-Specialist protocol. On the Cleveland Heart Disease benchmark the Feature-Specialist (split-role) protocol increased overall accuracy and macro-F1 relative to Generic Deliberative. Conclusion drawn by the authors: prompt-level agent organisation functions as a meaningful inductive bias and should be treated as a core design variable.
    2. "Specialize Roles, Mix Deployments: Pushing the Cost–Accuracy Frontier of LLM Agent Teams," arXiv:2606.20629 (May 2026). — Introduces AgentCARD, a role-aware benchmark with a role-decomposed evaluation harness, unified cost model, Pareto-frontier analysis and a Shapley-based diagnostic for role bottlenecks. Reports heterogeneous role-specialised teams improving accuracy by up to 44% over cost-equivalent homogeneous teams, or matching the strongest homogeneous team at up to 12x lower per-task cost. Crucially for this item, it also reports that *adding agents without role specialisation* pays the cost of multiple calls but recovers only the accuracy of one — i.e. the lift comes from specialisation, not from agent count. That is precisely the mechanism PRESUMPTION-001 presumes.
    3. "When Does Multi-Agent RL Improve LLM Workflows?" arXiv:2605.24202, together with figures surfaced alongside it: multi-agent methods raising long-horizon planning accuracy from a 14–47% baseline to 96.0–99.5%, with average code and math gains of 3.87–7.62% and 9.0–17.93%. — Establishes that multi-agent decomposition can produce large, task-dependent gains, while framing the question as conditional ("when does").
    4. "Traceability and Accountability in Role-Specialized Multi-Agent LLM Pipelines," arXiv:2510.07614. — Supports a *secondary* benefit of the 14a/14b split that the presumption does not itself claim: role-specialised pipelines are more traceable and auditable. For C2A2, where provenance chains are a first-class artifact, this is a real net-quality contribution independent of raw extraction accuracy.
    5. "Efficient and Verified Research Data Extraction with LLM," Algorithms, doi:10.3390/a19030214. — Extraction-domain analogue: a schema-guided, verification-focused pipeline that separates extraction from verification, explicitly trading a small amount of recall for a larger gain in precision. Directly relevant because 14a (extract stated assumptions) and 14b (infer unstated presumptions) have opposite recall/precision profiles — 14b is a low-precision, high-inference task that would plausibly degrade 14a's precision if fused into one prompt.
    6. Horling, B., & Lesser, V. (2004). "A Survey of Multi-Agent Organizational Paradigms." The Knowledge Engineering Review, 19(4), 281–316. — Carried forward from the cycle-0 block as background on organisational paradigms.   Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: This cycle located, for the first time, a study of the exact design the disposition asked for: a compute-matched, single-variable comparison of a unified/deliberative protocol against a split-specialist protocol, in which the split protocol won on accuracy and macro-F1. Combined with AgentCARD's finding that the accuracy lift in agent teams comes specifically from role specialisation rather than from adding agents, the mechanism underlying PRESUMPTION-001 now has real empirical backing rather than the analogical hand-waving of the April block. The extraction-domain evidence adds a plausible task-specific reason the split should help here in particular: 14a is a precision-oriented extraction task and 14b a recall-oriented inference task, and pipelines that separate such stages trade a little recall for a larger precision gain. The presumption is therefore better supported than it was in April — but the support is analogical (clinical tabular classification, agent teams, data extraction), not direct, and the strongest counter-evidence is compute-based and has not been ruled out.

  Caveats:
    - The decisive confound is compute, not architecture. "Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets" (arXiv:2604.02460) reports that single-agent systems consistently match or outperform multi-agent systems when reasoning tokens are held constant, and attributes apparent multi-agent advantages to computational rather than architectural benefit. A 14a+14b split doubles the compute against a unified Agent 14; unless C2A2 has run a token-matched comparison, the presumed gain is unfalsified rather than confirmed. This is the sharpest open question and it is not closed by any source above.
    - Source 1's win is partial and comes with a cost: the Feature-Specialist protocol shifted the operating point toward greater specificity and reduced sensitivity. Applied to C2A2, a split that improves precision while lowering sensitivity is *actively bad* for 14b, whose whole function is surfacing unstated presumptions nobody noticed — a recall task. The same paper warns that poorly chosen, weak or noisy evidence channels may shift the sensitivity–specificity balance in undesirable directions, and that multi-agent role structure requires explicit validation rather than an assumption of safety or robustness. That is close to a direct instruction not to hold this presumption unexamined.
    - Domain distance: none of the located evidence is on assumption/presumption extraction from architectural documents. Clinical tabular classification and agent-team benchmarks are different task classes with different error structures.
    - Coordination overhead is asserted in the April block but was not quantified by anything found this cycle. AgentCARD's cost model is the nearest instrument, but it prices deployment cost, not the reconciliation cost of merging 14a and 14b outputs downstream.
    - Self-referential hazard: this item's evaluation is being performed by the very split architecture (15a/15b) whose sibling presumption it is. A split-architecture agent assessing whether splitting helps is not a neutral instrument. Recommend 15c weight this accordingly.
    - Scope: the presumption claims quality improvement, not cost-effectiveness. Sources 2 and 3 largely measure the cost–accuracy frontier; a quality-only claim can be true while the net trade is bad, and vice versa.
