GMP out-of-tolerance handling (gmpsop, "Instrument calibration procedure for pharmaceutical industry"; gxpready, "Why Calibration Programs Fail GMP Audits"; IPS, "Pharmaceutical Calibration Compliance"; Frederick National Laboratory, "Equipment Calibration Program"). — The most directly applicable rule and it is the exact negation of the presumption. When an instrument is found out of calibration the required sequence is: tag it, *stop its use*, raise a deviation, assess product impact, implement corrective action. The presumption 508 surfaces — that naming the defect licenses finishing the run — is the specific practice this regime forbids. Continued use of a known out-of-tolerance instrument is not a judgement call in this discipline; it is a stop condition.
    2. GMP retrospective impact assessment practice (same sources; NIST GMP 11, "Assignment and Adjustment of Calibration Intervals"). — Supplies the second half. Organisations must "identify measurements made with the instrument since last calibration and assess whether the drift affects product quality," and inspectors treat noting an out-of-tolerance condition and proceeding without a formal impact assessment as "a serious gap that raises questions about whether the organization understands the effect of instrument failures on... data integrity." That is a precise description of recording a defect in REVISE-233 and dispositioning fourteen items anyway. The prescribed scope is retrospective, covering prior results as well as current ones.
    3. "Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM Evaluation Panels." arXiv:2605.29800 (Apple Machine Learning Research). — Establishes that the named defect is real and quantified, not a hedge. Nine judges across seven model families deliver about two independent votes; panel accuracy falls 8-22 points below what independence would give, and the best single judge matches or beats the panel. Aggregation over correlated inputs demonstrably fails to add information and can subtract it.
    4. "CARE: Confounder-Aware Aggregation for Reliable LLM Evaluation." arXiv:2603.00039. — The strong form of the defect 15c named: with correlated judge errors driven by shared latent confounders, standard aggregation rules "provide little gain or even amplify systematic mistakes." Negative net gain from an aggregation step is a documented outcome, so the instrument in question is not merely uncertain but potentially inverting.
    5. "More Convincing, Not More Correct: Self-Play Reward Hacking of Reference-Free LLM Judges." arXiv:2607.05904. — Adds a second, independent defect class for reference-free judging, relevant because 15c's dispositions have no external reference. Supports treating a self-flagged judge as under-qualified rather than merely noisy.
    6. Static-analysis and CDS self-audit practice (retrieved for ASSUMPTION-489; PMC4052586). — Cross-domain corroboration: in both software and clinical alerting, a detector found to be low-precision is remediated before its output is acted on, and independent triage rather than self-triage is the norm.

  Strength of support: Strong
  Summary: The presumption is squarely contradicted by the discipline that has thought hardest about it. In regulated metrology an instrument found out of tolerance is tagged and withdrawn from service, a deviation is raised, and a *retrospective* impact assessment identifies every measurement made since the last known-good calibration; noting the condition and carrying on is explicitly named as an inspection finding indicative of a failure to understand data integrity. The parallel to 508 is close to exact, including the retrospective scope, which matters because the item observes that not only the fourteen current dispositions but every prior one is of unknown quality. The defect itself is well evidenced rather than hypothetical: correlated-error work shows nine judges from seven model families collapsing to about two independent votes with panel accuracy 8-22 points below the independence benchmark, and confounder-aware work shows that aggregation over correlated inputs can amplify systematic error — a negative-net-gain regime. An instrument for which negative net gain is a live possibility, self-identified, and unquantified is the paradigm of one requiring qualification before further use. The item's proposed remedies are both well matched to this literature: measure 15a/15b source-citation overlap under full blocking (the qualification measurement), and add a status value marking items produced under a self-flagged instrument (the tagging step).
  Caveats: (a) The GMP regime governs product release with patient-safety and legal consequences; C2A2's dispositions are internal analytic judgements. The proportionate transfer is the *tag-and-assess* discipline, not a hard stop — and a literal stop-use rule would be self-defeating here, since the only agent that could re-run the dispositions is the flagged one. (b) The correlated-error literature quantifies loss for *panels*; 15c is a single reconciler over two correlated inputs, a related but not identical configuration, and I found no source measuring net gain for that specific topology. The claim that it can worsen the aggregate is supported in kind, not in magnitude. (c) 508's own reasoning has a recursive problem the literature sharpens: the judgement that the instrument is defective was itself produced by the instrument, so the disclosure is only as reliable as the thing it impugns — which argues for external measurement rather than for either trusting or discounting the self-report. (d) The retrieved GMP material is practitioner and regulatory-guidance writing; NIST GMP 11 and the arXiv sources carry the empirical weight, and the latter are preprints of unverified peer-review status. (e) Publication bias: papers reporting that judge ensembles fail are more publishable than null results, so the magnitude of the defect may be overstated in the retrieved set.
