    1. Ivers, N. et al. (2012). "Audit and feedback: effects on professional practice and healthcare outcomes." Cochrane Database Syst Rev CD000259.pub3. PMID 22696318. Updated 2025 as CD000259.pub4. — The decisive quantitative source. Across the randomized evidence base, audit and feedback yields a weighted median adjusted absolute compliance improvement of 4.3%, IQR 0.5% to 16%. Measuring a party's own deviation and reporting it back to them — the closest available analogue to validating a premise about an agent's conduct — has a median effect near zero and a lower quartile indistinguishable from nothing. The presumption 506 names is therefore not merely unproven; the best-evidenced comparable intervention says it is usually false.
    2. Ivers et al. 2025 update (CD000259.pub4), moderator analysis. — Identifies the conditions under which feedback does change behaviour: comparison against high-performing peers, measurement of the recipient's own practice, delivery by a respected peer with an existing relationship, multiple modalities, an explicit target, and an action plan. A validated premise written to a registry with no propagation step possesses none of these. This converts 506 from an observation into a prediction with a mechanism.
    3. Implementation-science literature on the know-do gap (Bauer & Kirchner et al. via "Implementation of Implementation Science Knowledge: The Research-Practice Gap Paradox," PMC6899530; "So what? Elevating the impact of implementation science," Implementation Science Communications, doi:10.1186/s43058-025-00831-9; WHO/MEASURE Evaluation, "Bridging the Know-Do Gap"). — Establishes the general finding as one of the field's founding premises: "knowing what to do does not ensure doing what we know," and despite the rapid growth of implementation science "the knowledge-practice gap in health care is still substantial, with low rates of adoption and limited use of evidence-based interventions." The recursive finding in PMC6899530 is directly on point for a self-awareness layer: implementation science itself fails to implement its own knowledge.
    4. Rull, V. et al. / "Knowing but not doing: Quantifying the research-implementation gap in conservation paleobiology." Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution 11:1058992 (2023). — Cross-domain quantification of the same gap in a discipline with no clinical incentives, establishing that it is a general property of knowledge systems rather than an artifact of healthcare.
    5. CAPA and closed-loop corrective-action practice (thefdagroup, "Definitive Guide to CAPA"; KPI Depot, "CAPA Effectiveness"; Atlas Compliance). — Supplies the architectural remedy 506 says is missing. Effectiveness checks are mandatory and a CAPA is not closed until data confirms the problem is resolved; recurrence at 6-12 months post-closure is the definitive metric, and recurrence above 10-15% is read as evidence the fix was never verified. The existence of this machinery as a regulatory requirement is evidence that finding-to-behaviour propagation does not occur unless it is built.
    6. Internal-audit follow-up practice (ACCA, "A brief guide to follow up"; ISACA Now 2025, "Follow Up Audits and the Auditor's Impact"; GSA Internal Audit Followup Handbook; Chambers, "Followup Audits Are a Waste of Time!"). — The professional standard requires the chief audit executive to establish a follow-up process to confirm management actions were effectively implemented or that senior management formally accepted the risk, with escalation matrices and 60-day overdue thresholds. Direct precedent for 506's proposed remedy: name the governed party per finding and verify the change.

  Strength of support: Strong
  Summary: This is among the best-evidenced items in the batch, and the literature does not merely support the presumption's identification — it inverts the default. The know-do gap is a founding observation of implementation science, quantified across disciplines, and the closest measured analogue to C2A2's mechanism (audit and feedback: measure a party's conduct, report the deviation back) produces a median absolute improvement of 4.3% with a lower quartile of 0.5%. On that base rate, 110 premises would be expected to have changed nearly nothing, and the same-day recurrence 506 cites is a normal draw rather than a surprising one. The Cochrane moderator analysis explains why: feedback works when it is peer-delivered, individually targeted, repeated, and paired with an action plan, and a premise written to a registry with no propagation step has none of those properties. Two mature professional disciplines — quality systems and internal audit — maintain mandatory verification machinery (effectiveness checks, follow-up audits, escalation thresholds) built on the explicit assumption that findings do not propagate to behaviour unless propagation is engineered and confirmed. The item's proposed test, one pass naming the governed agent per premise and searching for an attributable change, is exactly the effectiveness check both disciplines require and is the cheapest available way to convert this from inference to measurement.
  Caveats: (a) All the strongest sources concern humans with agency and competing priorities. The mechanism differs: clinicians receive feedback and fail to act, whereas an agent with no propagation channel never receives it at all. The effect sizes therefore bound optimism rather than predict the C2A2 outcome — if anything they are generous, since 4.3% presupposes a channel exists. (b) The item's core structural claim — that no agent definition contains a propagation step — is an internal architectural fact outside search scope, and it is the load-bearing one. If a propagation path exists and is merely weak, this becomes an effectiveness problem rather than an architecture problem, and the remedies differ. (c) The evidence here substantially overlaps ASSUMPTION-488's; they are one finding seen from two angles and should not be counted as independent confirmations. (d) A caution on the remedy: the audit literature's own reflexive critique (Chambers) is that follow-up processes can themselves become ritual, generating status reports rather than changes — which is the same failure one level up, and is the pattern PRESUMPTION-509 names. A propagation step that produces a "propagated: yes" field would reproduce ASSUMPTION-486's defect exactly. The measure must be a behavioural change in the governed agent's output. (e) CAPA and internal-audit sources are practitioner/regulatory material; source 1 carries the evidential weight.
