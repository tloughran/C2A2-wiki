  Sources:
    1. Isomura, T., Kotani, K., Jimbo, Y., Friston, K., 2023. "Experimental validation of the free-energy principle with in vitro neural networks." Nature Communications 14. — NEW to this file and the strongest empirical source available. Reverse-engineering of in vitro rat cortical neuron networks performing causal inference confirmed quantitative predictions of the FEP: variational free-energy minimisation quantitatively predicted network responses and plasticity. Establishes predictive validity of the FEP at one specific scale, which is the only scale where such validation currently exists.
    2. arXiv 2502.21217, 2025. "Dynamic Markov Blanket Detection for Macroscopic Physics Discovery." — NEW. Important for the "fundamental structure" leg: supplies a method for *detecting* Markov blankets in dynamical data rather than stipulating them by modeller fiat. If blankets can be recovered algorithmically at multiple scales, the claim that boundaries are objectively present rather than imposed becomes testable.
    3. arXiv 2506.05794, 2025. "Markov Blanket Density and Free Energy Minimization." — Carried forward (April cited it generically). Introduces a continuous scalar field for blanket strength, extending FEP treatment to weakly bounded agents and distributed cognitive architectures — the regime C2A2's own agent population occupies.
    4. Entropy 27(2):143, 2025. "As One and Many: Relating Individual and Emergent Group-Level Generative Models in Active Inference" (MDPI; PMC11853804). — Carried forward and re-confirmed. A collective of active-inference agents maintaining a group-level Markov blanket can constitute a larger active-inference agent with its own generative model; any agent at one scale can simultaneously be composed of nested agents below and be a constituent of a larger agent above. This is the most direct support for the scale-recursion half of the assumption.
    5. Kirchhoff, M., et al., 2018. "The Markov blankets of life." J. R. Soc. Interface 15(138):20170792. — Carried forward. The Markovian structure of living systems "iterates recursively across all levels of biological organization," with superordinate slower/larger-scale dynamics emerging from and constraining subordinate faster/smaller-scale dynamics.

  Summary: The supporting base for the weaker reading of ASSUMPTION-022 — that Markov-blanket boundaries recur across scales and that the FEP is applicable at each — has strengthened since April 2026, gaining both an experimental validation at the neural-network scale and a method for detecting blankets from data rather than positing them. The scale-recursion claim is well supported by the group-level active-inference literature, which explicitly licenses nesting in both directions. The stronger reading — that the FEP "applies literally at every level" — remains unsupported and is, per the field's own critical literature, further than most FEP researchers will go. The gap between the two readings is unchanged since April; only the evidence for the weaker reading has grown.

  Caveats: (a) Empirical validation exists at exactly one scale (in vitro cortical networks); there is no comparable validation at cellular, organismal, social, or computational-agent scales, so "every level" is an extrapolation from n=1. (b) Most supporting authors use applicability language, not universality language; the FEP critical literature (Bruineberg et al., "The Emperor's New Markov Blankets"; Entropy 23(2):238) argues the framework conflates statistical and ontological uses of the blanket and is structured so as to resist disconfirmation — which, if right, makes "applies literally" unfalsifiable rather than supported. (c) The extension from biological to AI/computational systems (C2A2's own case) is the least validated leg. (d) Blanket-density work supports application to weakly bounded systems precisely by *relaxing* the sharp inside/outside distinction, which cuts against "fundamental" as the item states it. (e) Sources 2 and 3 are preprints.
