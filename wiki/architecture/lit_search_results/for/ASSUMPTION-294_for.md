  Strength of support: Strong
  Summary: The directional claim is among the best-established results in collective-judgment research: the evidential value of agreement among judges is a function of the independence of their error processes, and aggregation gains vanish as errors become correlated through shared formation, shared information, or social influence. Formal treatments (bracketing, error-cancellation, ensemble diversity in ML) and experiments (Lorenz et al., Becker et al.) all support it. The strong rider — that same-formation agreement is "near-chance noise" — overstates the literature: correlated judges still carry some signal, just heavily discounted; agreement among same-formation judges is redundant evidence, not zero evidence.
  Caveats: "Near-chance noise" should be read as rhetorical sharpening; the supported claim is "sharply discounted evidential weight." Quantifying the discount requires a model of the error-correlation structure, which the dyad-MMA design does not yet specify. ML ensemble-diversity results transfer cleanly to LLM-agent MMAs (same-base-model agents have highly correlated errors).
