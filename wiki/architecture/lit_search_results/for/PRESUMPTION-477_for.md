  Summary: Modern LLM summarisation is genuinely strong on well-structured, standardised, largely extractive content — RCT reports with fixed reporting conventions, news articles with an inverted-pyramid structure. That is precisely the domain in which the supportive evidence was collected. Interpreting a philosopher's metaphysical position is not that domain: the content is contested, non-standardised, and the load-bearing material is exactly the nuance and qualification that abstractive summarisation compresses away. Note further that Gartlehner's headline 96.3% still implies roughly one element in twenty-seven is wrong — a rate that is fine for pooled evidence synthesis and fatal when a single claim is carrying the whole inference, which is the situation here.
  Caveats: The support does not transfer to the actual task (position attribution in contested philosophy) and does not scale to the actual stakes (overturning a months-standing classification and seeding a downstream channel). SummaC is offered as support only in the conditional sense: it describes a screen the system does not currently run.
