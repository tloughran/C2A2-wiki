    1. Laudan, L. (1977). "Progress and Its Problems: Toward a Theory of Scientific Growth." University of California Press. (PhilPapers rec/LAUPAI; reviewed in Science 199(4327):415, doi:10.1126/science.199.4327.415) — The strongest theoretical grounding located: Laudan makes the *solved problem* the basic unit of scientific progress, and measures a tradition's progress by problem-solving effectiveness (empirical problems solved, minus anomalies and conceptual problems generated). This directly licenses using problem/solution pairs as an atomic, countable unit of progress.
    2. Laudan, L. "From Theories to Research Traditions." This is the closest philosophical analogue to C2A2's "tradition" framing plus PRS as its measured output.
    3. Newell, A., & Simon, H. A. (1972). "Human Problem Solving." Prentice-Hall (reissue ISBN 9781635617924). See also Newell, A. (1967), "Some issues of representation in a general problem solver," AFIPS Spring Joint Computer Conference, doi:10.1145/1465482.1465579. — Supplies the missing middle term: the problem space / representation is a distinct, separately specifiable component between problem and solution, and mis-representation invalidates solution search. This is the best available grounding for R being a first-class element rather than an artifact.

  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: The three PRS components each have independent, respectable grounding, but no located source validates the triplet as a *complete* or *sufficient* model of research progress. Laudan supplies the strongest single warrant — the solved problem as the basic unit of progress, with progress measured by problem-solving effectiveness within a research tradition — which maps well onto C2A2's use of P and S as atomic countable units. Newell & Simon supply the R: problem representation is a distinct component whose mis-specification invalidates solution search, and Simon & Lea extend this to multiple simultaneous representations. Contemporary NLP work (SemEval NLPContributionGraph, xKG) is direct empirical precedent that scholarly contributions can be reliably decomposed into triple-structured units at scale. What remains unsupported is the *closure* claim: no source found compares PRS against alternative progress models (cyclical, branching), and none establishes that P, R, S exhaust what progress consists of.

  Caveats:
    - Laudan's unit is the two-place solved problem, not a three-place triplet; the R term is imported from a different literature (cognitive problem-solving) and the two have not been jointly validated.
    - The co-evolution literature, while supporting P and S as real units, actively undermines any *sequential* reading of PRS: Dorst/Cross show problem and solution spaces develop reciprocally, and some contexts show solution→problem co-evolution (existing solutions used to structure the problem space). If C2A2 reads PRS as an ordered pipeline, this literature cuts against it; if C2A2 reads PRS as three co-present facets, the literature supports it.
    - Laudan's own meta-criteria have been criticised as resting on normative intuitions and risking circularity in justification (noted in the Lakatos/Laudan comparison sources above) — so the "progress = problem-solving" framing is itself contested, not settled.
    - Domain scope: the design-science and NLP evidence is from engineering/CS; transfer to the humanities- and philosophy-heavy traditions C2A2 actually indexes (contemplative, metaphysical, theological material) is untested.
    - The 2026 AI-research-agent benchmarks operationalise task/solution units for *executable* research; C2A2's traditions are largely non-executable, so the precedent is weaker than it first appears.
