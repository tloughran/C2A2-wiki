  Summary: The corrective proposition is well supported and the queue's specific question — is there an established practice for recording the dependency graph that cron times encode? — has a clear affirmative answer. Sculley et al. supply the frame: in pipeline systems the expensive, invisible debt lives in the connective tissue, and data dependencies in particular lack the analysers and linkers that make code dependencies tractable, so they go unrecorded by default. The craft literature supplies the name and the diagnosis: temporal coupling is a recognised design smell whose defining property is that the required ordering is NOT EXPRESSED IN THE ARTEFACT, which is exactly why each of 14b's three runs could read its schedule position as an environment fact rather than as a design consequence. The orchestration field has converged on a specific remedy — promote the implicit temporal edge to a declared data dependency so the consumer triggers on the producer's completion rather than on a clock — and it states the failure it is fixing in terms that match 14b's cases precisely: a downstream job that runs on stale data emits no error, produces plausible output, and reports something that is true only of what it could see. That is the item's central observation, in the industry's own words. The correctness argument is the operative one: with an implicit temporal edge, whether a run's assertion is true BY CONSTRUCTION or BY MEASUREMENT depends on a fact (did the upstream finish?) that the run never checks and cannot represent; with a declared edge, that fact becomes a precondition rather than a coincidence.
