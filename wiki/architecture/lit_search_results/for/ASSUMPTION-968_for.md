  Summary: P1 is unusually well supported for an item in this register — the primary source was retrieved in full and every figure in the assumption's statement checks out, with two important refinements. First, the assumption's "+80%" is precise: 80.9%, and it is specific to *centralized* coordination on *Finance-Agent*, not a general multi-agent result. Second, the assumption's "-70%" is the extreme end of a range: the reported degradation is 39-70%, on PlanCraft, and applies to *every* multi-agent variant tested. Quoting -70% as the headline figure is defensible only as a worst case, and if the assumption is used to argue against multi-agent coordination for C2A2's pipeline, the honest figure is "39-70% on one sequential-reasoning benchmark." Two findings not carried into the assumption are arguably more useful to C2A2 than the two that were: the error-amplification result (independent 17.2x versus centralized 4.4x, with the orchestrator functioning as a validation bottleneck) speaks directly to several other items in this batch about missing checks, and the tool-coordination trade-off gives an operational predictor. P2, by contrast, has no literature support and cannot have any: whether C2A2's 14 -> 15a/b -> 15c -> 15d chain is "sequential" in the paper's sense is a classification of C2A2's own topology, and the paper can at most supply the criteria (strict ordering dependencies, low decomposability, tool density) against which someone must judge it.

  Caveats: (a) P2's transfer gap is substantial and I want it on record. The paper's "sequential tasks" are sequential *reasoning* tasks — PlanCraft planning, where a single agent must maintain a dependency chain within one problem and the measured harm comes from communication overhead fragmenting that reasoning ("leaving insufficient cognitive budget for the actual task"). C2A2's pipeline is a sequential *workflow* of distinct stages with distinct deliverables and explicit handoffs. These are not the same object. It is entirely possible for a workflow to be strictly ordered while each stage is internally parallelizable — 15a and 15b running concurrently on the same items is exactly that — in which case the paper's sequential penalty may not apply to the pipeline at all, and may apply only within stages. Importing the -39-70% figure to the pipeline level is not licensed by the source. (b) Single study, no independent replication located. R² = 0.513 for the predictive model is modest; roughly half the variance in architecture-appropriateness is unexplained. (c) Benchmark specificity: each headline figure comes from one benchmark (Finance-Agent for the gain, PlanCraft for the penalty), so both are single-point estimates, and the paper's own framing ("depending on the specific configuration") is more cautious than the assumption's. (d) The study measures task *performance*, not the properties C2A2's pipeline is optimised for — independence between 15a and 15b, auditability, provenance. A topology that scores worse on the paper's metrics might still be correct for C2A2 if it buys independence, and nothing in the source addresses that trade.
