  Sources:
    1. Leadholm, N., Clay, V., Knudstrup, S., Lee, H., Hawkins, J., 2026. "Thousand-Brains Systems: Sensorimotor Intelligence for Rapid, Robust Learning and Inference." Neural Computation 38(6):845 (MIT Press; preprint arXiv 2507.04494). — NEW, and the key upgrade. The April file cited only the arXiv preprint; this is now peer-reviewed in Neural Computation. Substantively, it describes cortical columns' predictive mechanisms being used to *minimise expected free energy* and to select moves that reduce ambiguity — an explicit, named formal contact point with active inference rather than a parallel-description analogy. Evaluates Monty on 3D object perception and joint recognition/pose estimation.
    2. arXiv 2506.21554, 2025. "Finding Similar Objects and Active Inference for Surprise in Numenta Neocortex Model." — NEW to this file. Applies active-inference machinery (surprise minimisation) directly to a Numenta neocortex model, i.e. an independent group performing the TBT↔active-inference mapping in working code rather than in prose.
    3. arXiv 2508.16877, 2025. "A coalgebraic perspective on predictive processing." — NEW to this file. Supplies a category-theoretic (coalgebraic) formalisation of predictive processing, which is the kind of machinery that could in principle make "structural, not surface" a checkable claim rather than an assertion. Does not itself perform the TBT mapping.
    4. arXiv 2507.12473, 2025. "The Generalist Brain Module: Module Repetition in Neural Networks in Light of the Minicolumn Hypothesis." — NEW to this file. Independent treatment of the repeated-module (Mountcastle) principle that both TBT and hierarchical active-inference architectures rely on, supporting the claim that the shared structure is architectural rather than terminological.
    5. Levin, M., 2019. "The Computational Boundary of a 'Self'." Frontiers in Psychology 10:2688. — Carried forward; supplies the cellular-cognition leg. Remains the only source for that leg and makes no contact with TBT.
