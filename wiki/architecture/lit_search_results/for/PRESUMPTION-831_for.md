  Original statement: The unstated presumption that an identifier carries the polarity of the claim it names. The schema has no field recording whether an id's Solution supports or undercuts the sentence it anchors, so the only remedy available to all four of today's runs was withdrawal. Tested here as an ontology/schema question: does the published literature support the position that polarity must be a first-class, explicitly-typed relation rather than something inherited from the identity of the referent?

  Sources:
    1. Shotton, D., 2010. "CiTO, the Citation Typing Ontology." Journal of Biomedical Semantics 1(Suppl 1):S6 (SPAR Ontologies; maintained with S. Peroni, contributions from P. Ciccarese and T. Clark). — The canonical existence proof for the remedy 831 says is missing. CiTO has exactly two base object properties (cito:cites / cito:isCitedBy) with forty-one sub-properties, including cito:supports, cito:confirms, cito:disagreesWith and cito:refutes. Polarity is modelled as the *type of the edge*, never as a property of the cited identifier — precisely because the identifier alone cannot carry it.
    2. Willighagen, E., 2020. "Adoption of the Citation Typing Ontology by the Journal of Cheminformatics." Journal of Cheminformatics, doi:10.1186/s13321-020-00448-1 (see also the 2023 follow-ups "Two years of explicit CiTO annotations" and "A look back at a pilot of the citation typing ontology," same journal). — Documents a real journal deploying typed, polarity-bearing citation relations in production, showing the schema change is implementable rather than merely theoretical, and the follow-ups report on sustained use.
    3. Cayrol, C., & Lagasquie-Schiex, M.-C., 2010. "Bipolar Argumentation Frameworks with Specialized Supports." (conference paper, November 2010; see also their "Bipolarity in argumentation graphs: Towards a better understanding.") — A BAF is formally a 3-tuple of arguments, an *attack* relation and a *support* relation, generalising Dung's abstract argumentation. Support and attack are primitive, disjoint, first-class relations; the arguments themselves are polarity-neutral atoms. This is the formal statement of 831's diagnosis: polarity lives on the relation, not on the node.
    4. Asif, I., Tiddi, I., & Gray, A. J. G., 2021. "Using Nanopublications to Detect and Explain Contradictory Research Claims." Proceedings of the IEEE 17th International Conference on eScience (eScience 2021), Innsbruck, pp. 1–10, doi:10.1109/eScience51609.2021.00010. — Uses the nanopublication four-graph model (head / assertion / provenance / publication-info) to detect contradictions between claims over the Cooperation Databank. Demonstrates that contradiction detection becomes tractable only once the assertion and its provenance are separately addressable — i.e. only once the schema has somewhere to put the polarity.
    5. Nanopublication specification and guidelines (nanopub.org; see also Kuhn, T., et al., "Nanopublications: A Growing Resource of Provenance-Centric Scientific Linked Data," arXiv:1809.06532). — Establishes the design principle that an assertion and the evidential stance toward that assertion are distinct named graphs. Work extending nanopublications with knowledge provenance explicitly notes the need to track "supporting and conflicting pieces of evidence" per assertion.
    6. "Biomedical Knowledge Graph Embeddings with Negative Statements" (the TrueWalks approach), 2023, arXiv:2308.03447; companion benchmark "Benchmark datasets for biomedical knowledge graphs with negative statements," arXiv:2307.11719 [author list not independently confirmed — cite by title/arXiv id only]. — Argues that the overwhelming majority of KG relations are positive statements, that this is a crucial limitation under the Open World Assumption, and that representations must be able to differentiate positive from negative statements and honour the semantics of negation. Direct support for the general claim that a schema without a negation/objection field silently coerces every edge to positive.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: Every modelling tradition consulted has independently arrived at the same conclusion 831 reaches by inference: polarity cannot be carried by a referent identifier and must be an explicit, typed property of the relation. CiTO is the clearest case — it exists specifically because a bare bibliographic reference is polarity-free, and it solves the problem by defining forty-one typed sub-properties of cites, among them supports, confirms, disagreesWith and refutes. Bipolar argumentation frameworks make the same commitment formally, treating support and attack as primitive relations over polarity-neutral argument nodes. The nanopublication and knowledge-provenance literature adds the further point that contradiction becomes machine-detectable only once the assertion and the stance toward it are separately addressable, and Asif, Tiddi & Gray (2021) demonstrate this working end-to-end. The negative-statements-in-KGs literature generalises the failure: a schema with only positive edges does not represent "no polarity recorded", it silently represents "positive". This is exactly the coercion that turned four unrecorded polarities into four withdrawals.

  Caveats: (a) These are prescriptive design literatures — they show that a polarity field is standard practice and is implementable, not that adding one would empirically have prevented the four withdrawals. (b) CiTO adoption in practice is thin; the Journal of Cheminformatics case is notable precisely because it is unusual, so the cost side of adding the field is not addressed by these sources. (c) Bipolar argumentation frameworks are contested internally — the semantics of "support" has been split into deductive, necessary and evidential variants, so a single boolean polarity field may under-specify. (d) Whoever implements this should note that a two-value field (supports / undercuts) is weaker than CiTO's typed vocabulary and weaker than SciFact's three-value scheme (SUPPORTS / REFUTES / NOINFO); the third value matters, since "polarity not yet determined" must be distinguishable from "supports".
