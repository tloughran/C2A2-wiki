  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is strongly supported and has been settled in engineering practice for four decades. Knight and Leveson is the decisive precedent: independently written programs failed together far more than independence predicts, and did so through DIFFERENT errors, because they shared a specification — which is exactly the structure here, where thirteen files, their commentary and every static check share one upstream key and therefore constitute one channel wearing many labels. Common-cause failure modelling turns this into arithmetic: CCF dominates the unreliability of redundant systems by defeating the very coincidence that redundancy purchases, contributes a large fraction of safety-system unavailability in the best-studied domain there is, and is addressed by attacking coupling factors rather than root causes. The robustness-analysis literature supplies the epistemology — convergence counts only across causally independent lines — so mutual consistency among derived artefacts is not corroboration of the key, it is a measurement of how faithfully the derivation ran. Master-data practice draws the operational conclusion C2A2 needs: because downstream checks cannot in principle see an upstream fault, the control belongs at the point where the reference value is set or changed, with provenance attached. On this literature, the count of thirteen consistent files is not weak evidence of correctness; it is no evidence at all, and its comfort value is the hazard.
