  Strength of support: Strong
  Summary: The presumption 507 surfaces is one that every discipline responsible for published numbers has explicitly refused to make. National statistical agencies do not merely resolve discrepancies; they pre-commit to written revision and correction policies specifying what happens to already-published figures when an error is later found, and at least four independent institutions maintain such standards with a common core obligation to notify users promptly. Data engineering has converged on the same posture from the systems side, treating quality as a boundary control with an explicit rejection-or-quarantine decision at each trust boundary and lineage-aware impact assessment to identify affected downstream assets — the whole design intent being to stop suspect data from silently contaminating descendants. The most recent work applies this directly to agentic pipelines, defining invalidation as a semantic operation over dependency identities such that only true descendants of a changed artifact require recomputation. Against all of that, an open counting dispute with no withdrawal, no suspension, no arbitration rule, and continued downstream production is the specific state these mechanisms exist to prevent. Notably, the arbitration rule 507 says is missing is available and cheap: ASSUMPTION-490's metrology finding supplies it (fix a reference definition, re-derive against it), and lineage supplies the scoping rule for what must then be recomputed.
