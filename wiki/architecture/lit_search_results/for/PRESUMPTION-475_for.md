  Strength of support: Weak
  Summary: The measurement literature does grant the presumption's most charitable reading: two enumerations of one population may legitimately differ when their inclusion definitions differ, and a bounded, characterised difference does not invalidate either. This is the genuine grain of truth. But every one of these sources treats the difference as something to be CHARACTERISED — Bland-Altman exists to compute the bias and the limits of agreement, not to license ignoring them. Not one source supports the presumption's operative clause, that the two series may coexist WITHOUT reconciliation and each be "presumed valid within itself." The support is for coexistence-with-characterisation, which is the opposite of what the system is doing.
  Caveats: Support is confined to the existence of legitimate definitional differences. It does not extend to leaving the difference unquantified, and it certainly does not extend to downstream figures silently inheriting whichever series they happened to draw from.
