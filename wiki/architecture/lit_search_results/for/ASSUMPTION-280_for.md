Web-scraping-for-research ethics (Brown, Gruen, Maldoff, Messing, Sanderson, Zimmer 2025, "Web scraping for research: Legal, ethical, institutional, and scientific considerations," Big Data & Society / arXiv 2410.23432). — Transparency about data provenance and the absence of consent is a recommended baseline practice for research using found/scraped data. Supports disclosing seed-source and non-approval status.
    2. Association of Internet Researchers (AoIR) ethics guidance. — Recommends transparency and contextual integrity when handling identifiable online content; not implying participant endorsement is a recognized norm. Supports the non-endorsement disclaimer.
    3. Data-provenance / metadata-for-reproducibility practice (also the basis of C2A2's own provenance protocol). — Recording and surfacing how records were obtained is established good practice; the popover + source-of-truth doc is the in-product expression of provenance disclosure.

  Strength of support: Moderate-Strong

  Summary: That a tool listing communities from scraped public pages should DISCLOSE that provenance and explicitly NOT imply endorsement is well supported as a minimum transparency/ethics bar — research-ethics guidance for scraped data, AoIR norms, and provenance practice all endorse it. Disclosure-plus-non-endorsement is uncontroversially better than silent listing that implies approval. The support is for disclosure as a NECESSARY minimum.

  Caveats: The literature supports disclosure as necessary, not as sufficient. Whether in-product disclosure DISCHARGES the consent obligation (vs. opt-in or don't-list) is a separate, stronger claim — surfaced as PRESUMPTION-313 and contested there. So 280's "must disclose rather than imply endorsement" is well grounded; the adjacent "disclosure is enough" inference is not part of 280 and is handled under 313.
