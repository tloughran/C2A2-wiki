  Summary: Psychometrics gives expert judgment a recognized, legitimate role in establishing content validity, and there is historical precedent for instruments authored by one or a few experts later becoming authoritative (FCI lineage). However, the literature consistently treats face validity as the weakest validity evidence and operationalizes expert certification through multi-expert panels (CVI, Delphi). No source endorses N=1 self-certification of one's *own* tradition's milestones as authoritative; the FCI became authoritative via downstream empirical validation. Support exists for the expert-judgment component, not for the sufficiency claim.
  Caveats: Transfer condition: support holds only if MM-of-1 certification is treated as a *candidate-generating* validity stage pending later corroboration, not terminal certification. Self-certification of one's own framework adds a conflict-of-interest dimension the panel literature does not cover.
