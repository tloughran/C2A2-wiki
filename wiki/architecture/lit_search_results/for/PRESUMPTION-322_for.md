  Strength of support: Moderate
  Summary: There is genuine support for the behavioral half of this presumption: digital-trace methodology holds that event streams are valid, often superior, measures of what an entity *does* — non-intrusive, complete within their capture boundary, and free of self-report bias; empirical studies validate trace-derived indicators against known constructs. However, the same literature it rests on explicitly bounds the claim: traces measure activity-level constructs well and latent constructs (quality, purpose, "substance") poorly, and insists traces are indicators rather than truth. So "captures what the agent does" is supported; "captures what the agent *is*" is supported only with per-construct validation the project has not done.
  Caveats: Support is conditional on (a) capture completeness — the known capture gap (cf. ASSUMPTION-290) and unmapped interactive sessions violate the "complete within boundary" premise; (b) construct validity work mapping events to claims about agents, which trace methodology treats as mandatory, not optional. The is/does distinction is load-bearing.
