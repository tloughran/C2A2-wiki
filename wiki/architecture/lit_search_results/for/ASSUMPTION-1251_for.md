  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: Table-structure theory (Wang 1996; Hurst 2000, 2006; Zanibbi et al. 2004) establishes exactly the default the ruling states: header labels are the category/index dimension of the abstract table, distinct from the entries they index, and header/data cell function is a per-table interpretation decision rather than something fixed by position or string length (Fang et al. 2012). The header-category work of Nagy and Seth shows that headers can be meaning-bearing structured objects that get harvested into ontologies. Separately, ADA v. Delta Dental (7th Cir. 1997) supplies a clear doctrinal statement of the exception clause: when a table is an authored taxonomy, its labels and ordering are the creative work itself. Taken together, the literature supports the two-class distinction the ruling draws. What it does not supply is a decision procedure for the class "authored structure": the table-understanding literature decides header-vs-data by layout and content features, and the copyright literature decides authorship by originality of selection and arrangement; neither offers a test that maps cleanly onto "is this table a structure the author is proposing?"

  Caveats: (a) Support for the exception clause is largely analogical (copyright doctrine, KO practice) rather than from document-structure extraction, where the table's *purpose* is generally not modelled. (b) Fang et al. and the Nagy/Seth line treat headers as apparatus-to-be-classified; they do not treat any header as "the content" — the strongest form of the ruling is not found there. (c) The ruling was generalised from a single error (the ladder letters); the literature supports the distinction in principle but says nothing about how often the exception class arises in a corpus like the sandbox, so the decidability question raised in the 14a hint remains open. (d) Author lists for items 3, 4, 5 and 7 were not confirmed from the result snippets; titles and venues were. Not covered: the digital-humanities "apparatus criticus" literature on editorial apparatus vs. text, the linguistics of metalanguage/object-language, and in-vault tradition wikis.
