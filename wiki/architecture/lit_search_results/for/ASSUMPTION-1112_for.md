  Summary: The item is correct, and the literature states it more usefully than the item does.
  Walters & Wilder separate fabrication from citation error by construction and find the second class
  large in its own right — 43% of the *real* GPT-3.5 citations and 24% of the *real* GPT-4 citations
  carry substantive errors. Within that class the field ranking is the inverse of what C2A2's checks
  cover: **22% of real cited works have incorrect years, while fewer than 7% of GPT-4 citations have
  even minor errors in author, title, journal or publisher.** A from-the-thinker check therefore
  interrogates the most reliable field and leaves the least reliable one unexamined. The paper names
  the mechanism, and it is C2A2's exact exposure: reporting online posting dates rather than original
  publication dates — what happens whenever a devotional site, blog or newsletter republishes older
  book material under a new posting date. The URL check fares no better: fewer than one in ten real
  citations carried a link, links were *more* common on fabricated citations, a third of links on real
  citations were inaccurate, and roughly three in four scholarly URIs that still resolve now return
  changed content. Resolution is evidence about a server. One incidental finding strengthens the item
  beyond its own claim: the same Rohr passage sits on cac.org attributed to *Essential Teachings on
  Love* (Orbis, 2018) in 2019 and, per the vault's own record, to *Eager to Love* (2024) in 2026. The
  class is not confined to generated citations; ordinary republication produces it, and this vault
  ingests republished devotional material routinely.
