  Summary: The corrective proposition is supported, but by a different route than the item's framing suggests, and the strongest support is structural rather than numerical. The decisive result is the estimator one: capture-recapture, the standard method for estimating faults remaining after an inspection, requires at least two independent inspectors by construction, because the estimate is derived from the OVERLAP between them. It follows that a run auditing its own instrument cannot produce a residual-defect estimate of any quality — "eight defects found" is a catch count with no denominator — and the recurrence of four of the eight on a second night is precisely the observation a low, unmeasured single-pass detection probability predicts. The inspection literature adds two design facts. Fagan's method separates the author from the reader as a matter of construction, and Porter, Votta and Basili's controlled experiments show that the detection value lives in MULTIPLE INDEPENDENT INDIVIDUAL PREPARATIONS rather than in any meeting — collection meetings produced no net gain — and that checklists do not substitute for a second reader. That makes the corrective cheap and specific: one independent reader, working alone. The effectiveness figures usually quoted alongside this argument (inspection ~55-60% against testing ~25-45%) are industry-survey grade and I could not resolve the competing single-inspector detection rates, so no number from this file should be quoted. And the intuitive psychological mechanism — authors cannot see their own errors — has recently failed a registered replication and is reported here as NOT supporting the item.
