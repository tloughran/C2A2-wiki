  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: There is a reasonably strong literature that verbalised confidence from instruction-tuned models is informative: Tian et al. (2023) show it is better calibrated than the model's own token probabilities for RLHF models, and several applied studies (BMC MRM 2025; arXiv 2602.00052; arXiv 2604.24470) show accuracy rising monotonically with reported confidence and human review of the low-confidence tail delivering large accuracy gains. Gligorić et al. (2025) give this use a statistical footing: LLM confidence can steer which items get a human label while preserving valid inference. This supports the pipeline's actual usage — treating the 70 `low` cells as the review set and the distribution as a quality signal — better than it supports the stronger literal claim of calibration. Every supportive study established the relationship between confidence and accuracy on a gold sample first; none treats the relationship as given.

  Caveats: (a) The support is for confidence being *informative*, not *calibrated*: the same literature (seen incidentally, e.g. arXiv 2606.03437 "Large Language Models Are Overconfident in Their Own Responses"; arXiv 2605.27752 "Asking Is Not Enough: Protocol Sensitivity in LLM Confidence Calibration") reports systematic upward bias and sensitivity to prompt protocol; a three-level ordinal field is coarser than the numeric scores studied. (b) Cross-batch comparability (batch 3's `high` = batch 7's `high`) was not directly addressed by any source found; the protocol-sensitivity result cuts against it if batches differed in prompt or context, and for it if the spec was identical. (c) No source found addresses anchoring by a supplied prior (the 92% `voice` default); the 92.4% outcome remains equally consistent with calibration and with anchoring, as 14b noted. (d) Benchmarks in Tian et al. are factual QA, not taxonomy assignment; transfer to a 60-odd-node controlled outline is assumed. (e) Author lists for items 3–7 not confirmed from snippets. (f) Search scope: web search only (approx. 5 queries) across LLM calibration, hybrid human-LLM annotation, and uncertainty-driven statistical inference. Not covered: the psychology of confidence-accuracy calibration in human coders, model-specific calibration reports for the worker model actually used, in-vault tradition wikis. Preliminary search; 14b's 50-cell blind re-label is the decisive test and is cheap.
