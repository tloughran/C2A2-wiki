  Sources:
    1. Greene, M., 1998. "'The Surest Proof:' A Utilitarian Approach to Appraisal." Archivaria 45:127–169.
       [fetched, full abstract] The strongest defence of use as value, and it supports the EXTERNAL limb.
       Greene measures use by "scholars and the general public" through three rate-of-use studies, and
       grounds the criterion in Eastwood's claim that archives "are social creations for social purposes"
       whose only validity is "the value that an institution or society places on them", so that use is
       "the only measurement we have of that value". The warrant explicitly depends on the users being the
       society the archive serves, i.e. a consumer population external to the archive's own appraisal
       apparatus. The abstract also states the opposing position: "if archives are objectively
       identifiable evidence ... then use is irrelevant".
    2. Harnad, S., 2008. "Validating Research Performance Metrics Against Peer Rankings." Ethics in Science
       and Environmental Politics (submitted version). [fetched, full text] Supports the CIRCULAR limb in
       general form: "In and of themselves ... metrics are circular: They need to be jointly tested and
       validated against what it is that they purport to measure". Also: "Trying to validate unvalidated
       metrics against unvalidated metrics is no better than trying to validate peer review with peer
       review". Jointly analysing metrics for a common factor "would still be circular because neither the
       metrics nor the factors have been validated against their external criterion". Escape from
       circularity is defined as correlation with an EXTERNAL criterion. A use signal generated by the
       assessing system's own instrument has no such criterion by construction.
    3. Seith/Koller/Briscoe in-house-use study (Library Research / College & Research Libraries), with the
       Trueswell critique and a Seton Hall replication. [search-result] Weeding on circulation alone would
       have eliminated at least 112,000 recently used volumes per million. More than 30% of monographs had
       only one kind of use. Critics faulted Trueswell for "the equation of usage with circulation". This
       is the empirical form of the circular limb: when "use" means what the institution's own instrument
       records, material used outside that instrument is valued at zero and removed, and the removal then
       confirms the low count.
    4. Boles, F. & Young, J. M., 1985. "Exploring the Black Box: The Appraisal of University Administrative
       Records." American Archivist 48(2):121ff. [search-result] Critique of Schellenberg's premises; the
       proposed model weighs value-of-information against costs-of-retention and implications. A secondary
       summary of the Minnesota Method notes that use-based appraisal "deals only with current and/or past
       user needs" and can favour majority populations. That is a limit on the external limb: even external
       use is a lagging, population-biased proxy.

  Summary: The literature, read above snippet level, supports 15c's conditional by independent limbs.
    Greene (1) gives the appraisal profession's strongest argument for use as the measure of value, and it
    is explicitly a claim about use by an external public, licensed by archives being social creations for
    that public. Harnad (2) states the general principle that metrics are circular unless validated against
    an external criterion, which a system's own consumption instrument cannot supply. The library
    in-house-use studies (3) show the circular case empirically: where "use" equals what the institution's
    instrument records, unrecorded use is invisible and deselection is self-confirming. No source states
    the conditional in 15c's words or in terms of consumer identity. It is a synthesis of these sources
    rather than a quoted finding, though no longer only 15c's synthesis from two half-arguments.

  Caveats: (i) Greene was read at abstract level; his three use studies and his treatment of objections
    were not read. (ii) Harnad's subject is research-assessment metrics, so the transfer to "consumer
