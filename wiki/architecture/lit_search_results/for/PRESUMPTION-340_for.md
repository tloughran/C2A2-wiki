  Sources:
    1. Levitt & List (various); Bolger, Davis & Rafaeli, 2003. "Diary Methods: Capturing Life as it is Lived." Annual Review of Psychology. — Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA) and diary-based methods are widely accepted as high-validity participant-generated records; compliance rates above 95% have been reported. This establishes a clear precedent for participant-authored records achieving evidential standing without an independent external rater.

    2. Strijbos & Fischer, 2007. "Methodological challenges for collaborative learning research." Learning and Instruction. / O'Connor & Joffe, 2020. "Intercoder Reliability in Qualitative Research: Debates and Practical Guidelines." International Journal of Qualitative Methods. — Established qualitative methodology literature acknowledges that structured coding and member-checking can substitute for independent inter-rater reliability in certain conditions, particularly when transparency and procedural rigour are documented. This supports the plausibility of a dyad's structured ledger functioning as evidence without a separate external assessor.

    3. McLeod, 2010. "Case Study Research in Counselling and Psychotherapy." SAGE. / Stiles, 1993. "Quality control in qualitative research." Clinical Psychology Review. — Single-case intensive design traditions (including n-of-1 and couple-based research) permit participant-generated records as primary evidence when formal inter-rater checking is impractical, provided the records are structured, contemporaneous, and procedurally anchored. The analogy to a dyad's agreement ledger is direct.

  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: There is a meaningful literature supporting participant-generated, contemporaneous records as valid evidence. Diary and EMA studies show very high compliance and ecological validity and are treated as primary (not merely supplementary) data. Qualitative methods literature accepts member-checking and intra-rater procedures when external raters are unavailable. The specific framing of a "dyad ledger as Level-3 evidence without an external rater" is not directly addressed in the literature, but the underlying conditions it requires (structured protocol, contemporaneous recording, agreed procedure) are well-documented as sufficient for validity in analogous single-case and dyadic study designs. The gap is that the C2A2 maturity model's category "Level-3" is not a standard measurement concept, so the claim's validity depends partly on how that tier is defined internally.

  Caveats: Most supporting precedents come from clinical, psychological, or organisational research contexts where participant-generated records are validated against external anchors at least once at protocol development. The C2A2 dyad has no reported external anchor. EMA validity also depends on prompt density and temporal proximity; retrospective ledger entries may introduce recall distortion. The single-coder literature is explicit that full-corpus solo coding increases interpretive drift risk.

  Search scope: Searched for: (1) validity of self-report and participant coding, (2) diary/EMA validity without external raters, (3) dyadic research methodology and joint records, (4) member-checking as reliability substitute, (5) single-observer and single-coder acceptability. Preliminary — a more systematic search of measurement validity in n-of-1 and dyadic research is recommended.
