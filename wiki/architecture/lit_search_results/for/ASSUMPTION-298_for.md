Academy of Management Review. — Contingency framework: stretch goals can benefit organizations with recent strong performance and slack resources; paradoxically most seductive to those least positioned to benefit.

  Strength of support: Weak
  Summary: The closest supporting literature is goal-setting theory: hard specific goals do increase effort and performance, which gives the practice some theoretical grounding. The stretch-goals literature adds a narrow contingency case for over-promising — it can pay off where there is recent success and slack capacity, and "accelerating capability" is arguably a slack-generating condition not present in the historical samples (a genuine gap in the claim's favor: most stretch-goal studies assume static capability, whereas under reliable exponential capability growth, today's over-promise can be tomorrow's moderate goal). However, no source supports over-promising as a general *planning* method; Sitkin et al. find no positive main effect, and the planning-fallacy literature (Kahneman & Tversky 1979; Flyvbjerg) attributes systematic overrun precisely to optimistic capability extrapolation.
  Caveats: Support transfers only if (a) capability growth is independently verified rather than assumed, (b) failure cost is low, and (c) commitments are revisable. "Firm expectation of over-delivery" is exactly the inside-view forecast reference-class forecasting warns against.
