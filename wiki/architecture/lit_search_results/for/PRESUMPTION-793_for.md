  Strength of support: Moderate-Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is well supported and, in software engineering, is close to a founding commitment of the field. Lehman's laws deny that a real-world-embedded artefact has a "finished" state at all: it is either continually adapted or it becomes progressively less satisfactory, and its quality declines unless rigorously maintained. The cost literature, though loosely sourced, converges on the same conclusion from the other side — the phase that begins at feature-completion is the majority of the lifecycle by cost, so declaring completion at the end of production declares completion before most of the work. Software sustainment practice treats transition-to-sustainment as a planned event with a named receiving owner, which is exactly the artefact a bare retirement recommendation lacks. The sharpest empirical evidence is Avelino et al.'s abandonment study: among popular open-source projects that lost their core developers, only 41% found a new core maintainer, so the majority outcome of an owner departing WITHOUT a named successor is no owner. And the dependency-health literature establishes that this failure is silent — abandoned artefacts continue to be consumed, and consumers overwhelmingly do not notice or replace them. The specific configuration 14b names — a producer proposing its own retirement while four defect classes in its output corpus are open and growing — is precisely the configuration this literature identifies as the origin of an unowned maintenance burden, and it compounds PREMISE-118's unmet retrospective-assessment obligation by removing the party positioned to discharge it.

  Caveats:
    (a) DOMAIN TRANSFER IS PARTIAL AND MUST BE STATED. Every source concerns SOFTWARE — code that is executed, depended upon, and must track a changing environment. The artefact here is a generated wiki corpus. Lehman's E-type criterion (embedded in and coupled to a changing real-world domain) plausibly holds for a corpus that is read, linked and cited by downstream agents, but it is an ARGUMENT, not a measurement, and no located source studies generated-text corpora. The maintenance-share percentages in source 2 in particular should not be transferred as numbers.
    (b) THE LITERATURE DOES NOT SAY A PRODUCER MAY NEVER BE RETIRED. It says the retirement must be a HANDOVER to a named party, and that sustainment must be planned and resourced. A retirement recommendation that names a successor owner, transfers the four open defect classes with their 118 obligations, and states who now closes them is fully conformant with everything cited here. The defect 14b names is the absence of that, not the retirement.
    (c) SOURCE-QUALITY ASYMMETRY, DECLARED. Only source 3 was verified directly this run. Sources 2, 4 and 5 were read at snippet level from secondary and industry material and their primary references were not retrieved. Source 1 is canonical but was verified only against summaries. The argument's weight rests on source 3 and on source 1's structural claim; if the maintenance-cost percentages were struck entirely, the conclusion would stand.
    (d) PUBLICATION/SELECTION BIAS. The abandonment and dependency-health literatures study systems that were consequential enough to be worth measuring. A corpus nobody consumes has no maintenance burden and its producer can be retired freely. The item's force therefore depends on the corpus actually having downstream consumers, which this search did not establish and which is measurable in-house.
    (e) PER PREMISE-777, the counts "307," "308" and "four defect classes" are unmarked self-reports by the producing layer. The argument above does not depend on any of them — only on the co-occurrence of a completion declaration with non-empty open defect classes.
