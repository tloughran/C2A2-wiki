  Strength of support: Weak (bordering on None)

  Summary: The only literature that supports the presumption in any way is the entity-popularity finding (Mallen et al. 2023). Parametric recall is relatively reliable for highly popular entities, so an LLM might correctly recall the canonical works of a very prominent author. Beyond that, the citation-hallucination literature measures error and fabrication rates (roughly 18–65% across models and tasks) that are incompatible with using recall alone as a rejection criterion. No source was found that validates LLM recall of an author's bibliography as a substitute for text comparison in duplicate or reissue detection. Note also that rejecting an item as a reissue requires the model to *positively* match it to a prior work, and confabulated matches are the documented failure mode.

  Caveats: (a) Studies measure citation *generation*, not verification of a given candidate against recalled works. Verification may be somewhat easier, but no study was found that measures it. (b) Model generations differ, and newer models with retrieval or tools are out of scope. (c) Reissues often carry changed titles or subtitles, which makes metadata-level recall even less reliable.
