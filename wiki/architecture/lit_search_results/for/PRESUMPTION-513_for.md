Most constraints are not physical but *policy* constraints — rules, procedures and habits — and these "are the most common and often underlie other constraints." They are also harder to see than physical ones because they present as good management, which is precisely how a standing practice of enumerating rather than executing would present. A system that has correctly identified six cheap measurements and taken none in fifteen days exhibits the textbook profile of a policy constraint being read as an information constraint.

  Strength of support: Moderate-to-Strong
  Summary: The presumption 513 surfaces is the founding observation of an entire research discipline. Implementation science exists because knowing what to do does not produce doing it, and decades in, the gap remains substantial across health care and, independently, conservation — a field with no comparable incentive structure. When researchers have gone looking for what the gap consists of, the answer has been capacity, coordination authority, and usable translation rather than missing knowledge, which is exactly 513's alternative diagnosis. The Theory of Constraints, which the pipeline invokes on its own behalf, points the same way: policy constraints are the most common category and are specifically characterised as harder to detect than physical ones because they look like sound practice. Fifteen days of correctly identified, sub-hour, unexecuted actions is the presenting signature of a policy constraint, and the recommendation to identify a sixth cheap action is improvement at a non-binding constraint — which by Goldratt's own reasoning yields no throughput gain. Internal audit supplies the practical confirmation from the profession that produces recommendations for a living: unimplemented recommendations are a standing structural problem addressed with follow-up processes and escalation authority, not with better recommendations. The item is also the batch's cheapest to settle and it says so: a single executed measurement falsifies it immediately.
  Caveats: (a) The strongest sources concern multi-actor organisations where the knowledge-holder and the actor are different parties with different incentives. C2A2's situation is narrower and possibly simpler — a pipeline that produces recommendations and a single human who acts on them — and "attention or authority" may reduce to the reviewer-capacity finding in PRESUMPTION-512 rather than to anything about policy or governance. The two items are closely coupled and 512 may be the more precise statement of the same constraint. (b) Fifteen days is a short window and n=6 actions is a thin base; the know-do literature measures gaps over years. Non-execution over fifteen days is also consistent with ordinary scheduling rather than a structural constraint, and the item's own 30-day ratio test is the appropriate measurement. (c) The item risks a self-sealing structure: any further analysis it prompts can be read as confirming that the pipeline analyses rather than acts. Its stated falsifier (one executed measurement) is what prevents this, and that falsifier should be preserved explicitly in any disposition. (d) TOC sources are practitioner and encyclopaedic; sources 1-3 and 6 carry the empirical weight. (e) Publication bias runs mildly in the item's favour here, since documenting an implementation gap is the raison d'être of the field cited.
  Recommendation: SUPPORTED

  PAIR VERDICT (513 vs ASSUMPTION-482): The literature leans toward 513, though neither item is refuted. 482 is correct that measurement gaps are a real and frequently binding constraint on inquiry — the discovery-tools literature establishes that across the full population of scientific breakthroughs — and correct that the enumerated tests are cheap and discriminating. But 482's evidence comes overwhelmingly from settings where the instrument did not yet exist, whereas the situation actually described is one where the instrument exists, costs under an hour, and has not been used. That is the domain where 513's evidence lives, and it is a larger and more directly applicable body: a founding discipline-level finding replicated across unrelated fields, TOC's own doctrine naming policy constraints as the most common category, a profession that built mandatory follow-up machinery because correct recommendations do not execute themselves, and a Cochrane median effect of 4.3% bounding what measurement-and-reporting alone can achieve. The two claims are compatible — the six measurements can be both correct and non-binding — but as a diagnosis of what currently limits throughput, 513 is the better-supported reading. The asymmetry of the test settles the practical question: executing one measurement costs under an hour, falsifies 513 outright if it happens, and simultaneously delivers 482's claimed benefit. Both items therefore recommend the same next action, which is the strongest available reason to take it.
