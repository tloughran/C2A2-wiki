  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is very well supported and is, in several fields, foundational rather than novel. Formal verification has had a name for it since 1997 (vacuity), software testing has had an operational remedy since 1978 (mutation), and the life sciences have treated the positive control as a validity precondition for far longer. All three converge on the same claim: a check's silence is uninformative until you have demonstrated the check can speak. The practitioner testing literature adds the precise mechanism 14b observed — coverage certifies execution, not evaluation, and silent exception handling produces checks that run and conclude nothing. Chaos engineering supplies the empirical precedent that operators of high-consequence systems deliberately inject faults to validate their *detectors*, which is exactly the missing instrument here. The weakest link is not the principle but the contemporary agent-specific evidence: the two most directly analogous sources (7, 8) are 2026 arXiv preprints I could not verify.

  Caveats: (a) Domain transfer is by analogy in every case. None of the strongly-verified sources concerns a multi-agent documentation pipeline; they concern model checkers, unit test suites, immunoassays and distributed services. The *principle* transfers cleanly; the appropriate frequency and design of a negative/positive control for a C2A2 instrument does not follow from any of them. (b) Mutation testing and fault injection both carry a known cost profile (mutation is computationally expensive; chaos experiments require blast-radius control) which this search did not price for C2A2. (c) The literature supports "install a control," not "four fail-open instruments in one day implies a systemic defect" — the base rate of fail-open instruments in comparable architectures is not established by anything found here, so the *severity* inference in 14b's item is not corroborated, only its *direction*. (d) Publication bias runs in favour of the corrective proposition: papers proposing vacuity detection, mutation testing and chaos engineering all have an interest in establishing that unfired checks are untrustworthy. I found no source arguing the opposite, which is itself weak evidence given that no one publishes "our green suite was fine."

  Search scope: Comprehensive on the formal-methods and software-testing side (vacuity, coverage, mutation) and on assay control doctrine. Moderate on fault injection. Preliminary on contemporary agent-runtime silent-failure taxonomies — the 2026 preprints surfaced are exactly on point and none were verified; a targeted retrieval pass on arXiv:2606.14589 and arXiv:2607.17525 is recommended before either is relied on.
