Bland & Altman, method-comparison / Limits of Agreement literature (MedCalc manual; PMC5585060 meta-analysis framework). — Establishes the standard way to validate that two instruments agree: report bias (mean difference) and limits of agreement. Supports the general legitimacy of validating a new resolver against an old one via an agreement study.
    2. Gwet / Landis & Koch inter-rater agreement methodology (ResearchGate "Evaluation of Inter-Rater Agreement and Inter-Rater Reliability"). — Provides the accepted framework and thresholds for concluding two raters/instruments agree, grounding the use of a measured concordance statistic as evidence of equivalence.
    3. "A Tutorial on Sample Size Calculation for Inter-rater and Intra-rater Agreement Studies," PMC12935580. — Confirms that a validation subset can, in principle, license inference about agreement PROVIDED the subset is adequately sized and representative — supporting subset-based validation as a legitimate design when sampling is proper.
    4. Bland-Altman subgroup guidance (MedCalc; Reporting Standards review PMC7278016). — Notes that agreement studies "routinely report multiple bias and SD estimates ... on different subgroups," precisely because aggregate agreement can conceal per-stratum disagreement — a supporting-but-qualifying source.

  Strength of support: Weak-to-Moderate

  Summary: The method-comparison and inter-rater-agreement literatures support the FORM of the inference — validating a new resolver against a reference by measuring agreement is a recognized, legitimate practice, and a properly designed subset can license claims about instrument equivalence. To that extent the presumption rests on sound methodology. However, the literature simultaneously and repeatedly warns that (a) validity of a subset generalization depends on the subset being representative and adequately powered (a 9-page subset is small and its selection basis is unstated), and (b) aggregate (<2%) agreement can conceal substantial per-stratum disagreement — the exact failure mode this presumption ignores. So the literature supports "subset+aggregate agreement CAN be evidence" but not the stronger claim that it "establishes census-wide delta validity."

  Caveats: Support hinges entirely on subset representativeness and size, neither of which the claim demonstrates; 9 pages is likely underpowered for stratum-level guarantees. Aggregate concordance is a headline statistic that mathematically does not bound worst-case per-stratum disagreement (Simpson-paradox / subgroup-masking risk). The agreement literature actively recommends stratified reporting precisely to avoid over-generalizing an aggregate — so it qualifies as much as it supports.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED
