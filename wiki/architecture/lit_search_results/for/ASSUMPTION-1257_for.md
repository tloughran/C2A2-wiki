  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: The literature supports the column as a *defensible* level of organisation between neuron and organism, on three grounds. (1) Historical-anatomical: Mountcastle's column has been the standard mesoscale unit of neocortex for ~65 years and Churchland & Sejnowski (1988) list it explicitly as a level in their widely reused ladder of nervous-system organisation. (2) Theoretical: the Thousand Brains papers (Hawkins et al. 2017, 2019) give the column a specific, complete function — learning object models via reference frames and voting — which is exactly the reading Tom's cell invokes. (3) Practical: large-scale modelling programmes (Markram et al. 2015) and current reviews (Mathis 2023) treat the column as the natural unit for reconstruction and for world-model learning. So a rung "B" between Neuronal and Personal has clear precedent. Note, however, that the Churchland–Sejnowski ladder inserts several further levels (maps, systems) between column and organism, so "B" as the *only* rung between N and P is a compression, not a direct transcription.

  Caveats: The strongest counter-current is Horton & Adams 2005, "The cortical column: a structure without a function," Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 360: 837-862 [VERIFIED: title, journal, year seen in search results; page range NOT verified], which reviews 50 years of column research and concludes the column may have no unifying function and that "column" has come to cover heterogeneous structures. Support is therefore conditional: the column is defensible as a *level of description* (anatomical repeat, mesoscale modelling unit) more securely than as a *functional unit with a settled job*. The Thousand Brains functional account is a theory, not an established finding; its empirical status is contested and it is the Numenta interpretation Tom's cell explicitly adopts ("This is the Brain's interpretation"). Domain-transfer issue: even if the column is a valid neural level, using it as a rung in a general "layered account" of a knowledge system imports a neuroscience-specific unit into a ladder whose other rungs are not neural.
