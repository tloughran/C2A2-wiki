  Sources:
    1. Nosek et al., 2018 (PNAS), "The preregistration revolution." — Preregistration reduces researcher degrees of freedom by committing design/analysis before seeing outcomes; "tying one's hands" is the core mechanism, which a machine gate operationalizes exactly.
    2. Bakker et al., 2020 (PLOS Biol / PMC7725296), "Ensuring the quality and specificity of preregistrations." — Structured, constrained preregistration formats restrict opportunistic use of researcher degrees of freedom better than unstructured ones; a machine-enforced structured gate is the recommended direction.
    3. Registered Reports model (Chambers). — Stage-1/Stage-2 separation, with the plan locked before data, is a mature template; the study's "Appendix G Stage 2 invariant" mirrors this established design.
    4. Adherence reviews (TeVaarwerk/gambling review; Psychological Science adherence study). — Undisclosed deviations are frequent; a mechanism that makes the prereg-before-run ordering auditable addresses a documented, real failure mode.

  Strength of support: Moderate-Strong (for enforcing the timing/ordering invariant)

  Summary: Preregistration's efficacy rests on locking commitments before outcomes are seen, and structured/enforced formats outperform voluntary ones. A machine gate that guarantees "prereg exists and is frozen before the run" enforces precisely the auditable, order-of-operations property the literature identifies as valuable, and directly counters the common failure of post-hoc, unlogged plan changes.

  Caveats: The literature supports enforcing the *ordering*; it does not claim automated gating guarantees the deeper goal (honest adherence at the analysis stage), which is where deviations actually slip (see 15b). Support is for the gate doing the enforceable part well, not for it being sufficient.
