  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is strongly supported, and Star and Strauss supply it almost verbatim: work is only ever seen through a selected set of indicators, and the artefact-change indicator is precisely the one that renders preparatory, articulating and deliberative work invisible. The digital-trace methodology literature converts this from a sociological observation into a measurement claim — trace data are found byproducts with a decomposable error structure dominated by coverage error, and the standard corrective is to model the recording mechanism rather than to treat traces as behaviour. Rubin gives the formal reason the inference fails outright rather than merely weakening: absence of a trace is uninformative unless the missingness mechanism is known, and here it is not. Naur supplies the directional half that is this item's actual residual: the most valuable output of intellectual work is a theory that no artefact carries, so the trace's coverage is worst exactly where the work matters most. The reflexive sting is real — a project whose stated purpose is to record thinking is measuring itself with an instrument that is constitutionally blind to thinking, and reporting the blindness as absence. That is not a noisy estimate of attended work; it is a lower bound being read as a total.
