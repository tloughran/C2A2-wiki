  Sources:
    1. Consilience / convergence of evidence (Wikipedia, "Consilience"). — Snippet-level. States the
       principle that evidence from independent, unrelated sources can converge on strong conclusions,
       and that the strength of support scales with how many independent methods agree AND how DIFFERENT
       those methods are — techniques with the fewest shared characteristics give the strongest consilience.
       This is the direct warrant for treating convergence as a single strengthened signal.
    2. Walton, D. and Reed, C. (approx. 2008), "Evaluating Corroborative Evidence" / "Corroborative evidence"
       (ResearchGate 225434740; Academia.edu 5994354). — Snippet-level; authorship inferred from the
       argumentation-theory venue and not confirmed, treat the attribution cautiously. Makes the
       distinction the assumption needs: corroborative evidence does not merely add — it strengthens the
       probative weight of the other evidence, whereas merely convergent evidence accumulates without
       that multiplicative effect.
    3. Urbaniak, R. and Janda, P. (2020), "Probabilistic models of legal corroboration."
       The International Journal of Evidence & Proof (SAGE), vol. 24. — Snippet-level. Addresses the
       formal problem of independent witnesses testifying to the same proposition, and notes the
       difficulty of finding a probabilistic support measure that captures it — i.e. the phenomenon is
       real and recognised even where its formalisation is contested.
    4. Merton, R. K. (multiple discovery / "multiples"), via Wikipedia "Multiple discovery" and
       "List of multiple discoveries". — Snippet-level. Merton argues independent multiple discovery is
       the dominant rather than subsidiary pattern in science, and that multiples signal that
       preconditions in the shared stock of knowledge have been met — i.e. co-arising is itself
       informative about the state of the field, which is close to what "paradigm flag" is trying to name.
    5. Sensitivity to Evidential Dependencies in Judgments Under Uncertainty (PMC9285361). — Snippet-level.
       Notes that on Bayesian models, testimony from independent informants has more evidential value
       than dependent testimony.

  Strength of support: Moderate to Strong

  Summary: The literature supports both halves of the assumption, but for a reason more specific than
    "avoid duplicates." Consilience says that agreement among independent lines is worth more than the
    sum of the lines, and worth most when the lines are most dissimilar in method. Corroboration theory
    adds the mechanism: a genuine corroborating report raises the probative weight of the report it
    corroborates, rather than sitting beside it. Merton's work on multiples supplies exactly the frame the
    phrase "paradigm flag" is reaching for — simultaneous independent arrival at the same content is
    read, in the sociology of science, as a signal about the readiness of the surrounding knowledge state,
    not as three people wasting effort. So collapsing three co-arisings into one item is right, provided
    the collapse UPGRADES the item's weight rather than discarding two-thirds of the evidence. The
    assumption as phrased ("one flag rather than three") is ambiguous on that point and could be
    implemented either way.

  Caveats: Every source conditions its verdict on independence, and this is the whole game. Consilience is
    strongest where methods share fewest characteristics; the Bayesian result is that INDEPENDENT testimony
    outweighs dependent testimony, which entails that dependent testimony does not get the bonus. Where the
    three proposals were generated by same-family agents sharing a base model, prompt scaffold, or upstream
    context, the independence premise fails and the consilience warrant does not transfer — see
    PRESUMPTION-900, where I found substantial literature on exactly that failure. A second caveat: the
    formal-modelling literature (Urbaniak & Janda) reports that no probabilistic support measure cleanly
    captures corroboration, so "one flag with more weight" is not yet a quantity anyone can compute; it is
    a qualitative upgrade. Third: Merton's multiples are usually independent in the strong sense
    (different people, different labs, no shared channel), which co-arising proposals inside one pipeline
    are not.

  Recommendation: SUPPORTED

  Operational note (not part of the recommendation): the literature makes the collapse rule depend on a
    prior determination the intake layer does not currently make — whether the three arisings were causally
    independent. The same three proposals warrant an upgrade if independent and a DOWNGRADE (pseudoreplication)
    if not. A single "deduplicate" action cannot serve both cases.
