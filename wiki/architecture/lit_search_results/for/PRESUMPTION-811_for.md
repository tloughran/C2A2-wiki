  Summary: The corrective proposition is strongly supported, and the support is unusually concrete
  because one field has counted. Bettenburg et al. read 44,349 Eclipse bug reports and found that the
  relation between records and defects is many-to-many, contested, and settled by a human triager on
  operational grounds: about 20% of Eclipse reports and 30% of Mozilla reports are duplicates; roughly
  31% of master reports are NEWER than one of their own duplicates, because the master is chosen by
  how far along the fix is rather than by any fact about the defect; and many byte-identical reports
  are deliberately NOT merged because they were filed against different components to reach different
  teams. One of the paper's own enumerated causes of duplication is titled "Multiple failures, one
  defect," and its terminology section keeps report, failure and defect as three separate objects
  precisely because two reports of the same failure need not concern the same defect. The Eclipse data
  also contains 811's own shape: 40 reports filed by one user in one minute against a single bug, and
  33 reports of one defect filed by 33 different users over 900 days. The formal apparatus behind all
  of this is Avizienis et al.'s fault/error/failure chain, in which a fault is the ADJUDGED cause —
  so a record observes a failure while naming its locus is a hypothesis — and Chillarege's ODC, whose
  architecture makes defect TYPE and TRIGGER cause-attributes explicitly orthogonal to severity and
  impact effect-attributes. IEEE 1044 supplies the historical proof that this had to be built: its
  1993 edition treated "anomaly" as a synonym for error, fault, failure, incident, flaw, problem,
  gripe, glitch, defect and bug alike. Where this file departs sharply from a clean confirmation is on
  what follows. The same paper that supports the diagnosis measures the cost of the cure: closing
  duplicates and discarding them loses information that is significant at p < .001 on every dimension
  examined, and the authors' recommendation is to MERGE, not to collapse.
