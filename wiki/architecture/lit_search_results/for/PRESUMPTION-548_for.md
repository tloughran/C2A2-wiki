  Sources:
    1. Altman & Bland (1995), "Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence," BMJ 311:485. — The canonical statement: a non-observation licenses "absence" only when an observation would have been expected. A KNOWN-LAGGING channel is precisely the case where an item's appearance is NOT expected on time, so the non-appearance carries little information about true absence. Directly supports the presumption.
    2. Bayesian absent-evidence analysis (Sober; Stephens, "A Bayesian Approach to Absent Evidence Reasoning," Informal Logic). — Absence of evidence is evidence of absence "to the precise degree that evidence would have been expected had the claim been true." Formally, when P(observation | item exists) is LOW (because the feed lags), the likelihood ratio is near 1 and the clean scan barely updates toward "nothing to do." Supplies the quantitative form of the presumption: the lag drives the detection probability down, so silence ≉ absence.
    3. Closed-world vs open-world assumption (KR/database theory). — Negation-as-failure / the closed-world assumption treats "not derivable" as "false," which is valid ONLY under exhaustive, timely observation. A delayed feed breaks the exhaustiveness precondition, so applying a closed-world "no intake ⇒ nothing to do" over an open-world (lagging) channel is a category error. Supports flagging the inference as unsound given the known lag.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: Strongly supported across epistemology, Bayesian confirmation, and knowledge representation. All three converge on the same conditional: treating a non-observation as confirmed absence is licensed only when detection would have been likely and observation is exhaustive/timely. A decisions feed KNOWN to lag two days violates exactly that precondition — the probability that a real item would already be visible is low, so a clean scan is weak evidence of "nothing to do." The KR framing sharpens it: applying a closed-world assumption over a channel that is in fact open-world (delayed, possibly incomplete) is unsound. The correct reading of Agent 16's clean scan is "no item visible YET," not "no item exists."

  Caveats: Absence CAN be strong evidence of absence when detection is likely and the search is exhaustive (see 15b) — so the support is conditional on the channel actually lagging; if the lag is quantified as small, the inference strengthens.
