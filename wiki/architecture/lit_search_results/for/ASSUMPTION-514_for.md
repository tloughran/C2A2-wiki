  Sources:
    1. Zell, E. & Krizan, Z. (2014). "Do people have insight into their abilities? A metasynthesis." Perspectives on Psychological Science 9(2):111-125. — Meta-analysis: mean self/objective ability correlation ~ r=.29 (~8% shared variance). Self-report alone is a weak estimator of true performance; an external criterion is needed to calibrate it.
    2. Kruger, J. & Dunning, D. (1999). "Unskilled and unaware of it." J Personality and Social Psychology 77(6):1121-1134. — The metacognitive deficit that produces miscalibration is worst exactly where competence is lowest; the proposed cure is external feedback / skill acquisition, i.e., an outside referent.
    3. Tarski, A. (1933/1936). "The concept of truth in formalized languages" (undefinability theorem). — Formal analogue: a sufficiently strong system cannot define its own truth predicate; adequacy must be established from a stronger/outer system. Supports the requirement for an external referent when a system evaluates its own outputs.
    4. Rubin, D.B. (1976). "Inference and missing data." Biometrika 63(3):581-592. — A completeness claim is only valid under a stated model of the missingness mechanism; with no external anchor (a seeded denominator), the mechanism is unobservable from inside the sample, so completeness cannot be asserted.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The rule is strongly supported and, notably, is the general form of PREMISE-124 (already INCORPORATED 2026-07-23). Empirical self-assessment research (Zell & Krizan; Kruger & Dunning) shows self-measures are weakly correlated with objective performance and require external criteria to calibrate; formal results (Tarski) and missing-data theory (Rubin) give the same conclusion from logic and statistics respectively. Convergent support from three independent literatures.

  Caveats: This item largely restates PREMISE-124; INCORPORATE would duplicate the register (flag for 15c consistency check).
