  Summary: The first half of the item is solid. Rohr invokes Scotist univocity in his own voice, with
  the doctrine named, predicating being in one voice across a list that explicitly includes God, and
  drawing an identity inference straight from it — verified at source. The disagreement he enters is
  real: Scotus's univocity argument was built against the analogy position of Henry of Ghent and,
  indirectly, Aquinas, and Scotus supplied a criterion of univocity (unity sufficient to generate a
  contradiction) that is itself usable as the test 1116 wants. Radical Orthodoxy stakes its central
  genealogical claim on this difference being consequential, naming as its consequence the placing of
  God and creature on the same plane of existence. The worry is not manufactured. Two things stop a
  clean SUPPORTED. First, the specialist Scotus literature — Cross, Williams, Horan, Ingham, and the
  constructive thesis of the 2024 *Religions* paper — largely holds that univocity is a **semantic**
  thesis about concepts rather than a metaphysical thesis about being, and that analogy without a
  univocal core collapses into equivocation; on that reading Rohr's use of Scotus does not commit him
  to what the item says. Second, and more seriously for the vault: **the Thomist conjunct is
  undocumented.** The Stump tradition wiki does not mention analogy anywhere, and no Stump text was
  located taking a position on analogical predication. The item reads a real disagreement between
  *Scotus and Aquinas* onto a pairing of *Rohr and Stump*, and only one of those four attributions
