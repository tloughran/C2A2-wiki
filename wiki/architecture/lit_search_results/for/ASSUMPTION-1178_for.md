  Sources:
    1. Hoffman, D. D., Singh, M., & Prakash, C. 2015. "The Interface Theory of Perception." *Psychonomic
       Bulletin & Review* 22(6): 1480–1506. — The decisive source, and it supports the vault's distinction
       from inside Hoffman's own framework. The paper does not argue against a single undifferentiated
       "veridicality"; it erects a graded taxonomy of perceptual strategies — *naive realism*, *critical
       realism*, and *interface* — and runs evolutionary games and genetic algorithms across all three. The
       critical realist strategy is defined as one that preserves *some* of the structure of the world without
       claiming that perception resembles it; the strict interface strategy preserves none. The published
       result is specifically that *strict interface* strategies drive naive realist *and critical realist*
       strategies to extinction. Hoffman's thesis is therefore not "perception is non-veridical" but "the
       total variant beats the selective variant" — which presupposes the very distinction the item says the
       vault has collapsed. If the two claims had a single referent, the FBT theorem would have nothing to
       prove.
    2. Cohen, J. 2015. "Perceptual Representation, Veridicality, and the Interface Theory of Perception."
       *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 22(6): 1512–1518. (Commentary in the ITP forum.) — Argues that the
       notion of veridicality doing the work in the interface theory is idiosyncratic and that the theory's
       conclusions turn on which notion is in play. This is direct published evidence that "non-veridical"
       is an ambiguous predicate whose senses must be separated before claims stated in it can be compared —
       exactly the failure mode the item names. A companion commentary in the same forum ("Reverse Engineering
       the World," *Psychon Bull Rev* 22(6), 2015; author not verified) presses the same point from the
       inference side.
    3. Clay, V., Leadholm, N., & Hawkins, J. 2024. "The Thousand Brains Project: A New Paradigm for
       Sensorimotor Intelligence." arXiv:2412.18354. See also Hawkins, J. 2021. *A Thousand Brains: A New
       Theory of Intelligence*, Basic Books. — Establishes the Hawkins side of the contrast in the terms the
       item uses. Each learning module models whole objects and "represents information through spatially
       structured reference frames"; models are built and corrected by sensorimotor interaction, i.e. scored
       against sensory ground truth on every movement. Reference frames are explicitly map-like and preserve
       real spatial structure. In Hoffman's own taxonomy this is a *critical realist* position, not an
       interface position: the model is constructed, not given, but it is structure-preserving and
       error-corrected against the world. Hawkins' claim is that perception is a model, not that it is
       disconnected from the geometry it models.
    4. Seth, A. K. and Clark, A. — the "controlled hallucination" formulation. See Clark, A. 2013,
       "Whatever Next? Predictive Brains, Situated Agents, and the Future of Cognitive Science," *Behavioral
       and Brain Sciences* 36(3): 181–204, and Seth, A. K. 2021, *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness*,
       Faber/Dutton. — Independent corroboration that "constructed" and "non-veridical in Hoffman's sense" are
       different predicates. The formulation is deliberately two-word: the *hallucination* limb concedes that
       percepts are internally generated, and the *controlled* limb insists they are tightly coupled to the
       world, with the distinction between perception and hallucination drawn precisely by whether that
       control is intact. This is a third published position that occupies the selective, ground-truth-anchored
       slot the item assigns to Hawkins, and it is routinely distinguished from Hoffman's total claim in the
       secondary literature.
    5. "Debunking Interface Theory: Why Hoffman's Skepticism (Really) Is Self-Defeating." *Synthese* (2022),
       DOI 10.1007/s11229-022-04021-1 (author list not verified); and "Realism Is Almost True: A Critique of
       the Interface Theory of Perception," arXiv:2111.03864 (author list not verified). — Two critiques whose
       shared strategy is to grant the bounded game-theoretic result and reject the total metaphysical
       conclusion drawn from it. The very availability of that move confirms the item's premise: the formal
       result and the total non-veridicality thesis are separable, and a selective reading survives the
       arguments that defeat the total one.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The distinction the item asserts is not merely defensible; it is drawn explicitly in Hoffman's own
  primary paper, where naive realism, critical realism and the strict interface are three separately
  formalised strategies and the published result is that the third defeats the first two. A conflation of
  Hawkins with Hoffman therefore does not merely blur two authors — it erases the content of the FBT result,
  which is a claim *about the gap between* selective and total non-veridicality. The Hawkins material
  independently places him in the critical realist slot: reference frames are structure-preserving, map-like,
  and corrected against sensorimotor ground truth, which is what the item means by "anchored to real geometry,
  scored against ground truth." Cohen's forum commentary supplies published confirmation that "veridicality"
  is an ambiguous term in this debate and that arguments stated in it are sensitive to which sense is meant.
  The predictive-processing "controlled hallucination" formulation occupies the same selective slot and is
  routinely distinguished from Hoffman's position. On the second half of the question — whether any published
  treatment addresses Hawkins and Hoffman *jointly* — the search returned nothing. No paper, chapter or review
  was found that compares the Thousand Brains Theory with the Interface Theory of Perception.

  Caveats: The strongest source cuts in an unexpected direction and this should be recorded plainly: it
  supports the *distinction* while showing that the distinction is Hoffman's, not a novel observation. The
  item's value is therefore in noticing an application-level conflation in the vault, not in identifying a
  gap in the literature. Placing Hawkins in Hoffman's "critical realist" category is this searcher's
  classification, made on the basis of what each says about structure preservation; neither author performs
  the placement, and Hawkins does not use the vocabulary of veridicality at all, so the mapping is an
  interpretive step and could be contested. The Cohen and Synthese citations were verified by title, venue and
  DOI/page range but author lists were not fully verified in every case, and the two critique papers are
  marked accordingly. Nothing in this search bears on the item's operational limb — that the number of
  affected Hawkins × Hoffman convergence entries has never been counted — which is a vault-internal fact not
  testable against literature. Search scope: good — covered the ITP primary paper and its 2015 commentary
  forum, the FBT theorem and two published critiques, the Thousand Brains primary literature, and predictive
  processing; did NOT cover the ecological-psychology response to ITP (Gibson-lineage; a partial reference to
  it surfaced but was not pursued), which is the tradition most likely to hold that *neither* claim is
  correctly stated.
