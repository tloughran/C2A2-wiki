    1. ServiceNow Developer Blog, "Every Dashboard Is Green. The Service Is Down. Explain That!" — Supports the item's central distinction with the field's own formulation: "infrastructure health and service health are not the same thing." The green dashboard is not lying about its inputs; it is reporting faithfully over a source set that does not cover the failing dependency. That is precisely the item's diagnosis — a faithful read of the wrong read-set — and the blog treats it as the normal, expected condition of component-oriented reporting rather than as a bug.
    2. ThousandEyes, "Why You Shouldn't Trust (Only) the Status Page." — Names the structural cause: reporting that aggregates individual component status "rather than take a holistic view of the entire service delivery chain" produces summaries that are individually accurate and collectively misleading. Establishes that a status layer is by default an independent parallel account, not a view over the system — the item's inverted presumption is the industry's default failure.
    3. Data lineage literature: DataHub, "Data Lineage vs. Data Observability"; Atlan, "Data Lineage and Data Observability"; Datadog, "Understanding data lineage"; Sifflet, "What is Data Lineage?" — Supplies both the framing and the remedy. Provenance "traces the exact origin and history of the data itself"; column-level lineage "traces every field from source to dashboard" and "maps how individual fields are derived or transformed." The existence of a mature tool category whose entire purpose is binding derived claims to their sources is evidence that the unbound case is a recognised, costly, and general problem — and it is the direct precedent for the item's proposed test (enumerate each summariser's read set and compute coverage of the artifacts it makes claims about). "Coverage of the claimed-about artifacts" is lineage coverage under another name.
    4. Data observability literature: Databricks, "What is Data Observability?"; Decube, "Benefits of Data Observability and Lineage." — Adds the freshness dimension the item's case needs. Observability monitors "freshness delays, volume anomalies, schema changes," and the combination of lineage plus observability is credited with faster root-cause analysis on "broken dashboards." Supports binding each claim not only to a source but to a freshness bound, since a summariser reading a stale-but-present artifact fails identically to one reading nothing.
    5. "When Errors Become Narratives: A Longitudinal Taxonomy of Silent Failures in a Production LLM Agent Runtime" (arXiv:2606.14589). — Domain-matched empirical precedent that an LLM summarisation job can emit confident, well-formed output derived from a degraded or wrong source path, and that the artifact stays "plausible enough to pass casual inspection for weeks." Supports the item's severity claim about unattended stretches specifically: the defect is invisible exactly when nobody is checking.
    6. "Ontological uncertainty and epistemic humility in an age of automation" (arXiv:2605.23614). — Frames the general obligation as "knowing what precision was earned over and what was assumed, propagating that awareness through the chain of inference rather than laundering it." A summariser that does not declare its read set launders scope: the reader cannot distinguish "no failures occurred" from "no failures appear in the four files I read."

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The item's inference is well supported and the literature states it more sharply than the item does. Monitoring practice holds that a summary layer is, by default, an independent parallel account rather than a view over the system, and that its component-level source set is the specific reason summaries can be individually accurate and collectively false — the "faithful read of the wrong read-set" the item describes. That this is a general and expensive problem is evidenced by the existence of a mature commercial tool category, data lineage, whose stated purpose is binding every derived field to its source, plus data observability for the freshness half. The item's proposed in-house test is a direct instance of lineage coverage measurement and needs no invention. The LLM-runtime taxonomy supplies domain-matched precedent that this failure occurs in agent fleets and persists for weeks under low scrutiny, which supports the item's specific severity claim about unattended operation. The epistemic-humility framing adds the strongest practical prescription: a summariser should publish its read set alongside its claims, so that "no failures" is legible as scoped rather than absolute.

  Caveats: (a) The lineage/observability literature assumes a declared, machine-readable pipeline — tables, columns, DAGs. C2A2's summarisers assemble their read set dynamically and by natural-language reasoning, so there is no static graph to instrument. Lineage transfers as a *goal* but the standard tooling does not transfer, and the retrieved sources offer nothing on lineage for LLM-assembled read sets. This gap is a partial NOVELTY. (b) This item and ASSUMPTION-480 describe the same 2026-07-19 morning from two angles and offer two distinct mechanisms — component-vs-service scope mismatch (480) versus read-set non-coverage (503). Both are supported in the abstract; this search does not discriminate which occurred, and per ASSUMPTION-479's discipline that discrimination should precede any remedy. Enumerating the run's actual read set settles it cheaply and settles both items at once. (c) Sources 1-4 are vendor and practitioner material; sources 5-6 are preprints. There is little peer-reviewed work in this area. (d) Full read-set coverage is not achievable in general — a summariser that read every artifact would face the context cost PRESUMPTION-498 names. The realistic target is declared partial coverage, not complete coverage, and the literature does not address how to choose the read set under a budget.
