  Sources:
    1. Stump, E. 2012/2013. "Emergence, Causal Powers, and Aristotelianism in Metaphysics." In R. Groff
       & J. Greco (eds.), *Powers and Capacities in Philosophy: The New Aristotelianism*, Routledge,
       pp. 48-68. — Stump's own statement of her causal position. She argues that a whole's systems-level
       causal power is conferred by the *substantial form* configuring the whole: the power of a water
       molecule to form hydrogen bonds "is not realized in the lower-level properties of the components
       taken in isolation" but in those properties *as organized into the whole*. This is formal
       causation doing level-specific causal work. Final causality is not the operative category here.
    2. Rooney, J. D. 2015. "Stumping Freedom: Divine Causality and the Will." *New Blackfriars*.
       DOI:10.1111/nbfr.12135. — A critical study of Stump's account whose central reported thesis is
       that *Stump argues God acts only by formal causality on the will*. An unsympathetic reader
       characterising her position still characterises it as formal, not final. That is the strongest
       kind of attributional evidence: a critic's summary that agrees with the claim under test.
    3. Stump, E. 2003. *Aquinas*. Routledge. — The standing reference for her reading of Aquinas's
       metaphysics; the analytic-Thomist reconstruction of hylomorphism, soul, and simplicity for which
       she is principally cited. Consulted as the corpus-level check on whether a final-causal
       commitment is stated in her own voice; none surfaced in this search.
