  Sources:
    1. Lorenz, J., Rauhut, H., Schweitzer, F. and Helbing, D. (2011). "How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect." PNAS 108(22). — The load-bearing empirical result. Mild social influence reduces opinion diversity and drives convergence onto *less* accurate collective estimates, while group confidence rises. If convergence is the scored target, the system optimises toward the condition this experiment shows degrades collective accuracy while increasing the appearance of success. That combination — accuracy falling while the success signal rises — makes convergence-scoring not merely a suboptimal target but an inverting one, which places PRESUMPTION-500 in the same class as PRESUMPTION-505.
    2. Page, S.E., diversity prediction theorem; Hong, L. and Page, S.E. (2004), PNAS. — Formal grounding: collective error = average individual error − diversity. Diversity enters the accuracy identity as a subtracted term, so a scoring function that rewards convergence is directly penalising a component of collective accuracy. Establishes that the presumed success signature and the actual success condition are in tension by construction, not by accident.
    3. MacIntyre, A. (1988), Whose Justice? Which Rationality?; (1990), Three Rival Versions of Moral Enquiry (via Polanyi Society, Tradition and Discovery 26:2, "MacIntyre's Tradition-Constituted Enquiry in Polanyian Perspective," and Ontological Atlas). — The most directly relevant philosophical source, and it supplies the alternative signature the item says has no instrument. Rational comparison between traditions is possible but "always from within a particular historically extended community of enquiry"; traditions are evaluated "by their capacity to address their internal crises and to provide accounts of their rivals." The criterion of successful engagement is the ability to give an account of the rival *from inside one's own framework* — explicitly not merger, and explicitly not requiring a shared vocabulary. MacIntyre further holds that incommensurability between rival traditions "does not entail either relativism or perspectivism," which forecloses the usual objection that non-convergence means no progress was made.
    4. Brand, C. et al. (2025). "The Ideological Turing Test: A Behavioral Measure of Open-Mindedness and Perspective-Taking." Cognitive Science 49(10), e70126 (PMC12519043; PubMed 41084386). — Supplies a validated, published instrument for the missing signature. Measures "the accuracy with which people represent the arguments of their ideological opponents," is behavioural rather than self-report, and is structurally unfakeable: "if you understand arguments for the opposing position you can produce them; if you believe you understand them but cannot produce them, then you don't really understand." Scoring is by opponent-rated indistinguishability from a genuine holder. This is mutual registration without endorsement, operationalised, with reported evidence of high mutual understanding across three topics and a demonstrated correlation with reduced derogation of opponents.
    5.   Summary: PRESUMPTION-500 is the highest-value item in the batch and the search supports it on all three of its clauses. That convergence is the wrong target is established twice over — formally by the diversity prediction theorem, where diversity is a subtracted term in collective error, and experimentally by Lorenz et al., where mild influence toward convergence lowers accuracy while raising confidence. The second result matters most: it means a convergence-scored detector would report improving success exactly as collective accuracy degrades, which is an inverting instrument rather than merely a poor one. That the alternative signature is coherent and not a retreat into relativism is MacIntyre's central claim, and he states the criterion in nearly the form the item wants: a tradition's engagement with a rival is measured by its capacity to give an account of that rival. That the alternative signature has no instrument is the one clause the literature contradicts in C2A2's favour — the Ideological Turing Test is a published, peer-reviewed, behavioural, unfakeable measure of exactly this, and C2A2 can adopt rather than invent it. Finally, the LLM diversity-collapse results establish that convergence is the *default drift* of the substrate, so an unstated presumption that convergence indicates success would systematically mistake substrate homogenisation for research progress.

  Caveats: (a) The most serious caveat is a confound in the recommended instrument. The Ideological Turing Test assumes the two parties do not share a mind. C2A2's traditions are instantiated by agents drawn from a common pretrained model, so an agent may produce a rival tradition's arguments fluently from shared pretraining rather than from genuine registration acquired in dialogue. The test would then score high at baseline and be insensitive to the very thing it is meant to measure. A pre-dialogue baseline ITT score is mandatory before any post-dialogue score is interpretable; without it the instrument is uninformative. This connects to ASSUMPTION-477's correlated-pretraining problem — the same shared-substrate confound, in a different role. (b) The diversity prediction theorem applies to estimation with a numeric ground truth; inter-tradition dialogue has no such target, so the decomposition is a motivating analogy here, not a literal identity. (c) MacIntyre is a philosophical argument, not an empirical finding, and his own account requires historically extended communities with internal crises and continuous practice — agent-instantiated traditions running for fourteen days do not obviously satisfy that condition. (d) The item's claim that the presumption is "nowhere stated" is an internal-record claim outside search scope; I make no finding on it. (e) Nothing found supports abandoning cross-connection counts entirely — the literature's position is that connection-counting and convergence-scoring are different measurements, and the item may conflate them.
