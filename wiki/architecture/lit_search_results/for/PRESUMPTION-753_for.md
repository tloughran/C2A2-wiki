  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: The presumption is supported in the strong sense that the ambiguity it names is real, published, and unresolved rather than a local confusion. Fanelli (2013) and Fang, Steen & Casadevall (2012) analyse the same secular rise in retractions and reach opposing readings — improving detection versus increasing misconduct — and the disagreement persists in the 2025 policy discussion of retraction rate as an institutional metric, where the "intense scrutiny versus worse behaviour" confound is stated as a live objection to adopting the measure. This is direct support for the item's claim that a retraction rate does not by itself distinguish convergence from instability: it is a joint measure of the defect rate and the detection rate, and the two cannot be separated without an independent estimate of one of them. Fanelli's framing is the most directly usable for C2A2: retraction counts index the detection-and-correction apparatus, so a rising count in a system that has just strengthened its review layer is confounded by construction.

  Caveats: The scientific-publishing analogy is imperfect. Journal retraction is a rare, costly, adversarial, reputation-bearing event with a formal process; a C2A2 flag withdrawal is cheap, internal, and frequent, so base rates and incentive structures differ by orders of magnitude and the confound may not transfer with the same weights. None of the located sources addresses oscillation — repeated reversal of the *same* claim — which is the instability signature 753 is closest to; they address the aggregate rate only. I found no source giving a statistical test that separates the detection component from the defect component, so the literature supports the existence of the ambiguity without supplying a remedy.
