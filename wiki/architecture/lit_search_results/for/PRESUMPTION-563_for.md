  Sources:
    1. Cronbach, L.J., & Meehl, P.E. (1955). "Construct Validity in Psychological Tests." Psychological Bulletin 52(4):281-302. — The founding statement of construct validity: validation is required precisely whenever a measure is to be interpreted as a measure of an attribute that is *not* operationally defined, and it proceeds by embedding the construct in a nomological network of theoretical propositions against which the measure's predictions can be tested. The direct implication for the presumption: without an antecedent specification of the construct, there is nothing against which a candidate measure can be validated, and the choice collapses into operationalism — the construct becomes whatever the measure measures. This is the presumption's claim, stated as the field's foundational methodological requirement.
    2. Choi, W., Hecht, G., & Tayler, W.B. (2012). "Lost in Translation: The Effects of Incentive Compensation on Strategy Surrogation." The Accounting Review 87(4):1135-1163 (SSRN 1438212); see also Choi, Hecht & Tayler, "Strategy Selection, Surrogation, and Strategic Performance Measurement Systems," Journal of Accounting Research. — Coins and experimentally demonstrates *surrogation*: decision-makers come to treat a measure as if it *were* the strategic construct, optimising the measure in place of the strategy. The mechanism identified is attribute substitution, requiring that the measure be highly accessible relative to the construct — which is exactly the condition when the construct is unspecified and the measure is a clean formula. The finding that surrogation is most severe with a *single* measure of a construct, and mitigated by multiple measures and by involvement in choosing the strategy, gives the presumption a specific predicted failure mode rather than a general worry.
    3. Manheim, D., & Garrabrant, S. (2018). "Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law." arXiv:1803.04585. — Decomposes proxy failure into Regressional, Extremal, Causal and Adversarial mechanisms, each describing how optimisation degrades the relationship between a proxy and the goal it stood for. The taxonomy presupposes and requires a goal distinct from the proxy in order to say anything at all — which supports the presumption's point that the interesting failure here is prior to and worse than Goodhart: where no goal is separately specified, there is no distinction left for Goodhart to degrade, and the substitution is definitional rather than gradual.
    4. Hughes, E., Dennis, M.D., Parker-Holder, J., Behbahani, F., Mavalankar, A., Shi, Y., Schaul, T., & Rocktaeschel, T. (2024). "Position: Open-Endedness is Essential for Artificial Superhuman Intelligence." ICML 2024, PMLR 235:20597-20616 (arXiv:2406.04268). — The source of the construct: a system is open-ended *from the perspective of an observer* if and only if the sequence of artefacts it produces is both novel and learnable to that observer. Two scope conditions are explicit in the definition and are load-bearing for the presumption: the definition is observer-relative, so adopting it requires naming the observer and its learning model; and it is a definition of *open-endedness*, offered in a position paper as a property of learning systems, not a definition of progress toward a research goal. Importing it therefore does import a definition — the paper's own framing says so.
    5. Wang, R.Y., & Strong, D.M. (1996). "Beyond Accuracy: What Data Quality Means to Data Consumers." Journal of Management Information Systems 12(4):5-33. — Establishes empirically that quality is multi-dimensional and *contextual* — data have quality only relative to the task at hand — so a single substrate-independent measure cannot carry a task-relative quality judgement on its own. Supports the presumption's scope-condition concern about substrate-independent metrics.

The accounting literature supplies the empirically demonstrated mechanism by which this happens even to people who would deny it: surrogation, driven by the measure's accessibility relative to the construct, and most severe with a single measure — which is the present configuration exactly. Goodhart-taxonomy work sharpens the diagnosis by showing that its own failure modes require a goal distinct from the proxy, so the underdetermined-construct case is prior to Goodhart rather than an instance of it. Finally, the referent itself supports the presumption: Hughes et al. define novelty-plus-learnability as *observer-relative open-endedness*, a property of learning systems argued for in a position paper, so adopting it as a progress metric imports both a definition and an unnamed observer. The in-house design question — whether any independent written specification of "progress" exists in C2A2 — is decisive, and the literature's prescription if it does not is to write the specification first.

  Caveats: (a) The literature supports "a metric adopted without a prior construct specification defines rather than measures," not "learnable novelty is a bad metric"; if C2A2's progress construct were written down first, novelty-plus-learnability could well validate well against it, and the surrogation literature's own mitigation (multiple measures of the construct) would apply.
