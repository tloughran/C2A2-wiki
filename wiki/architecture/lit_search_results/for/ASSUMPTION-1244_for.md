  Supporting evidence found: Yes (for the tendency); Partial (for "by construction")

  Sources:
    1. BCS (The Chartered Institute for IT), "Understanding and driving change with maturity models."
       — Snippet-level. States the basic premise of maturity modelling explicitly: scored on a five-point
       scale, "the higher your level of maturity, the better your organisation's ability to learn lessons
       and improve." The normative ascent is stated as the premise of the method, not as an
       interpretation of it. This is close to direct support for "by construction."
    2. Bjorgaard, N. (2020), "Stage Models of Adult Development: A Critical Introduction."
       Integral Review, vol. 16 no. 2. — Snippet-level. Reports that stage models risk sliding from
       description into prescription, becoming hierarchies "where higher stages are better and lower
       stages are deficient," and that despite theorists such as Kegan cautioning against this reading,
       "the language of 'higher' and 'lower' stages carries its own gravitational pull." This is the
       key source: it supports the tendency strongly while explicitly framing it as a pull, not an entailment.
    3. Pelaez, M. et al., "A Critique of Stage Theories of Human Development" (chapter 17; PDF hosted at
       faculty.fiu.edu). — Snippet-level. Argues stage theories define development as movement through
       fixed stages rather than through dynamic person-environment interaction, and that stages tell us
       what is likely at a level but not how or why — i.e. the ordering does explanatory work it has not
       earned.
    4. Meridian University, "Adult Development Theory and the Work of Transformation." — Snippet-level,
       non-peer-reviewed institutional page; low weight. Notes that developmental structures typically posit
       the successor stage as "more evolved," while also noting that stages do not always evolve upward and
       individuals can devolve under disorder and distress.
    5. Capability Immaturity Model (Wikipedia). — Snippet-level. A semi-serious counter-model to the CMM,
       cited here as evidence that the ascending frame is strong enough to have provoked a formal parody
       inverting it.
    6. Rohr, R. (2011), Falling Upward: A Spirituality for the Two Halves of Life. Jossey-Bass.
       — Reviews only (Christian Century 2011; Spirituality & Practice). The title itself encodes ascent
       ("upward"), and the structure is two-stage with the second stage filling the container the first
       stage built. Reviews note the framework leans on Jung. I found NO substantial critique of ascent-
       encoding in Rohr specifically; the critiques surfaced were about orthodoxy and about gender coverage.

  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: The literature strongly supports the substance of the assumption and stops just short of its
    modal force. On the maturity-model side, ascent is not an inference at all — BCS states higher-is-better
    as the premise of the method, and the CMM literature describes the five levels as running from random
    to optimized, with organisational performance claimed to improve as the level rises. On the developmental-
    psychology side, Bjorgaard's phrasing is the most useful finding in this cohort: the slide into "higher
    is better" is a "gravitational pull" that operates even when the theorist explicitly warns against it.
    That is a claim about a very strong and hard-to-resist tendency, which is not quite the same as
    entailment. So: an ascending reading of `Stage: N` is the default that must be actively disabled, and
    the literature suggests attempts to disable it usually fail.

  Caveats: "By construction" is stronger than what I found. Three things cut against strict entailment.
    (i) Kegan and others explicitly design against the normative reading, which shows the normative content
    is separable in principle even if it reasserts itself in practice. (ii) Some models admit regression /
    devolution, which an ascending-by-construction frame could not accommodate. (iii) The Capability
    Immaturity Model demonstrates that the same ordinal scale can be run downward, so the ordering itself
    is not what carries the norm — the labelling and the surrounding claims do. A further scope limit:
    the maturity-model sources are organisational-process literature and the stage sources are developmental
    psychology; Pathway 35 draws on a contemplative-tradition lineage (Rohr) where I found essentially no
    critical literature on ascent-encoding at all, so the transfer to that limb is untested. Also note that
    Rohr's own second-half-of-life stage is entered through falling, not climbing, which complicates a
    simple ascent reading of that particular source.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED

    Finding: The general claim is NOT novel — the normative-ascent problem in stage and maturity models is
      well covered in both the developmental-psychology critique literature and the software-process
      literature, and the 14a novelty flag should be downgraded on that limb. What I did NOT find is any
      literature examining whether contemplative-tradition stage frameworks (Rohr and his sources) encode
      normative ascent in the same way that psychometric stage models do. The Rohr critique literature I
      surfaced addresses theological orthodoxy and gender coverage, not the structural ascent question.
    Implication: The stakes stated by 14a hold. Because ascent is the default reading and resists
      suppression, a bare `Stage: N` line in a metrics snapshot will be read as "better than Stage N-1"
      regardless of intent. If Pathway 35 does not mean that, the snapshot needs either a non-ordinal
      label or an explicit adjacent statement that stages are not ranked — and per Bjorgaard, even that
      may not hold.

      and developmental-psychology limbs.
