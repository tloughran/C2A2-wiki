  Summary: There is exactly one piece of real FOR evidence and it is the NTSB record — more than 12,000
  narrative recommendations over six decades with acceptance above 82%, from a body with no power to
  compel. That is a genuine demonstration that a well-specified recommendation, written in prose to a
  named addressee, can be taken up at high rates at scale, and it is why this result is
  PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED rather than NO-SUPPORT-FOUND. But the qualifiers are severe and they run to the
  heart of the presumption. "Accepted" at NTSB reportedly includes recipients who merely said they
  would act; GAO found 35% of decade-old aviation recommendations not fully implemented; and the
  reported reason is the absence of an enforced implementation deadline — which is precisely the
  structural feature C2A2's named-but-unowned measurements lack. The register's own PREMISE-164 already
  drew the right conclusion from this same pair: the medium is not the variable, the ADDRESSEE,
  OBLIGATION and READ PATH are. Meanwhile the best-quantified body on the general mechanism, already
  held at PREMISE-116, places the expected effect of a written finding on behaviour at 4-8% absolute,
  with every one of its known moderators absent from a premise written to a registry. On the specific
  base rate the routed question asked for — postmortem action-item completion — I found nothing
  traceable, only untraced blog figures in the 30-40% range which I decline to treat as evidence. And
  on the structural-features limb I found no measured effect size for named ownership or deadlines at
  all. The honest summary: the presumption is supported only in the case where an addressee, an
  obligation and a deadline exist, and the estate's four unowned measurements are the case where they
  do not.
