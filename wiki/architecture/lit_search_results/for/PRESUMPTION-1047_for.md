  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: The literature strongly supports the premise that publicly accessible information about identifiable living persons carries ethical obligations beyond availability (Zimmer 2010; boyd & Crawford 2012; Nissenbaum 2011; AoIR 2020), and that aggregation/profiling is itself a distinct harm (Solove 2006; Nissenbaum). Grief and bereavement are recognised as high-vulnerability contexts warranting heightened care (AoIR 2020; Myles et al. 2019), and the online-mourning review finds this area under-governed in practice. This supports the "why a stated position is needed" part of the claim. Direct empirical or normative evidence on the specific comparison (individual-agent restraint versus a stated policy) is thin; it is supported by inference from these sources and from the general role of published codes and review structures, not by a source testing the comparison.

  Caveats:
    - The strongest codified source (AoIR 3.0) explicitly favours process-based deliberation, "guidelines, not recipes", and ethical pluralism over fixed rules. This partly cuts against the policy-over-judgement framing; a stated policy in AoIR terms would be a documented deliberative framework and not a rule set. This is flagged for 15b's direction and for reconciliation, not argued here.
    - Most sources concern researchers handling social-media/user data about ordinary users; domain transfer to an automated system tracking the published work of (often public) scholars and writing to them is not established. Public-figure status may reduce the strength of the consent line.
    - The consent-focused sources concern research publication and IRB-style review; a tracking pipeline is not clearly a research project with an ethics board.
    - Scholarly-attribution limb (Rohr/Armas byline for curated/devotional material): only a pointer source (Allen et al. 2014) was found; no substantive support established. Search on this limb was preliminary.
    - Possible publication bias: normative critique literature over-represents the "caution" position.
    - Search scope: preliminary search (6 web searches, 8 fetches; tradition wikis not consulted in this run; the wiki directory was not searched for citations) — broader search recommended for professional codes on biographical treatment of living scholars, bereavement-data ethics, and attribution/curation norms.
