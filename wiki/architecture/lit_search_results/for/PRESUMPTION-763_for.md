  Summary: No published source was found asserting or demonstrating that existence verification warrants a completeness claim, and the search turned up a consistent structural reason why: the two properties are estimated by entirely different instruments. Completeness of a retrieved set is a population-inference problem, handled in evidence synthesis by capture–recapture / capture–mark–recapture estimation of the unobserved remainder and used as a formal stopping rule (sources 1 and 2). Adequacy of a *single* citation to the assertion it anchors is a content-verification problem, handled by manual comparison of assertion against source, with published error rates of ~14.5% and a majority of content errors classed as major (Mogull 2017; Jergas & Baethge 2015). Liu, Zhang & Liang (2023) show the same gap in generated text: half of sentences are not fully supported despite real, retrievable citations. Existence verification sits underneath both and provides evidence for neither. This is a clean NO-SUPPORT-FOUND: the presumption is not merely unevidenced, it is orthogonal to the constructs the literature actually measures.
