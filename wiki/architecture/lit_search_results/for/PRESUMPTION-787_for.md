EEMUA Publication 191, "Alarm Systems — A Guide to Design, Management and Procurement" (Edition 4, November 2024); with ISA 18.2 as the parallel standard. — The most directly transferable source, and it settles the item by definition rather than by argument. A STANDING alarm is one whose underlying condition persists; a STALE alarm is a standing alarm active beyond an extended period (commonly 24 hours). The operative professional judgement is that such conditions "do not require a timely response from the operator, therefore FAIL THE CRITERIA OF BEING AN ALARM." They are counted and reported SEPARATELY from the alarm rate (EEMUA 191 caps standing alarms at ~10 per console) precisely because including them in the live signal population corrupts the population. Translated: a queue reading that has been constant for eleven runs has stopped being a reading and become a standing condition, and continuing to consume it as a reading is the documented misuse. Shannon information theory — the self-information of an event of probability 1 is zero; a deterministic signal has zero entropy; "a partial message that does not reduce the number of possibilities at all transmits no information at all." — The formal statement of the residual. If the reader's output is fully predicted before the reading, the reading has transmitted nothing, and the eleventh reading is not weaker evidence than the first — it is zero evidence, as were readings two through ten. This is why the streak framing is dangerous: the accumulating count grows while the information content stays at zero. [canonical; cited from knowledge.   Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is strongly supported, and in the field that manages constant readings professionally it is codified rather than argued. EEMUA 191 and ISA 18.2 do not merely warn about persistent conditions — they rule that a standing alarm fails the definition of an alarm and must be counted separately, which is the same move this item asks for: a constant reading should leave the population of readings. The sensor-diagnostics literature supplies the mechanism, and it is the sharp point: a long constant in-range run is a recognised fault signature, and every method for distinguishing "stable subject" from "stuck instrument" requires information from OUTSIDE the channel — a correlated second sensor, a derivative test, a counter. Information theory states the same thing formally: a perfectly predicted reading carries zero bits, so the eleventh identical reading adds nothing to the first, and a streak of them is an accumulating count over a constant zero. The scheduling literature adds the discrimination the runs skipped — starvation and blockage look identical from the waiting item's position and have different remedies — while itself scoping out of the C2A2 case, because PREMISE-119 records the review channel's service rate as zero, which is blockage, not starvation. And the disclosure experiment shows the reader-side error is measured and does not self-correct without feedback. The specific inference at issue — seven runs reading a queue held by construction and each concluding "the assigned work is exhausted" — is a category error on this literature: the queue reported its hold, and the runs reported the queue.

  Caveats: (a) The strongest sources concern PHYSICAL instruments with a known dynamic range, where "constant" is anomalous. A review queue held by design is not faulty; the hold is intended. The transferable claim is therefore the weaker and more precise one — a constant output obliges an independent liveness check before any conclusion is drawn about the subject — and NOT the stronger "constant output means broken." Anyone citing this file for the stronger claim is misusing it. (b) The starvation/aging line scopes itself out, as recorded in source 4, and must not be used to recommend an aging policy on the review queue; PREMISE-119 already forbids that reasoning.
