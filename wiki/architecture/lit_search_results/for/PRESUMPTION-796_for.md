  Summary: The content question splits into two, and the literature answers them differently. **On EXISTENCE the answer is yes, and it is well attested.** ISO/IEC 17025 §6.5 imposes metrological traceability on ALL measurement results with no risk-based carve-out and the arrangement demonstrably works, with the regress terminating in ILAC peer evaluation of the accreditation bodies rather than in a self-validating apex. The IIA mandates an external quality assessment for every internal audit activity at least five-yearly. Meta-monitoring is applied without exception in mature SRE practice, and C2A2's own PREMISE-086 already adopts it. So a defensible class of universal meta-controls exists, and the question "may a meta-control be universal?" is answered affirmatively by practice. **On the DISTINGUISHING CRITERION the answer is that no located source states one**, and the counterweight is serious: the audit profession itself ran the experiment. PCAOB AS2 imposed near-universal prescriptive control testing at exactly this meta level and was replaced within three years by AS5's risk-based scoping because universality was disproportionately costly. Proportionality doctrine in the Green Book, AU 319 and prudential supervision is stated generally with NO meta-level exception. What reconciles the two halves — and this is a synthesis of the sources, NOT a claim any of them makes — is a COST-SCALING property. Every warranted universal meta-control located here is O(1) in the number of object-level items: traceability is a once-per-instrument structural property; the IIA assessment is one five-yearly event; a heartbeat is one signal. Every meta-control that was universal and then abandoned was O(N): AS2 required per-control testing across the population, so its cost scaled with the thing it governed and it degraded exactly as PREMISE-131 and PREMISE-139 predict, into checks recorded as performed. On that criterion, universality is warranted for a meta-control whose cost does not scale with the object-level population, whose execution sits outside the party it governs, and whose failure is itself perishable and therefore self-announcing — and is NOT warranted otherwise, meta-level status notwithstanding. That criterion is defensible and consistent with every source found; it is also, as far as this search can establish, unstated in the literature.
