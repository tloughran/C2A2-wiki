  Summary: Bibliometrics and scientometrics literature consistently treats "direct" relational evidence (co-authorship, co-attendance, direct citation) as evidentially distinct from "inferred" relational evidence (bibliographic coupling, co-citation, topic/embedding similarity), and several sources explicitly rank these by directness/strength of signal. This supports the general principle behind ASSUMPTION-825: that documented external co-presence (two things appearing together in a third-party source) is a qualitatively different evidential category from a connection an analyst constructs via similarity/analogy.
