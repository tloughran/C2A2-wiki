  Original statement: Two success criteria imported from two traditions are presumed compatible because both
    are inside the paradigm being assembled. The Friston-derived criterion makes success = belief overlap;
    the Levin-derived criterion makes success = landing in an empty region. Maximal overlap is the least
    novel region, so adopting both leaves the accelerator with no failure condition.

  Sources:
    1. Kuhn, T. S. 1977. "Objectivity, Value Judgment, and Theory Choice." In *The Essential Tension*,
       University of Chicago Press, pp. 320–339. — Kuhn's five shared values (accuracy, consistency, scope,
       simplicity, fruitfulness) are individually imprecise and *jointly conflicting*, and there is no
       algorithm that prescribes how to apply them in a given case. This is the closest thing in the
       philosophy of science to a direct statement of the presumption's structural claim: possessing two
       criteria without a weighting rule does not yield a determinate verdict, it yields underdetermination.
       Kuhn's point is stronger than "the criteria might conflict" — it is that conflict is the normal case
       and that the resolution is not itself criterial.
    2. Lakatos, I. 1970. "Falsification and the Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes." In Lakatos &
       Musgrave (eds.), *Criticism and the Growth of Knowledge*, Cambridge UP, pp. 91–196. See also Musgrave
       & Pigden, "Imre Lakatos," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (retrieved 2026-08-24). — Lakatos
       supplies the failure condition the presumption says is missing: a programme is *degenerating* when the
       hard core plus auxiliaries stop predicting novel facts, or when the novel facts predicted cannot be
       tested. Critically, Lakatos does not treat insulating the hard core from refutation as illicit *per se*
       — he treats it as the mechanism by which a programme becomes unappraisable. The SEP entry states the
       consequence flatly: a research programme can be falsifiable in some senses and yet unscientific.
       This is direct theoretical grounding for "no failure condition" being the diagnostic to watch for.
    3. MacIntyre, A. 1977. "Epistemological Crises, Dramatic Narrative and the Philosophy of Science."
       *The Monist* 60(4): 453–472; and MacIntyre, A. 1988. *Whose Justice? Which Rationality?*, University
       of Notre Dame Press. — MacIntyre's resolution of the incommensurability problem is the one that most
       directly supports this item: rational comparison between traditions does *not* depend on the existence
       of common decision criteria; it depends on each tradition's capacity to *fail by its own standards*.
       A synthesis that has removed its own capacity to fail by its own standards has therefore removed the
       only thing MacIntyre identifies as making tradition-constituted rationality rational. The vault's
       diagnosis — that the danger is the loss of the failure condition rather than the incommensurability
       as such — is MacIntyre's own emphasis.
    4. Pugh, J. K., Soros, L. B., & Stanley, K. O. 2016. "Searching for Quality Diversity When Diversity Is
       Unaligned with Quality." *Parallel Problem Solving from Nature (PPSN XIV)*, LNCS 9921, Springer. —
       The closest *formal* analogue found. This paper isolates precisely the case where the diversity
       (behavioural-novelty) axis and the quality axis are unaligned, and shows that combined search degrades
       under that condition rather than benefiting from both. It establishes that a novelty criterion and a
       quality criterion are not automatically composable, and that alignment between them is a *precondition*
       for the combination working, not a background fact.
    5. Pugh, J. K., Soros, L. B., & Stanley, K. O. 2016. "Quality Diversity: A New Frontier for Evolutionary
       Computation." *Frontiers in Robotics and AI* 3:40. — States the mechanism explicitly: the radical shift
       away from objectives toward divergence "discards with it the ability to specify up front what is good
       and what is bad, and to have that notion influence the search." That is the presumption's claim in
       the evolutionary-computation register: a pure emptiness/novelty criterion has no failure condition,
       and QD algorithms exist specifically to re-supply one (via a per-niche local-competition or archive
       structure). Compare Lehman, J. & Stanley, K. O. 2011, "Abandoning Objectives: Evolution Through the
       Search for Novelty Alone," *Evolutionary Computation* 19(2): 189–223, which is the source of the
       pure-emptiness criterion and which is explicit that unbounded novelty search becomes unfocused in
       large behaviour spaces.
    6. Lorenz, J., Rauhut, H., Schweitzer, F., & Helbing, D. 2011. "How Social Influence Can Undermine the
       Wisdom of Crowd Effect." *PNAS* 108(22): 9020–9025. — Empirical support for the specific half of the
       item that says maximal overlap is not a success signal: in a 144-participant experiment, mild social
       influence reduced group diversity *without* reducing collective error, while increasing participants'
       confidence. Convergence rose, accuracy did not, confidence did. An overlap-maximising success criterion
       would have scored this as success.

  Strength of support: Moderate-to-Strong

  Summary: Every component of the presumption is separately well grounded, and the components are grounded in
  literatures that do not cite one another, which strengthens rather than weakens the case. Kuhn establishes
  that a plurality of shared evaluative criteria is not self-resolving and that no algorithm supplies the
  weighting; Lakatos supplies the vocabulary in which "no failure condition" is a diagnosis (degeneration)
  rather than a complaint; MacIntyre establishes that a tradition's rational standing depends specifically on
  its capacity to fail by its own standards, which is exactly the capacity the item alleges has been forfeited.
  Independently, the quality-diversity literature has met the same structure formally: a novelty criterion and
  a quality criterion are composable only when aligned, degrade when unaligned, and the alignment is a
  condition to be checked rather than assumed. Lorenz et al. supply direct experimental evidence that
  convergence and accuracy come apart while confidence tracks convergence — the failure mode an overlap
  criterion is blind to. What the literature does *not* supply is a treatment of the composite object: no
  source was found that studies multi-tradition synthesis systems as a class and shows that they
  characteristically acquire unfalsifiable success conditions. That specific generalisation is unattested.

  Caveats: The support is compositional, not direct. Kuhn, Lakatos and MacIntyre are analysing human research
  traditions over decades; the item concerns an automated accelerator operating over days, and none of the
  three offers a test that could be run on such a system. The QD material is the strongest *structural*
  analogue but the transfer is by analogy: "belief overlap" is not a fitness function and "landing in an empty
  region" is not a behaviour-characterisation vector, and no source establishes that the analogy is anything
  more than a shared shape. The item's sharpest claim — that maximal overlap *is* the least novel region — is
  treated here as a definitional consequence of the two criteria as stated, not as an empirical finding; it is
  supported by construction, not by citation. Note also a publication-bias hazard specific to this search: the
  philosophy-of-science literature on degenerating programmes is overwhelmingly retrospective and is applied
  to programmes that had already lost credibility, so it over-represents cases where the diagnosis was
  correct. Search scope: moderate-to-good — covered Kuhn/Lakatos/MacIntyre on incommensurability and
  appraisal, novelty search and quality-diversity, and the wisdom-of-crowds convergence literature; did NOT
  cover multi-objective optimisation theory (Pareto-dominance arguments about non-comparable objectives),
  which would formalise the "no failure condition" limb more tightly, nor the AI-alignment literature on
  specification gaming, which addresses the adjacent case of an agent satisfying a criterion while defeating
  its purpose.

  Recommendation: SUPPORTED
