  Summary: The corrective proposition is well supported and its central move — that the gate's
  necessity is an empirical question rather than a standing commitment — turns out to have a
  classical, quantitative answer that predates the whole HITL literature. Deming's all-or-none rule
  states the case exactly: with p the true defect rate of the incoming stream, k1 the per-item
  inspection cost and k2 the downstream cost of a missed defect, the cost-minimising policy is NO
  INSPECTION when p < k1/k2 and 100% inspection when p > k1/k2, with partial inspection dominated
  in both directions. Deming's own worked case is a firm inspecting every unit of a stream two
  orders of magnitude cleaner than its break-even and thereby maximising cost — the failure mode
  802 describes, in a manufacturing register. The de-implementation literature supplies the
  operational version for checks whose defect rate is not directly measurable: classify a routine
  check as low-value when it shows LOW YIELD or a LOW RATE OF CHANGE IN MANAGEMENT, and note that
  the second criterion is the demanding one — a gate that finds things but changes nothing is still
  contributing nothing. That is precisely the override-rate measurement 14b's lit pipeline proposed
  and the day's framing did not connect to. The governance literature completes the picture from
  the other end: an oversight body whose rejection rate approaches zero is understood to be
  ratifying rather than screening, and low amendment rates are the standard proxy for it — with an
  incentive structure (no penalty for ratifying, social cost for challenging, nobody auditing the
  quality of challenge) that is fully present in a single-maintainer vault. Taken together these
  establish 14b's claim: silence IS a datum, the datum has a standard name (yield), and reading it
  is normal practice rather than a novel move. On timing, 14b's risk statement is also supported —
  a dark period is the only window in which the counterfactual is cheaply available, and it closes
  when the backlog is worked, because after adjudication one can no longer observe what the
  unadjudicated items cost.

  Caveats:
    (a) THE OVERRIDE-RATE MECHANISM IS ALREADY REGISTER-HELD AND THIS NARROWS THE DISPOSITION.
        PREMISE-121 holds override rates of 49-96% and reviewer degradation under load; PREMISE-090
        already denies per-item attendance as a standing cadence; PREMISE-102 already holds that a
        zero-throughput channel becomes standing non-coverage. If a disposition treats this file as
        new support for "the review gate is a bottleneck" or "override rates are high," it is
        re-minting 121 and 003. The disjoint contributions are the DECIDABILITY criterion (R1), the
        admissibility and DECAY of silence-as-measurement (R2), and the instrument-design
        asymmetry (R3).
    (b) DEMING'S FOUR CONDITIONS DO NOT HOLD HERE, AND THIS IS THE LARGEST WEAKNESS IN THE FILE.
        The kp rule assumes: one part type; a downstream test that catches the defect; a defective
        input FAILS downstream and a good one PASSES (i.e. a perfect detector); and available
        replacements. C2A2 violates at least two. Vault items are heterogeneous, not one part type,
        and the value of review varies enormously across them — which is PREMISE-121's
        value-weighted-triage clause arriving from the other side. More seriously, THERE IS NO
        RELIABLE DOWNSTREAM TEST: a wrong premise entering the register does not fail loudly later;
        it propagates silently, which is the entire concern of PREMISE-096 (no self-produced
        artefact may certify itself). Where the downstream detector is imperfect, k2 is not a
        repair cost but an unbounded propagation cost, and the break-even threshold k1/k2 collapses
        toward zero — which pushes the answer back toward inspection. The kp rule is cited here as
        proof that the QUESTION IS DECIDABLE and as the correct SHAPE of the calculation. It is NOT
        cited as evidence that the answer for C2A2 is "no inspection," and it must not be quoted
        onward that way.
    (c) THE CHESTERTON'S-FENCE OBJECTION IS REAL AND IS NOT ANSWERED HERE. "Absence of evidence of
        the failure is not evidence that the failure could not occur." A gate may serve latent
        functions beyond its named one — in C2A2's case, the existence of a review gate may
        discipline what agents are willing to propose, independently of whether anything is ever
        rejected. That deterrent effect is invisible in the override rate BY CONSTRUCTION, and a
        yield measurement taken during a dark period cannot see it either, because agents knew the
        gate existed. Any override-rate finding must be reported with this confound named.
    (d) THE MEASUREMENT IS SELF-MEASUREMENT AND PREMISE-124 BINDS IT. An override rate computed by
        the fleet, over items the fleet produced, adjudicated against a standard the fleet wrote,
        has no external referent. PREMISE-124 requires an external baseline, a seeded denominator,
        or an explicit UNCALIBRATED tag. The cheap fix is FAULT SEEDING — deliberately route a
        small number of known-defective items through the gate and see whether they are caught —
        which is also the only design that separates "the gate rejects nothing because nothing is
        wrong" from "the gate rejects nothing because it is not looking."
    (e) n = 1 REVIEWER, AND THE GOVERNANCE ANALOGUE IS A POOR FIT ON THIS AXIS. The rubber-stamp
        literature concerns bodies with multiple members, social dynamics, reputational incentives
        and a management counterparty. C2A2's gate is one person with finite hours who is also the
        system's owner and beneficiary. What transfers is the DIAGNOSTIC (a near-zero rejection
        rate is informative about the gate). What does not transfer is the CAUSAL STORY (capture,
        deference, social cost of dissent). PREMISE-121 already names the n=1 workflow-mismatch
        residual for the clinical literature; the same residual applies here and is not reduced by
        adding a second poorly-fitting analogue.
    (f) A NON-OPERATING GATE IS NOT THE SAME OBSERVATION AS A RATIFYING GATE, AND CONFLATING THEM
        WOULD BE AN ERROR. Twelve days of silence is consistent with (i) the gate contributing
        nothing, (ii) the reviewer being unavailable for reasons entirely unrelated to the gate's
        value, and (iii) PREMISE-121's degradation mechanism, where the queue's own size is what
        stopped the gate. These are three different worlds and the silence alone does not
        discriminate. What DOES discriminate is downstream outcome: over the dark period, did any
        unadjudicated item cause an observable harm? That is the measurement 14b's risk statement
        is really pointing at, and it is available now and not later.
