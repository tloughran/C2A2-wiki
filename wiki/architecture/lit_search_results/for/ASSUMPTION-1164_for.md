  Strength of support: Moderate
    (Strong for the philosophical limb; Weak-to-None for the empirical limb.)

  Summary: On the philosophical limb the claim is well corroborated and is close to
    consensus across three of the four major post-positivist appraisal frameworks. Lakatos
    makes generativity the explicit criterion — a programme is progressive iff it keeps
    predicting novel facts and its positive heuristic keeps producing new refutable
    variants, and the accumulated hard core is precisely what does *not* count. Kuhn makes
    the paradigm's constitutive function the definition of which puzzles exist. Laudan
    makes the unit of progress the problem rather than the true proposition. Firestein,
    the source the assumption rests on, states the thesis directly. On the empirical limb
    the picture is much thinner: I found no study that measures rate or quality of
    question-generation and tests it as a predictor of a field's subsequent productivity.
    The nearest empirical work measures *combinatorial novelty* of published papers and
    finds it predicts long-run top-percentile citation and follow-on research, which is a
    proxy for opening question-space rather than a direct test.

  Caveats:
    - Laudan is only a partial ally. His fundamental unit is the *solved* problem, and
      overall problem-solving effectiveness nets solved problems against generated
      anomalies. A field that generated many questions and solved none would score badly
      on Laudan's criterion. Read strictly, Laudan supports "problems, not truths" more
      than he supports "questions generated, not facts banked."
    - Lakatos's criterion is novel *predictions that are then tested and confirmed*, not
      question-count. Generativity in his sense is inseparable from subsequent empirical
      success. An operationalisation that scores only question-generation, with no
      corroboration term, is not Lakatos's criterion.
    - Firestein is a trade book by a working neuroscientist, not a peer-reviewed
      philosophical or empirical contribution. Its evidential weight is as a well-articulated
      statement of the thesis, not as a test of it.
    - The empirical proxy (combinatorial novelty) measures novelty of *published output*,
      not of questions posed. Wang/Veugelers/Stephan's own headline finding is that
      standard indicators mis-measure novelty, which limits how far any bibliometric
      operationalisation of "question generation" can be trusted.
    - Domain transfer: all of this literature concerns human scientific communities over
      decade timescales. Applying it to a PRS Problem slot in an agentic wiki pipeline is
      a substantial extrapolation that none of these sources licenses.
    - The selection path of Firestein as the corroborating source is not assessed here;
      it is routed to PRESUMPTION-851. If that selection path is compromised, the
      philosophical support above still stands independently on Lakatos, Kuhn and Laudan,
      which were reached by direct search rather than through the original citation.
