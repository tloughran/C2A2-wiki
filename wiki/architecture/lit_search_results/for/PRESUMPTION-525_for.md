  Summary: The presumption is strongly supported by the general logic of convergent evidence: agreement is probative only to the extent the agreeing sources are independent, and correlated sources (shared premises, shared milieu, collaboration) should be discounted toward a single datum. This is the same independence principle the vault already applies to 15a/15b (PREMISE-111) and to reproduction-is-not-replication (PREMISE-120). Applying it to McGilchrist/Kastrup — who share an intellectual milieu — is consistent and warranted: their agreement cannot be counted at full weight as "independent convergence" without checking premise-independence.

  Caveats: Sharing a milieu does not *prove* dependence; two collaborators can still reach a conclusion by genuinely distinct arguments. The correct output is a required independence check, not an automatic discount to zero.
