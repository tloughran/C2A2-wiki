    1. Nisbett, R.E. & Wilson, T.D., 1977. "Telling more than we can know: Verbal reports on mental processes." Psychological Review 84(3):231-259. — The deepest theoretical grounding: subjects produce confident, fluent, plausible accounts of their own processing that are demonstrably not the actual causes of their behaviour. The accounts are not lies; they are constructions. Applied here: an agent's narration of what it did and counted is a construction, and its fluency is not evidence of its accuracy. Regulators, enterprise procurement and courts "treat self-reported testing differently from third-party verified results," and "marketing claims, internal testing summaries, or whitepapers" are increasingly held insufficient. An agent's unmarked figure sits at the bottom of this hierarchy — it is internal, self-generated, and unattested.   Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is strongly supported and the support is unusually convergent, because three separate traditions have each concluded that a producer's account of its own process is not evidence about that process. Nisbett and Wilson establish it for cognition; audit doctrine codifies it as an evidence hierarchy with self-generated internal records ranked lowest; security engineering establishes that a self-written log lacking external binding is categorically not forensic evidence. The 2026 agent-observability literature translates this into a requirement C2A2 measurably fails: the standard trajectory model has a distinct tool-and-service view recording call inputs, outputs, timestamps and status, and it exists because the model's own narration cannot substitute for it. The contemporary benchmark work supplies the base rate that makes the gap consequential — agents do claim completions they did not perform, at rates high enough to be measured. The honest conclusion is the one 14b reaches: with no tool-output channel, unmarked figures in this pipeline are assertions, and no amount of care by the asserting agent changes their evidential class.
