  Sources:
    1. Grech, J. G., O.D.C. (n.d., c. 1950s). "The Dark Night of the Ascent of Mount Carmel." Melita
       Theologica 6(2). Univ. of Malta OAR. [fetched, full text] A Carmelite commentator reads the Ascent
       and Dark Night (which he treats as "one complete work") as a single upward journey to "the high
       estate of perfection". He quotes the Argument: the stanzas describe "the manner of ascending to the
       summit of the Mount". He states outright that "there is no standstill: the soul must go always
       forward and onward in perfection." This is direct support: in its classical Thomist-Carmelite
       reception, John's framework encodes normative, irreversible ascent. Note that the means of ascent
       is privation ("night"). Descent is built into the ascent as its mechanism and is not a separate
       axis.
    2. Underhill, E., 1911. Mysticism. Via the Gan (Monash PhD, 2017) abstract and search summaries.
       [fetched (abstract only) + search-result] Underhill expands the triple way (purgation, illumination,
       union) to five ordered stages (conversion, purgation, illumination, surrender/dark night, union).
       The ordinal structure is explicit. Gan reads it through Hegelian dialectic, which is suggestive of
       non-linear movement but was not read beyond the abstract.
    3. Teresa of Ávila, Interior Castle. Via search-result paraphrase and the CCEL text listing.
       [search-result] The seven mansions are traditionally mapped onto the purgative/illuminative/unitive
       ascent (support). Search summaries also surface Teresa's own remark that faculties which have "gone
       out of the castle" for "days and years" come back but do not readily re-enter, "because the habits
       they have formed are hard to conquer". That is primary-text evidence that the scheme admits exit and
       regression. Exact chapter location NOT verified this pass.
    4. John of the Cross, Dark Night II.18–20 ("This secret wisdom is also a ladder"; ten degrees of the
       mystic ladder). [search-result: chapter titles only] The relevant commentary text, which the
       counter-reading would need (ascending and descending on the same ladder; humiliation and exaltation
       paired), was NOT retrieved. Nothing is attributed to it beyond its chapter titles.
    5. Rohr, R., 2011. Falling Upward. [carried from cycle 0, not re-read] Entry to the second half of life
       is through falling. This is the counter-instance inside Pathway 35's own source.

  Strength of support: Moderate for "classical contemplative frameworks are received as normative ascent"
    (one full-text scholarly source, consistent with the traditional triple-way mapping). Weak for "by
    construction". The same frameworks build descent (night, privation, falling) into the mechanism of
    ascent, and Teresa's text admits exit/regression.

  Summary: The targeted pass found that classical contemplative stage schemes are not a counter-example to
    "ascending by construction" in their standard reception. Grech's Carmelite reading of the Ascent/Dark
    Night is explicitly directional and irreversible ("always forward and onward"). Underhill's five-stage
    mystic way and Teresa's seven mansions are both ordinal. What separates them from psychometric
    maturity models is that descent or privation is the ENGINE of the ascent rather than a regression from
    it, and Teresa at least admits leaving and failing to re-enter. That matches Rohr's "falling upward".
    The frame stays ascending in its telos, but the route is non-monotone. On that reading a bare
    `Stage: N` would still be read as ranking, consistent with MONITOR-589's stakes note. Located modern
    scholarship arguing that these schemes are non-linear or non-normative was NOT found on this budget.

  Caveats: (i) One full-text source, a mid-century confessional commentary. It is representative of the
    traditional reception, not of critical scholarship. (ii) Teresa and John primary passages are at
    search-result level; chapter references are unverified. (iii) Cloud of Unknowing effectively
    unsearched. (iv) The Carmelite literature is large and largely in Spanish/Italian; English web search
    under-samples it. (v) The descent-as-mechanism reading is the searcher's synthesis across sources, not
    a claim found stated in one source.
