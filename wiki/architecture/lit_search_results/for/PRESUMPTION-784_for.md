  Strength of support: Moderate to Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is well supported, and the decisive move is definitional rather than empirical. Since Jansen and Bosch, the mainstream position in software architecture has been that an architecture just IS its set of design decisions — from which it follows immediately that "no new tool, prompt or schema" says something about implementation and nothing at all about whether a decision was taken. The decision-threshold frameworks in actual use agree: both the ADR tradition and the one-way/two-way-door distinction key ceremony to reversibility and downstream constraint, and neither offers effort as an exemption criterion anywhere. The knowledge-vaporization literature supplies the cost of getting this wrong and, importantly, its asymmetry — rationale is nearly free to capture at the moment and expensive or impossible to recover afterwards, which is why an entire sub-field exists to reconstruct decisions after the fact. The 2026 preprint work names C2A2's case almost exactly as the "undocumented architectural experiment," where work concludes without producing or invalidating an ADR and the decision is made off the record. Support is Moderate-to-Strong rather than Strong only because the strongest and most specific source is an unreviewed preprint and the definitional argument, while standard, is a normative position in a field with dissenters.

  Caveats: (a) The literature does NOT support "every change needs a DECISION."
