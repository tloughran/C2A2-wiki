    1. Chang, H., 1995. "Circularity and Reliability in Measurement." Perspectives on Science, 3(2), 153–172. / Chang, H., 2004. "Inventing Temperature: Measurement and Scientific Progress." Oxford University Press. — Chang's central argument is that epistemic circularity in measurement is not inherently vicious if approached iteratively and coherentistically. His model of "epistemic iteration" holds that instruments and standards can be mutually refined in a bootstrapping process without collapsing into mere internal consistency — provided each iteration produces genuinely new constraints on the system. This is the most direct philosophical support for the claim that reflexive falsification can be non-circular.

    2. Meehl, P.E., 1978. "Theoretical risks and tabular asterisks: Sir Karl, Sir Ronald, and the slow progress of soft psychology." Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology. / Popper, K., 1959. "The Logic of Scientific Discovery." — The Popperian tradition supports the claim that a test is genuine if it is capable of falsifying the hypothesis — the source of the test (internal or external) is less important than whether the test criterion was specified independently of the outcome. A pre-committed falsification criterion applied to one's own system retains severity if the criterion was genuinely constraining. Rubin (2025, Synthese) nuances this: preregistration does not automatically improve Popperian severity, but pre-commitment to criterion does strengthen the test's independence.

    3. Krantz, Luce, Suppes & Tversky, 1971. "Foundations of Measurement." Academic Press. / Borsboom, D., 2005. "Measuring the Mind." Cambridge University Press. — Measurement validation literature distinguishes internal consistency (coherence within a system) from construct validity (correspondence between the system and an external referent). A self-applied falsifier can establish internal consistency with genuine epistemic value, but construct validity requires some external anchor. The literature supports non-circularity in the internal consistency sense while flagging the construct validity gap.

  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: The philosophical literature on epistemic circularity — particularly Chang's coherentist framework — provides the strongest available support for the claim. Chang argues explicitly that iterative, bootstrapped measurement processes are non-viciously circular provided each iteration introduces genuinely new constraints rather than merely recycling existing assumptions. This maps onto the C2A2 situation: if the falsification criteria were specified independently of the ledger content they are applied to, and if the criteria are capable of returning a genuinely negative verdict, the test has a legitimate form. The measurement validity literature introduces an important distinction: internal consistency (achievable via self-application) versus construct validity (requires external anchor), and the claim's scope should be read in light of this distinction. Pre-registration and pre-commitment literature (Popper, Mayo, Rubin) supports the non-circularity claim conditional on independent criterion specification.

  Caveats: The strongest caveat is from the LLM measurement literature (2025 preprint on self-rating bias): when the same system that generates content also scores it, reliability metrics are inflated and validity claims are non-transferable because they measure coherence of the model's own outputs rather than alignment with an external standard. This is directly relevant to an AI agent applying falsification criteria to a ledger it co-produced. Chang's framework requires genuine new constraints at each iteration; if the C2A2 falsifier was itself produced by the same dyad that produced the ledger, the independence condition may not be fully met. The claim is strongest if the falsification criteria were specified at pipeline design time, not post-hoc.
