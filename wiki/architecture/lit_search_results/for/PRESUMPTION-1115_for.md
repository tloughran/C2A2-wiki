    2. MacKenzie et al., 2003 (Ecology; colonization and local extinction) [search-result], and MacKenzie, Nichols,
       Royle, Pollock, Bailey & Hines, 2006, "Occupancy Estimation and Modeling" (book) [search-result]. Ignoring
       undetected presence biases occupancy, colonization and extinction estimates. Relevance: the bias extends to
       change-over-time claims such as "nothing new".
    3. Wintle, B.A., Kavanagh, R.P., McCarthy, M.A. & Burgman, M.A., 2005. "Estimating and dealing with detectability in
       occupancy surveys for forest owls and arboreal marsupials." J. Wildlife Management 69(3):905–917.
       [search-result] False-absence rates give the survey effort needed to call a species absent at a stated confidence.
       Example: about 18 visits for 90% confidence on owls, about 5 for sugar gliders. Relevance: a minimal
       interpretable statement is "absent with confidence X given effort Y".
    4. Wintle, B.A., Walshe, T.V., Parris, K.M. & McCarthy, M.A., 2012. "Designing occupancy surveys and interpreting
       non-detection when observations are imperfect." Diversity and Distributions. [search-result] Detection
       probability alone is insufficient. The prior probability of presence is also needed to compute P(present |
       not detected). Relevance: a minimal coverage statement needs scope/effort AND some prior.
    5. Garrard, G.E., Bekessy, S.A., McCarthy, M.A. & Wintle, B.A., 2008. "When have we looked hard enough? A novel
       method for setting minimum survey effort protocols for flora surveys." [search-result] Notes that EIA still
       defaults to assuming that a present species will be detected. A related Garrard et al. paper (EIA detectability)
       finds that the effort needed far exceeds the effort typically spent. Relevance: empirical precedent that the
       presumption in PRESUMPTION-1115 is a known, documented failure mode in practice.
    6. Rethlefsen, M.L. et al., 2021. PRISMA-S (16-item extension for reporting literature searches). [search-result]
       Requires naming each database/platform, the exact strategies, and record counts per source. Relevance: the
       documentation analogue. A "nothing new" search result is interpretable only with a list of sources reached. Note
       that PRISMA-S addresses transparency, not null-result inference.

  Strength of support: Strong. The core principle is stated verbatim in a highly cited primary source (fetched abstract)
    and is backed by a quantitative literature on effort-to-infer-absence.

  Summary: Imperfect-detection ecology rejects inferring absence from non-detection unless detection is certain. It
    supplies the machinery (detection probability, repeat surveys, effort-for-confidence) to make a non-detection
    interpretable. Applied practice (EIA) is documented as committing exactly the error this presumption names.
    Combining these sources (Wintle 2012; PRISMA-S), the minimal coverage statement is: which units the instrument could
    see (k of N), the effort spent, and either an estimated detection probability or an explicit "unknown beyond
    scope". Under this literature, a registry seeing 1 of 70 tasks can support no claim about the other 69.

  Transfer caveats: (i) Occupancy models assume closure and repeated visits. C2A2 instruments often make one pass, so
    detection probability can rarely be estimated, only bounded by coverage. (ii) Several C2A2 cases are structural
    zero-coverage (never reached the arXiv page) rather than probabilistic imperfect detection. That makes the case
    stronger, but the statistical machinery is not needed. (iii) PRISMA-S support is analogical.
