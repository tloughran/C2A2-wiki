  Sources:
    1. Campbell, D.T. (1979). "Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change." Evaluation and Program Planning 2(1):67-90. — Source of Campbell's Law: "the more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures and the more apt it will be to distort and corrupt the social processes it is intended to monitor." This is the general theoretical grounding for the presumption's claim that a headline completeness figure, once it is the thing reported, becomes reachable by routes other than the work it was meant to summarise. Campbell's own worked examples (crime statistics reclassified between categories to move clearance rates) are specifically about *classification* as the cheapest corruption route, which is the exact mechanism named in the presumption.
    2. Doran, T., Fullwood, C., Reeves, D., Gravelle, H., & Roland, M. (2008). "Exclusion of Patients from Pay-for-Performance Targets by English Physicians." New England Journal of Medicine 359(3):274-284. — The best-documented real-world case of a coverage metric that *permits exclusion*. Under the UK Quality and Outcomes Framework, physicians could "exception report" patients out of the denominator of a quality indicator, so achievement was measurable against a roster the measured party partly defined. The paper's value here is that it is the study that established the practice is measurable at all and that some practices were exception-reporting at rates far above their peers — i.e. that a self-narrowed denominator is a real, observed, quantified mechanism in a serious accountability regime, not a hypothetical one. Note its overall conclusion is reported honestly under Caveats: median exception reporting was low (~2.8% of available points) with little evidence of *widespread* gaming.
    3. ISO/IEC 27001:2022, clause 6.1.3(d) — Statement of Applicability. — A standards-body referent for a coverage regime that formally permits exclusion. The standard requires the organisation to produce an SoA stating, for every Annex A control, whether it applies, with justification for inclusion *and for exclusion*, plus implementation status. Two features support the presumption directly. First, the standard treats "excluded" as a live category that must be argued for, not merely asserted — i.e. it recognises that the exclusion route to a clean coverage figure needs its own evidentiary burden, which "4 excluded" as a bare count does not discharge. Second, and more sharply, ISO 27001's denominator is *fixed externally* by Annex A's reference control set (93 controls in the 2022 edition), precisely so that the measured party cannot set its own denominator. C2A2's "21" has no equivalent external fixture. The standard's design is thus evidence that the presumption's concern is the one the control-assurance field has already engineered around.
    4. Inozemtseva, L., & Holmes, R. (2014). "Coverage Is Not Strongly Correlated with Test Suite Effectiveness." Proceedings of the 36th International Conference on Software Engineering (ICSE 2014), pp. 435-445 (ACM Distinguished Paper). — Empirical study over five large open-source Java systems (~100 KLOC each, 1000+ test methods) using mutation-based fault detection. Finding: once test-suite *size* is controlled for, the correlation between coverage and effectiveness is low to moderate, and stronger coverage criteria do not yield better insight into suite effectiveness. This is the direct external evidence for the presumption's core assertion that a coverage figure "is reachable by classification as well as by work": a high number is compatible with the underlying assurance work not having been done, because the metric measures reach and not detection. Read alongside Marick, B. (1999), "How to Misuse Code Coverage," Proceedings of the 16th International Conference on Testing Computer Software, whose standing recommendation is that coverage never be used as the primary criterion of completeness but only as a hint for where testing is absent — which is exactly the demotion the presumption implies for "0 uncovered."
    5. Spoor, P., Airey, M., Bennett, C., Greensill, J., & Williams, R. (1996). "Use of the capture-recapture technique to evaluate the completeness of systematic literature searches." BMJ 313(7053):342-343. — Addresses the presumption's sharpest and most specific point: the self-set denominator. The method exists because in a literature search the true total is unknown and cannot be read off the search's own output; completeness must be *estimated* from the overlap between two or more independent ascertainment methods, and the estimate routinely reveals a substantial missed fraction (later applications in this line report captures on the order of 68% of the estimated true total from four large databases). The methodological principle transfers exactly: a count produced by one enumerating pass over a roster is the numerator of an unknown denominator, and the only established way to bound what the enumerator missed is a second, independent enumeration. It also supplies the concrete in-house test — re-enumerate the control roster by an independent route and compare overlap — which is stronger than merely fixing the reader.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: Supported, and EXTERNALLY ANCHORED (Campbell 1979 in Evaluation and Program Planning; Doran et al. 2008 in NEJM; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clause 6.1.3(d); Inozemtseva & Holmes, ICSE 2014; Spoor et al. 1996 in BMJ). The presumption makes two separable claims and both are supported by distinct literatures. On "reachable by classification as well as by work," Inozemtseva and Holmes is direct empirical evidence that a coverage figure and the assurance it implies come apart once suite size is controlled, and Campbell's Law plus the QOF exception-reporting case supply the mechanism and a real accountability regime in which the exclusion route was actually exercised and measured. On the self-set denominator, the capture-recapture literature is the strongest support available: it exists as a method only because a single enumerating pass cannot establish its own completeness, and the standard remedy is an independent second enumeration rather than a repair of the first. ISO/IEC 27001 then provides the constructive contrast that makes the presumption's worry concrete: an established control-coverage regime permits exclusions but fixes the denominator externally in Annex A and requires per-control justification for each exclusion, neither of which a bare "21 controls - 4 excluded" satisfies. The presumption's most damaging observation — that the enumerating component is where the day's failure was located — is not itself addressed by any literature found, but it is the exact condition under which capture-recapture reasoning says the single-pass count is uninformative rather than merely imprecise.

  Caveats: (a) Doran et al. 2008 cuts partly against the strong reading and is reported here as it stands: their headline conclusion was that exception-reporting rates were generally *low* (median ~2.8% of available points) with little evidence of widespread gaming. It establishes that the exclusion route exists, is measurable and is used unevenly; it does not establish that it is typically abused. Cited as mechanism evidence, not as prevalence evidence. (b) Inozemtseva & Holmes is about code coverage in Java systems with mutation-based fault proxies; the transfer to a governance control roster is by analogy on the structure of the metric (reach vs. effect), not by domain. Mutation score as an effectiveness proxy is itself contested in the testing literature. (c) The ISO/IEC 27001 clause reference was verified via multiple independent secondary descriptions of clause 6.1.3(d), not by reading the standard text, which is paywalled. Under PREMISE-132 this citation is at exactly the epistemic level the sibling presumption PRESUMPTION-573 is about, and it is flagged as such rather than presented as fully verified; the clause number and the requirement's substance are consistent across sources but the wording is not quoted. (d) Campbell's Law is a widely cited aphorism from a program-evaluation essay, not an experimental result; it frames the mechanism rather than demonstrating it. (e) Capture-recapture assumes independence between the two ascertainment routes; two enumerations sharing the same broken reader would violate that assumption and would produce a falsely reassuring overlap — a live risk here, and the specific thing an in-house re-enumeration would need to avoid. (f) None of the sources shows that any of the 21 controls is in fact miscounted, that any of the 4 exclusions is unjustified, or that uncovered controls exist. They support the narrower claim that "0 uncovered" against a self-enumerated denominator does not carry the assurance it appears to, especially when the enumerator is known to have failed. (g) Search scope: moderate-to-comprehensive. Five targeted searches plus two citation-verification searches. Not searched: the audit-sampling literature on population completeness testing, and DO-178C structural-coverage justification practice for dead/deactivated code, both of which are likely to add support.
