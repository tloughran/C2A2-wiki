  Strength of support: Weak

  Summary: The literature supports a considerably weaker proposition than the one stated.
    A single well-chosen, topic-matched channel does capture the large majority of
    retrievable material — MEDLINE alone reached 92.3% coverage over 120 systematic
    reviews, and subject-specific databases add unique records only when the topic matches
    their specialism — and the field accepts capture-recapture stopping rules, so
    "coverage sufficient" is a declarable state in principle. The impact of what is missed
    is also not reliably demonstrable: Schmucker et al. found the effect of unretrieved and
    grey material on pooled estimates to be unpredictable rather than uniformly
    conclusion-changing. What the literature does not supply is any warrant for the
    specific move in the assumption: declaring coverage *complete* on a substitute channel
    precisely because the primary channel is known unreadable. Nor did two dedicated
    queries locate any validation study of an institutional lab web page as an
    authoritative and complete roster of group members.

  Caveats:
    - The same sources that supply the supporting numbers report median single-database
      *recall* of only 71–76%, materially lower than coverage, and note that 16% of
      included references were found in only one database. The supportive reading rests on
      the coverage figure; the recall figure is the more conservative measure of the same
      quantity.
    - The 92.3% and 83–91% figures are medians over reviews. They license a statement about
      the expected case, not about any particular case, and specifically not about a case
      selected *because* its primary channel failed. Channel failure is not independent of
      the content the channel would have carried.
    - The 94% MEDLINE-alone figure is topic-specific (depression) and was encountered as a
      secondary report. It should not be transferred to a different domain without
      verification of the primary source.
    - Domain transfer is the dominant risk. All the quantitative work above concerns
      bibliographic databases indexing published literature with high mutual overlap. A
      laboratory's own web page and whatever channel it substitutes for are not
      overlapping indexes of the same corpus; the overlap statistics that make single-source
      substitution tolerable in systematic review do not obviously obtain.
    - Encountered incidentally rather than sought: PRISMA 2020 keeps "reports not retrieved"
      as a separate line in the flow diagram from "records screened and excluded," so the
      reporting standard in this field treats a blocked channel and a searched channel as
      distinct accounting categories. This bears directly on the assumption and is recorded
      here for completeness of scope; it is not offered as supporting evidence.
    - "A real zero, not a search failure" is a claim about the *mechanism* of the null.
      Nothing in the retrieved literature licenses inferring mechanism from a substitute
      channel's silence.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED
