  Strength of support: Weak

  Summary: Indexing speed is the presumption's strongest point. Web and Scholar-style search pick up new scholarly items faster than curated databases, and arXiv's source lag is days, which is short compared with a 30-day window. So for work posted on arXiv or prominent web venues more than about a week before the search, discovery is plausible. No source supports the stronger inference that a *null* result is reliable evidence of absence. No study was found that measures recall of general web search for one author's recent outputs over a short window. Studies of retrieval quality emphasise poor recall control and poor reproducibility.

  Caveats: (a) Recall of a general web search tool (not Scholar) for name-plus-recent queries is unmeasured. Ranking may bury new items under older, highly linked pages. (b) Thinkers in the C2A2 traditions publish in books, podcasts, talks, blogs and journals with embargoes, which have different indexing behaviour. (c) Items posted in the last few days of the window are likely to be missed (see ASSUMPTION-1739). (d) Evidence is about Google Scholar and does not transfer directly to the search API the agent actually uses.
