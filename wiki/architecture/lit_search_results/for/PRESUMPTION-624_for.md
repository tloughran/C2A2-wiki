    1. Sen, I., Flöck, F., Weller, K., Weiss, B. & Wagner, C., 2021. "A Total Error Framework for Digital Traces of Human Behavior on Online Platforms" (TED-On). Public Opinion Quarterly; arXiv:1907.08228. — Formalises coverage error (the platform user population is selective with respect to the target population) and unit error (the observed unit is an account, not a person) as first-class error terms in trace-based measurement. Communication Methods and Measures, 2024 (Taylor & Francis 10.1080/19312458.2024.2393165). — Empirical study of multi-device undercoverage: the true value of a behavioural variable is the sum across ALL devices an individual uses, so single-channel instrumentation systematically undercounts.   Strength of support: None

  Summary: I found no literature supporting the proposition that an activity metric computed from a single observation channel can, without further argument, support claims about activity in general. The relevant literatures — total error frameworks for digital traces, web-tracking undercoverage, passive-data measurement of media diets, and developer-productivity metrics — converge on the opposite: coverage error and unit error are named, expected, and typically large, and the standard remedy is either multi-channel instrumentation or an explicit statement of what the channel cannot see. Two findings sharpen the concrete instance. First, undercoverage is defined precisely as the gap between one instrument's reach and the sum of the subject's activity across all channels, which is what an unmounted corpus creates by construction. Second, the operationalisation literature shows that even WITHIN a single well-instrumented channel, the choice of what counts as an event changes the indicator materially — so "autonomous day" is doubly a property of the instrument. The one partial concession available to the FOR direction: single-channel proxies are treated as acceptable in the passive-sensing literature when a validation study against a reference measure has been run and coverage shown to be high. No such validation exists for the autonomy series, and the eight-file report is the first observation of the reference channel.

  Caveats: (a) The absence of support is for the general claim; single-channel metrics are routinely and defensibly used for WITHIN-CHANNEL claims ("no interactive Cowork session appears in the transcripts" is fully supported by the transcripts). The unsupported step is the relabelling to "autonomous day." (b) None of the sources concerns filesystem mtimes or agent-pipeline telemetry specifically; all transfer is by structural analogy to survey/trace measurement and software metrics. (c) The developer-productivity material is the closest domain match but the peer-reviewed portion of it is thinner than the trace-measurement material; much of what this search surfaced was practitioner commentary, which I have not cited. (d) Per 14b's own note, one sentence attributing eight file rewrites to the human is not itself a measure of the size of the out-of-channel work; the literature bounds the DIRECTION of the bias, not its magnitude here. (e) 14b marked this item as testable in-house rather than by literature; the in-house test (mount the Summa vault, compare mtimes across the 27-day series) would settle the magnitude question that literature cannot.

  Search scope: Comprehensive on digital-trace total-error frameworks, coverage/undercoverage in tracking data, and log-versus-self-report divergence; adequate on activity-proxy validity in software engineering and passive sensing. Not searched: sensor-coverage and missing-data literature in wearables/actigraphy, and the ecological "detection probability" literature (occupancy modelling), which formalises exactly the "absence of observation is not observation of absence" problem and would strengthen any downstream synthesis. Broader search recommended.
