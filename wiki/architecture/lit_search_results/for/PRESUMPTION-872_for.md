  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: Support for this presumption exists but is conditional in a consistent and specific way across every source that provides it. Three independent standards regimes accept records of the general kind a conversation transcript is: the EU AI Act's Article 12 makes system-generated application-level logs the mandated record of a high-risk AI system's operation; NIST SP 800-92 treats application logs as first-class audit sources rather than as a fallback; and FDA/GxP data-integrity practice under ALCOA+ accepts contemporaneous narrative records, including electronic lab notebooks, as fully adequate regulated records of what was done. W3C PROV and its PROV-AGENT extension show that agent activity narratives are expressible in an accepted provenance model, so there is no categorical bar to a transcript-shaped record. Most directly, Xing et al. (2026) document that courts, auditors and regulators are already relying on LLM conversation records to attribute responsibility and reconstruct decision-making — the transcript is being used as the record in practice. What none of these sources supplies is unconditional support: each attaches integrity conditions (automatic generation, contemporaneity, attributability, tamper-evidence, completeness), and both 2026 agent-specific papers exist precisely because those conditions are not satisfied by an unaugmented transcript.

  Caveats: (a) Every supporting standard is conditional, and the conditions are not free. Article 12 requires *automatic* recording; ALCOA+ requires attributability, contemporaneity, originality and tamper-resistance with validated systems; NIST 800-92 assumes controlled log integrity. A transcript that can be regenerated, branched, edited or selectively exported satisfies contemporaneity and attribution but not originality or tamper-evidence — which is the entire motivation of the VCT paper. (b) Xing et al. support the claim's *descriptive* half (transcripts are used as records) while their stated problem is its *normative* half (whether they can be relied on), so the source must not be cited as unqualified endorsement. (c) Wang et al.'s survey supports trace-based accountability but specifies a trace as spanning tool outputs, memory items and environment observations — a conversation transcript qualifies only to the extent it actually contains those, which is an empirical property of a given system's transcript format, not a general one. (d) Domain-transfer risk on the ALCOA+ analogy is real: an ELN is authored by a human accountable under a quality system and countersigned; the analogy weakens where the author of the narrative and the subject of the narrative are the same non-accountable process. (e) The FDA and NIST sources were read via secondary summaries and landing pages rather than originals.

Every supportive standard located presupposes that the record is generated by a mechanism independent of, or at least not authored by, the actor whose conduct is being recorded (automatic recording under Art. 12; validated instrument capture under ALCOA+; system-emitted application logs under 800-92; Activity records attributed to but not composed by the Agent under PROV). No source found in the supportive direction affirms that narration authored by the agent about the agent constitutes an adequate activity record. This sub-claim is the load-bearing one for C2A2 and it is unaddressed here; treat as novel pending the against-direction search.
