  Summary: The corrective proposition is well grounded in general and adequately grounded in this specific domain. Northcutt et al. is the load-bearing citation: designated ground truth in mature, heavily-used benchmarks carries error rates of 3-10%, so "reference" is a role and not a guarantee, and even modest reference error rates can reorder model rankings. Applied here, that means a repair pipeline comparing two derived artefacts has no principled basis for a fixed error direction. The ASR error-correction corpus corroborates the *first* half of 14b's finding decisively and, I think, more strongly than 14b claims: the directional assumption is not just an interpretive habit but is encoded in how training pairs are synthesised (weak-ASR generation, TTS-ASR cascades, edits applied to references). A model trained that way is structurally incapable of proposing that the reference is wrong. The one source that would directly establish direction reversal in ASR — human references scoring worse than machine transcripts — is snippet-only and unattributed, and is the weakest link in this file.
