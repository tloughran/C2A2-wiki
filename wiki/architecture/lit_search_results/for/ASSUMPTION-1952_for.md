[snippet only, secondary]. Kahneman's format has scientists who disagree agree in advance on the positions to test and on the standards for deciding which side is supported, with a neutral arbiter (Mellers) who co-designs and co-authors. Clark and Tetlock's Penn project later scaled this up with multilab studies, special issues and a meta-study of outcomes. Relevance: direct precedent for (a). Rivals jointly signing off on a result is attested as workable, but in a facilitated, project-bounded setting.
    2. Tetlock & Mitchell, as summarised in the same results; Isch, Tetlock & Clark 2025, Theory and Society (participant survey). [snippet only]. Per the snippets, collaboration is hardest when falsification criteria are unclear, when methods are disputed, and when there is background intellectual or political tribalism. The 2025 paper is described as the first systematic assessment of the method by participants. Relevance: conditional support. Joint certification works, but only under conditions that cross-worldview disputes often lack.
    3. MacIntyre secondary literature: New Blackfriars (Cambridge) issue on MacIntyre and narrative rationality; "In defense of the rationality of traditions" (Canadian Journal of Philosophy, via Cambridge Core); Nottingham eprint 13930 (MacIntyre and Lindbeck on tradition). [snippet only, secondary]. Rational enquiry is carried on from within a tradition, and a tradition is judged by its own standards. A rival prevails when it solves the problems that stalled the other, explains why the other failed, and keeps continuity with it. Relevance: theoretical grounding for (b), since a result should satisfy each tradition's own standards. But see Caveats: one snippet states MacIntyre does not require the defeated tradition's members to recognise the defeat.

  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: Adversarial collaboration is a documented, repeated practice in which rival researchers agree criteria in advance and co-author the outcome, so mutual sign-off by opposed practitioners is workable in principle. MacIntyre's account supports judging claims against each tradition's internal standards, which gives the dual-certification rule a theoretical rationale. The support is for facilitated, bounded projects with a neutral arbiter, not for a standing gate on every output. The literature found names the conditions under which joint resolution is hardest (unclear criteria, contested methods, tribalism). Those conditions fit a physicalist/non-physicalist dialogue.

  Caveats:
    - Adversarial collaborations certify a jointly designed study, not a stream of individual concessions or answers. Moving from one to the other is an extrapolation.
    - A neutral arbiter is a standard feature of the format. DECISION-084 as stated names none.
    - MacIntyre (per the secondary snippet) does not make rival recognition a precondition of rational defeat. That weakens the claim that certification by both sides is necessary, though it is consistent with it being sufficient or desirable.
    - All sources are snippets of secondary material. The primary texts were not fetched.
