  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: Expertise studies treat competent-practitioner status as conferred by recognition from existing practitioners, and even the leading test (the imitation game) is disputed. This supports the item's point that "mature members" cannot simply be assumed identifiable. The peer-review literature shows that gates needing expert sign-off strain on recruitment and lengthen delays even when the pool is large and the incentives are institutional. A gate that needs two such sign-offs from opposed traditions, with no named certifiers, would be expected to face these pressures at least as much. The literature therefore supports the concern that the gate needs an explicit recruitment and capacity path.

  Caveats:
    - Peer-review strain is an analogy. The scale, incentives and pool size differ from a single-designer research system.
    - Fox et al. found reviewer fatigue was not the primary cause of declining acceptance, so the mechanism of strain is unsettled.
    - Nothing found addresses dual (rival-side) sign-off specifically, or the independence of certifiers from the outputs they certify.
    - Adversarial-collaboration recruitment practice was not searched separately.
