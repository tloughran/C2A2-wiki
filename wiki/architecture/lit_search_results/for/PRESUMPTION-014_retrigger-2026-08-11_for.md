    1. Mitchell, M., 2026. "On Evaluating Abstraction and Analogy in Humans and Machines." Current Directions in Psychological Science (SAGE), doi 10.1177/09637214261433528. — NEW. Current authoritative review of how to tell genuine abstraction from surface pattern-matching in machines; establishes that the structural/surface distinction is empirically testable rather than merely rhetorical, and sets the methodological bar for claiming structural signal.
    2. Zitnik Lab (Harvard Medical School), 2026. "Exploring Relational Reasoning Capabilities in LLMs with REL." ICML 2026. — NEW. Builds a benchmark with *explicit control over the underlying relational structure* — precisely the missing control in earlier work. Supports the premise that relational structure in LLM outputs is a measurable, manipulable variable, not an artifact of surface overlap.
    3. Musker, S., et al. 2024/2026. "Semantic Structure-Mapping in LLM and Human Analogical Reasoning." (ResearchGate 381580723; related journal version in J. Memory & Language, "LLMs as models for analogical reasoning," 2025.) — Advanced LLMs match human performance across many analogy task variations, consistent with genuine semantic structure-mapping rather than lexical association.
    4. "The Curious Case of Analogies: Investigating Analogical Reasoning in Large Language Models." arXiv 2511.20344. — NEW. Directly supportive mechanism: successful analogical reasoning is strongly associated with higher *token-level structural alignment* between source and target; structural alignment functions as an internal signal predicting analogical success. This is the closest thing found to a measurable discriminator between structural and surface signal.
    5. "Modelling Analogies and Analogical Reasoning: Connecting Cognitive Science Theory and NLP Research." Transactions of the ACL, 2026 (MIT Press, TACL.a.632). — NEW.   Summary: The supporting base has moved meaningfully since April 2026. Where cycle 1 relied on 1990s human-cognition classics, the 2026 literature supplies direct machine-side evidence: relational structure in LLM analogical output is measurable, manipulable under controlled benchmarks, and correlates with an identifiable internal signal (structural alignment). This upgrades the plausibility that cross-tradition signals emitted by LLM agents can be structural rather than merely lexical. It does not establish that C2A2's particular signals *are* structural — that remains an unmeasured empirical claim about this system. The literature supplies the instruments; C2A2 has not yet applied them.

  Caveats: The same 2026 literature is explicit that LLMs are "structurally limited in their ability to perform explicit relational mapping as defined in Structure-Mapping Theory," fall back on surface cues when relational encoding is weak, and are vulnerable to semantic distractors. Support weakens sharply if C2A2 never measures structural alignment on its own cross-tradition signals — the presumption would then be asserting an untested property that the literature says is contingent, not automatic. Domain gap remains: no benchmark covers cross-intellectual-tradition mapping, where the ground truth for "correct structural correspondence" is itself contested.
