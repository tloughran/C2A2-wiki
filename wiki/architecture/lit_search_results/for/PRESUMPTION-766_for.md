  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is well supported and has a twenty-year pedigree in software architecture research under the name "knowledge vaporization." The literature's consistent position is that enactment does not record a decision, that the artefact is not a substitute for the rationale, and that decision records must therefore be produced deliberately and separately. The strongest evidence is indirect but hard to argue with: a research line exists (RecovAr, UnArch) whose entire purpose is to reconstruct architectural decisions from history artefacts after the fact, which presupposes that enacted decisions routinely go unlogged in real projects. C2A2's case is therefore an instance of a documented, common, expensive class rather than an anomaly. Decision-provenance work extends the argument to automated actors specifically, and the 2026 agent-tracing survey restates it as a "process-level accountability gap" — an agent's correct action is compatible with any rationale or none.

  Caveats: (a) The ADR and vaporization literature concerns *human* architects working at a deliberative pace; C2A2's case is an agent closing a candidate as a side effect of doing work, which is a faster and less visible path to the same loss. Whether the remedies transfer — ADRs assume someone notices a decision is being made — is not addressed by anything found here, and I think this is the real open question. (b) Sources 5, 6 and 7 are unverified and 5 in particular I could not attribute; the two empirical rationale studies should not be cited downstream without retrieval. (c) The literature supports "this decision should have been recorded"; it does not support 14b's implicit stronger claim that the *closure was illegitimate*. An agent may have had authority to close by action and still owed a record. Those are separable and the register should keep them separate. (d) Publication bias: the ADR/rationale field is normatively committed to more documentation and does not publish null results about its costs.

  Search scope: Comprehensive on architectural decision records, design rationale and knowledge vaporization. Moderate on decision provenance in autonomous systems. Preliminary on the specific case of decisions closed by agent action inside an agent pipeline — the 2026 preprints found are adjacent rather than on point, and this narrow question may be under-served.
