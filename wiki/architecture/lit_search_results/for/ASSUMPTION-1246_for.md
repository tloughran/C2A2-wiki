  Sources:
    1. FDA two-trials rule, discussed in "Beyond the Two-Trials Rule" (arXiv:2307.04548) and
       "Combining Evidence from Clinical Trials in Conditional or Accelerated Approval"
       (arXiv:2112.11898). — Snippet-level. The regulatory standard requires "at least two adequate and
       well-controlled studies, each convincing on its own" before approval, usually implemented as two
       independent studies each significant at one-sided alpha = 0.025. The stated rationale is
       substantiation — the need for replication of a finding. This is a real, long-standing, and
       consequential n=2 promotion threshold with an articulated justification.
    2. Two-witness rule (US Legal Forms, "Two-Witness Rule: Understanding Its Legal Significance").
       — Snippet-level, secondary legal-reference source. An independent institutional instance of n=2
       as a sufficiency threshold (the constitutional treason clause), showing the convention is not
       peculiar to statistics.
    3. General threshold-tuning literature (Sanction Scanner, "Threshold Tuning and Risk Based Calibration
       in Transaction Monitoring"; Conifers AI glossary on confidence-threshold calibration;
       arXiv:2604.23183 on escalation criteria for AI incident response). — Snippet-level, mixed
       vendor-blog and preprint quality; low weight individually. Consistent message: thresholds are set
       by explicit balancing of investigation cost against missed detection, and should be fixed in
       advance so escalation is consistent.

  Strength of support: Weak to Moderate

  Summary: The two searches turned up more than expected. There is a genuine and well-established basis
    for n=2 as a promotion threshold — the FDA two-trials rule is exactly a rule that a finding is promoted
    to a decision only on a second independent confirmation, and the two-witness rule is an independent
    institutional instance of the same convention. Both rest on the same underlying idea: one observation
    may be an artefact, a second independent one is the minimum that distinguishes signal from artefact.
    That is a real warrant for n=2 in kind. What I did not find is any warrant for n=2 in this specific
    application. The threshold-calibration literature is uniform that thresholds should be derived from an
    explicit cost trade-off between false promotion and missed promotion, and no such derivation exists for
    the pipeline's mention count. The arXiv:2307.04548 title ("Beyond the Two-Trials Rule") also indicates
    the convention is itself under active challenge in its home domain.

  Caveats: The transfer is weak on the point that matters most. The two-trials rule requires each trial to
    be independently convincing AND independent of the other; the pipeline's two mentions may be neither —
    they may be two mentions in the same context, by the same agent, or downstream of one another. The
    monoculture and correlated-error findings recorded under PRESUMPTION-900 apply here directly: two
    non-independent mentions are approximately one mention, and an n=2 rule with no independence check
    approximates an n=1 rule. Second, both borrowed thresholds come from high-cost, low-frequency decision
    settings (drug approval, treason conviction) where the asymmetry favours caution; a wiki intake layer
    has the opposite cost profile, so the same number does not obviously follow. Third, this item was
    searched at two queries only by instruction and should be treated as under-searched.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED

  Residue status: CARRIED, NOT RESOLVED. The searches establish that n=2 is a recognised convention with a
    substantiation rationale, which is more than "arbitrary." They do NOT establish that n=2 is calibrated
    for this pipeline, and the applicable calibration literature says the number should fall out of a
    cost trade-off that has not been performed here. Recommended carry-forward question, cheaper than
    further literature search: does the pipeline's promotion rule check that the two mentions were
    independent? If not, the threshold's nominal value is not the operative one.
