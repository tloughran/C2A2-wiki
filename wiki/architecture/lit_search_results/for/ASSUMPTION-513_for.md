  Sources:
    1. Grol, R. & Grimshaw, J. (2003). "From best evidence to best practice: effective implementation of change in patients' care." The Lancet 362:1225-1230. — Foundational: valid evidence does not implement itself; changing practice requires deliberate, active implementation strategies. Passive dissemination reliably fails.
    2. Morris, Z.S., Wooding, S. & Grant, J. (2011). "The answer is 17 years, what is the question: understanding time lags in translational research." J R Soc Med 104(12):510-520. — Source of the ~17-year figure; documents the translation gap AND (relevant to the scope-guard) that the lag is a property of large multi-stage clinical pipelines, not a universal constant.
    3. Balas, E.A. & Boren, S.A. (2000). "Managing clinical knowledge for health care improvement." Yearbook of Medical Informatics. — Classic estimate that only ~14% of original research reaches practice, and only after long delay; the default fate of a finding is non-adoption.
    4. Knowledge-translation reviews (e.g., ConductScience / CMAJ knowledge-translation syntheses, 2006-2012) estimating >$200B in wasted funding attributable to research that never reaches practice. — Establishes the know-do gap as a robust, cross-domain empirical regularity.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The core assumption — that a validated finding requires an explicit carrier to reach the practice/agent it governs — is strongly and independently supported across health-services and implementation science. The literature's central lesson is precisely that evidence does not propagate on its own: dissemination failure is the modal outcome absent an active implementation mechanism. The 17-year figure (Morris et al.) both supplies the assumption's own scope-guard and confirms that the *magnitude* of lag scales with pipeline complexity — so the qualitative claim (no propagation without a mechanism) transfers to C2A2 even though the quantitative lag does not.

  Caveats: The clinical literature concerns human organizations with many hand-offs; C2A2 is a single-maintainer, file-based system, so the *size* of the gap is not comparable (the assumption already concedes this). What transfers is the structural claim, not the timescale.

  Recommendation: SUPPORTED
