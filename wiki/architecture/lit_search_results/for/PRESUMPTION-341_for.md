    1. MacPherson & Sherif, 2020. "Effects of using structured templates for recalling chemistry experiments." Chemistry Education Research and Practice (PMC4759737). — Controlled study showing that structured templates significantly improve completeness of factual information captured and reduce idiosyncratic variation between recorders. Templates prompt capture of specified items that free-form recording misses, supporting the claim that a well-designed template constrains sole-scribe distortion.

    2. Enago Academy / Scribbr, "Observer Bias in Research" (review literature). — Methodological guidance literature identifies standardised observation procedures as the primary mechanism for reducing observer bias in single-observer designs. Standardised, clear procedures promote consistency and reduce the effect of individual recorder perspective.

    3. O'Connor & Joffe, 2020. "Intercoder Reliability in Qualitative Research." International Journal of Qualitative Methods (Sage, 1609406919899220). — Establishes that once a validated coding frame exists, full-corpus coding by a single coder is widely accepted practice across qualitative disciplines. The critical validity condition is the protocol quality, not the number of coders executing it.

  Strength of support: Moderate

  Summary: The literature offers moderate support for the conditional form of this claim: when the scribing protocol is highly structured, uses verbatim or checklist-style templates, and is contemporaneous, sole-scribe designs can achieve acceptable reliability. The evidence is strongest for factual/procedural content (lab notes, structured interviews) and weakest for interpretive or evaluative content. The C2A2 claim as stated ("does not shape") is a strong universal form; the literature supports only the weaker "shapes less when protocol is structured." The critical moderator is whether the AI agent's scribing role involves interpretation or selection, not merely transcription — the literature supports the latter condition but not the former.

  Caveats: Studies on template-based recording also note a trade-off: structured templates improve completeness of specified fields but may suppress emergent or unexpected content that free-form recording would capture (MacPherson & Sherif). For AI scribes specifically, there is limited direct evidence; the closest analogues are structured clinical documentation and court transcription. The claim as written ("does not shape") is stronger than the literature supports; "shapes less than unstructured scribing" is what evidence backs.
