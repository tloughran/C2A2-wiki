  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is strongly supported and the software-engineering evidence is unusually direct, because static analysis has been running this experiment at industrial scale for two decades. The consistent finding is that a valid, surfaced finding does not become a fix: 35-91% of warnings go unactioned, a substantial population is explicitly suppressed, developers often do not even record their triage decisions, and each additional low-value alert reduces the chance the next one is read. Google's Tricorder experience converts this into a design rule that maps directly onto C2A2's situation — findings must be delivered into the workflow at the moment of change with a fix path, because a backlog that relies on a later reader does not get drained. The handover literature supplies the same conclusion from an independent direction: knowledge does not survive a shift boundary unless the boundary has a structured protocol, and the failures are attributed to the absence of standardisation rather than to individual carelessness. C2A2's seventh recurrence is what the literature predicts, and the alert-fatigue mechanism predicts the eighth will be even less likely to be caught.

  Caveats: (a) The static-analysis literature's dominant explanation for non-action is *false positives*, which is not C2A2's situation: a retracted trap and a truncated file are true positives, so the fatigue mechanism transfers but the false-positive explanation does not. The support is therefore partly for the right conclusion via a mechanism that may not apply, and 15b may reasonably press on this. (b) None of these sources measures fix rates for *true* positives specifically in a low-false-positive setting, which is the case that would be most decisive. I judge this an actual gap. (c) Sources 4 and 5 are cited from my own domain knowledge rather than from this session's searches and were not re-verified; they are well-known papers but the specific claims attributed to them should be checked. (d) The handover analogy is loose — clinical handover concerns patient state, not defect backlogs — and the interventions (standardised verbal protocols, read-back) do not obviously translate to agents. (e) Publication bias: the static-analysis community has an interest in explaining non-adoption in terms of tool design rather than organisational incentives.

  Search scope: Comprehensive on static-analysis warning actionability and suppression. Moderate on alert fatigue and on clinical/industrial handover failure. Preliminary on fix rates for confirmed true-positive defects surfaced without a gate — which I believe is genuinely under-studied and is the sharpest version of C2A2's question.
