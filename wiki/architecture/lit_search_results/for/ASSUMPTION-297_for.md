  Strength of support: Moderate
  Summary: The structured-consensus literature supports two components of the rule: (a) defining an explicit stopping rule in advance is itself a recognized methodological virtue that most consensus studies fail to meet, and (b) the "revisit" step approximates the stability criterion (consistency of positions across successive passes) that Delphi methodologists recommend over raw consensus. Candidate exhaustion as a sufficiency condition has no direct analogue, but it resembles "saturation" criteria in qualitative methods (Guest, Bunce & Johnson 2006 line). What the literature does not support is adequacy without a stability check separated in time — a single same-session revisit can reflect anchoring rather than stable agreement.
  Caveats: Delphi stopping rules assume independent anonymous rounds; a live dyad lacks anonymity, so closure can encode social momentum. Adequacy claim transfers only if the revisit is genuinely capable of reopening items (ties to ASSUMPTION-295) and ideally occurs after a temporal gap (ties to PRESUMPTION-333).
