  Sources:
    1. Goodhart 1975 / Strathern 1997 ('Improving Ratings'). - When a measure becomes a target it ceases to be a good measure; guarding against this requires not rewarding the proxy (productivity) as the goal (usefulness).
    2. Campbell 1979. 'Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change.' (Campbell's Law) - The more a quantitative indicator is used for decision-making, the more it is subject to corruption pressures.
    3. Mayo 2018; Popper 1959. - Asymmetric (falsificationist) testing: a clean FAIL is informative; a PASS licenses only 'not yet refuted / necessary condition met', never proof. The asymmetry is the correct epistemic stance.
    4. Measurement validity: necessary-vs-sufficient condition framing prevents treating a passed proxy as established usefulness.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: Both halves of the assumption are well grounded. The usefulness != productivity firewall is a direct application of Goodhart's/Campbell's law - rewarding the productivity proxy as if it were the usefulness target is exactly the failure mode those laws describe. The asymmetric test (strong FAIL, provisional necessary-condition PASS) is the standard falsificationist/severe-testing posture, which the literature endorses as the correct way to avoid confirmation by a gameable proxy.

  Caveats: Asymmetry reduces but does not eliminate Goodhart pressure: necessary conditions can themselves be gamed, and meta-gaming of the test design remains possible (Biagioli/Lippman, 'Gaming the Metrics'). Operationalizing 'usefulness' non-circularly is the hard, unfinished part.
