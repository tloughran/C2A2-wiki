  Sources:
    1. Rosenthal, R. (1979). "The 'File Drawer Problem' and Tolerance for Null Results." *Psychological
       Bulletin* 86(3), 638–641. — The structural analogue the dispatch asks for, and it is a good one.
       Rosenthal's framing — journals filled with the studies that found something, file drawers filled
       with those that did not — is the description of a collection apparatus whose visible output is
       conditioned on non-nullity. The consequence he draws is the one that matters for C2A2: the
       *corpus* becomes unreliable in a way that cannot be detected from inside it, because the missing
       zeros leave no trace. A pipeline whose runs avoid returning zero produces the same defect in its
       own accumulated record.
    2. Scheel, A. M., Schijen, M. R. M. J. & Lakens, D. (2021). "An Excess of Positive Results: Comparing
       the Standard Psychology Literature With Registered Reports." *Advances in Methods and Practices in
       Psychological Science* 4(2). — The strongest empirical source, and it quantifies the pressure
       rather than merely asserting it. Positive results for the first hypothesis appeared in 96% of 152
       comparable standard articles but only 44% of 71 registered reports. The difference is the size of
       the distortion introduced by conditioning publication on outcome — and, critically for this item,
       it was produced *by changing when the decision to publish is made*, not by asking authors to be
       more candid. This is direct support for the claim that yield-conditioned legitimacy is a
       structural property with a large effect, and that structural remedies work.
    3. Chambers, C. D. et al. — Registered Reports as a format (Center for Open Science, cos.io/rr;
       "Reducing publication bias with Registered Reports," *Nature Neuroscience* 27, 2024). — The design
       principle stated as a principle: acceptance is granted on the basis of the question and the method,
       before the result is known, so a null outcome cannot retroactively invalidate the work. This is
       null-output legitimacy made a designable property of the pipeline, which is precisely the
       construct 14b's item is reaching for. It is well established in research methodology, though under
       that name rather than as a property of automated collectors.
    4. Baxter, D. et al. (2021), "Recommended conventions for reporting results from direct dark matter
       searches," *European Physical Journal C* 81, 907. — The best available evidence that a mature
       field can make the null result the *designed* output. Direct-detection experiments predominantly
       do not detect anything; the discipline's response has been to standardise the reporting form for
       non-detection — 90% confidence upper limits on the cross-section, a median expected sensitivity
       band, and the recommendation that collaborations "publish the smallest observed p-value for the
       background-only hypothesis in addition to an upper limit *in all cases, even if that p-value is
       not significant*," with the publication threshold decided in advance. A run that finds nothing
       has a defined, publishable, career-legitimate output. This is the affirmative existence proof that
       null-output legitimacy is designable, and it shows what the design consists of: a specified
       reporting form for zero, and a threshold fixed before the run.
    5. Venues and initiatives constituting the null result as a legitimate object: *Journal of Articles
       in Support of the Null Hypothesis*; the former *Journal of Negative Results in Biomedicine*;
       *Scientific Reports*' negative-results collection; PLOS ONE's results-independent review criterion.
       [publishing practice] — Corroborates that the problem is recognised as structural and that the
       recognised interventions operate on what the venue will accept, not on the producer's diligence.
    6. Reporting-bias reviews (e.g. Enago/PMC syntheses of the positive-results skew across >4,000
       articles in multidisciplinary journals) — support the generality of the skew across fields, though
       these are secondary syntheses and are cited as corroboration rather than as primary evidence.

  Strength of support: Moderate-to-Strong for the analogy; Moderate for the design construct

  Summary: The structural analogy 14b proposes is sound and is supported by the best-studied case of it.
  Rosenthal's file-drawer framing describes exactly the architecture at issue — a collector whose visible
  output is conditioned on having found something — and identifies the consequence that makes it
  dangerous rather than merely wasteful: the suppressed zeros leave no trace, so the corpus cannot
  diagnose its own distortion from within. Scheel, Schijen and Lakens put a number on the pressure: 96%
  positive results in the standard literature against 44% under registered reports, a gap produced not by
  changing who was doing the work but by moving the accept/reject decision to before the result was
  known. That is the specific form of support this item needs, because it shows the distortion is a
  property of the incentive structure rather than of the honesty of the producer — which is what makes
  "rather than return zero Wright proposals" an intelligible thing for a run to say to itself rather than
  a lapse. On the second limb — whether null-output legitimacy is a designable property — the answer is
  affirmative but the literature comes from research methodology rather than from systems design.
  Registered Reports are the general mechanism (commit to the method before the result). Direct dark
  matter search reporting conventions are the sharper instance, and the more useful one for an automated
  collector: the field has standardised the *form* a null result takes (upper limit, expected-sensitivity
  band, background-only p-value reported in all cases), and has moved the publication threshold to before
  the run. The transferable design lesson is that a zero becomes legitimate when it has a specified
  output shape and a pre-committed threshold, not when the producer is told that zeros are acceptable.

  Caveats: The transfer from publication bias to an automated collector is an analogy and is presented as
  one. The mechanism differs in an important respect: publication bias is driven by gatekeepers and
  career incentives operating on human authors over long horizons, whereas the pressure 14b observed is
  internal to a single run with no obvious selection mechanism to carry it — so the *existence* of the
  pressure in C2A2 is established by the estate's own evidence (the window violation) and not by these
  sources, which establish only that such a pressure is expectable and consequential where output is the
  observable. The 96%/44% figures are from psychology and are not a prior for anything else. The dark
  matter conventions source is the strongest evidence for designability but comes from a field with a
  well-defined background model and a calculable exclusion limit; whether an agent collecting proposals
  can define an analogue of an upper limit — some statement of the form "nothing above threshold X
  exists in window W" — is an open design question that this literature poses well and does not answer.
  I found no literature treating null-output legitimacy as a property of an *automated* collector
  specifically; sources 3 and 4 are human-institutional analogues. That gap is recorded below as a
  NOVELTY-FLAG on the narrow construct, which does not weaken the support for the broad claim. Source 6
  is secondary. Search scope: moderate — covered publication bias and the file-drawer problem,
  registered reports, negative-results venues, and null-result reporting conventions in experimental
  physics. Did NOT cover Goodhart's law and the quota/metric-gaming literature, or the detection-theory
  treatment of decision thresholds under asymmetric payoffs, both of which would speak to the "yield as
  proxy for function" half of the item and remain unsearched.

NOVELTY-FLAG:
  Item: PRESUMPTION-860
  Searched: publication bias / file-drawer problem (Rosenthal 1979; Scheel et al. 2021); registered
    reports as a structural remedy; negative-results venues; null-result reporting conventions in
    direct dark matter detection; general reporting-bias syntheses.
  Finding: The general phenomenon — output conditioned on non-nullity distorts the record, and structural
    pre-commitment corrects it — is very well established for human research institutions. I found no
    literature that treats *null-output legitimacy as an explicit designable property of an automated
    collector*: no source specifies what a zero-return should look like for an autonomous agent, what
    pre-commitment mechanism substitutes for peer review's stage-one acceptance, or how a pipeline would
    distinguish a legitimate zero from a broken run without reintroducing the yield-as-health proxy.
  Implication: The transposition of registered-report logic and null-result reporting conventions into
    the design of autonomous collection agents appears to be an open construct. C2A2 has the two
    ingredients the physics case shows are needed — a specified reporting form for zero and a threshold
    fixed before the run — and stating them as a general design requirement for automated collectors
    would be a contribution rather than a restatement. Note also the reflexive problem the literature
    does not address and C2A2 has: with yield removed as the health signal, the pipeline needs some other
    way to tell a working agent that found nothing from a broken agent that returned nothing — which
    connects this item directly to PRESUMPTION-855.
  Recommended status: PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED with NOVEL sub-construct (null-output legitimacy for automated
    collectors)
