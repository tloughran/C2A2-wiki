  Sources:
    1. Spoor, P., Airey, M., Bennett, C., Greensill, J. & Williams, R., 1996. "Use of the capture-recapture technique to evaluate the completeness of systematic literature searches." BMJ (PMID 8760743). — The originating application: overlap between two independent ascertainment methods yields an estimate of the total population of relevant articles, and hence of the number missed. Establishes that "how much did I not see" is an estimable quantity, not merely a disclosable ignorance.
    2. Kastner, M. et al., 2009. "The capture-mark-recapture technique can be used as a stopping rule when searching in systematic reviews." Journal of Clinical Epidemiology (S0895435608001509). — Converts the estimate into an operational rule: search until the estimated remaining population falls below a threshold. This is exactly the conversion PRESUMPTION-628 says the pipeline does not perform.
    3. "Boosting qualifies capture-recapture methods for estimating the comprehensiveness of literature searches for systematic reviews." Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, 2011 (S0895435611001107). — Methodological refinement addressing the dependence between sources that biases the naive Lincoln-Petersen estimate; relevant because the C2A2 crawler's sources are highly dependent.
    4. Bramer, W.M., Giustini, D. & Kramer, B.M.R., 2016. "Comparing the coverage, recall, and precision of searches for 120 systematic reviews in Embase, MEDLINE, and Google Scholar: a prospective study." Systematic Reviews, 5:39. — 4,795 included references across 120 reviews checked against original search results: Embase retrieved 97.5%, MEDLINE 92.3%; no single source was acceptable alone. Gives empirical per-channel recall figures, i.e. a measured denominator for what one channel misses.
    5. Bramer, W.M. et al., 2017. "Optimal database combinations for literature searches in systematic reviews: a prospective exploratory study." Systematic Reviews, 6:245. — Extends to combinations: best recall from MEDLINE + Embase + Web of Science + first 200 Google Scholar results. Supports the design conclusion that coverage is a property of the channel SET and can be estimated for a given set.
    6. "A practical guide to evaluating sensitivity of literature search strings for systematic reviews using relative recall." Research Synthesis Methods, 2025 (PMC12621535). — Relative recall / benchmarking: test a search against a pre-defined known-relevant set to obtain a measured sensitivity figure. The lowest-cost method available to an automated pipeline, since it needs a seed set rather than a second independent crawl. The same paper reports that such validations are RARE in published reviews — which is the general form of the C2A2 gap.
    7. "Estimating Absolute Web Crawl Coverage From Longitudinal Set Intersections." arXiv:2603.15416. — Web-scale analogue. Reviews Lawrence & Giles' application of pairwise URL overlap across six search engines to estimate indexable web size via Lincoln-Petersen, and notes that positive dependence between engines inflates overlap, making such estimates a LOWER BOUND. A bound, not a point estimate, is precisely the deliverable PRESUMPTION-628 asks for.
    8. Bar-Yossef, Z. & Gurevich, M., 2008. "Random sampling from a search engine's index." Journal of the ACM. — Near-uniform sampling methods that correct for ranking bias in coverage estimation; with Henzinger et al. (2000) and Anagnostopoulos et al. (2006), establishes a mature methodology for estimating what an index does not contain.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The claim is well supported and the methods are mature in two independent literatures. Capture-recapture has been applied to search completeness in systematic reviewing since at least 1996, has been developed into a formal stopping rule, and has known bias corrections for source dependence. Database-coverage studies supply directly measured per-channel recall (Embase 97.5%, MEDLINE 92.3% against 4,795 known-included references), demonstrating that "what fraction does this channel see" is an empirically answerable question rather than an unknowable. Relative recall provides a cheap version usable by an automated agent: benchmark the crawler against a hand-built seed set of items known to qualify and report the fraction recovered. The web-scale literature (Lawrence & Giles via arXiv:2603.15416; Bar-Yossef & Gurevich) shows the same machinery applied to crawler coverage specifically, and is explicit that dependence between sources makes overlap-derived estimates a lower bound — which is exactly the right output form for a disclosure like the Wolfram specialist's. So the transformation PRESUMPTION-628 says is missing is not merely conceivable; it is standard practice with named methods and reported failure modes.

  Caveats: (a) Capture-recapture requires two or more genuinely independent ascertainment channels with sufficient overlap; the C2A2 case has one crawler and two blocked routes, so a second channel would have to be built (e.g. a different retrieval path, or a periodic manual sweep) before the method applies. This is a real cost, not a formality. (b) Where sources are positively dependent — which is the norm for search channels covering the same site — estimates are biased toward under-counting the missed population, so they bound from below only. (c) Relative recall requires a benchmark set whose construction is itself a search, so it estimates sensitivity relative to a quasi-gold standard rather than to truth. (d) The database-coverage figures are specific to biomedical systematic reviewing with librarian-mediated searches; they establish that measurement is possible, not that any particular number transfers to a personal-website crawler. (e) None of these methods converts a disclosure into a bound retrospectively without new data collection — the item's remedy is instrumentation, not re-reading the existing output.

  Search scope: Comprehensive across capture-recapture for search completeness, relative recall / search-string sensitivity validation, systematic-review database coverage, and web-index size and coverage estimation.
