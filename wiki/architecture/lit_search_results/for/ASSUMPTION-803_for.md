  Summary: The assumption is right about the general point and not confirmed on
    the specific one that was supposed to do the work. On the general point the
    evidence is strong: "allostasis / predictive regulation" is not a homonym
    but a term with a single traceable lineage from Sterling 2012, taken up by
    Barrett and Simmons in an explicitly Bayesian active-inference framework
    that unifies allostatic control and interoception, and elaborated across a
    now-substantial affective-science literature whose paper titles conjoin the
    two vocabularies on purpose. The Friston-Levin link is stronger still and
    needs no inference at all: Friston, Levin, Sengupta and Pezzulo co-authored
    "Knowing one's place" in J. R. Soc. Interface in 2015, applying variational
    free-energy minimisation to morphogenetic pattern regulation and stating the
    continuity with active inference in neuroscience explicitly. So two of the
    three pairings are established, one of them by co-authorship. The third is
    not. Searching the Fredrickson lab's own published output returned the lab's
    own idiom — upward spirals, vagal tone, autonomic flexibility, vantage
    resources — and not Friston's; the located mechanism claims are reciprocal-
    causation claims about positive affect and cardiac vagal tone rather than
    claims about predictive regulation of the internal milieu. The bridge from
    active inference into affective science that the search did find runs
    through Barrett, not Fredrickson, and Barrett and Fredrickson are distinct
    programmes. On Levin's side there is a parallel lexical caution: the term
    his lab uses is anatomical homeostasis, and the structural similarity to
    allostasis is real but the shared-term claim rests on the Friston
    co-authorship rather than on Levin's own vocabulary. The consequence for the
    item's stated payoff is direct: the move of the Levin-Fredrickson pair from
    surface analogy to structural homology was to be licensed by a
    Fredrickson-lab paper using Friston's idiom, and no such paper was located,
    so that move is not yet earned.

  Caveats: This is a negative result on a specific citation, and negative
    citation results are weaker than positive ones — the paper may exist and my
    search may have missed it. I searched the PEP Lab site's indexed material
    and general queries pairing Fredrickson with allostasis, predictive
    regulation, active inference and interoception; I did not do a full-text
    search of Fredrickson's corpus, did not check her book chapters or
    co-authored theoretical pieces outside the lab site, and did not check
    whether a lab member has published such framing under their own name. A
    targeted follow-up is warranted before this is treated as settled. Separately,
    every source in this file was located by title, abstract or search summary;
    none was opened, so no quotation here should be treated as verbatim from the
    paper and several volume/page figures are from established knowledge rather
    than confirmed. Source 3's supplementary entries have uncaptured author
    lists; source 5's Animal Cognition author list is incomplete; source 6's Kok
    et al. author list is from established knowledge. On the substantive side,
    one important scope point: even where the term is genuinely shared, shared
    terminology across Sterling, Friston, Barrett and Levin does not by itself
    establish that the *same mechanism* is being described at the same level of
    grain — bioelectric set-point maintenance in a planarian and interoceptive
    prediction error in agranular cortex are both describable as predictive
    regulation while differing in almost everything else. Terminological
    convergence is necessary for structural homology and is not sufficient for
    it, and this file establishes the former without touching the latter.
