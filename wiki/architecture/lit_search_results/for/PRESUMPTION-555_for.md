    1. Huang, J. et al. (2023), "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet," arXiv:2310.01798 (ICLR 2024). — LLMs frequently cannot reliably verify or correct their own reasoning without external feedback; performance can degrade after self-"correction." A stated verdict about a formal relation is therefore not a reliable determination.
    2. "A Closer Look at the Self-Verification Abilities of LLMs in Logical Reasoning," arXiv:2311.07954; and multiple-choice/confidence findings (arXiv:2501.09775) showing models are frequently high-confidence when wrong, especially under reasoning. — Self-verification of logical/formal claims is unreliable and confidence is decoupled from correctness; the assertion "Q_A is identical to the generative model" carries no calibrated truth guarantee.
    3. Foundational proof theory (Tarski; the standard that a mathematical identity is established by derivation) and autoformalization-faithfulness work ("Know Your Limits: On the Faithfulness of LLMs as Solvers and Autoformalizers," arXiv:2606.16118). — Exact identity of two formal objects is settled by proof, not by an agent reporting a verdict; autoformalizers demonstrably misinterpret formal representations. Supports attaching a proof obligation (or an external checker) rather than accepting a stated verdict.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: Strongly supported and independently anchored OUTSIDE the pipeline (proof theory plus a substantial empirical LLM-reasoning-limitation literature). A formal-mathematical identity is, by definition, established by derivation; and the empirical record shows LLM self-report of correctness is unreliable and poorly calibrated, with high confidence often accompanying wrong answers. Therefore an agent stating "Q_A is identical to the blanket generative model" produces a claim to be checked, not a determination that settles the question. The sound procedure attaches a proof obligation or an independent verifier before a candidate is adopted as CROSS — an application of the verify-before-trust family (PREMISE-049) and the external-referent requirement (PREMISE-124) to formal-identity claims.

  Caveats: For some candidates the identity may be genuinely easy and an agent may be correct; the claim is not that agents are always wrong but that self-report is not the right acceptance criterion for a proof-shaped question. Lightweight external checks (symbolic algebra, a proof sketch reviewed against the formal definitions) can discharge the obligation cheaply (see 15b).
