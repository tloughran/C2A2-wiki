    4. Habermas/deliberative-democracy measurement tradition, e.g. Steenbergen, M. et al., 2003. "Measuring Political Deliberation: A Discourse Quality Index." Comparative European Politics. — Precedent that deliberation quality and consensus are measured as separate constructs.
  Strength of support: Moderate
  Summary: The peace half of the claim has direct empirical grounding: the Ideological Turing Test literature operationalizes exactly the proposed KPI — an account of the other side that the other side itself endorses — and shows it measures understanding rather than agreement, with measurable depolarization effects. The deliberation-quality literature independently supports separating mutual-comprehension metrics from consensus metrics, grounding the claim that peace ≠ agreement needs its own KPI distinct from convergence measures. The general principle that distinct objectives need distinct indicators is standard in multi-criteria performance measurement. The progress half ("MacIntyre number") has no literature as a defined metric; MacIntyre's tradition-constituted-enquiry framework (After Virtue, 1981; Whose Justice? Which Rationality?, 1988) provides conceptual grounding for progress-within-a-tradition, but no one has operationalized it as a KPI.
  Caveats: Support transfers from human dyads/polities to this system only if endorsement is elicited from the actual tradition-holders (or faithful proxies); LLM-simulated endorsement is an open validity question. The MacIntyre-number side is unvalidated and project-novel.
