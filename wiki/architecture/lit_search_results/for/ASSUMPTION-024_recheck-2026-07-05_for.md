    1. Zheng, X., et al., 2025. "Evidence triangulator: using large language models to extract and synthesize causal evidence across study designs." Nature Communications 16 (s41467-025-62783-x). — Triangulation across study designs is treated as essential for establishing causality, and is now being operationalized at scale with LLMs. Strong continued support: the strategy is not merely defended philosophically but actively engineered into AI-assisted research pipelines.
    2. Liron, Y. & Nissan-Rozen, I., 2024. "Triangulation, Incommensurability, and Conditionalization." Philosophy of Science. — Recent formal philosophy-of-science treatment refining when triangulation is coherent under Bayesian updating; the premise's core (convergence is evidentially significant given independence) survives with sharpened conditions.
    3. "Triangulation in biomedical research: navigating an ocean of uncertainty," 2025 (PMC12304056). — Current methodological endorsement in biomedicine: at least two sufficiently independent methods on the same question enhance robustness and reproducibility of conclusions; restates the Wimsatt condition that failure of independence produces illusory robustness.
    4. Kwon, A., et al., 2025. "Correlated Errors in Large Language Models." arXiv:2506.07962. — Bears directly on the watch item: across 349 models and 12,032 questions, LLM errors are substantially correlated, and MORE accurate models have MORE correlated errors; ensemble/Condorcet gains require error independence that overlapping training corpora undermine. Supports the premise by empirically vindicating its independence condition — and quantifying when AI-derived evidence streams fail it.
    5. "Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM Evaluation Panels," 2026. arXiv:2605.29800. — Shows N nominally independent LLM judges can carry the effective evidential weight of ~2; direct quantitative machinery for discounting convergence among AI-assisted streams.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The premise remains well supported and actively load-bearing in current science: triangulation is being formalized further in philosophy of science (Liron & Nissan-Rozen 2024), endorsed in current biomedical methodology, and built into LLM-based evidence-synthesis systems published in Nature Communications in 2025. The most important new material concerns the watch item: the 2025-26 correlated-errors literature converts the premise's independence proviso from a philosophical caveat into a measured empirical constraint for AI-assisted research — multiple LLM-derived analyses (especially from same-family or similarly trained models) are demonstrably NOT independent evidence streams, and convergence among them must be discounted accordingly (in the panel study, nine judges ≈ two effective votes). This does not weaken the premise; it confirms Wimsatt's original warning that non-independent convergence yields illusory robustness, and supplies quantitative tools for applying it.

  Caveats: For C2A2 specifically: convergence among outputs of Claude-family agents, or between agents and the LLM-mediated searches they run, has sharply reduced evidential weight; independence claims should be argued at the level of data sources and methods, not at the level of "separate agent runs." Triangulation's force also depends on the evidence lines bearing on the same well-posed proposition (the incommensurability condition in Liron & Nissan-Rozen); loosely related supportive findings do not triangulate.
