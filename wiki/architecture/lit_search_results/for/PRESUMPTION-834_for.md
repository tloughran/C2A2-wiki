  Sources:
    1. Zheng, S., Adams, B., Hassan, A.E. (2025). "On Build Hermeticity in Bazel-based Build Systems." IEEE Software 42(6). (Preprint: https://mcislab.github.io/publications/2025/ieeesw-shenyu.pdf) — The closest available empirical analogue, and it is very close. Traced 150 million Linux filesystem calls across the builds of 70 Bazel projects and found that *none* achieved a fully hermetic build: 2,439 dependency packages were drawn from the host rather than from the declared specification, 98.6% of projects depended on non-hermetic top-level toolchains, and only 28.6% used any external tool (Docker/CI config) to manage those — of which 70% pinned no version. Key findings that map directly onto PRESUMPTION-834: (i) the declared contract (WORKSPACE.bazel/MODULE.bazel) systematically omits the host facts that determine build success; (ii) projects carry a median of 12 host dependencies *not* installed by default, "risking build failures on new build machines"; (iii) CI-image drift changes these silently because "the CI environment [is] not under their control." A quantified demonstration that capability is a property of the session's environment, not of the declared contract.

    2. Yin, Z., Ma, X., Zheng, J., Zhou, Y., Bairavasundaram, L.N., Pasupathy, S. (2011). "An Empirical Study on Configuration Errors in Commercial and Open Source Systems." SOSP '11, pp. 159–172. — 546 real-world misconfigurations across a commercial storage system and four open-source systems. Establishes configuration/environment errors as a dominant cause of failure, with a documented subclass of path parameters that are never validated at startup (the NGINX `proxy_store` directory-path example). Supports "provisioning facts determine outcome and are checked by nothing."

    3. Huang, P., Guo, C., Zhou, L., Lorch, J.R., Dang, Y., Chintalapati, M., Yao, R. (2017). "Gray Failure: The Achilles' Heel of Cloud-Scale Systems." HotOS '17. — Supplies the reason the mount is "monitored by no instrument": differential observability, where the system's failure detectors report health because they are observing the wrong thing. Explains why an environment-determined failure produces no environment-shaped signal.

    4. Lamb, C., Zacchiroli, S. (2021). "Reproducible Builds: Increasing the Integrity of Software Supply Chains." IEEE Software 39(2): 62–70. — Frames the property PRESUMPTION-834 implicitly demands — identical results given identical *declared* inputs — as an integrity property of the supply chain, and identifies unstable environment inputs as one of its two failure sources. Supports treating an undeclared mount dependency as an integrity defect rather than an operational nuisance.

    5. Fourné, M., Wermke, D., Enck, W., Fahl, S., Acar, Y. (2023). "It's like flossing your teeth: On the importance and challenges of reproducible builds for software supply chain security." IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy (S&P '23), pp. 1527–1544. — Qualitative study of practitioners attempting environment-independent builds; documents that eliminating implicit environment dependencies is understood as important and is nonetheless consistently deferred because the dependencies are invisible until they break. Supports the "named in no contract" half specifically.

    6. Bajaj, R., Fernandes, E., Adams, B., Hassan, A.E. (2023). "Unreproducible builds: time to fix, causes, and correlation with external ecosystem factors." Empirical Software Engineering 29(11). — Measures how long environment-caused irreproducibility takes to diagnose and fix, and correlates it with external ecosystem factors outside the project's declaration. Supports the claim that these failures are both consequential and slow to attribute.

    7. Practitioner literature on configuration drift (multiple independent sources: Wiz, env0, Motadata, and CI/CD guidance). — Converges on: "a deployment that works in staging may fail in production due to differences in configuration"; drift arises from manual changes, inconsistent automation, and external integrations; and untracked settings cause behaviour to diverge across environments while the declared artefact is identical. Non-peer-reviewed but uniform and operationally mature; establishes that "the environment, not the spec, decides" is common knowledge in practice.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: This presumption has an unusually direct empirical analogue in the build-hermeticity literature, and that literature contradicts the presumption emphatically. Zheng, Adams and Hassan traced 150 million filesystem calls across 70 projects using a build system whose *advertised core feature* is hermeticity, and found that not one achieved it: 2,439 dependency packages were supplied by the host and named in no build specification, 98.6% of projects depended on undeclared top-level toolchains, and projects carried a median of twelve host dependencies not present by default on a fresh machine — a directly measured "the session, not the contract, determines whether this works." They further found that CI-image drift silently changes these dependencies and that resulting failures are hard to attribute because the environment is outside the declaring party's control. Yin et al.'s configuration-error corpus independently establishes environment and unvalidated path/resource facts as a dominant, unchecked failure cause; Huang et al. explain why no instrument fires (the detectors observe the declared thing, not the provisioning fact); and the reproducible-builds line of work — Lamb and Zacchiroli on determinism-as-integrity, Fourné et al. on why practitioners defer eliminating implicit environment dependencies, Bajaj et al. on how long these take to diagnose — establishes both that the fix is known and that it is consistently not done. The presumption 14b surfaced is, in this literature's terms, the assumption of hermeticity in a non-hermetic execution environment, and the empirical answer to it is that hermeticity is never free and is almost never actually achieved.

  Caveats: (a) The hermeticity and reproducible-build results concern build systems, not agent sessions; the structural analogy (declared inputs vs. ambient host resources) is tight, but the base rates and the specific remedies — SBOMs, pinned toolchains, sandboxing — do not transfer directly to a mount-provisioning problem. (b) None of the sources studies *capability declaration* as such; the literature on pre-flight resource checks and capability manifests was not located in peer-reviewed form and this remains a gap. (c) The claim's inferential punch — that mount presence predicted outcome *better than any contract property* — is a single-day, n=29 local finding; the literature supports that environment facts are powerful and unmonitored predictors, not that they dominate in this instance. (d) The configuration-drift material is vendor literature. (e) A note for 15b: the same literature that supports the presumption's falsity also shows the remedy is expensive — Zheng et al. find developers abandoning Bazel because hermeticity's maintenance costs outweigh perceived benefits — so "declare and pre-flight-check every environment dependency" is not a cost-free prescription.
