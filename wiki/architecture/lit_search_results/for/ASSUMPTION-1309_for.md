  Summary: The item has two limbs and the intake correctly identified that only the second is a
    literature question. Limb (b) — that the remedy for a high pre-answered rate is a check against the
    existing base *before* commissioning fresh search effort — is well supported: it is exactly what
    Cochrane's guidance prescribes before starting a new review, what PROSPERO registration exists to
    enable, and what living systematic reviews implement as a standing mechanism. The redundancy
    literature supports the premise that duplicated synthesis carries real cost. The material objection
    is that the analogy is imperfect in a way that matters: in systematic review, the prior synthesis is
    a *published, externally reviewed* artifact, whereas an ACTIVE premise here is a self-produced
    artifact of the same pipeline. PREMISE-174 and PREMISE-096 both bear: a register that can expand but
    not contract will, under a pre-route grep, become progressively harder to falsify, because the only
    mechanism that has ever re-exposed a premise is a search that was run anyway. That is OPEN-192 stated
    as a mechanism, and it is the reason the recommendation is PARTIALLY rather than fully supported.

  Caveats:
    - The remedy as written ("a register grep") is a surface-form check. PREMISE-187 governs: a check
      over surface form cannot decide whether content means what it was meant to mean, and a gate that
      appears to check what it cannot decide is worse than no gate. A grep will produce both false
      "pre-answered" verdicts (lexical match, different claim) and false negatives (same claim, different
      vocabulary — PREMISE-081's false-negative point). Neither rate is measured.
    - PREMISE-107's objection is not answered. Two mechanisms produce an 82% pre-answered rate: (i) 14a/b
      are routing items that are already settled, or (ii) the register's premises are broad enough that
      almost any item finds a lexical match. These predict different remedies and the discriminating test
      — does a human adjudicate the pre-answered verdicts as correct? — has not been run.
    - The literature's remedy is "check, then decide whether to update," not "check, then exclude."
      Cochrane's guidance leads to a decision about whether a *new* review is warranted or an *update* of
      the existing one; neither outcome is "do nothing." Mapping it onto exclusion-from-search is a
      stronger step than the source licenses.
    - Declared interest, restated: 14a is a named party to the remedy. So, on the other side, is 15a —
      a pre-route grep reduces this agent's inflow. Both directions of interest are on the record.
    - n = 2 cycles, 11 items. The 82% figure has an enormous confidence interval; by the Clopper-Pearson
      form of PREMISE-182 an 9/11 point estimate is compatible with a true rate well below 60%. The rate
      should not be quoted without its interval.
