  Sources:
    1. Closed-loop communication literature: "Closed Loop Communication Training in Medical Simulation," StatPearls (NCBI Bookshelf NBK549899); Härgestam, M. et al. (2013), "Communication in interdisciplinary teams: exploring closed-loop communication during in situ trauma team training," BMJ Open (PMC3808778). — Direct theoretical grounding, and it is precisely the point PRESUMPTION-502 makes. Closed-loop communication exists because sending is not receiving: the loop is only closed when the recipient acknowledges (check-back) and the sender confirms the acknowledgement. A call-out without a check-back is, by the model's own definition, an open loop — which is exactly "flagged for X" with no acknowledgement channel. The construct is standardised, trained, and measurable.
    2. Handoff literature: Patient Safety and Quality (AHRQ), "Handoffs: Implications for Nurses" (NCBI NBK2649); ACOG Committee Opinion (2012), "Communication Strategies for Patient Handoffs." — Supplies the definition that makes the item's severity claim precise: a handoff is "the transfer of patient information and knowledge, along with authority and responsibility, from one clinician or team to another," and properly executed handoffs "are interactive and include the opportunity for questions and answers." Two things follow directly. First, a non-interactive, unacknowledged flag does not transfer responsibility — so the sending agent's belief that it has discharged the finding is unfounded. Second, the item's framing that "every 'flagged for X' deferral reads in the record as a completed escalation" is the documented failure: responsibility appears transferred in the record while nobody holds it.
    3. Quantified harm: AHRQ/Joint Commission figures via the sources above — poor communication is a contributing factor in more than 60% of hospital adverse events in the US, and communication failures were implicated in ~30% of malpractice claims and more than 1700 deaths over five years. This is the strongest empirical precedent available for the cost of unacknowledged handoff, in the domain that has studied it most.
    4. Responsibility diffusion: CareRev, "Closed-Loop Communication in Healthcare." — States the mechanism in the exact form the item needs: "when no one is specifically responsible, it is easy for everyone to assume someone else handled it." Supports the item's inference that the failure is structural (no addressee-with-obligation) rather than a lapse by either agent.
    5. Aviation precedent: FAA position-relief briefing standards for air traffic controllers, and Crew Resource Management, via PMC6777853, "Communication: Is There a Standard Handover Technique to Transfer Patient Care?" — The FAA "has published standards for transfer of responsibility for air traffic controllers that outline the importance of a structured process to promote safe handovers." Establishes that in a second high-reliability domain the response to this exact risk was to mandate a structured, acknowledged transfer protocol — analogous support for the item's proposed instrumentation.
    6. Systematic-review evidence: "Use of structured handoff protocols for within-hospital unit transitions: a systematic review from Making Healthcare Safer IV" (PMC12232517). — Establishes that structured handoff protocols are an evaluated intervention class with a systematic-review evidence base, not merely a recommended practice. Supports the item's remedy (instrument flags with recipient and acknowledgement) as an intervention with precedent rather than a novel proposal.

  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: PRESUMPTION-502 names a failure mode that two high-reliability industries have independently identified, formalised, mandated protocols against, and evaluated in systematic review. The closed-loop communication model exists specifically to deny the presumption the item surfaces — that transmission constitutes delivery — and defines the loop as closed only on acknowledgement plus confirmation. The clinical handoff literature adds the element that gives the item its severity: a handoff transfers *responsibility*, and an unacknowledged flag therefore leaves responsibility unheld while the record shows it discharged. That is worse than no flag, because it retires the sender's obligation without creating anyone else's, and the responsibility-diffusion literature names the resulting state directly. The associated harm figures in healthcare are among the largest attributed to any single process defect, and aviation reached the same conclusion independently, which is strong evidence that the mechanism is domain-general rather than an artefact of clinical work. The item's proposed remedy — instrument cross-agent flags with a named recipient and an acknowledgement, then measure what fraction of the last month's flags were ever read — maps directly onto the structured-handoff intervention class and onto the check-back primitive.

  Caveats: (a) All strong evidence is from human teams under time pressure with a live patient or aircraft; the transfer to asynchronous agent handoffs is analogous, not direct. In particular, human handoff failures are largely attributed to memory, interruption and hierarchy effects that do not apply to agents, while the agent case has a mechanism the clinical case does not — the recipient may simply never read the artifact. The diagnosis transfers; the causal model does not. (b) The clinical harm figures establish the cost of communication failure broadly, not of unacknowledged flags specifically; they should not be cited as an effect size for this mechanism. (c) The item's claim that this is the "first observed instance" is an internal-record claim outside search scope. (d) Acknowledgement protocols have a documented cost the item does not weigh: they add latency and, if the acknowledgement becomes routine, produce a rubber-stamp that restores the original failure with an audit trail attached — the same normalisation dynamic PRESUMPTION-504 names in this batch. The systematic review's own finding is that structured protocols help, not that they eliminate. (e) Measuring "was it ever read" requires read-set instrumentation that PRESUMPTION-503 indicates does not currently exist, so the two items' remedies are coupled.
