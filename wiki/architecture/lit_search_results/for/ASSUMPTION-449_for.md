  Summary: The formal record supports the claim's core contrast. Hoffman's join theorems and Fields et al.'s agent networks assert that subjects COMPOSE into further subjects; Levin asserts that selves NEST with real sub-selves. Kastrup's system is designed on the opposite principle: no fundamental micro-subjects, no combination, only dissociation of one subject. On the narrow question "do subjects have subjects as proper parts, fundamentally?", Hoffman and Levin say yes and Kastrup says no. Miller (2021) independently defends the non-equivalence of the decombination and combination problems. A wiki edge labelled simply "agreement" between Kastrup and Levin/Hoffman is under-specified at minimum, and the claim that the label is wrong is supported.
