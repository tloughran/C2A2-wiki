  Summary: I scored the affirmative reading as the wiki has been using it — namely that (A) a dissociative boundary of the analytic-idealist kind does satisfy the Markov-blanket conditional-independence condition, and (B) the restriction operation in Arkani-Hamed's positive geometry and the observer restriction in Wolfram's ruliad are the same operation. Sub-claim A has real published backing, but indirectly: the Fields–Friston–Glazebrook–Levin programme has established that Markov blankets are substrate-neutral and that information-theoretic access barriers (holographic screens) satisfy the blanket condition, which removes the main formal objection to counting a phenomenal-inaccessibility boundary as a blanket. The one source that argues the idealist case explicitly (Austermann 2026) does exactly what the assumption asks and even names Kastrup's dissociation dynamics — but it is an unrefereed single-author preprint with four recorded downloads, and I would not build a wiki claim on it alone. Sub-claim B found nothing: across searches of the amplituhedron/positive-geometry literature and the ruliad/observer-theory literature, no published work identifies, compares, or even juxtaposes the two restriction operations; the closest Wolfram-side formalisation (Elshatlawy et al. 2025) treats observer restriction as foliation and coarse-graining, which is a partition operation, not a boundary-stratification of a positive geometry. The two wiki questions that were "closed negatively on one run's reading" therefore look correctly closed for B and prematurely closed for A.

  Caveats:
    - Source 5 (Austermann) is the only item that directly targets the assumption, and its provenance is weak: PhilArchive self-archive, no journal venue, no citations, added February 2026.
