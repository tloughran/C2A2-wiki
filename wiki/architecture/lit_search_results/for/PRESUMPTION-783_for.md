  Strength of support: Strong

  Summary: The corrective proposition is strongly supported from three independent directions and is already, in substance, this project's own validated position. Formally, the reject option is a third action carrying an explicit cost term, and its optimality is undefined without that term — so a refusal reported with no cost is not a priced decision. Psychologically, omission bias is one of the better-replicated asymmetries in judgement: identical harms are judged less culpable when they arise from inaction, which predicts precisely the pattern 14b observed — seven refusals each narrated as a virtue, none as an exposure. Economically, Reinertsen's account explains why the cost stays invisible rather than merely unrecorded: development queues are digital and therefore uncountable, most organisations never quantify cost of delay at all, and untrained intuition about it is off by more than an order of magnitude. The deferred-maintenance literature supplies the end state of leaving that cost unrecorded — a backlog measured in the trillions, accumulating at multiples of the avoided cost, invisible until realised. The convergent conclusion is sharper than "restraint has a cost": it is that an instrument which records refusals and not their consequences will report restraint as a monotonically increasing virtue no matter what the underlying position is, which makes the seven-refusal figure uninterpretable as evidence of good judgement.

  Caveats: (a) THE ASYMMETRY GENUINELY RUNS THE OTHER WAY IN SAFETY DOMAINS, and I found that evidence while searching for support, so I report it. Stop-work-authority practice explicitly accepts a high false-alarm rate — the located material's formulation is "I would rather stop work a dozen times unnecessarily than miss the one time it really mattered" — because the loss function is catastrophically asymmetric. PREMISE-133 makes the same point from the other side: abstention is a WEAKER commitment with a distinct error profile, and refusing to certify is conformant behaviour, not a fault. So the item must not be read as "seven refusals is too many." It supports only "the cost of the seven was not measured, and the instrument cannot measure it." (b) Reinertsen's cost-of-delay machinery assumes a datable economic value for delivery; C2A2's deferred items mostly have no revenue clock, so the framework transfers as a discipline (name what deferral costs) rather than as a calculation. (c) The infrastructure multipliers come from advocacy sources and are not research-grade. (d) Source 4 and the general claim are already register-held, so this file's disjoint contribution is Reinertsen, the omission-bias literature, and the deferred-maintenance analogue.
