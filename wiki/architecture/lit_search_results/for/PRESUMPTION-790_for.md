    1. Deming, W.E. — operational definitions; "The New Economics" and "Out of the Crisis," and the standard restatement of his position: "there is no true value of any characteristic, state, or condition that is defined in terms of measurement or observation." His own worked example is exactly this item's shape — there is no true value of the number of inhabitants within a boundary, because a count depends on the arbitrary rules applied to carry it out, and repetition of a count will exhibit variation. — This is the direct refutation of the presumption as worded: the disagreement is not a reporting inconsistency to be tidied, because there was never a single number for the readings to be inconsistent WITH. The corollary Deming draws is the operative one: any datum presented without its operational definition should be questioned, since without the definition one is likely to draw incorrect conclusions from it. [canonical; the formulations above located this run via W. Edwards Deming Institute pages and the Curious Cat management archive.     2. Bland, J.M. & Altman, D.G. (1986). "Statistical methods for assessing agreement between two methods of clinical measurement." The Lancet 327(8476):307-310. — The methodological standard for exactly this situation, and its central discipline is the one C2A2 skipped. The product of comparing two methods is not a winner and is not a correlation; it is the LIMITS OF AGREEMENT — the mean difference plus and minus 1.96 standard deviations of the differences — i.e. the spread is the deliverable. The paper exists because correlation was being misused for this purpose: two methods can correlate at r = 0.99 and disagree by an amount that matters, since correlation measures linear association and not agreement, and it inflates with the range of the sample. Translated: four DB sizings do not have a right answer to be recovered; they have a limits-of-agreement interval, and that interval is the finding. [canonical; the method, the 1.96 SD construction and the correlation critique verified this run via MedCalc, Analyse-it and the American Journal of Ophthalmology methods paper. The 1986 Lancet original was NOT re-read. NOTE: PREMISE-114 already holds "limits of agreement, not a correct value," so this source is REGISTER-HELD and is disclosed as non-independent.]
    3. Measurement Systems Analysis / Gauge R&R (AIAG MSA reference manual; ASQ and practitioner treatments). — Supplies the recording obligation that PREMISE-114 does not impose. An entire industrial discipline exists whose primary object of study IS the spread across repeated readings, decomposed into REPEATABILITY (same operator, same instrument, same part, repeated) and REPRODUCIBILITY (different operators, same instrument, same part). The acceptance thresholds are quantitative and routine — %R&R below 10% good, 10-30% marginal, above 30% unacceptable and the measurement system must be improved before the process is judged — and results are expressed against process variation or tolerance. The transferable point is not the thresholds but the sequencing: in this discipline you CANNOT interpret a reading against a specification until the measurement system's own variation has been quantified and found small relative to the tolerance. C2A2 has four readings, a tolerance (3.2 GB free) and no R&R figure. [verified this run at PRACTITIONER level — ASQ, Quality-One, 1factory and Six Sigma Study Guide treatments located and consistent with one another.     4. ISO 14253-1:2017, "Geometrical product specifications (GPS) — Inspection by measurement of workpieces and measuring equipment — Part 1: Decision rules for verifying conformity or nonconformity with specifications"; with ILAC-G8 as the parallel guidance. — The genuinely disjoint contribution of this file, and the one that speaks to the decision the item says is actually at stake. The standard governs precisely the case where a measured value falls close to a specification limit. Its rule is that the tolerance is REDUCED by the measurement uncertainty when proving conformity and EXPANDED by it when proving non-conformity — a guard band — leaving an explicit zone in which NO CONFORMITY DECISION MAY BE TAKEN. The standard's design property is worth stating because it inverts the incentive C2A2 currently has: the acceptance zone GROWS as measurement uncertainty falls, so the standard rewards better metrology rather than better arguing. Applied here: 4.65 GB against 3.2 GB free, with an unquantified spread across four readings, sits in a regime where the correct output is not a number but a decision rule plus a guard band, and "pick the largest" and "pick the latest" are both outside the standard.   Summary: The corrective proposition is well supported and, in three separate disciplines, is the founding premise of the discipline rather than a finding within it. Deming denies the presumption at the root: a quantity defined by a procedure has no value independent of the procedure, so repeated counts will vary and there is no single true number the readings are being inconsistent with. Bland-Altman establishes that the deliverable of a method comparison is the spread — the limits of agreement — rather than a winner, and that reaching for a summary that hides the spread is the specific error the method was invented to stop. Gauge R&R adds the sequencing obligation that the register does not currently impose: the variation of the measurement system must be quantified before any reading is interpreted against a tolerance, and C2A2 has a tolerance and four readings and no such figure. ISO 14253-1 is the disjoint contribution and it addresses the decision the item says is actually at stake — where a value sits near a specification limit, the standard requires a guard band and defines a zone in which no conformity decision may be taken, which means "pick the largest" and "pick the latest" are both non-conformant procedures. Data fusion adds the architectural warning that agreement or majority among readings produced by runs sharing a codebase measures copying rather than truth. Against all of that, PREMISE-114 already holds the incommensurability result and the limits-of-agreement framing, and PREMISE-145 already holds that withholding a discrepant own-measurement is a reporting bias — so the honest verdict is PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED, with the partiality reflecting register coverage rather than weak literature.
