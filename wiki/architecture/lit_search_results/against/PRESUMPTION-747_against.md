  Sources:
    1. Merton, R.K., 1961 (and later "multiples" literature, e.g. Garfield essay, garfield.library.upenn.edu/essays/v4p660y1979-80.pdf) — Merton catalogued 261+ multiples and argued the "singleton" (unique) discovery, not the multiple, is the historical anomaly. This directly challenges any presumption that independent convergence is rare/special evidence of correctness — if multiples are the statistical norm in active research areas, arriving at a similar idea independently is unremarkable and carries weak evidential weight either way.
    2. Simonton, D.K. (cited via research on 579 historical multiples/789 scientists) — found that a combined chance + zeitgeist model, not individual insight, best explains multiples. This undermines reading convergence as corroboration of a specific idea's correctness: if the driving cause is "ripeness" of the shared intellectual environment (shared inputs, shared tools, shared problems), then two parties converging tells you about the environment's readiness, not about the truth-value of the idea reached.
    3. Matt Clancy (summarized via construction-physics.com, "How Common is Multiple Invention?") — estimates roughly 2-3% base rate for simultaneous scientific discovery and up to ~8% chance of reinvention within a decade, and notes that once one party publishes, others abandon or never report their independent identical results — meaning the observed rate of "recorded" multiples undercounts true convergence, which makes case-by-case corroboration-vs-competition judgments (like MC0001) inherently underdetermined by the visible record alone.
    4. Losos, J.B. et al., 2011, "Convergence, Adaptation, and Constraint," Evolution 65(7) (bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.wustl.edu PDF) — establishes, in the analogous biological literature on convergent evolution, that convergence can occur "for reasons unrelated to" the shared selective pressure being tested, i.e. convergence is not self-certifying evidence of the causal story attached to it. Applied by analogy: two parties reaching the same idea does not by itself tell you whether they reached it for the same (valid) reasons.
    5. Grokipedia "Multiple discovery" summary and Wikipedia "Multiple discovery" — note the competing "zeitgeist" vs. "chance" explanations remain actively debated rather than resolved, meaning there is no settled theoretical basis in the literature for defaulting to a "corroboration" reading over a "coincidence/competition" reading.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The multiple-discovery literature directly challenges the presumption in two ways. First, it establishes that independent convergence is common (not a rare, hence evidentially weighty, event) in active research areas, which weakens any inference from "the field converged too" to "therefore the idea is validated." Second, the leading explanatory models (zeitgeist/chance combined, per Simonton) locate the cause of convergence in shared background conditions rather than in the correctness of the specific idea, meaning convergence is evidence about environmental readiness, not truth. The analogous convergent-evolution literature reinforces that convergence can arise for reasons unconnected to the mechanism being credited. None of this literature resolves whether MC0001 specifically was corroboration or competition — it undermines the idea that there's a principled default answer at all, which is precisely the inconsistency the item flags (recorded as corroboration on 08-08, competition on 08-09, from identical evidence).

  Specific risks: If C2A2 systematically reads convergence as corroboration, it risks (a) false confidence — inflating its own idea's validity based on an event (independent arrival) that the literature shows is common and weakly diagnostic, and (b) failing to notice genuine competitive obsolescence — cases where the field solved the same problem better/faster without any of C2A2's machinery, which is a "we are non-essential" signal being relabeled as validation.

  Mitigations available: The item's own proposed discriminator — auditing all ~90 CROSS entries for whether they are joined by documented contact/citation versus C2A2's own post-hoc inference of similarity — is well-supported by the literature: Merton's own approach to distinguishing genuine multiples from mere thematic similarity relied on checking priority claims and contact, not surface similarity. This in-house audit is the right test and should be run before assigning corroboration/competition labels case by case, rather than defaulting either way.

  Recommendation: CHALLENGED

The relevant question for C2A2 isn't "is convergence common in science generally" but "for this specific idea, was there causal contact (C2A2's output leaking into or being downstream of field discourse, or vice versa) or genuine independence" — and no general theory of multiples can answer a specific provenance question.
