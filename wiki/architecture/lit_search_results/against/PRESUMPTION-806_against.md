### Challenging evidence found: Yes — strong, and partly by arithmetic on the item's own figures

### The arithmetic (performed this run on the item's stated numbers)

The item supplies its own null model and does not use it. Taking the health contract's figure at face value — **39 of 110 runs never finished, a base rate of 35.5%**:

- **The day's failure count is at base rate, not above it.** Nine deliverable-less runs at N=25 is **36.0%**, against a base rate of **35.5%**. The day the item treats as anomalous is, to within half a percentage point, an average day. The item's own risk clause — "at N=25 the fleet lost nine runs in one day" — is a restatement of the base rate, not evidence of an excursion above it.
- **The 08:00 triple is a ~4.5% event, before correction.** If the three runs in that window fail independently at 35.5% each, P(all three fail) = 0.355³ = **0.0447**.
- **After correcting for the look-elsewhere effect, it is not even that.** The 08:00 slot was not nominated in advance; it was selected *because* all its runs failed. With roughly ten candidate time windows in a day, the probability that at least one window shows an all-fail cluster is on the order of 1 − (1 − 0.045)^10 ≈ **37%**, i.e. more likely than not. A pattern that appears in a bit over a third of purely independent days is not evidence of coupling.
- **The shared-signature observation has no denominator.** Four of nine failures sharing "a stall at a permission-gated or environment-gated tool call" is only informative against the historical fraction of failures carrying that signature. If it is the modal failure mode across the 39 — which is plausible in an agent fleet — then four of nine is the expected value, not a coincidence. **That denominator is not in the item and was not, on the item's own account, computed.**

None of this shows the failures *are* independent. It shows the evidence offered does not discriminate.

### Sources

1. **Poisson clumping / Poisson bursts.** [**verified this run — Wikipedia article fetched in full**, with primary chain: Aldous, D. (1989), *Probability Approximations via the Poisson Clumping Heuristic*, Applied Mathematical Sciences 77, Springer; and the classic V-2 rocket impact analysis (Clarke, 1946), which fit the Poisson distribution and thus showed the apparent London impact clusters to be "the expected result from a chance dispersion"] — The direct challenge. "Random events may appear to occur in clusters, clumps, or bursts... If λ is small, events are rare, but may nevertheless occur in clumps purely by chance. **In many cases there is no other cause behind such indefinite groupings besides the nature of randomness following this distribution.**" The V-2 case is the exact historical analogue of the item's reasoning: a wartime population inferred targeting from bomb-impact clusters, and the Poisson fit showed the clusters were chance. The article also gives the honest boundary condition, which I record because it favours the item: not all clumping is chance — earthquake aftershocks cluster from genuine local coupling and follow a Weibull rather than a Poisson pattern. The methodological point is that **the two cases are distinguished by a test, not by inspection.**
2. **The clustering illusion.** [verified this run] — The cognitive counterpart, and it names the specific error: "the tendency to erroneously consider the inevitable streaks or clusters arising in **small samples** from random distributions to be non-random," caused by "a human tendency to underpredict the amount of variability likely to appear in a small sample." N=25 with nine events is a small sample. The literature also supplies the correct response — "whether a cluster of events in space or time is genuinely non-random can be tested using tools such as the Poisson distribution... or runs tests for sequential data" — which is a two-minute calculation the fleet has not done and which I have partially done above.
3. **NUREG/CR-6268 Rev.1, *Common-Cause Failure Database and Analysis System: Event Definition and Classification* (U.S. NRC / Idaho National Laboratory).** [verified this run — document located at nrcoe.inl.gov and nrc.gov; definitional content read via search returns, full document not read] — The challenge from how CCF is actually established. The NRC's operative definition requires **three** conditions jointly: components fail within a selected time window such that PRA mission success would be uncertain; components fail **because of a single shared cause *and* coupling mechanism**; and components fail within the established component boundary. The middle condition is the one the item cannot yet meet — 14b says so itself ("the transcripts show the signature but not the gate"). Note also *how* the NRC populates its database: analysts **review failure reports individually** from NPRDS, EPIX and Licensee Event Reports to classify events as independent or common-cause. CCF is established by engineering review of causes, **never by observing that failures clustered**.
4. **ICDE (International Common-Cause Data Exchange) coding guidelines, and the NEA ICDE Topical Report on multi-unit CCF events.** [verified this run via oecd-nea.org listing and the NTNU CCF chapter (ntnu.edu, "Root causes and coupling factors — CCF definition — Beta-factor model")] — Supplies the structure the item's investigation is missing. A CCF is the product of **two** distinct things: a **failure cause** (the condition to which failure is attributed) and a **coupling factor** (what allows that cause to reach multiple components). Named coupling mechanism categories are design, hardware, function, installation, maintenance, procedures, interfaces, **location**, and environment. ICDE analysis codes each event for event cause, coupling factor, detection method, corrective action and CCF root cause. This is a per-event forensic protocol, and it is what "establishing common cause" means. The item has a candidate coupling factor (the permission gate / the 08:00 slot) and no cause, and has not coded a single event.
5. **Beta-factor model limitations.** [verified this run via the NRC training module "Module H — Common Cause Failures" (ML12160A320), the SINTEF PDS report on CCF in safety instrumented systems (SINTEF A26922), and the NTNU chapter] — The challenge to the item's named remedy. The beta-factor model is the most-used CCF model, but its documented limitations are precisely disqualifying here: **limited data, so generic values are used with applicability problems for the specific plant**; **screening values that can skew results**; **no cross-system modelling**, since data is collected per system; **no modelling of diverse components**; and — most relevant — **causes are not explicitly modelled, the treatment being statistical rather than explicit**. A beta factor estimated from 39 failures in one fleet with no cause coding would be a number with no content. The literature's own direction of travel is away from this: recent work extends the alpha-factor method for "enhanced common cause failure analysis" and applies Bayesian inference with causal structure and missing data, precisely because magnitude-only treatments "do not consider the cause of those events."
6. **The item's own base-rate statement — "over 110 runs, 39 never finished."** [internal to the fleet; not literature] — Cited as a source because it is the null model the item needed and did not apply. Its existence in the health contract means the arithmetic above was available at zero cost.

### Strength of challenge: Strong

### Summary

The hypothesis is reasonable and the day's pattern is worth investigating; what fails is the inference offered in support of it, and it fails on the item's own numbers. Nine failures in twenty-five runs is 36.0% against a stated base rate of 35.5% — the day is not an excursion, it is the base rate reproduced almost exactly. The 08:00 triple is a 4.5% event under independence, and since the window was selected after the fact from among roughly ten, the probability that *some* window would show an all-fail cluster on a purely independent day is around 37%. The shared-signature observation has no denominator at all: four of nine failures sharing a permission-gate stall is uninformative until someone states what fraction of the historical 39 carried that same signature, and if it is the modal mode then four of nine is exactly what independence predicts. Poisson clumping is the standing result that random events form clumps with no cause behind them but randomness, and the V-2 impact analysis is the canonical demonstration that a population reasoning as this item reasons was wrong; the clustering illusion is the cognitive name for the error and specifically implicates small samples. The reliability field is, if anything, more severe: the NRC's CCF definition requires a shared cause *and* a coupling mechanism established within a component boundary, ICDE codes every event for cause, coupling factor, detection method and root cause, and the NRC's own database is built by analysts reading individual failure reports. Nowhere is common cause inferred from the observation that failures clustered — the clustering is the trigger for the investigation, never its conclusion. And the model the item names as the test would not settle it either: beta-factor's documented limitations are limited data forcing generic values, screening values that skew, no cross-system modelling, and causes not explicitly modelled at all. A beta factor computed here would be a statistic standing in for the forensic work that has not been done. 14b's own "moderate confidence — the transcripts show the signature but not the gate" is, on this reading, exactly right and should not be revised upward by anything currently in evidence.

### Specific risks

The risk of accepting the inference is a **misdirected repair**. If the fleet concludes that a shared gate exists and that redundancy is therefore nominal, it will act on the most visible candidate — de-conflict the 08:00 slot, add a timeout on permission-gated calls — and then observe the failure rate stay near 35.5%, because the day was at base rate all along and the slot was selected post hoc. Worse, the intervention will be recorded as a fix, and the next unremarkable clustering will read as regression. This is the standard cost of acting on a clustering illusion: real effort spent, no effect, and a false causal belief now embedded in the record.

The second risk is doctrinal and larger. The item's conclusion — "adding another monitor adds no independent evidence" — is a strong claim about the fleet's whole redundancy argument, and it is being drawn from a 4.5%-before-correction event. If that conclusion enters the architecture, every future argument from redundancy will be discounted by a finding that was never established. Note that the item explicitly links this to PRESUMPTION-799 "from the other direction"; if 799 is also resting on weak correlational evidence, the two will mutually reinforce without either having been tested. **That coupling should be checked before either is promoted.**

The risk of dismissing the item is genuine and I want it recorded at full strength, because the arithmetic above does not exonerate the fleet. **A base rate of 35.5% never-finished is a severe reliability problem whether or not the failures are correlated.** The independence question is interesting; the 35.5% is alarming, and it is being discussed as a backdrop rather than as the finding. There is also a specific and cheap thing the item is right about that the arithmetic does not touch: **a stall with no timeout and no artefact is a defect regardless of its cause**, because it produces a failure that leaves no evidence — which is why the denominator needed to test the item's hypothesis does not exist. The absence of timeouts is both a reliability defect and the reason the fleet cannot diagnose its own reliability.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Run the test the clustering literature names, before anything else.** A runs test on the sequence of run outcomes ordered by start time, and a Poisson goodness-of-fit on failures per time window, together cost minutes and decide the question the item is asking. This is the single highest-value action available and it requires no new instrumentation.
(b) **Compute the missing denominator.** Classify all 39 historical never-finished runs by failure signature. If permission-gate stalls are the modal signature, today's four-of-nine is expected; if they are rare historically and spiked today, the item's hypothesis strengthens sharply. This is the observation that would actually move the posterior, and it can be done from existing transcripts.
(c) **Adopt the ICDE structure rather than the beta factor.** For each of the four candidate events, code: event cause, coupling factor, detection method, corrective action, root cause. Four events coded is worth more than any parametric model fitted to 39 uncoded ones, and it directly addresses the gap 14b named — signature without gate.
(d) **Fix the timeout regardless.** A stall with no timeout and no artefact should be repaired on its own merits, independent of the correlation question. It converts every failure into an unanalysable one and is therefore upstream of the fleet's inability to answer this item.
(e) **Do not fit a beta factor** on 39 uncoded failures from a single fleet. The documented limitations — generic data, screening values, no cause modelling — mean the resulting number would be a statistic with no forensic content, and it would be cited later as though it had some.
(f) **De-conflict the 08:00 slot anyway, but as a hypothesis with a stated prediction**: record in advance what failure rate the fleet expects afterwards, and check. Under the null the rate stays at ~35.5%, and that is the cheapest available experiment on the coupling question.

### Recommendation: CHALLENGED

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-806

**Strongest counterargument:** The item's own numbers refute its inference, and the refutation takes about a minute. The health contract records 39 never-finished runs out of 110 — a 35.5% base rate. Today the fleet lost nine of twenty-five, which is 36.0%. The day the item reads as a correlated-failure event is, to within half a percentage point, a completely ordinary day. Under independence at that base rate, three runs in one window all failing is a 4.5% event, and the window was not nominated in advance — it was picked because its runs failed. Across roughly ten candidate windows, the chance that at least one shows an all-fail cluster on a purely independent day is about 37%: more likely than not. The shared-signature observation is weaker still, because it has no denominator anywhere: four of nine failures stalling at a permission-gated call means nothing until someone says what fraction of the historical 39 stalled the same way, and if that is the fleet's usual way of dying then four of nine is precisely what independence predicts. This is the oldest known trap in failure analysis. Poisson clumping is the standing result that random events form clumps with no cause behind them but randomness; the clustering illusion is the cognitive tendency to under-predict how much clumping randomness produces in small samples; and the V-2 rockets over London are the canonical case in which a whole population inferred targeting from impact clusters and a Poisson fit showed chance. The reliability field, which deals with correlated failure professionally, has built its entire method around not making this inference. The NRC requires a shared cause *and* a coupling mechanism within a defined component boundary, and populates its CCF database by having analysts read individual failure reports; ICDE codes each event for cause, coupling factor, detection method and root cause. Clustering is what starts a CCF investigation. It is never what concludes one. And the tool the item names would not settle it either — beta-factor's documented limitations are exactly limited data forcing generic values, screening values that skew results, no cross-system modelling, and causes not explicitly modelled, which is why the current literature is moving to alpha-factor extensions and Bayesian causal treatments. Fitting a beta factor to 39 uncoded fleet failures would produce a number with no forensic content and considerable rhetorical force, which is the worst combination. 14b graded this moderate confidence and said the transcripts show the signature but not the gate. That was the correct grade, and nothing in the day's evidence licenses raising it. What the day *does* license is alarm at a 35.5% never-finished rate, which is a severe problem whether the failures are correlated or not, and which the item treats as background.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** Either the fleet must code its failures forensically — cause, coupling factor, detection method, root cause, per event, on the ICDE pattern — so that a shared gate is *demonstrated* rather than inferred from timing; or it must at minimum run a runs test and a Poisson fit against its own 35.5% base rate before treating any clustering as evidence. And separately from the correlation question: every gated call must carry a timeout that produces an artefact on expiry. Without that, failures leave no record, the denominator needed to test this very item cannot be constructed, and the fleet is in the position of trying to diagnose correlated failure using data whose absence is itself the symptom.

**How to test:** (1) **Runs test plus Poisson goodness-of-fit on the existing 110-run history**, minutes of work, decides whether failures cluster beyond chance across the whole record rather than on one selected day. This should be done before anything else in this file is acted on. (2) **Signature census of all 39 never-finished runs.** If permission-gate stalls are historically modal, today's four-of-nine is unremarkable; if they are historically rare, the item's hypothesis becomes strong and should be promoted immediately. This is the highest-information single action. (3) **The de-confliction experiment with a pre-registered prediction:** move the 08:00 triple apart, state in advance that the null predicts a continuing ~35.5% rate, and check after a fortnight. (4) **Timeout instrumentation as a precondition** for (1)–(3) going forward, since none of these tests improves without failures that leave artefacts.

## SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG

**Affected items:** PRESUMPTION-802, PRESUMPTION-803, PRESUMPTION-804, PRESUMPTION-806, PRESUMPTION-807

**Common vulnerability:** All five items diagnose a real defect and then propose, explicitly or implicitly, **to replace a word with a number** — silence with an override rate, volume with a coverage figure, "determinate" with a definition (and escalation rate as its measure), independence with a beta factor, "backlog" with a service-rate calculation. The deeper pattern: **the fleet is reaching for measurement as the remedy for every epistemic defect, without the accompanying discipline of stating in advance what the measurement's null is and what value would change a decision.** Because 14b's items are graded High risk and read as calls to action, each number is likely to be adopted, and each will then be cited as evidence in exactly the register the items were written to criticise. This is Goodhart at the level of the self-awareness pipeline itself.

**Literature basis:** Inozemtseva & Holmes (ICSE 2014) on coverage as a weak proxy; Goodhart's law as applied to engineering metrics (de Pauw 2022; Wayne); Rose's prevention paradox on the unmeasurability of non-events; the FISC 99%-approval/24.4%-modification disparity as a demonstration of an unidentified oversight metric; Poisson clumping (Aldous 1989; Clarke 1946) and the clustering illusion on small-sample inference; NUREG/CR-6268 and ICDE coding guidelines on cause-and-coupling establishment versus statistical inference; beta-factor model limitations (NRC Module H; SINTEF A26922); Little's law stability and conservation-of-flow assumptions.

**Risk level:** High

**Recommendation:** Adopt a standing rule for the assumption/presumption pipeline: **no measurement is instituted without (i) a stated denominator and its provenance, (ii) a stated null — what the number would read if the hypothesis were false, and (iii) a stated decision rule — what value would change what action.** Any item whose remedy is a number and which cannot supply all three should be recorded as UNTESTABLE-AS-FRAMED rather than promoted.
