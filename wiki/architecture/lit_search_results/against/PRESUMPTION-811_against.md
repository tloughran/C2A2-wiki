### Specific risks (of adopting the corrective as framed)

1. **Licensing the sweep the reviewers avoided.** Any schema that marks some records "type-level" creates a class of entries a cheap reviewer may propagate without verifying at the body. Smith et al. is the measurement of what that costs on mostly-correct artefacts: as likely to break as to fix. **The item's remedy could institutionalise the exact failure its evidence shows six reviewers avoiding.**
2. **Promotion without warrant.** Deciding which entries are type-level *is* the inductive step PREMISE-135 governs, and it owes a population, a termination criterion, and an out-of-sample severe test. A schema field does not supply any of the three; it supplies a checkbox where a warrant is required, and the checkbox will be ticked from the instances seen — which PREMISE-135 explicitly denies is a licence.
3. **A class-wide schema change on a one-day sample.** "Whether the storage form is wrong for the whole class of recorded repairs" is a claim about **every standing note in the vault**, drawn from six same-day observations in one table. PREMISE-135's symmetry clause applies to the replacement: name the worst-covered instance of the *new* schema and go and look at it. That has not been done.
4. **Register inflation and false independence.** PREMISE-135 already holds the epistemic content. Minting 811 separately risks the pipeline later citing 135 and 811 as two independent premises (PREMISE-111 non-independence; PREMISE-105 duplication check).
5. **Misreading a working control as a missing one.** The reviewers verified at the body and declined. If that behaviour is a control, the finding is that the control **works** and should be written down and *cheapened*, not that the register is broken. Rewriting the register is a large change justified by a small and favourable observation.

### Risk of dismissing the item — recorded at full strength

Two things in the item are correct and my challenge does not touch either. **The key form genuinely misleads.** `Levin PRS-30 → 17` looks like a mapping and is not one; a reviewer who has not read the evidence could reasonably act on it, and one run had to state explicitly that "the band table is a list of observed wrong-uses, not a rename table" — the need to say that is itself the defect. **And the discipline is costly and unowned.** 14b records it as the stated cause of the token overrun, and it is held "only by whichever run happens to verify at the body." A control that depends on every future reviewer independently re-deriving it, at cost, on every entry, is not a control — it is luck with good manners, and the six successes are a sample of six. My challenge is about the *direction* of the fix, not about whether something needs fixing.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Fix the label, not the schema.** Rename the register (`observed wrong-uses`, not `corrections`) and change the entry form from `PRS-30 → 17` to something that carries its own scope — e.g. `Day 158, bullet N: PRS-30 used where body says 17`. This removes the misleading affordance at near-zero cost and requires no class-wide change.
(b) **Make "verify at the body" a stated, cheap step rather than an unwritten virtue.** The control worked six times; write it into the reviewer procedure so it does not depend on rediscovery. This addresses the real risk (cost and unownedness) without touching storage form.
(c) **Do not add a type-level tier without PREMISE-135's three requirements.** If any entry is ever promoted to rule status, it must carry a stated population, a stated termination criterion, and **one severe test on a day that was not among the instances that produced it**. Absent all three, the entry stays instance-scoped — which is the safe default and the standard form.
(d) **Adopt the standards' separation explicitly.** IEEE 1044 / ODC shape: record the instance, attach type attributes for aggregate analysis, and treat propagation as a separate decision with its own evidence. This gives 14b the type-level *analysis* he wants while denying the type-level *authority* that is the risk.
(e) **Before any class-wide claim, count.** Enumerate the standing notes in the vault and classify each as instance-evidenced or population-evidenced. If most are instance-evidenced and correctly consumed, 811's broad claim is over-drawn; if many are being consumed as rules, the count is the finding and justifies the schema work. **This has not been done and is cheap.**
(f) **Route to PREMISE-135 rather than minting fresh**, recording 811 as a field observation confirming 135 plus a labelling defect in one table.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-811

**Strongest counterargument (the strongest case against the item's direction, at full force):** The item watched a control work six times in one day and filed it as a schema defect. Read the evidence as written: six independent runs encountered a recorded correction, checked the body, found the id correct there, and declined to propagate. Zero corruptions. In one of those cases the standing note proposed *opposite* fixes on different days, so a sweep would have broken both — meaning the register's entries are not merely under-scoped, they are **mutually inconsistent at type level and individually correct at instance level**, which is exactly what a set of honest observations looks like and exactly what a rule cannot look like. The item takes this and concludes the storage form is wrong. The literature concludes the opposite, and it has numbers. Smith, Barr, Le Goues and Brun evaluated two repair tools on 998 bugs, built patches against one test suite, and — crucially — evaluated them against a held-out suite the patches had never seen. Patches that satisfied their construction evidence did not generalise; patch quality tracked the coverage of the suite used to build it; and for programs that already passed most tests, **the tools were as likely to break tests as to fix them**. A recorded correction in the C2A2 band table is a patch, its single wrong bullet is the construction evidence, and the rest of the vault is the held-out set. It is a mostly-correct artefact. Propagating by id is the generalisation step the paper measures, and the measured expectation is break-even at best. The authors add, honestly, that novice humans overfit the same way and that automated repair does no worse — which is not an exoneration of the register, it is a warning that the *inference*, not the tooling, is the hazard, and cheap reviewers are exactly the population that makes it. The standards agree and have agreed since the early nineties. IEEE 1044 exists to give "a uniform approach to the classification of software **anomalies**" with "a core set of **attributes**" — an anomaly is an instance, and its type is an attribute hung on it for causal analysis and process improvement. ODC groups defects into types explicitly to turn the defect stream into *a measurement on the process*, not into a set of instructions for editing other artefacts. The separation of instance-as-evidence from type-as-analysis is not an oversight in defect tracking; it is defect tracking's central design decision, taken deliberately, and C2A2's register already conforms to it. And the register has settled the epistemics itself. PREMISE-135 is ACTIVE and states that terminality is purchased by enumerating the domain, not by accumulating instances; that there is no schema-level warrant for "generalise from the instances seen"; and that a claim of terminal generality owes a population, a termination criterion, and one severe test on a kind that was not among the instances that produced the abstraction. It is grounded on Norton's material theory of induction, Lakatos, Mitchell's version spaces — where the specific boundary is the conservative hypothesis and contraction is monotonic — and Mayo on severity. If PREMISE-135 is being enforced, the six reviewers were performing it, the register's instance-scoped entries are the specific boundary correctly held, and the true finding of the day is **that a validated premise is operating in the field**. That is a better finding than a schema defect, and it is being thrown away. What remains, and I do not want it lost in the argument, is small and real: `Levin PRS-30 → 17` *looks* like a rename rule, and one run had to write a sentence explaining that it is not. That is a **labelling defect in one table** — fix the label and the entry form, write "verify at the body" into the reviewer procedure so the control stops depending on rediscovery, and the cost 14b correctly identifies goes away. What must not happen is the proposed direction: a schema that marks some entries type-level, because that is a licence to sweep issued to precisely the cheap future reviewer the item is worried about, ticked from the instances seen, which PREMISE-135 says is no warrant at all.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** (1) Instance-scoped storage remains the **default**, and any promotion to type level satisfies PREMISE-135's three conditions — stated population, stated termination criterion, and **one severe test on a day not among the instances that produced the entry**. (2) The register's **entry form carries its own scope on its face** (`Day 158, bullet N: ...`), so no reader can mistake an observation for a rule. (3) "Verify at the body before propagating" is a **written, cheap procedural step**, not an unwritten virtue held by whichever run happens to be careful. (4) Type-level information, if wanted, is kept as **analysis attributes** on instances (the IEEE 1044 / ODC shape) and carries **no propagation authority**. If all four hold, the six near-misses become structurally impossible without any class-wide schema change, and the vault's standing notes remain what the evidence says they are.

**How to test:** (1) **The held-out test, directly borrowed from Smith et al. and the decisive experiment here.** Take a sample of standing-note entries. For each, apply the correction *as if it were a rule* to every co-named instance in the vault, **on a scratch copy**, then have an independent reader check each affected site against the body. Count fixes and breaks. Prediction under my challenge: on a mostly-correct corpus the counts are comparable, reproducing the paper's result and settling the direction question empirically in-house. Prediction under 14b: sweeps are overwhelmingly correct and the six refusals were over-caution. **Either result is decisive and neither requires new literature.** (2) **The enumeration test.** Classify every standing note in the vault as instance-evidenced or population-evidenced, and separately as consumed-as-observation or consumed-as-rule. The claim "every standing note is a set of instances wearing the costume of a rule" is a census question and has never been counted. (3) **The severity test the item needs and did not run.** The High grade requires that one corruption be expensive enough to outweigh a zero-out-of-six observed rate. State the cost of one propagated corruption and the cost of the verification discipline per entry, and compare. 14b names the discipline as the cause of the token overrun, so the denominator is available. (4) **The affordance test.** Show a reviewer who has not read the evidence an entry in the current form and an entry in the proposed scoped form, and ask what each licenses. If the current form reliably reads as a rule, the labelling defect is confirmed and the cheap fix is justified without any schema change.
