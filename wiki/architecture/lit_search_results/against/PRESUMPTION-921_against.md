  Summary: Every major account of what a tradition or research community is — moral philosophy
  (MacIntyre), philosophy of science (Kuhn, Lakatos), sociology of science (Crane, Merton, Collins) —
  identifies the tradition with a community carrying an argument, not with an author. MacIntyre's living
  tradition is constituted by internal disagreement; Kuhn's paradigm is what a community shares; Lakatos's
  programme is judged on the protective belt its followers build; Crane's invisible college and Collins's
  networks are the empirical and structural forms of the same point. Merton's obliteration by
  incorporation adds a bibliometric twist: a successful founder is cited less as the tradition matures, so
  a founder-only corpus undercounts precisely the traditions that have become schools. The presumption is
  therefore not a simplification of the standard view but a contradiction of it; and its consequence in
  C2A2 — the Stage 2 intra-tradition consensus metric being undefined at N=1 — is not a corner case but
  the metric correctly reporting that the object it was designed for is absent.

  Specific risks: (a) The intra-tradition consensus metric is meaningless for all 14 traditions, not just
  for some; any Stage 2 output that relies on it is measuring nothing. (b) Where a named thinker's
  position has been substantially revised or extended by collaborators (the Friston programme is the
  clearest case: the active-inference belt is largely built by others), the wiki records the hard core and
  misses the belt, so "the tradition says X" is really "the founder once said X". (c) Cross-tradition
  comparison (Stage 3 and above) inherits the error: comparing Levin-the-author to Friston-the-author is
  not comparing traditions, and any convergence found may be a convergence of two individuals'
  rhetorical habits. (d) Obliteration means the founder's corpus is least representative for the most
  established traditions (Friston, Carroll, Wolfram), so the error is anti-correlated with the
  tradition's importance. (e) Traditions with dead or inactive founders (Rohr as popular teacher, Wright)
  are frozen at the founder's last text; the tradition's live activity is invisible.

  Mitigations available: Redefine the tradition unit as founder + a bounded belt: for each named thinker,
  enumerate the N most-cited co-authors and critical interlocutors (Crane's invisible college, bounded by
  Collins's small-numbers law at 3–6), and include their primary texts in the tradition corpus. Rename the
  present wikis "author wikis" and reserve "tradition" for the enlarged unit. Redefine the Stage 2
  intra-tradition consensus metric over founder-vs-belt agreement, which is well-defined and is in fact
  the interesting quantity (Lakatos's progressive/degenerating distinction lives there). Where a belt
  cannot be enumerated (Rohr, Loughran), mark the tradition as N=1 explicitly and exclude it from
  consensus metrics rather than reporting undefined.

STEELMAN:
  Item: PRESUMPTION-921
  Strongest counterargument: There is no account of traditions, schools or research programmes in the
  philosophy or sociology of knowledge on which a tradition is an author. Every one of them makes the
  community, and specifically the community's internal argument, constitutive: MacIntyre's living
  tradition is an argument; Kuhn's paradigm is what is shared; Lakatos's programme is the belt the
  followers build; Crane and Collins show empirically that the work is done by a network. And Merton
  shows that the founder's own bibliography is the worst place to look for a successful tradition,
  because success is measured by the founder ceasing to be cited. The C2A2 tradition wikis are therefore
  author wikis, and the Stage 2 consensus metric returning undefined at N=1 is not a bug in the metric;
  it is the metric reporting that the object it was designed to measure is not present.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Either the system's use of "tradition" is stipulative
  and every downstream consumer knows it means "author" (in which case the Stage 2 metric and all
  cross-tradition claims must be rephrased as cross-author claims), or each named thinker is in fact a
  singleton whose position has no belt — which is false for at least Friston, Levin, Hoffman, Carroll,
  Wolfram and Arkani-Hamed, all of whom have named collaborators building on the core.
  How to test: For each of the 14 traditions, count primary texts by the named thinker vs by co-authors
  and named interlocutors in the tradition's last five years of output (Google Scholar / OpenAlex). If
  the founder's share of the tradition's recent output is below ~50% for a majority of the 14, the
  author-as-tradition model misrepresents the majority of C2A2's own corpus. Separately, apply Merton's
  test: in the most recent 50 papers using the tradition's core term ("active inference",
  "bioelectric code", "conscious agents"), count those that do not cite the founder.
