Solution-specific information alone is insufficient for practitioners, who ALSO require resource capacity and capable personnel. The word is "also": the literature treats knowledge and capacity as jointly necessary, not as competing diagnoses. This challenges the either/or structure that 482 and 513 jointly impose.
    2. Theory of Constraints, standard exposition (Wikipedia; Six Sigma Study Guide; Stratrix; retrieved 2026-07-21). Most constraints are not physical but arise from policy and practice, and policy constraints are harder to identify and to manage. On balance this favours 513's diagnosis over 482's. But TOC also holds that the constraint is what limits throughput "even when nothing goes wrong," and that after elevating one constraint the constraint moves — so a system may have a genuine information constraint behind a policy constraint, and naming one does not retire the other.
    3. Alert-fatigue and override literature (Nanji et al., PMID 24166725; workload effects, PMC5387195; alert-fatigue measurement systematic review, PMID 42148822; retrieved 2026-07-21). Override rates of 49–96% and acceptance falling with volume support the attention-constraint reading. But the systematic review notes that alert fatigue is routinely blamed for override rates without the mechanism being established — a caution against asserting the attention diagnosis on the same evidentiary footing the item denies to the enumeration diagnosis.
    4. CAPA effectiveness-check literature (Deltek; The FDA Group; retrieved 2026-07-21). Findings recur when the effectiveness check is dropped, which describes a process constraint distinct from both attention and authority — a third candidate the item's binary does not admit.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: The item is on stronger ground than ASSUMPTION-482 and is nonetheless over-committed. Theory of Constraints does favour it: in knowledge work the constraint is usually a policy — an approval step, an authority boundary — rather than a missing input, and policy constraints are the ones organisations habitually fail to see. Fifteen days of cheap identified unexecuted actions is real evidence, and the override-rate literature supplies a plausible mechanism. But the implementation-science literature is explicit that solution-specific information and resource capacity are jointly required, which means "attention or authority, NOT enumeration" imposes a dichotomy the field does not recognise, and the correct reading is that both can bind at once. Two further qualifications. The alert-fatigue systematic review warns that the attention diagnosis is habitually asserted without its mechanism being demonstrated — the same evidentiary standard the item applies to 482 should apply to itself. And the CAPA literature offers a third candidate the binary excludes: the constraint may be a missing effectiveness-check step, which is neither attention nor authority and has a different, cheaper fix. On the falsification claim, the item overstates: it says a single executed measurement falsifies it immediately, but one execution in fifteen days is consistent with a severe attention constraint that occasionally admits an item, so a single execution weakens the item without settling it.
  Specific risks: If the item is right and unaddressed, the pipeline diagnoses indefinitely and each diagnosis registers as progress — the risk as stated. If the item is over-applied, C2A2 concludes that identifying cheap actions is worthless and stops producing discriminating tests, which would remove the one output that is currently cheap and correct while leaving the attention constraint untouched. Third risk: the constraint is a missing process step, and both the attention and enumeration diagnoses miss it.
  Mitigations available: Reject the either/or and hold both. Run the item's ratio measurement (cheap actions identified versus executed over 30 days) alongside 482's execution of one measurement; the two tests are complementary and cost the same. Do not treat a single execution as decisive — pre-register the threshold, e.g. three of five executed within fourteen days, before running. Distinguish attention from authority from process, since the three have different fixes: attention wants triage, authority wants a delegation rule, process wants an effectiveness check. Apply the item's own standard to itself and demonstrate the mechanism rather than assert it.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-513
    Strongest counterargument: The item wins the argument against 482 and then makes the same error in the opposite direction. Theory of Constraints does support it — in knowledge work the binding constraint is usually a policy, an approval boundary or an authority limit rather than a missing measurement, and policy constraints are the ones organisations most reliably fail to identify from the inside — and fifteen days of cheap, identified, unexecuted actions is exactly the observational signature. But the implementation-science literature the item invokes does not say that capacity replaces knowledge as the constraint; it says solution-specific information is insufficient BECAUSE practitioners also require resource capacity and capable personnel. Both are necessary; neither is the constraint in the exclusive sense the item's "not enumeration" demands. So the framing that makes 513 a rival to 482 is itself the weakest part of 513, and a pipeline that accepts it would swing from "we need more measurements" to "measurements are pointless" without either position being established. There is also a self-application problem: the alert-fatigue systematic review notes that attention-based explanations for non-action are routinely asserted without the mechanism being demonstrated, which is the precise complaint 513 lodges against 482. And the falsification claim is too generous to itself in one direction and too harsh in another — a single executed measurement in fifteen days is compatible with a severe attention constraint that occasionally lets something through, so it does not falsify the item immediately, while a measurement executed with no downstream disposition change would falsify 482 and 513 together, pointing at a third constraint neither names.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: That the two diagnoses are held simultaneously rather than adjudicated, and that the falsification threshold is pre-registered as a ratio over a window rather than as a single event.
    How to test: One experiment settles the pair. Execute exactly one of the five named measurements and record two outcomes: whether it was executed at all within fourteen days, and whether any disposition changed within seven days after. Not executed favours 513. Executed and a disposition changed favours 482. Executed with no disposition change falsifies both and indicates a missing effectiveness-check step. Pre-register the threshold (e.g. three of five executed in fourteen days) before starting.
