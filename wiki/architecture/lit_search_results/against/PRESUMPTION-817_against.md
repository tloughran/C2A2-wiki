### Summary

The item finds a real gap and proposes three remedies, of which two are challenged and one is right for a reason the item does not give. The refutation rate is the weakest. Retraction rates are the field's canonical example of a quality indicator confounded with detection effort: Fang and Casadevall's retraction index correlates strongly and positively with journal impact factor, and the standing interpretation is that high rates may reflect intense post-publication scrutiny rather than worse work. Transferred here the confound is not a caveat but a disqualification — a refutation rate on 14a/14b output rises when 15a/15b/15c work harder and falls when the lit pipeline is idle, so its denominator is the fleet's scrutiny budget and it measures the auditor while carrying the audited's name. It is also gameable in both directions simultaneously: hedge the headlines and it falls, skip a cycle and it falls again, which under Goodhart makes a published counter an incentive to overturn less. The arithmetic is worse than the confound. PREMISE-124 forbids self-measurement of the pipeline's own accuracy without an external baseline or seeded denominator, and PREMISE-136 holds that a quantity scoped to one run of a daily pipeline has a single-digit denominator by construction with pooling not a free route. The item's own two points, entered as first values, read 2/2 = 100% — and the item says "that is not a rate" while proposing a field to keep it in, which is precisely how a non-rate becomes a rate three weeks later. The hypothesis register fares slightly better but still fails on shape. The whole body of practice governing operational records that must later carry evidential weight has converged on append-only: INSERT only, no UPDATE or DELETE, WORM under SEC 17a-4, and a mutable log failing SOX no matter how detailed its entries. Reaching back into a prior night's changelog to mark a finding withdrawn is the operation that practice exists to prohibit; the prescribed alternative is to leave the record standing and append a dated, addressed correction that references it. And this vault already holds that alternative in the right form: PREMISE-117's publish-then-revise, with a revision flag, a described change, a pre-committed corrections policy, and withdrawal reserved for output not fit for purpose. Hedging carries a measured cost in actionability, and PREMISE-121 makes it acute here — degradation tracks low-information items, and the changelog is Tom's principal summary of thirteen unattended days, so the fastest way to make it not get read is to hedge it. Fischhoff's counterweight is recorded honestly: decision-makers who are not told how shaky the evidence is must guess. The reconciliation is per-claim calibrated markers rather than a wholesale change of register. Finally, the two events the item counts are better described than counted: a pipeline that overturns its own headline within one cycle has a correction latency of about a day, against PREMISE-123's ~17-year benchmark for knowledge reaching practice. Same events, opposite sign. **What survives, and is strengthened rather than weakened by this search, is clause (C).** PREMISE-145 clause (5) already requires that a withheld figure be logged with its value, its stated reason, and the timestamp at which the reason was identified relative to the disagreement — a withdrawal record, obligatory, for measurements. No equivalent exists for findings. That is a live inconsistency in the register, the item found it, and it is the part of the item that should be acted on.

### Specific risks

**If the refutation rate is instituted:** the fleet publishes a number that moves inversely with the quality of its own auditing, and it will be read as a quality signal because it is named like one. The first-order damage is a false reading; the second-order damage is the incentive. A team that watches "findings overturned since" go up when its lit pipeline gets sharper will, without anyone deciding to, hedge its headlines and defer its refutations. That is Goodhart operating on the one mechanism the item itself calls "the design working," and it would be installed by an item written to protect it.

**If the changelog moves to hypothesis register:** the single artefact Tom is most likely to read on return becomes longer, more qualified and harder to act on, at a moment when PREMISE-121 says reviewer response is most sensitive to information density. The predictable outcome is not better-calibrated trust; it is that the summary is skimmed. And the item's stated fear — the most-trusted register being the least qualified — is not answered by making it less readable, only by making its corrections findable.

**If the withdrawal field is added by mutating the changelog:** the fleet loses the one property that makes the changelog usable as evidence later. Every downstream citation of a prior night becomes ambiguous between "what was written then" and "what it was later amended to say," and PREMISE-145 clause (5)'s whole point — that **timestamp order is what makes stopping bias auditable** — is destroyed by the very field intended to serve it.

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength, and it is substantial.** Clause (C) is correct and the register makes it sharper than the item does: the metrics template has fields for items added and items tested and none for items withdrawn, while PREMISE-145 clause (5) already obliges a withdrawal record for withheld measurements. The fleet is therefore in the position of having validated an obligation and not built the field for it — and PREMISE-118's rule applies, that naming a defect in an instrument triggers an obligation rather than licensing continued use. There is also a real asymmetry PREMISE-145 clause (1) already documents from the other side: Henrion and Fischhoff's bandwagon effect and Jeng's PDG analysis show that deference to incumbent published figures produces slow, self-concealing drift with artificially tight error bars. A changelog series whose overturns leave no trace is exactly the structure that produces such drift. **The item is right that the absence matters; it is wrong about what to put there.**

### Mitigations available

(a) **Build the correction register PREMISE-117 already specifies, and do not touch the changelog.** Append-only: a dated correction object that names the superseded claim, the reason, and the run that overturned it. This satisfies clause (C), preserves evidential integrity, and is the shape both official-statistics practice and audit-log practice prescribe.
(b) **Do not publish a refutation rate.** If a count is kept at all, keep it under PREMISE-124's UNCALIBRATED tag with its denominator and its scrutiny caveat stated in the same line, and forbid its conversion to a percentage under PREMISE-168.
(c) **Publish correction latency instead, which is the unconfounded quantity.** Median days from a headline being written to it being overturned. It measures the thing the item cares about, it improves when the pipeline improves, and it is not gameable by hedging.
(d) **Add per-claim confidence markers, not a change of register.** The changelog's declarative voice is an asset under PREMISE-121; a one-word marker on individual claims ("measured" / "inferred" / "single-source") gets Fischhoff's benefit without the actionability cost.
(e) **Extend PREMISE-145 clause (5) from measurements to findings explicitly**, since the obligation already exists for the neighbouring object and the inconsistency is the item's real discovery. Log the withdrawn finding's content, the reason, and **the timestamp order** — clause (5)'s whole point, and the thing a "findings overturned since" counter would not capture.
(f) **Record the two events as the design working, once, in prose**, rather than as the first entries of a rate. Two points is not a base rate and PREMISE-136 says it will not become one at this cadence any time soon.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-817

**Strongest counterargument:** The item has found a real hole and proposes to fill it with the two instruments least suited to the job, while the vault already holds the right one unbuilt. Take the refutation rate first, because it is the load-bearing proposal. Retraction rate is the canonical worked example of a quality indicator that is not one: Fang and Casadevall's retraction index correlates strongly and *positively* with journal impact factor, and the standing reading of that correlation is that a high rate signals intense post-publication scrutiny rather than worse science. Transfer it and the transfer is fatal. A refutation rate over 14a/14b output goes *up* when 15a, 15b and 15c work harder or get sharper, and *down* when the lit pipeline is idle, over budget, or simply skipped. Its denominator is the fleet's own scrutiny effort. It is a measurement of the auditor wearing the auditee's name, and it is gameable in two directions at once — hedge every headline and it falls, skip a cycle and it falls again — so a published "findings overturned since" counter creates a standing incentive to overturn less, which is Goodhart operating directly on the mechanism the item calls "the design working." The arithmetic finishes the argument before the confound needs to. PREMISE-124 forbids self-measurement of the pipeline's own accuracy without an external baseline or a seeded denominator, and PREMISE-136 holds that a quantity scoped to one run of a daily pipeline carries a single-digit denominator by construction with no rephrasing available and pooling not free. The item's two observations, entered as first values, read 2/2 — a hundred per cent refutation rate, the most misleading number this fleet could publish. The item says outright "that is not a rate" and then asks for a field to keep it in, which is exactly the mechanism by which a non-rate becomes a rate three weeks later and is cited as one. The hypothesis register fails on shape rather than on principle. Every body of practice governing operational records that must later bear evidential weight has converged on append-only — INSERT only, no UPDATE or DELETE, WORM storage under SEC 17a-4, and a log in a mutable store failing SOX no matter how detailed its entries — because a record you can reach back into is a record whose past state is unreconstructable. Marking a prior night's finding withdrawn *in that night's changelog* is the precise operation that practice exists to forbid, and it would destroy the property PREMISE-145 clause (5) says matters most: that timestamp order is what makes stopping bias auditable. Hedging has its own price, measured in actionability, and PREMISE-121 makes the price acute — degradation of reviewer response tracks low-information items, and this changelog is Tom's principal summary of thirteen unattended days, so the surest way to make it unqualified is to make it unfinished. Fischhoff's counterweight is real and belongs in the file: decision-makers not told how shaky the evidence is are forced to guess. But that argues for calibrated per-claim markers, not for changing a document's voice. And the two events themselves are better named than counted: overturning a headline within one cycle is a **one-day correction latency**, against PREMISE-123's benchmark of a ~17-year median for validated knowledge reaching governed practice. Reported as a rate it is a defect count; reported as latency it is the system's best property. The part of the item that survives all of this is the part it states most quietly, and the register makes it sharper than the item does: PREMISE-145 clause (5) already *obliges* a withdrawal record — value, reason, timestamp — for a withheld measurement, and no equivalent field exists for a withdrawn finding. The fleet has validated an obligation and not built the slot. That is the finding, and the remedy is PREMISE-117's correction register, which is already specified and already unbuilt.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** The withdrawal record is safe and obligatory. The *rate* is safe only if it acquires an external baseline or a seeded denominator (PREMISE-124), states its scrutiny denominator explicitly, is never converted to a percentage (PREMISE-168), and is never used as a quality signal for the extraction layer — four conditions that together suggest not computing it. The hypothesis register is safe only if it is implemented as per-claim confidence markers on individual assertions rather than as a wholesale change of the document's voice, and only if the changelog remains append-only with corrections appended as separate dated objects that reference what they supersede. The distinction that must hold throughout is between **mutating a record** (forbidden) and **appending a correction** (required). If the fleet cannot maintain that distinction in whatever it builds, it is safer to build nothing, because a mutable changelog is worse than an uncorrected one.

**How to test:** (1) **Compute correction latency instead of refutation rate** for the existing two events and any future ones. It is the same data, it is unconfounded with scrutiny effort, and it is not gameable by hedging — this is the single substitution that resolves most of the file. (2) **Test the confound directly and cheaply**: correlate refutations-per-week against lit-pipeline runs-per-week. If the correlation is strong and positive, the rate is measuring scrutiny and must not be published as a quality indicator; this is one query over the existing record. (3) **Readability test on the register change**: produce one nightly changelog in hypothesis register alongside the declarative version and ask which one Tom finishes. n=1, but it is the n that matters, and PREMISE-121's mechanism predicts the answer. (4) **Discharge PREMISE-145 clause (5) for findings**: for the two known overturns, retrospectively log content, reason and timestamp order in an appended correction object. If that record turns out to be useful within a fortnight, the field is justified on evidence rather than on principle.
