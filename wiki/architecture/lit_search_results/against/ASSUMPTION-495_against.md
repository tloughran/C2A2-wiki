  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The literature challenges the *strong, unqualified* reading of ASSUMPTION-495. Convergence per se is not a pathology; the failure mode is imposed or premature convergence that collapses diversity before it has done its work. There is an optimum, not a monotone "divergence good, convergence bad." A Rung-2 metric that rewarded pure divergence would be as miscalibrated as one rewarding pure convergence. The challenge does not touch the weaker claim (a convergence-only metric is miscalibrated), which survives.

  Specific risks: If C2A2 over-corrects and designs a metric rewarding maintained divergence as such, it risks rewarding incoherence and unresolved disagreement, defeating the point of cross-tradition dialogue.

  Mitigations available: Score the *process* (independent evidence exchange, coupling) rather than either endpoint; distinguish earned convergence from imposed convergence; measure whether convergence followed information transfer (the +0.031 listening lift should be re-analyzed as coupling vs convergence, as the in-house test proposes).

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-495
    Strongest counterargument: The claim smuggles a false dichotomy. Levin's point is about *forced* homogenization removing the local degrees of freedom that let sub-agents solve problems — it is not a claim that agreement is bad. In a well-designed collective, convergence on a correct answer after genuine information pooling is the signal of success, and the Condorcet mechanism requires that independent signals be *aggregated* (a form of convergence). Treating convergence itself as pathological would forbid the collective from ever concluding anything.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The Rung-2 metric must reward earned convergence (post-information-exchange) and penalize only imposed/premature convergence and diversity collapse — a process metric, not an endpoint metric.
    How to test: Re-analyze the +0.031 listening lift to separate coupling (mutual responsiveness) from convergence (answer agreement); a Rung-2 metric should track the former.
