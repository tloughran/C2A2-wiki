  Sources:
    1. IAASB, International Standard on Auditing (ISA) 240, "The Auditor's Responsibilities Relating to Fraud in an Audit of Financial Statements" (and revised 2024/2025 edition). — Requires the auditor to incorporate an element of unpredictability in the nature, timing and extent of audit procedures, on the explicit ground that individuals familiar with the procedures normally performed are better able to conceal misstatement. This directly contradicts the normative reading of the item — that an inspection selector ought to be reproducible run-to-run. Under ISA 240 a fully deterministic, reproducible QC queue is a *weakness*, not a guarantee.
    2. Knight, J.C. & Leveson, N.G. 1986. "An Experimental Evaluation of the Assumption of Independence in Multiversion Programming." IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering SE-12(1):96–109. — 27 versions written independently from one specification showed roughly 50% common faults; the independence hypothesis was rejected at 99% confidence. Challenges the item's aggregation: five reports produced from the same corpus by agents sharing a definition of "residue" are correlated estimators, so their agreement on 90–96% is weaker evidence than five independent measurements would be, and a shared definitional error would be invisible.
    3. "Haphazard Sampling: Selection Biases and the Estimation Consequences of These Biases." Current Issues in Auditing, American Accounting Association, 7(2):P16. — Shows that haphazard (non-probabilistic, non-reproducible-but-not-random) selection produces estimates with unpredictable error and does not carry the assurance of statistical sampling. This is a *bounded* challenge to the item: it locates the real defect not in non-determinism per se but in whether the selection is probabilistically characterised. A random selector with a stable frame is fine; a haphazard one is not.
    4. Halderman, J.A. et al. "In Defense of Pseudorandom Sample Selection." EVT'08 (USENIX/ACCURATE Electronic Voting Technology Workshop). — Argues for pseudorandom (seeded, therefore reproducible *and* unpredictable-in-advance) selection in audit contexts, showing that reproducibility and unpredictability are not in conflict and can be obtained together. Challenges the framing that the queue must choose between an auditable fixed list and an unreproducible one. [author list beyond Halderman not confirmed in this search]
    5. Luo, Q., Hariri, F., Eloussi, L., Marinov, D. 2014. "An Empirical Analysis of Flaky Tests." FSE 2014, pp. 643–653. — Documents that order dependency and concurrency, not selection logic, dominate observed non-determinism. Suggests a competing explanation for two queues four minutes apart: the underlying frame changed (a concurrent writer, a mid-window ingest), in which case the selector is faithfully reporting a moving population rather than being unstable itself.
    6. PCAOB AU Section 350, "Audit Sampling." — Standard-setting text on sampling risk; establishes that coverage assurance in audit is probabilistic and expressed as sampling risk, not as a guarantee that a given item is inspected. Challenges the item's implicit standard of a deterministic coverage guarantee.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The observation stands — two calls four minutes apart returning 31 and 24 days with nine in common is a real measurement of instability. What the literature challenges is the inference that this defeats audit coverage. ISA 240 requires unpredictability in inspection selection as a fraud control, and PCAOB AU 350 frames coverage assurance as probabilistic rather than deterministic, so "no other run agrees this queue exists" is not by itself a defect. The genuine defect the literature does support is different and narrower: an unreproducible selector with an uncharacterised selection probability is *haphazard* sampling, whose error is unpredictable and which carries no assurance — that is the American Accounting Association finding, and it is fixable by seeding rather than by freezing the queue. Separately, the 90–96% residue figure rests on treating five reports as independent, which Knight & Leveson's result gives strong reason to doubt when the reports share a corpus and a definition.

  Specific risks: If C2A2 responds by making the QC queue deterministic and reproducible, it removes the unpredictability that ISA 240 identifies as a control and creates a queue whose contents any agent — or any degenerate generation process — can anticipate and satisfy, which is a worse failure mode than non-reproducibility. If it instead accepts the 90–96% residue figure as five-fold corroborated and sizes a remediation effort on it, a shared definitional error in "residue" propagates undetected into the plan. If the true cause of the two divergent queues is a concurrent writer rather than the selector, fixing the selector leaves the frame drift in place and the fix will appear to work while the population continues to move under every run.

  Mitigations available: Replace unseeded selection with seeded pseudorandom selection — the queue becomes reproducible from its recorded seed while remaining unpredictable in advance (Halderman et al.), satisfying both the audit-trail and the anti-gaming requirement. Record the frame snapshot (the day list and its hash) alongside the selection so "which queue did this run see" is answerable after the fact. Express coverage as sampling risk over a window rather than as per-run agreement. Re-derive the residue percentage once from a single explicit definition and treat the five reports as one estimate with an unknown correlated error, or have one report use a deliberately different operationalisation to break the correlation. Instrument the frame directly to distinguish selector instability from population drift.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-1132
    Strongest counterargument: The item treats reproducibility of the inspection queue as an unexamined good, but the profession that has thought hardest about inspection coverage takes the opposite view: ISA 240 requires an element of unpredictability precisely because a queue everyone can predict is a queue everyone can prepare for. So "a run can review a queue no other run agrees exists" is, in auditing terms, a feature — it is what surprise sampling looks like from the inside. The real question the item does not ask is whether the selection probability is characterised: a seeded random draw from a stable frame is both unpredictable and fully reconstructible, and gives probabilistic coverage guarantees, whereas an unseeded draw from a drifting frame is haphazard and gives none. And the 90–96% residue headline leans on five reports being independent, which the Knight & Leveson result says co-designed estimators are not; if all five inherited the same definition of residue, the agreement is close to vacuous and the whole severity assessment moves.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the selection probability of each work item is known, so coverage can be stated as a sampling risk rather than hoped for; (b) the frame is snapshotted and hashed per run, so divergence between two calls can be attributed to the selector or to the population, not left ambiguous; (c) at least one residue estimate is produced under an operationally different definition, so a shared definitional error would show up as disagreement; (d) any move toward reproducibility preserves unpredictability-in-advance (seeded, seed revealed after selection) rather than fixing the queue.
    How to test: Call the selector K times in a tight loop while holding the underlying corpus provably still (freeze writes, or snapshot and hash the day list before and after). If the queues still diverge with an identical frame hash, the selector is non-deterministic; if the frame hash changes, the population is drifting and the selector is innocent. Then, for the residue figure: have one agent recompute residue from a written-down definition produced without reference to the existing five reports, and compare. A gap larger than the spread of the five reports is direct evidence that the five were correlated rather than independent.
