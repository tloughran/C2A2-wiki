Evidential genres do not transfer between communities by exhibition; they must be socially established.
    4. Cappelen, H., 2012. "Philosophy Without Intuitions." OUP. — Disputes even the assumed evidential role of intuitions within philosophy, illustrating how contested evidence-status claims are inside the discipline — let alone for an engineered system imported from outside its norms.
  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: The reception history of empirical methods in philosophy challenges the transfer presumption directly. Experimental philosophy — methodologically far closer to philosophy's home practices than a systems-engineering commissioning report — has spent twenty years fighting for evidential standing and still faces the "doesn't show anything deep" objection and Williamson-style expertise defenses. Shapin & Schaffer show the deeper point: the commissioning-report genre works in physics because that community already agrees instruments are evidence; that agreement is the product of a specific historical settlement, not a property of working systems. A philosophy audience has no such settlement; the default reception of "here is a running system, therefore P" is to grant the system's existence while denying the inferential step to any philosophical conclusion (category error / "it works" ≠ "it's true").
  Specific risks: ISME's central rhetorical device fails with its intended audience: the report is read as engineering documentation, not argument; reviewers reject it as misunderstanding what philosophical evidence is; the genuine philosophical content is discounted along with the genre.
  Mitigations available: Pair the system with an explicit argument for WHY the system's behavior bears on the thesis (make the bridge premise a defended premise, not a presumed genre convention); frame the system as existence proof or counterexample-generator (roles philosophy does accept) rather than as confirming evidence; target venues already friendly to computational philosophy (e.g., synthetic philosophy, philosophy of AI) first.
  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: Philosophy does accept artifacts in specific roles: existence proofs defeat impossibility claims (a running system showing X is possible refutes "X is impossible" decisively), and computational models are increasingly cited in philosophy of mind/science (e.g., simulation results in social epistemology). If ISME's claims are possibility-shaped ("such a measurement practice can exist and cohere"), a commissioning report is exactly the right evidence and the audience-resistance literature about deep normative claims does not apply.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: ISME's theses are scoped as possibility/feasibility claims, or the bridge from system behavior to philosophical conclusion is explicitly argued; the target venue has prior tolerance for computational/empirical method.
    How to test: Circulate a precis to 2-3 philosophers of the target audience before committing the schedule; ask specifically whether the system functions as evidence for them and for which claims. Their answers test genre transfer cheaply and directly.
