    1. Latent ties (Haythornthwaite 2002). - Formed ties are frequently UNUSED; bridge existence does not entail bridge traffic, so coils may form without elevated sharing.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The hypothesis is plausible but faces standard network-causality challenges: bridges can be latent (formed, never trafficked); observed cross-A-B sharing after coil formation can be confounded by homophily or shared topicality rather than caused by the coil; and the chosen degree-matched null may be too weak to rule out topical/temporal artifacts. These do not refute H1 but show it can produce a false-positive 'effect' under inadequate controls.

  Specific risks: The falsifier could 'confirm' H1 on the basis of confounded or artifactual flow, validating the coil mechanism spuriously.

  Mitigations available: Add topic/time/author-matched nulls (not degree-only); test for latent (formed-but-unused) coils; use pre/post within-pair designs to net out homophily.
