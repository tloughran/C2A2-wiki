### Polarity note — what I am challenging

The item's *defective* belief is "a warning is a weaker failure." **14b's corrective — the proposition I searched AGAINST — is: the pass/warn/fail schema cannot express the difference between a small defect found, an instrument that did not execute, and a disagreement about the definition; the schema is therefore the defect, and every clean report is an upper bound on defects rather than a measurement of them.**

Four clauses, which do not stand or fall together:

- **(A) The three states are genuinely distinct objects and must not share one label.** Searched disconfirmatorily; **found nothing against it.** Standards-backed and correct. I say so plainly.
- **(B) "The schema cannot express the difference" — the arity of the schema is the defect.** **Challenged, and this is the load-bearing failure.** The operative variable in every mature severity regime I found is not the number of grades but whether each grade carries a *defined response*. A four-valued schema with no defined response per grade reproduces the fault.
- **(C) The generalisation from three same-day instances to "a warning currently encodes at least three distinct states" system-wide.** **Partially challenged.** n=3, and the three are not independent.
- **(D) The implied remedy — convert non-execution to `fail`.** **Challenged** on the alert-fatigue envelope, and challenged more sharply by the observation that it is a *fourth* misuse of the same schema rather than an exit from it.

### Summary

Searching against this item produced a split verdict with a clean seam. The claim that "did not execute" is a distinct object from "small defect found" is right, has a mature external analogue in clinical-laboratory practice — INVALID is a printed outcome distinct from NEGATIVE, caused by instrument, reagent or control failure — and is already held in this register with the vocabulary spelled out (PREMISE-142: SUCCEEDED / FAILED / NO-EVIDENCE, plus an evidence pointer). I found nothing against that and I am not going to manufacture something. What the search does contradict is the item's location of the defect and its remedy. The alarm-management standards, which are the only body of practice that has industrialised a graded severity schema under safety pressure, do not treat the number of grades as the design variable at all: ISA-18.2 rationalisation requires that every alarm carry a documented purpose, a defined operator response and a time to respond. C2A2's `warn` grade has no defined response, which is why "downstream every one of them is filtered out identically" — that is a response definition of *ignore*, applied uniformly. A fourth state added to a schema with no response definitions inherits the same fate on day two. The reliability literature makes this worse rather than better: the severity levels that already exist are, in the largest empirical study of the question, applied so inconsistently across human raters that the resulting datasets are unreliable — and the state the item wants to add requires the hardest judgement of the set. The metrology route the item's own testability clause names does not deliver: ILAC-G8's conditional pass/fail exists because a *taken* reading sits inside the uncertainty band of a limit; there is no metrological state for "no reading was taken," so the Critical grading borrows authority from a standard that is silent on the lead instance. Finally the evidence: two of the day's three instances are the same morning's work on the same corpus, one of them is a false-positive problem that a stricter gate aggravates, and the third — the 226 out-of-band days — looks like the schema working, since a middle grade meaning "a human must rule on the definition" is what a middle grade is for. What the clinical analogue actually prescribes is worth more than a label: an INVALID result triggers a **mandatory repeat**, and repeat resolves over 90% of them. The remedy that transfers is a forced re-run on a defined trigger, not a richer vocabulary of excuses.

### Specific risks

**The schema-expansion trap.** If 818 is dispositioned as "add a fourth state," C2A2 acquires a taxonomy whose grades are assigned by the same instruments that could not distinguish the states in the first place, with no response defined per grade. On the bug-severity evidence the new state will be applied inconsistently within weeks and will then be a *second* uninterpretable label rather than a fix. The failure would be invisible, because a taxonomy is judged by its schema and not by its inter-rater reliability unless someone measures the latter — which nothing in C2A2 currently does.

**The fail-conversion trap.** Making zero-extraction a failure at 21.5% is a large, cheap-looking change that lands outside every published tolerance for actionable-alert rates. The predictable end state is a gate that runs red so often it is bypassed by policy, at which point the guard has *negative* value: it consumes attention and licenses the habit of overriding guards.

**The re-mint risk.** PREMISE-142 already holds the three-valued vocabulary and the evidence-pointer requirement; PREMISE-131 already holds "a warning is not a control." Minting 818 separately produces two register entries for one mechanism, which PREMISE-138 bars and which leaves the next reader applying whichever they grep first.

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** The measurement is real and it is bad: **66 of 307 transcripts carried a pass mark from a guard that returned nothing.** That number is not challenged by anything in this file, it is a coverage catastrophe on a corpus the register consumes, and PREMISE-118 attaches a live retrospective-assessment obligation to it. Dismissing 818 as a duplicate would leave that obligation undischarged.

### Mitigations available

(a) **File 818 as an instance under PREMISE-142 rather than minting it** — 142 has the vocabulary, the reason and the evidence-pointer requirement.
(b) **Define the response, not the grade.** For each grade the guards emit, write down what happens next and who does it (ISA-18.2 rationalisation, in miniature). If the answer for `warn` is "nothing," that is the finding and it is one line.
(c) **Adopt the clinical trigger rather than the clinical label.** `NO-EVIDENCE` should force an automatic re-run against a fallback method (for zero-extraction: a punctuation-independent chunker), because repeat resolves the great majority of invalid results in the practice this is borrowed from.
(d) **Measure assignment reliability before trusting the taxonomy.** Have two decorrelated instances grade the same 30 warnings and report agreement. If it is low, the schema is not the binding constraint.
(e) **Check any fail-conversion against the alert-fatigue envelope first** (PREMISE-121, PREMISE-110's cost caveat), and prefer input-conditioned routing over global severity change.
(f) **Discharge PREMISE-118 for the 66 transcripts** — assessment, not automatic invalidation.

### Recommendation: PARTIALLY-CHALLENGED

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-818

**Strongest counterargument:** The item has correctly identified a real defect and then misidentified which part of the system is defective, and the field that has spent forty years professionalising exactly this problem says so directly. Concede the true part first, because it is the item's and it is not small: "the check did not run" is a different object from "a small defect was found," clinical laboratories print INVALID as a third outcome for precisely this reason, and this register already holds the corrective in PREMISE-142's three-valued vocabulary. But the item's diagnosis is that *the schema cannot express the difference*, and that is a claim about arity. Alarm management — ISA-18.2, EEMUA 191 — is the mature discipline for graded severity under safety pressure, and it does not regulate arity at all. It regulates *response*: every alarm must carry a documented purpose, a defined operator response, a corrective action and a time to respond. C2A2's `warn` has none of these, which is exactly why "downstream every one of them is filtered out identically." That sentence in the item is not evidence that the schema lacks a slot; it is evidence that the existing slot has no response attached. Add a fourth grade to a schema with no response definitions and on day two you have four grades and one behaviour. The empirical literature closes the trap: the largest study of severity data reports that raters do not agree on the grades that already exist and that the resulting datasets are unreliable — and the grade the item wants to add ("why did this instrument produce no reading?") demands the most contestable judgement in the set. Nor does the metrology route the item names support it. ILAC-G8's conditional pass and conditional fail exist because a reading *was taken* and sits inside the uncertainty band of a tolerance limit; metrology has no state for "no reading." So the Critical grading imports authority from a standards family silent on the lead instance. And the evidential base is thinner than the sentence "today's three instances show it cannot" implies: two instances are one morning's work on one corpus, one of those two is a *false-positive* problem that a stricter gate makes worse, and the third — 226 out-of-band days reclassified as a definitional disagreement — is a middle grade doing its job, since "a human must rule on this definition" is the paradigm case for a middle grade. What transfers from the clinical analogue is not the label but the obligation attached to it: INVALID triggers a **mandatory repeat**, and repeat resolves over 90% of invalid results. The fix that matches the evidence is a forced re-run on a defined trigger with a fallback method, not a richer vocabulary.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** The item is safe to act on where (i) the added state carries a **defined automatic response** — re-run with a fallback method, and escalation if the fallback also returns nothing — rather than a new label to be filtered; (ii) the **assignment reliability** of the resulting grades is measured before the taxonomy is trusted, because an unreliably-assigned grade is not information; (iii) any conversion of non-execution to `fail` is checked against the actionable-alert envelope and applied **selectively by criticality**, not globally at a 21.5% base rate; and (iv) it is filed under PREMISE-142 rather than minted beside it, so one mechanism gets one entry and one remediation. Where those four hold, the challenge in this file does not bite. Where any fails — and (i) and (ii) are the ones C2A2 has never done — the schema change buys nothing and costs attention.

**How to test:** (1) **The response-definition audit, and it is decisive and cheap.** For each grade every guard emits, write the answer to "what happens next, and who does it?" If `warn` maps to "nothing" everywhere, the item's diagnosis is refuted in one page — the defect is the response table, not the schema — and the fix is to fill the table. (2) **The reliability probe:** two decorrelated instances grade the same 30 stored warnings into {defect, did-not-run, definitional}. Report Cohen's κ. Below ~0.6 the fourth state is not measurable and adding it is cosmetic. (3) **The re-run test on the actual corpus:** run a punctuation-independent extractor over the 66 zero-extraction transcripts. If it resolves most of them — as repeat resolves >90% of invalid clinical results — then the state C2A2 needs is transient-and-retryable, not a new terminal grade. (4) **The override-rate forecast:** convert zero-extraction to `fail` on a shadow branch for one week and count how many runs are re-run versus overridden. If overrides dominate, the remedy has produced PREMISE-131's least-effective tier at the cost of a gate.
