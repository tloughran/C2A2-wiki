  Sources:
    1. Stiglitz (1989) "Markets, Market Failures, and Development" — price-system as information aggregation has well-established failure modes: missing markets, asymmetric information, externalities. Transfer of the analogy carries the failure modes too.
    2. Granovetter (1985) "Economic Action and Social Structure" — embeddedness critique: price-system framing systematically OMITS the social-trust substrate that small communities actually run on; analogy import risks ignoring the dominant coordination mechanism.
    3. Akerlof (1970) "The Market for Lemons" — information asymmetry collapses price-system coordination; small heterogeneous communities have severe asymmetries.
    4. Specific to FEP analogy: Sims et al. (2024 / multiple critiques) — FEP-as-coordination claims have been challenged for unfalsifiability and for sweeping under the carpet the question of what "shared scarcity model" even means.
    5. Domain-transfer literature (Gentner 1983) — structural homology requires explicit mapping; analogous-source need not entail analogous-mechanism.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The price-system analogy carries known failure modes (Stiglitz, Akerlof) that may apply more strongly to C2A2 than to actual markets. The embeddedness critique (Granovetter) is particularly sharp: small intentional communities run on trust/reciprocity, not price-information. FEP-as-coordination is itself contested. Analogy import without explicit mapping risks structural-mismatch.

  Specific risks: (a) Importing the analogy may foreclose attention to social-trust / reciprocity mechanisms that may dominate; (b) the analogy is most evocative when applied to many-agent peer collectives — present-state C2A2 is N≈1; (c) structural-homology claims often collapse on close inspection (Gentner caveats).

  Mitigations available: (a) Explicit mapping of source-target correspondences before importing; (b) treat the analogy as one of several coordination metaphors, not a charter; (c) hold the homology claim conditional on target-state architecture.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-CHALLENGED (Moderate)
