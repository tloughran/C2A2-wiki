  Sources:
    1. News-org standards (AP style, NYT desk style) — standard ordering by significance / recency outperforms idiosyncratic "personal-thread-closing" ordering when consumers vary across days.
    2. Recency effect (Glanzer & Cunitz 1966) — placing items LAST also has recall benefits; primacy is not the only ordering pattern with empirical support.
    3. Klein (1999) — naturalistic decision-making favors stable, predictable formats; ordering keyed to ephemeral context (morning-walk question) reduces structural predictability.
    4. Salience-bias literature (Bordalo et al. 2012) — leading with the most-salient-to-receiver item can crowd out items that have higher base-rate importance but lower salience.

  Strength of challenge: Weak

  Summary: There IS a counter-pattern: stable section-ordered reports outperform context-keyed ordering when consumer attention varies or when readers want predictability. Recency effects mean placing items last is also defensible. The challenge is bounded — it does not refute "lead with loop-closing"; it qualifies that the rule should not erode structural predictability.

  Specific risks: (a) Loop-closing detection may be unreliable, leading to loop-CLAIMING rather than loop-CLOSING (PRESUMPTION-250); (b) ad-hoc ordering reduces report navigability; (c) consumers may need to find a routine section reliably and find ordering varies.

  Mitigations available: (a) Lead with loop-closing + maintain stable section ordering for the body; (b) explicit "Re: your morning-walk Q on X" header that doesn't displace the standard structure; (c) only apply loop-closing-lead when confidence in loop identification is high.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-CHALLENGED (Weak)

    Strongest counterargument: Stable predictable report structure is itself a feature; readers learn where to look. Context-keyed leads erode this. If loop-closing detection is unreliable, the lead is misplaced and the structural predictability lost. The Minto-pyramid case for answer-first is strongest in a fresh briefing; daily briefings sit in an ongoing format relationship.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Either (a) reliable loop-detection with explicit confidence threshold, OR (b) loop-closing as a CLEARLY MARKED prefix to the standard structure, not a replacement.
    How to test: Score 20 sample days for loop-detection accuracy; if accuracy drops below threshold, the rule degrades to loop-claiming.
