  Sources:
    1. VARIERR NLI (Weber-Genzel et al. 2024, ACL). - A large fraction of apparent disagreement is annotation ERROR, not genuine variation; raw dissensus conflates the two.
    2. Krippendorff (reliability theory). - Without established reliability, a disagreement rate is uninterpretable as a property of the items rather than the instrument.
    3. Aroyo & Welty 2015 (caveat side). - Disagreement is signal ONLY once low-quality/erroneous annotation is filtered.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The challenge is that a raw dissensus rate is only as meaningful as the instrument producing it. The human-label-variation literature that legitimizes disagreement-as-signal simultaneously insists that genuine variation must be separated from annotation/instrument error before the rate means anything. For C2A2, dissensus produced by erratic adjudication, poorly-constructed axes, or correlated columns would masquerade as 'evidence about positions', when it is really instrument noise.

  Specific risks: A dissensus rate driven by instrument noise would be mistaken for a substantive finding about contestability, contaminating the constitutional detector's headline output.

  Mitigations available: Establish test-retest reliability of the dissensus measure; separate error from variation (VARIERR-style); report dissensus only above a measured noise floor.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-348
    Strongest counterargument: A disagreement rate with unknown reliability is not evidence about positions at all - it is an uncalibrated mixture of genuine under-determination and instrument failure, and presenting it as a detector output risks reifying noise.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The dissensus measure has demonstrated test-retest stability and a quantified error-vs-variation split.
    How to test: Re-run identical inputs across sessions; measure dissensus stability; hand-audit a sample to estimate the genuine-variation fraction.
