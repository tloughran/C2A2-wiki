  Summary: The item's guard has two moving parts — a label, and a set of falsifiers — and the
  literature challenges each by a different route. The label fails at the READING step: the best
  measured case of a formal, machine-readable, publisher-attached defeat marking is acknowledged in
  5.4% of downstream uses, and the continued-influence work shows that even acknowledged markings do
  not reliably suppress downstream reliance on the marked content. The falsifiers fail at the TESTING
  step: in the closest institutional analogue, roughly nine in ten preregistered studies deviate from
  their own preregistered plan, and there is direct evidence that the reviewers whose role it is to
  check the plan do not open it. And there is a third finding which is worse than either, because it
  says the guard may be actively counterproductive rather than merely inert: Nemeth's comparison of
  assigned devil's advocacy against authentic dissent found that assigned advocacy promoted cognitive
  bolstering of the original position and conferred little advantage over no dissent at all. C2A2's
  arrangement is assigned advocacy in its purest form — the constructing layer marks its own output
  "attack this", and the attacking, if it happens, is done by the same estate. Finally, the register
  already predicted all of this: PREMISE-188 holds that a header-level qualifier does not govern the
  body once the body is quoted, and its named remedy — per-claim attachment — is precisely what a
  "filed as to-be-attacked" header is not. NOTE THE SCOPE OF WHAT IS AND IS NOT CHALLENGED. Nothing
  here says the bridge should not have been constructed, or that filing falsifiers is worse than not
  filing them. The challenge is to the word **adequate**. On this evidence the label plus falsifiers
  is a necessary and cheap first step with a measured downstream efficacy somewhere in the single
  digits to low tens of percent, and the item treats it as sufficient.

  Specific risks: (a) The most likely concrete failure is silent and is already the estate's named

  Mitigations available: The literature converges on three, and the register already names two of
  them, so the increment is cheap.
    (i)   **Per-claim attachment, not header attachment.** PREMISE-188 clause (3) and the GRADE
          precedent. The provisional marking must be on the sentence that would be quoted, not on the
          document that contains it. Mechanically: any bridge claim carries an inline status token
          that survives copy-paste, and any consumer that renders a bridge claim without the token is
          the defect.
    (ii)  **Owner, date, adjudicator on each falsifier at mint time.** This is PREMISE-183 clause (1)
          applied to falsifiers: the closure test is defined when the item is opened, and a falsifier
          that has not been tested by its date becomes an event rather than a line. Without this the
          falsifiers are a preregistration nobody reviews.
    (iii) **Authentic rather than assigned dissent, or an explicit admission that it is assigned.**
          Nemeth's result does not forbid the practice; it says the practice as configured has little
          measured effect. The cheap version available to this estate is structural: the attack must
          be executed by a lane that did not construct the bridge and does not see the construction
          rationale — which is the same independence architecture 15a/15b already run on, and is
          therefore an extension of existing machinery rather than a new subsystem. PREMISE-107's
          cost guard applies: this is cheap and reversible, so doing it IS the discriminating test.
    (iv)  A negative note, carried deliberately: do NOT answer this with a stronger warning label.
          The CIE corpus is the reason. Increasing the salience of the marking is the intuitive
          remedy and is the one the evidence gives least support to.

STEELMAN:
  Item: ASSUMPTION-1297
  Strongest counterargument: The guard is being asked to do a job that no marking has ever been shown
  to do. Take the most favourable possible case for it — a retraction notice in biomedicine. That
  marking is formal, indexed, machine-readable, attached by the publisher of record, and its readers
  are professionally trained to check it. It is acknowledged in 5.4% of the citation contexts that
  follow it, and the retraction did not change how the papers were cited. C2A2's marking is a phrase
  in a wiki file, attached by the same layer that authored the thing being marked, read by agents that
  quote fragments. There is no reason to expect it to outperform 5.4%, and PREMISE-188 supplies the
  mechanism by which it will do worse: qualifiers are stripped preferentially in retransmission
  because they reduce the usefulness of what is being retransmitted. The falsifier limb is not
  stronger. Preregistration is the same idea with institutional force behind it, and roughly nine in
  ten preregistered studies deviate from their own plan while the reviewers who are supposed to check
  the plan do not open it. And then there is the limb that should actually worry the designer: Nemeth
  found that assigned devil's advocacy did not merely underperform authentic dissent, it fostered
  cognitive bolstering — participants generated more reasons why they had been right. C2A2 has
  constructed a bridge, labelled it "attack me", and left the attacking to itself. On Nemeth's result
  the predicted outcome of running that attack sincerely is a bridge that is more entrenched than
  before AND a record stating it survived adversarial review. That is not a failed guard; it is a
  guard that manufactures the warrant it was supposed to withhold. The continued-influence corpus
  closes the last exit: even a reader who sees the marking, believes it, and can recall it goes on
  using the marked content when reasoning downstream. The assumption's word is "adequate". Nothing in
  this evidence supports adequate. What it supports is "cheap, worth doing, and roughly a tenth as
  effective as the design assumes."
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: One of three conditions. (1) The marking is attached
  at the level of the QUOTABLE UNIT rather than the document, so that the failure mode PREMISE-188
  measures — the body travelling without the header — is structurally unavailable. (2) The falsifiers
  carry an owner, a date and an adjudicator, so that a falsifier which is not tested becomes a visible
  event rather than a silent line; without this the bridge's provisional status decays into settled
  status by the passage of time alone, which is exactly what the retraction data show happening. (3)
  The attack is executed by a party that did not build the bridge and cannot see why it was built —
  which converts assigned advocacy into something closer to authentic dissent, and is the only
  condition of the three that addresses Nemeth's finding rather than the labelling findings. C2A2
  currently satisfies none of the three. A weaker sufficient condition, worth stating because it may
  be the true one: if NOTHING downstream ever consumes bridges — if the bridge corpus is inert — then
  the guard's inadequacy costs nothing, but so does the bridge. That is a testable disjunction and
  the estate should know which horn it is on.
  How to test: Retrospective, exact, and runnable today from data already in the vault. **Test A (the
  label).** Enumerate every constructed bridge in the corpus that carries a provisional or
  "to-be-attacked" marking. For each, grep the vault for downstream quotations of its claims. Score
  each quotation on one binary: does the quoting location reproduce the provisional marking? The
  resulting fraction is this estate's local value of Hsiao & Schneider's 5.4%, and it is the item's
  truth value. PREMISE-188's own Applicable-to section already names the ready case — the 2026-08-26
  run's 198 SNIPPET-ONLY markings — so a first sample exists without touching bridges at all. **Test B
  (the falsifiers).** Enumerate every falsifier ever attached to any bridge record. For each, record:
  was it tested (y/n), by whom, and on what date. If the answer is "none, ever", the guard's second
  limb has never once operated, and its adequacy is not a matter of degree. **Test C (Nemeth,
  optional and more expensive).** Take three bridges. Have a lane with no access to the construction
  rationale attempt refutation; separately have the constructing lane run its own "attack". Compare
  the number of distinct defeaters generated. If the constructing lane generates fewer, the assigned-
  advocacy finding reproduces here and the guard should be re-architected rather than tuned.
