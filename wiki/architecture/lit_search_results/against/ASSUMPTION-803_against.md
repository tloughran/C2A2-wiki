       (authors, title, publisher and DOI confirmed this session; journal name [UNVERIFIED,
       probably Current Directions in Psychological Science]). The definitional content is exactly
       the objection: a *jingle fallacy* is the error of treating two or more distinct phenomena
       as the same because they are labelled with the same term. The systematic review reports
       these fallacies are widespread and that the areas affected explicitly include emotions and
       well-being, and biological psychology — both of the traditions at issue here. The
       literature's stated cause is also directly applicable: the fallacies arise from vague
       linkage between theoretical constructs and their empirical operationalisation, compounded
       across differing designs and methods. ASSUMPTION-803's inference — shared idiom therefore
       structural homology — is the canonical form of the fallacy this literature exists to name.
    2. Allostasis specifically has a documented operationalisation problem. "A systematic review
       of allostatic load in relation to socioeconomic position: Poor fidelity and major
       inconsistencies in biomarkers employed," Social Science & Medicine (PubMed PMID 28963986,
       2017; ScienceDirect record S0277953617305610; authors [UNVERIFIED]). Reported, from the
       abstract and search summary rather than the full text: across 26 studies there were 59
       biomarkers combined in 20 different ways, with no consistent method of operationalising
       allostatic load, and many studies lacked fidelity to the original concept in which stress
       was central. Corroborating work located this session: "Comparing different
       operationalizations of allostatic load measured in mid-life…" (PMID 35202971;
       Psychoneuroendocrinology record S0306453022000300; authors [UNVERIFIED]) and "Measuring
       allostatic load: Approaches and limitations to algorithm creation" (ScienceDirect
       S002239992200335X; authors and year [UNVERIFIED]), both of which report a persisting lack
       of consensus that limits cross-study comparability. This is the decisive empirical point:
       allostasis is not merely at *risk* of being a homonym across three distant traditions — it
       is already demonstrably non-uniform *within a single subfield that uses it constantly*.
    3. Day, T.A., 2005. "Defining stress as a prelude to mapping its neurocircuitry: no help from
       allostasis." PubMed PMID 16213079 (author surname, title, year and PMID confirmed this
       session; journal [UNVERIFIED — commonly Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology & Biological
       Psychiatry]; full text not read). Argues that the effort around allostasis has been
       directed at subsuming the concept of stress within it, with the effect of collapsing
       homeostatic responses and stress responses together, and that this is counter-productive
       because it distracts from the question of how an organism differentiates stressful from
       non-stressful challenges. The relevance is that allostasis has a documented history of
       *absorbing* neighbouring concepts rather than sharpening them — which is precisely the
       dynamic that would produce apparent cross-tradition convergence without any shared
       structure underneath.

    5. The unfalsifiability critique of the free energy principle, which bears on the Friston
       side. Located this session: "The Dialectics of Free Energy Minimization," Frontiers in

       Frontiers record; authors [UNVERIFIED]); LessWrong, "Why I'm not into the Free Energy

       virtually any phenomenon can be redescribed as free-energy minimisation, which makes it
       unclear what observation would count against it, and the framework is applied across
       cells, thermostats and brains alike. This matters for ASSUMPTION-803 in a specific way: if
       "predictive regulation" is a description that fits almost any adaptive system, then finding
       it in two traditions is very weak evidence that those traditions share structure, because
       the prior probability of the description fitting was already near one.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The assumption's inferential step — a lab uses another tradition's idiom, therefore
    the pair moves from surface analogy to structural homology — is the exact error the
    jingle-fallacy literature was constructed to name, and that literature reports the error is
    widespread in both of the subfields involved. The specific term at issue is a particularly
    bad candidate for the inference: allostasis has a documented failure of consensus *within*
    the single subfield that uses it most, with one systematic review finding 59 biomarkers
    combined 20 different ways across 26 studies and no consistent operationalisation, and Day
    (2005) arguing that the concept has been used to absorb neighbouring constructs rather than
    to sharpen them. Sterling's own reception literature records that later users often drop the
    brain-centric commitment that gave the term its content. On the Friston side, the standard
    unfalsifiability objection applies with force to this particular inference: if predictive
    regulation is a description that fits nearly any adaptive system, then observing it in two
    traditions carries almost no evidential weight about shared structure. And there is a
    specific independence problem — the active-inference/allostasis/interoception framing entered
    affective science through a well-known bridge literature, so an affective-science paper using
    Friston's idiom is most parsimoniously explained by citation, not convergence, and two texts
    are not independent evidence when one adopted its vocabulary from the other. Finally, and
    importantly for the record, I was unable this session to locate the Fredrickson-lab paper the
    assumption cites; the assumption's factual premise is therefore unverified as well as its
    inference being challenged.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-803
    Strongest counterargument: Terminological convergence is weak evidence, but it is not zero
      evidence, and the assumption may be making a more modest claim than the challenge attacks.
      If a lab that had no prior commitment to predictive processing independently reaches for
      predictive-regulation language to describe its own findings, that is at least a signal that
      the phenomenon it is describing has the shape the idiom implies — labs do not usually adopt
      alien vocabulary for no reason. The jingle-fallacy objection cuts both ways: the same
      literature also names the *jangle* fallacy, in which genuinely identical phenomena are
      obscured by differing labels, and an excessive fear of jingle produces jangle. There is
      also a real structural claim available that does not depend on vocabulary at all. Sterling's
      allostasis, Friston's active inference and Levin's bioelectric goal-directed morphogenesis
      each posit a system that (i) holds a target state, (ii) predicts departures from it, and
      (iii) acts in advance to prevent them. That is a shared formal skeleton, and if it can be
      written down in each tradition's own formalism — a free-energy functional, a bioelectric
      setpoint, a physiological regulated variable — then the homology claim stands on the
      mathematics regardless of whether anyone uses the word "allostasis." The right response to
      the homonym worry may therefore be to *drop the word* and test the structure, not to
      abandon the claim.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the Fredrickson-lab paper is pinned to a
      specific citation and the passage using the idiom is quoted, so the factual premise is
      checkable — this is prerequisite to everything else and is currently missing; (b) that
      passage is checked for whether it cites Friston, Barrett or the active-inference literature;
      if it does, the usage is adopted rather than convergent and the assumption's inference
      fails on independence grounds; (c) the homology claim is restated at the level of formal
      structure rather than vocabulary — what is the regulated variable, what is the prediction,
      what is the anticipatory action, in each of the three traditions — so that it can be
      assessed without depending on the word; (d) the three traditions' usages are checked for
      the *specific commitments* that distinguish allostasis from ordinary feedback control:
      anticipation before the perturbation, setpoint change rather than setpoint defence, and (in
      Sterling's version) brain-centricity; if any tradition's usage lacks these, the term is a
      homonym in that tradition; (e) the claim is graded rather than binary — "shared formal
      skeleton, divergent operationalisation" is a defensible and probably correct position, and
      is much stronger than either "homology" or "mere analogy."
    How to test: Tractable and largely bibliographic. First, retrieve the Fredrickson-lab paper
      and inspect its reference list for Friston, Barrett, Sterling or Schulkin; a citation
      settles the independence question immediately and is the single highest-value check. Second,
      build a three-column table — Friston/active inference, Levin/bioelectric, Fredrickson/
      affective science — with rows for regulated variable, mechanism of prediction, form of
      anticipatory action, timescale, and what would count as a failure of regulation. Convergent
      entries support homology; divergent entries with a shared label are a demonstrated jingle
      and refute the assumption directly. Third, apply the operationalisation test the allostatic-
      load literature supplies: ask what each tradition would *measure* to detect allostasis. If
      the three answers do not overlap — and given 59 biomarkers and 20 combinations within
      affective science alone, they very likely will not — the term is not shared in the technical
      sense the assumption requires. Fourth, run the discriminability check implied by the FEP
      critique: name a system that predictively regulates a bodily state but that none of the
      three traditions would count as an instance. If no such system can be named, the description
      is too broad to support a homology inference.

  Specific risks: If allostasis is a homonym across these traditions, then (i) the vault records
    a structural homology that does not exist, and every downstream link, cross-reference and
    synthesis built on the Levin-Fredrickson pairing inherits the error — this is the object-level
    risk and it is the kind that propagates furthest, because a claimed homology invites further
    connections; (ii) the error is self-reinforcing through vocabulary: once the vault uses one
    word for three things, subsequent extraction will find the word in all three places and read
    it as further confirmation, so the evidence base grows without the underlying claim gaining
    support; (iii) the failure is hard to detect from inside, because the surface signal —
    matching terminology — is exactly what a search would look for, and a jingle fallacy is
    invisible to text-similarity methods by construction; (iv) if the premise itself is
    unverified (I could not locate the paper), then an unlocated citation is currently doing
    load-bearing work, which is a provenance problem of the kind flagged in PRESUMPTION-695;
    (v) the reputational and epistemic cost is asymmetric: an over-claimed cross-tradition
    homology is the most visible kind of error a synthesis project can make, and this project's
    value rests on its cross-tradition claims being trustworthy.

  Mitigations available: (1) Pin the premise: cite the specific Fredrickson-lab paper and quote
    the passage, then check its references for adoption of the idiom. (2) Restate the claim at
    the level of formal structure — regulated variable, prediction, anticipatory action — and
    drop the shared word from the claim entirely, which is the move that makes the homology
    testable. (3) Apply the operationalisation test: what would each tradition measure? Divergent
    measurement is the strongest available evidence of homonymy and the allostatic-load literature
    shows it is likely. (4) Record the claim with an explicit grade — surface analogy / shared
    formal skeleton / structural homology — rather than as a binary, and require different
    evidence for each grade. (5) Add a standing check for jingle fallacies in cross-tradition
    linking generally: any link justified by shared terminology alone should be flagged as
    provisional until a structural correspondence is written out, since this is unlikely to be
    the only instance. (6) Carry the FEP-breadth caveat next to any Friston-derived homology
    claim, because the same objection will apply to every one of them.
