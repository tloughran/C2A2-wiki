  Search scope: Preliminary-to-moderate. The uncertainty-communication cluster is well covered. The specific mechanism the item asserts — that abstention causes a prior uncaveated figure to be re-used — was searched for under omission and anchoring framings and no direct study was retrieved.   Sources:
    1. van der Bles, van der Linden, Freeman and Spiegelhalter, 2020. "The effects of communicating uncertainty on public trust in facts and numbers." PNAS 117(14):7672–7683. PMID 32205438. Five experiments including a preregistered national replication and a BBC News field experiment, total n = 5,780. Numeric uncertainty communication has little effect on trust in the numbers and no effect on trust in the source; the small decrements observed attach mostly to VERBAL uncertainty. This supports the item's remedy — a numeric directional bound is the low-cost option — and simultaneously undercuts the implied urgency, since the harm from a caveated figure is small in the first place.
    2. Same source, on the verbal/numeric contrast: verbal hedges are ambiguous and carry the trust penalty; numeric ranges do not. A "directional bound" stated verbally ("at least as many as before") would inherit the penalty the study attributes to verbal expression; stated numerically it would not. The item does not distinguish these and the distinction is the operative one.
    3. Anchoring literature as surveyed in Furnham and Boo's review of the anchoring effect (ScienceDirect S1053535710001411) and in "Evidence that publication bias contaminated studies on visual event boundaries and anchoring effects" (PMC10990127, retrieved 2026-07-21). Anchoring effects are real but the corpus shows evidence of publication-bias contamination, so the strength of any inference that a withdrawn figure continues to anchor downstream users should be treated as weaker than the textbook presentation implies.

  Strength of challenge: Weak
  Summary: The remedy is in good shape and the claim is not. van der Bles et al. is the best available evidence on exactly this choice and it says numeric ranges can be published without cost to source credibility, so reporting a directional bound is well founded — but the same study also says the harm the item is trying to avoid is small, which weakens the case for treating this as a priority. The claim that abstention republishes the disputed figure is a specific causal assertion about downstream behaviour, and I searched for and did not retrieve evidence for it; the anchoring literature is the nearest support and it is both indirect and flagged for publication-bias contamination. The one substantive correction is that the item does not distinguish numeric from verbal bounds, and the literature says that distinction carries the whole effect: state the bound as a number, not as a phrase.
  Specific risks: Low. The main risk is misallocation — treating a small credibility effect as a live hazard, and doing so on an unevidenced mechanism. Secondary risk: a verbally phrased directional bound is adopted and incurs the trust penalty the numeric form avoids.
  Mitigations available: Phrase every bound numerically with an explicit relation ("≥ 41", not "more than before"). Test the claim rather than assume it: the item's own check — inspect the next artifact citing a connector count — settles the republication mechanism in one read.

    Strongest counterargument: The remedy is right for a reason the item does not give, and the diagnosis is asserted rather than shown. van der Bles et al. tested precisely this trade-off across five experiments and 5,780 participants including a BBC field experiment, and found that communicating numeric uncertainty barely moves trust in the numbers and does not move trust in the source at all — so publishing a range is close to free. But the same result cuts against the framing: if caveated figures cost almost nothing in credibility, then the harm the item is guarding against is small, and the claim that abstention silently reinstates an uncaveated figure is an empirical assertion about this pipeline's downstream behaviour for which no external literature was found. More consequentially, the study's sharpest finding is one the item does not register: the trust penalty attaches to VERBAL hedges, not numeric ones. A directional bound rendered as prose inherits the penalty; rendered as a number it does not. That is the actionable content here, and it is orthogonal to the abstention argument.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: That bounds are always emitted numerically, and that the republication mechanism is verified rather than presumed before it is used to justify a policy against abstention.
