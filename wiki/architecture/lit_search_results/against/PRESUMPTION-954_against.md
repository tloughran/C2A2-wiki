  Summary: The challenge is decisive and the estate wrote it itself. PREMISE-188 holds that an evidentiary
    qualifier travels with the claim or it does not travel, that a header-level caveat does not govern the
    body once quoted, and that the stripping is selective rather than careless. The measured bound is
    5.4%: even a formal published retraction is acknowledged in one citation context in twenty. Against
    that, a caveat in a provenance field that downstream consumers are not required to read is not a
    control at all — PREMISE-099 already names the general form, that annotation without gating does not
    prevent emission, and that deferability is a property of the enforcement point rather than of the
    documentation. The compounding problem is the one 14b names: PREMISE-201 is the first premise minted
    with a known-unverified load-bearing clause, and PRESUMPTION-948 establishes that the register has no
    mechanism to downgrade it. So the weakest link in the chain has been promoted to the estate's
    strongest epistemic object, in a register that cannot demote.

  Specific risks: A premise is the estate's strongest epistemic object and PREMISE-201 now carries a
    clause resting on a source that failed retrieval. Downstream consumers key on item type and status;
    the epistemic-weight table has no field for the defect, so the clause will be consumed at full premise
    weight by construction, not by oversight. REVISE-445 carries the same exposure with a relabelled
    "mid-teens to low twenties" estimate whose source, by 15b's own reading, does not publish it. And
    because PRESUMPTION-948 holds, neither can be downgraded. The failure is silent and directional: each
    reuse strengthens the claim, and the qualification is lost at the first hop.

  Mitigations available:
    - **Bind the qualifier to the quotable unit.** PREMISE-188 clause (3) already specifies this and names
      GRADE as the precedent. Operationally: the caveat belongs in the same string as the number
      ("≈mid-teens–low-twenties [SOURCE DOES NOT PUBLISH THIS FIGURE]"), not in an adjacent field. A
      marking that does not survive copy-paste is not a marking.
    - **Add the missing field to the epistemic-weight table.** One column — "load-bearing source verified
      Y/N/UNRETRIEVABLE" — with a rule that UNRETRIEVABLE caps the item's weight regardless of type and
      status. This is the enforcement point PREMISE-099 requires and its absence is the whole mechanism.
    - **Apply PREMISE-103 to PREMISE-201 clause (3).** The premise states that downgrading confidence is
      not a valid substitute for an explicit "unfounded pending retrieval" state. Clause (3) currently has
      the invalid form. Give it the valid one.
    - **Retire the number, keep the question.** REVISE-445's estimate can be replaced by the in-house
      measurement it was always standing in for, at which point nothing load-bearing rests on a figure no
      source publishes.
    - **Run 14b's one-cycle test** — follow both items forward and see whether any consumer cites the
      estimate without the caveat. One cycle answers it and costs nothing.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-954
    Strongest counterargument: The alternative to a caveated estimate is not a clean register — it is a
      silent gap, and the estate has already minted a premise saying that gaps are worse. PREMISE-117
      holds that continuing to publish under an unresolved dispute is codified professional practice
      (publish-then-revise, with a revision flag and a described change), and that withdrawal is reserved
      for figures not fit for purpose. PREMISE-145 goes further: withholding a discrepant own-measurement
      is a reporting bias, not a null act. On that reading, the run that relabelled rather than removed did
      exactly what official-statistics practice prescribes, and the criticism amounts to demanding that an
      honest analyst either fabricate certainty or say nothing. Moreover, the retraction analogue may
      overstate the case badly: retraction notices are stripped because they live in a *different
      document* from the claim and require a separate lookup, whereas the C2A2 caveat is in the same
      record as the number, read by the same consumer, in a register whose readers are a handful of agents
      operating under an explicit verification discipline — a far more favourable propagation environment
      than the open literature.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) consumers must actually read the provenance field
      — testable in one cycle and currently unmeasured; (b) the qualifier must be in the same *quotable
      unit* as the number, not merely the same document, because the failure mode is quotation and
      quotation takes the sentence, not the record; (c) there must exist a downgrade path, or (a) and (b)
      must hold perfectly forever, because an unqualified claim in an uncontractable register is permanent
      — this is why PRESUMPTION-948 makes 954 much worse than it would otherwise be; (d) the
      epistemic-weight table must have somewhere to record the defect, or the schema will overwrite the
      prose. (b), (c) and (d) are all false today.
    How to test: 14b's test as stated, with one addition that makes it a control rather than an
      observation. Follow REVISE-445 and PREMISE-201 clause (3) forward one cycle and count citations
      with and without the caveat — that is the observation. Then seed one deliberately caveated figure
      into a register and see whether it emerges uncaveated downstream within the same cycle — that is the
      control, and it converts a single-case observation into a measured stripping rate. Both are cheap.
