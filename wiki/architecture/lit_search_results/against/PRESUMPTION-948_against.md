  Summary: The challenge to the monotonicity limb is decisive and it is already inside the register.
    PREMISE-174 states in its own words that a base which expands and cannot contract cannot revise, and
    that its growth curve is therefore not a health signal. The proposed pre-route grep would convert the
    register's current *de facto* monotonicity into *de jure* monotonicity by closing the last channel
    through which a premise can meet a contradicting result. Greenberg's citation network is what that
    looks like at scale: a belief system that grows by amplification and citation bias while the refuting
    papers are simply not cited. But the opposite extreme is also unaffordable — PREMISE-106 holds that
    this queue is already unstable — so the correct object is not "search everything" but a stated
    re-search trigger, which is what the living-review literature supplies and what the register lacks.

  Specific risks: If this presumption is false and the grep is adopted as a filter, all 158 ACTIVE
    premises become unfalsifiable in one edit. Every downstream epistemic weight in
    `provenance_protocol.md` keys on a status that can move; if no status can move, the weights are
    measuring an invariant. The condition is self-concealing: the more premises accumulate, the higher
    the pre-answered rate climbs, and the more compelling the routing diagnosis looks — so the evidence
    for the wrong remedy grows monotonically with the size of the error.

  Mitigations available:
    - Run 14b's own count first: premises that have ever changed status after minting, across 158. If
      zero, the register is already monotonic as a matter of fact and the debate is about whether to
      install the property or repair it. One pass over the file.
    - Adopt the grep as an ANNOTATOR at origination rather than a FILTER at routing: attach the governing
      premise to the item and let the search proceed with the premise in view. This preserves the
      contraction channel and captures most of the claimed saving.
    - Give the register a re-search trigger borrowed from living reviews: a premise is re-exposed when
      (a) an item contradicts it, (b) its load-bearing source is found unretrievable or misreported, or
      (c) a stated interval elapses. (b) is live today — see PRESUMPTION-954 and PREMISE-201.
    - Add a status transition to `provenance_protocol.md`. ACTIVE → CONTESTED → (ACTIVE | RETIRED). The
      absence of re-entry is the mechanism; a status value is the cheapest possible fix for it.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-948
    Strongest counterargument: A register that could not be relied upon would not be a register. The
      whole value of accumulating validated premises is that future runs may stand on them without
      re-litigating; if every premise is permanently re-openable at every encounter, the estate pays the
      full cost of every question forever and PREMISE-106's unstable queue becomes catastrophically
      unstable. Moreover, monotonicity is not obviously the defect it looks like: the premises in this
      register are overwhelmingly *structural* claims about controls, records and inference — "a warning
      is not a control," "transmission is not delivery," "citing is not verifying" — and structural
      claims of that kind genuinely do not expire the way an empirical base rate does. An 82% pre-answered
      rate may be exactly what domain saturation looks like in a system whose domain is finite. On that
      reading the right response is to widen the intake, not to re-open the answers.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the premises must in fact be of the
      non-expiring structural kind — testable by classifying the 158 into structural vs. empirical and
      checking whether any empirical one rests on a figure that has moved; (b) there must exist at least
      one live channel by which a premise CAN be contradicted, even if it is rarely used; (c) the
      pre-answer must be checked at ORIGINATION, not only at routing — otherwise the saving is booked in
      the wrong place and the operational runs stay uninformed. (b) is the binding condition and it is
      currently unsatisfied.
    How to test: the count 14b names, plus one addition. Count status changes across 158 premises (expect
      zero). Then classify the 158 as structural or empirical and count how many empirical ones rest on a
      source flagged unverified or unretrievable. PREMISE-201 is already one such. If the empirical
      subset is non-trivial and the status-change count is zero, the register is monotonic over exactly
      the claims that most need revision, which is the worst case and is measurable today.
