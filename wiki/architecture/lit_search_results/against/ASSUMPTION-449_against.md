  Summary: The strongest counter-evidence is Kastrup's OWN testimony that dissociation is hierarchical and layered, with sub-alters inside alters. That is nesting. The claim's negative half survives (the "agreement" label is under-specified and should go), but its positive half — "incompatible mereologies" — does not.

    Strongest counterargument: The real disagreement is about the DIRECTION OF ONTOLOGICAL PRIORITY, not part-whole structure. All three thinkers accept multi-scale subjecthood. Kastrup insists the whole is prior and the parts derivative; Levin and Hoffman are agnostic or bottom-up. On that reading there is no contradiction to adjudicate at all — Kastrup answers "what is fundamental?", Levin answers "what has goals at which scale?", and Hoffman answers "what operations preserve agenthood?". These are different explananda, and treating them as competing mereologies is a category error that would produce a spectacular but spurious paradigm-shift claim.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: One would have to show BOTH that Levin's or Hoffman's sub-agents are ontologically FUNDAMENTAL (not merely explanatorily indispensable at their scale), AND that Kastrup's sub-alters are not genuine subjects. The first is doubtful — Levin's writing suggests scale-relative competency, not fundamentality — and the second is contradicted by source 1.
    How to test: (a) Textually: search Levin's corpus for any assertion of the FUNDAMENTALITY of lower-scale selves versus mere scale-relative competency. If it is the latter, the conflict collapses. (b) Formally: check whether Hoffman's join operation has a well-defined inverse — a split/decomposition theorem. If joins are invertible, composition and dissociation are duals and the "incompatible mereologies" framing fails outright.
