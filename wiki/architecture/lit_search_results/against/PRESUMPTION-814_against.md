### Specific risks

**If the throttle is adopted:** the fleet installs a control that cannot work, observes the queue stay at 37, and — this is the expensive part — records a control as in place. The next reader of the architecture will find "admission policy: throttled" and infer that the queue term has been managed. It has not. PREMISE-119 names this outcome exactly, which means the fleet would be walking into a documented trap with the documentation open.

**Second risk — constraint starvation on return.** The failure mode is asymmetric and the asymmetry is not in the item. Over-production costs a longer queue, which is a triage problem and is bounded by value-based screening. Under-production costs *the wrong thirteen days* — the transcripts are gone, the anomalies are unrecorded, and no amount of later capacity recovers them. Inventory here is not merely valuable, it is the only form in which the period is recoverable at all.

**Third risk — resolution by abandonment.** A suspension with no end condition and no responsible party is indistinguishable from cancellation. In a channel at 0 decisions in 41 days, "stop until reviewed" is functionally "stop."

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** Two clauses of it are not touched by anything above and I want them recorded rather than buried. (i) **"Every run treats its own continued output as unambiguously good"** is a real normative defect and none of my sources exonerate it. Buffers are justified by protective value, and a run that never asks whether its output has any is not running a buffer, it is running an open tap. (ii) **PREMISE-121 is on 14b's side of the ledger**: reviewer degradation tracks low-information items, and each additional weak item lowers the probability that *any* item is acted on. That is a genuine congestion externality and it means the volume question is not empty — it is just that the correct control is *value-based screening*, which PREMISE-119 already named as the better-evidenced alternative, and not a cap.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Do not install a throttle. Install the "produced and unreviewable" state**, which PREMISE-119 already marked ADOPTABLE NOW and which neither search direction has ever contested. It costs one enum value and it makes the buffer honest.
(b) **Screen on value, not on volume** (PREMISE-119's named alternative, PREMISE-121's named obligation). If the fleet must reduce load on Tom, reduce it by raising the bar per item — which is a quality gate the producers can apply — rather than by capping a rate they cannot make binding.
(c) **Size the buffer explicitly rather than defaulting to unbounded.** DBR's own rule is that buffer size tracks the variance of the constrained stage. State the intended buffer depth for a fourteen-day absence, and treat exceeding it as a signal to change *what* is produced, not to stop.
(d) **Discharge PREMISE-121's OPEN MEASUREMENT first**, since it is the input every version of this decision needs and it still does not exist: sum every open item awaiting Tom, attach a time estimate, report the total in hours. Until that number exists, "the eventual review is strictly harder" is an assertion with no magnitude.
(e) **Correct the item's evidential claim before it propagates.** "The alternative has never been proposed by any agent or contract" is false as written; PREMISE-119 proposed and excluded it on 2026-07-21. If that sentence enters the changelog uncorrected it will be cited later as evidence of an unexamined blind spot that was in fact examined.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-814

**Strongest counterargument:** The item's central factual claim is false of this vault's own register, and the falsity is decisive rather than incidental. It says the alternative to continued production "has never been proposed by any agent or contract." It was proposed on 2026-07-21, examined by both search directions, and *excluded by name* in PREMISE-119, which is ACTIVE: "EXCLUDED: a flat per-day admission cap." And the exclusion carries the arithmetic that settles the matter. When the service rate is zero, no reduction in the arrival rate bounds the queue. Four a day leaves 37 cards; one a day leaves 37 cards; zero a day leaves 37 cards. Throttling operates on the term that is not binding while the term that is actually zero — the consumer's presence — goes untouched, and it does something worse than nothing: it *reports a control in place*, so that the next reader of the architecture infers the queue has been managed when it has not. The operations literature the item invokes runs the same way once its boundary conditions are read. Buffers exist to stop a constraint being starved, and Theory of Constraints sizes them upward with the variance of the constrained stage; the variance of Tom's availability is the largest quantity in this system, so a thirteen-day absence is an argument for deeper protective inventory ahead of him, not shallower. There is a named model class for precisely this configuration — queueing-inventory with server vacations — in which the buffer is the thing that makes the system serviceable when the server returns. A fleet that had throttled since 08-02 and met Tom's return with four cards would have starved the constraint at the exact moment it became available, and would have done so having discarded thirteen days of transcripts that no later capacity can reconstruct. Even the WIP-limit advocates supply the boundary themselves: reducing WIP in an underutilised system reduces throughput without touching cycle time, and the named hazard is upstream starvation. The manufacturing analogy is doing more work in this item than it can carry — line-stop is a one-piece-flow control for repetitive work, and the practitioner literature is candid that the analogy, not the concept, is what breaks on transfer to non-repetitive cognitive work where restart is expensive and inputs are perishable. Finally, PREMISE-117's sentence transfers without modification: suspending dependent work behind an unresolved gap, in a channel with near-zero decision throughput, resolves the gap by abandonment rather than adjudication. A stop with no end condition and no responsible party is a cancellation wearing a pause's clothing. What genuinely survives is narrower and better than the throttle: that runs treat their own output as unambiguously good without ever pricing it, and that under PREMISE-121 each additional low-information item lowers the chance that any item is acted upon. The answer to both is value-based screening and a "produced and unreviewable" state — raising the worth of what enters the buffer, and labelling honestly what is sitting in it — neither of which requires anyone to stop.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** The buffer argument holds only while the consumer is **absent** rather than **saturated**, and PREMISE-119 makes that determination a *precondition* rather than an afterthought. If Tom is present and choosing not to review because the queue is unbearable, the congestion externality is real, service rate is not independent of arrival rate, and the item's direction becomes correct. So the fleet is safe continuing to produce only if (i) it establishes and records which of those two states holds, (ii) the buffer is explicitly sized rather than unbounded, with a stated depth for a fourteen-day absence and a stated response to exceeding it, (iii) items entering the buffer are value-screened so the buffer's information content per card is rising rather than falling, and (iv) the "produced and unreviewable" state exists on disk so the buffer is not counted as delivered work. Absent (i) the whole argument in this file is conditional on an unestablished fact, and I record that as its principal weakness.

**How to test:** (1) **Settle absent-versus-saturated.** One question to Tom, or one instrumented read-log on the review page. It is the precondition PREMISE-119 already requires and it decides the polarity of this entire item; nothing else here should be acted on first. (2) **Size the open ask in hours** — PREMISE-121's standing OPEN MEASUREMENT, still undischarged. Without a magnitude, "strictly harder" is unfalsifiable. (3) **Pre-register the throttle experiment if it is run at all**: state in advance that the null predicts the queue stays at 37, throttle one producer for a fortnight, and check. Under PREMISE-119's arithmetic the queue does not move, and that is the cheapest available refutation. (4) **Measure buffer information content**: for the next N cards, record whether Tom's disposition on return differed from what a null "approve all" would have produced. A buffer whose cards all get the same disposition is inventory; one whose cards get different dispositions is protective capacity.
