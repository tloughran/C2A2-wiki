    1. Israeli & Goldenfeld 2004, 'Computational Irreducibility and the Predictability of Complex Physical Systems', PRL 92, 074105 (arXiv:nlin/0309047; title seen; content from background recall): many computationally irreducible cellular automata can be coarse-grained into predictable effective rules. Irreducibility blocks exact shortcut prediction of a trajectory, not useful approximate prediction. So irreducibility is not what forces an inferential model to be approximate; good approximate models of irreducible systems exist.
    2. Cooper 1990 (exact inference in Bayesian networks is NP-hard) and Dagum & Luby 1993 (approximate inference to fixed precision is also NP-hard) (background recall); Kwisthout & van Rooij 'Bridging the Gap' (title seen; recall): the Bayesian limit is a worst-case complexity property of a model class (NP / #P hardness over network families). Computational irreducibility is an informally defined property of a single deterministic process's time evolution (no shortcut faster than simulation). Different object (model class vs single process), different measure (worst-case complexity vs simulation shortcut), different formal status.
    3. 'Computational Irreducibility and Computational Analogy' arXiv:1304.5247 (title seen): attempts to formalize CI precisely because Wolfram's notion has no standard complexity-class definition. This supports reading CI and NP-hardness as not the same limit by default.
    4. Variational free energy (Friston; background recall): the bound is approximate because of a chosen tractable family (e.g. mean-field). That is a design choice to avoid an intractable normalizing integral, which can be required even for systems whose dynamics are fully reducible (e.g. a static high-dimensional posterior with no time evolution at all).
  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: The two limits differ in object, measure and formal status. Irreducibility does not prevent approximate prediction (Israeli & Goldenfeld), and Bayesian intractability arises without any time evolution. At the level the claim-to-test asks about, they are distinct; the pairing is surface analogy under the 'bounded observer' genus.
