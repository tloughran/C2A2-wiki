### Polarity note — what I am challenging

The item's defective belief is "the reviewer's unit of work is the pair." **14b's corrective claim — the proposition I am challenging — is that the corpus-level finding is the higher-yield output, and that the unit of account should change so that corpus-level findings get a slot, a budget and a count.**

Two things I am **not** challenging, and I state them at the top so the challenge is not read wider than it is. (i) That corpus-level findings currently have no slot, budget or count. That is an observation about the schedule and it appears to be simply true. (ii) That Rule 5 favours moving grep-shaped work into code. Also uncontested. What I challenge is the inference from "the unit is unexamined" to "the unit is wrong," and specifically the implied ranking that corpus-level output *dominates* pair-level output. The inspection literature says the fixed small unit is the calibrated dose rather than an accounting accident, and the static-analysis deployment literature says corpus-wide rules carry a false-positive cost the item does not price.

### Strength of challenge: Moderate

### Summary

The item observes correctly that corpus-level findings have no slot, budget or count, and then infers that the unit is wrong. The inspection literature challenges the inference directly: a small, bounded, fixed unit of review work is not an artefact of the queue's data structure but the empirically-calibrated dose at which human defect detection works. The Cisco study — 2,500 reviews over 3.2 million lines — puts the ceiling at 200-400 lines per sitting, finds detection ability diminishing beyond 400, finds defect density below average in 87% of reviews conducted faster than 450 lines per hour, and finds detection rates collapsing after 60-90 minutes. Eleven runs independently proposing "drop from six pairs to two or three" is, on that evidence, eleven reviewers rediscovering a published result about dose, not eleven reviewers sharing a blind spot. The corpus alternative carries costs the item does not price. Its flagship proposal — flag every Bridges bullet naming a tradition without a PRS/CROSS/FLAG token — is a static-analysis rule over a corpus, and it is described entirely by its recall with no estimate of its precision. Untuned static rules run 60-90% false positives, above 90% in some enterprise settings, 76% in one industrial dataset at 10-20 minutes of manual triage per false alarm, and 56% of warnings in a 30-project corpus were never addressed at all. In a fleet where all eleven runs breached budget, an untuned corpus rule is a plausible candidate for the largest new cost proposed that day rather than a five-minute saving. There is an inferential cost too: a pair-level review makes one claim from one examination, while a sweep across 307 files creates 307 opportunities and reports those that fired, with no pre-registration and no false-discovery control — the same look-elsewhere structure this pipeline used to refute PRESUMPTION-806. Audit sampling supplies the structural warrant the item is trading away: the small unit is what makes a finding projectable to the population, ISA 530.14 requires that projection, and PREMISE-168 already carries Geddes/ISA 530 on block samples not being projectable. Two register items sharpen this further. PREMISE-135 holds that generality is purchased by enumerating the domain rather than accumulating instances, and every corpus finding the item praises is an unenumerated generalisation that has not been checked against that bar. PREMISE-162 holds that "eleven instances in one pass" is a catch count with no denominator, so the item's strongest quantitative comparison is between two numbers of which one has no denominator at all. **Against all this, PREMISE-136 is on the item's side and I record it at full strength: rescoping to the corpus is the cheapest legitimate route to a usable denominator, and this register already demonstrates two- and three-digit corpus-scoped denominators against single-digit run-scoped ones.** The honest conclusion is not that the corpus unit is wrong but that the two units have complementary statistical properties, the item's ranking is unearned, and the remedy shape is wrong: the grep belongs upstream of the pair queue as a tuned triage filter, not beside it as a rival unit of account.

### Specific risks

**The false-positive risk, and it is the concrete one.** If the fleet institutes corpus-level rules as scheduled, budgeted work without a precision requirement, it will deploy untuned detectors into a review channel that is already saturated and into a human queue that PREMISE-121 says degrades on low-information items. The measured consequence in the deployment literature is not that the rules fail but that they are **ignored** — 56% of warnings never addressed — and that developers learn to dismiss the channel. That is alert fatigue arriving by a new door, in a fleet that already has PREMISE-102 and PREMISE-121 recording it.

**The projectability risk.** Replacing or displacing a sampled instrument with a block-selected one loses the ability to say anything about the population. The fleet would gain findings and lose the capacity to project them — and PREMISE-168 would then bar it from reporting them as coverage, so the findings would arrive with no way to state what fraction of the corpus they characterise.

**The multiplicity risk.** Corpus sweeps are cheap to run and cheap to repeat. Without pre-registration of the rule or any false-discovery discipline, the fleet acquires an efficient generator of patterns whose base rate it cannot state. This is precisely the machinery that produced the refuted 08-14 headline; scheduling it as the primary unit of work industrialises it.

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** Three things in it are untouched by anything above. (i) **Rule 5 is right**: a token-presence check is grep-shaped work and a model is an expensive way to do it. (ii) **The scheduling gap is real**: there is no slot for producing a corpus finding, so the ones that exist arrived as byproducts, which means their production is unmanaged rather than absent — the worst of both. (iii) **PREMISE-136's denominator argument is a genuine statistical gain** and is register-held. A fleet that files this challenge and changes nothing has kept a calibrated dose and left a real inferential advantage on the table.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Keep the pair as the unit of *review*; add the corpus sweep as a *triage filter upstream of it*.** This is what tuned static-analysis deployment actually looks like — rule fires, precision is measured, survivors are routed to a human. It satisfies Rule 5, gets the eleven instances, and does not trade away projectability. It is also the only proposal here that requires no change to the unit of account.
(b) **Require a precision estimate before any corpus rule is scheduled.** Run the proposed Bridges rule once, count true and false fires by hand on a bounded sample, and state the precision. If it is above roughly 80% the rule is deployable; if it is at untuned SAST levels it needs tuning first. This is an hour and it is the difference between a saving and a cost.
(c) **Set the pair budget by the inspection evidence rather than by budget compliance.** The Cisco figures give a defensible dose; "two or three pairs" may well be correct *for the right reason*, and adopting it on the evidence rather than on token pressure makes it durable.
(d) **Apply PREMISE-135 to every corpus finding before it is banked.** State the population, the termination criterion, and one out-of-sample test. The band-table claim and the id-less-bullet defect class are both generalisations and neither has cleared that bar.
(e) **Pre-register corpus rules.** Write the rule before running it, record it, then run. This costs nothing and it converts a look-elsewhere generator into a test.
(f) **Take PREMISE-136's rescoping gain deliberately**: where a settling quantity has a single-digit run-scoped denominator, rescope it to the corpus *for the denominator*, without moving the review unit. The item's real prize is statistical power, and that can be had without changing the schedule's currency.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-816

**Strongest counterargument:** Eleven runs converging on the same remedy is the item's central evidence of a blind spot, and it is much better explained as eleven reviewers independently rediscovering a well-measured empirical result. The largest study of its kind — SmartBear's ten-month analysis of 2,500 reviews across 3.2 million lines at Cisco — found that reviewers should take no more than 200 to 400 lines at a sitting, that the ability to find defects diminishes beyond 400, that defect density is below average in 87% of reviews conducted faster than 450 lines per hour, and that detection rates collapse after 60 to 90 minutes of continuous work. A small, bounded, fixed unit is not the queue's data structure leaking into the work's accounting. It is the dose at which the instrument functions, and the fleet's runs found the edge of it and said so. On this reading "drop from six pairs to two or three" is not budget-compliance dressed as a remedy; it is the correct answer arrived at eleven times. The corpus alternative is then presented with a recall figure and no precision figure, which is exactly how detectors get deployed and exactly why they get ignored. "Flagging any Bridges bullet that names a tradition and carries no PRS/CROSS/FLAG token would have surfaced all eleven of these in one run" is a static-analysis rule, and untuned static rules run at 60 to 90 per cent false positives, above 90 per cent in some enterprise settings, 76 per cent in one industrial dataset where each false alarm cost ten to twenty minutes of manual inspection, and 56 per cent of warnings in a thirty-project corpus were never addressed by anyone. In a fleet where every run breached its budget, an unturned corpus rule is a candidate for the day's largest new cost, not its cheapest saving — and the failure mode is not that it misfires but that the channel it feeds gets tuned out, which is PREMISE-121's mechanism arriving through a new door. There is an inferential cost as well. One pair, one examination, one claim; one sweep across 307 files, 307 opportunities, and the ones that fired get written down. No rule was pre-registered and no false-discovery discipline was applied, which is the same look-elsewhere structure this very pipeline used to demolish the 08-14 headline by arithmetic. Audit sampling names what is being traded away: the small unit is what makes a finding projectable to the population, ISA 530.14 requires the projection, and this vault's own PREMISE-168 already carries Geddes and ISA 530 on block samples not being projectable. The item wants to swap a designed sample for a block selection and does not say so. Two further register items tighten the case: PREMISE-135 requires a stated population, a termination criterion and one out-of-sample test before any generalisation counts as terminal, and every corpus finding the item admires is an unchecked generalisation; PREMISE-162 holds that "eleven in one pass" is a catch count with no denominator, so the item's headline yield comparison sets a number against another number that has no base. And yet the item is not empty, because PREMISE-136 is on its side: rescoping to the corpus is the cheapest legitimate route to a usable denominator, and this register already contains two- and three-digit corpus-scoped quantities where run-scoped ones sit in single digits. The two units have complementary statistical properties. What is unearned is the ranking, and what is wrong is the remedy's shape — the grep belongs upstream of the pair queue as a tuned filter, not beside it as a rival currency.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** The corpus unit is safe to schedule where (i) each corpus rule carries a **measured precision**, not just a recall anecdote, and clears a stated threshold before deployment; (ii) each rule is **pre-registered** before it is run, so that a sweep is a test rather than a search; (iii) each corpus finding clears **PREMISE-135's** three requirements — population, termination criterion, one out-of-sample severe test — before it is banked as a finding; and (iv) each is reported in **PREMISE-168's** stratified form with its frame's provenance and no coverage percentage. Conversely the pair unit is safe to retain only if its budget is set by the inspection evidence rather than by token pressure — "two or three" adopted because 200-400 lines at a sitting is the calibrated dose, not because six did not fit. If all four conditions hold, both units can run in series and the item's genuine gain (PREMISE-136's denominator) is captured without losing projectability.

**How to test:** (1) **Measure the precision of the proposed Bridges rule.** Run it once over a bounded slice, hand-classify every fire as true or false, state precision and recall together. One hour, and it decides whether the item's flagship example is a saving or a cost — this is the single highest-value action in this file. (2) **Run the dose experiment on the pair unit**: for the next N runs, record pairs attempted against defects found per pair, and check whether yield per pair falls with run length as the Cisco curve predicts. If it does, "fewer pairs" is validated on evidence and the unit question is settled without changing the unit. (3) **Seeded defects**, per PREMISE-162's cheapest remedy: plant known faults across the corpus and see whether the pair route or the corpus route finds more of them. This is the only design that gives the two units a common denominator and it is what would actually settle the ranking claim. (4) **Apply PREMISE-135 retrospectively** to the three corpus findings the item cites as the day's most valuable outputs. If none states its population, the item's evidence for corpus superiority is a set of claims that have not yet met the register's own bar.
