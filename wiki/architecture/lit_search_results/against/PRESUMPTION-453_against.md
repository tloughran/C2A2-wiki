  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: This is the most cleanly challenged item of the six. The composition-effect literature (from Bickel et al. onward) establishes that when the incoming population is 100% of one stratum, aggregate category rates move mechanically, carrying no information about the behavior the metric was built to track. Health-services research formalizes the remedy — longitudinal indicators require case-mix adjustment or stratification when population composition shifts, as a precondition of validity, not a refinement. Goodhart/Strathern and the surrogation experiments (Choi et al.) add the behavioral layer: once the orphan count is watched or targeted, agents will either misread mix-driven inflation as synthesis failure or "fix" it by mechanically linking orphans-by-design pages — both of which damage the actual construct (synthesis health) while improving or explaining away the number. An unexamined dilution of this kind invalidates week-over-week comparability of the census's headline categories.

  Specific risks: (a) False alarm: orphan % rises mechanically, triggering wasted sewing effort or false "synthesis degrading" conclusions; (b) masked decay: genuine loss of connectivity among legacy pages hidden inside a mix-driven trend; (c) Goodhart response: agents sew orphans-by-design pages to make the number move, corrupting the design intent of those pages AND the metric simultaneously; (d) longitudinal record poisoned — later analyses cannot recover what the pre-dilution trend meant without page-type metadata.

  Mitigations available: Stratify the census by page cohort/type (orphan-by-design vs organic), reporting synthesis-health metrics only over the eligible population; add an explicit "design-orphan" tag or frontmatter flag consumed by the census agent; report both raw and mix-adjusted series (a case-mix-adjusted orphan rate); freeze interpretation rules in the census report template ("orphan % is not comparable across cohort-mix changes"); periodic metric review triggered by any deliberate change in page-creation policy.

    Strongest counterargument: A rate whose denominator absorbs a new subpopulation that is 100% in one category by construction measures the growth policy, not the synthesis process. This is not a subtle statistical concern: it is the composition effect that Bickel et al. showed can fully reverse conclusions, combined with the case-mix literature's finding that unadjusted longitudinal indicators are invalid across population shifts, and Goodhart/surrogation dynamics ensuring the error will not stay passive — agents will act on the distorted number. Continuing to read the orphan/sparse/connected trend as "synthesis health" without stratification is measuring a known artifact and calling it a signal.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Census reports would need to already exclude or separately report orphans-by-design (denominator restricted to the eligible population); consumers of the census would need to know the growth policy and discount the headline rate accordingly; and no agent may target the raw orphan count. Alternatively, safe if the metric's declared purpose is redefined as "vault composition tracking" rather than "synthesis health."
    How to test: Recompute the last several census snapshots with orphans-by-design excluded from the denominator and compare trends; if the stratified and raw series diverge, the dilution is quantified and the raw series' invalidity as a health measure is demonstrated. Also check transcripts for any agent action taken in response to orphan-rate movements (evidence of surrogation already operating).
