  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: This is the clearest direct contradiction in the batch. The item states that acknowledgement is never the measure of delivery and that installing it produces a green metric over an unchanged failure. High-reliability practice says close to the opposite: read-back/hearback is the foundational mechanism of closed-loop communication, and the operating-theatre study found directed closed-loop communication associated with increased task completion, which is delivery-to-action, not delivery-to-receipt. The I-PASS data show a structured acknowledgement protocol moving both adherence and safety culture by large margins. The item's underlying concern is legitimate — a receipt that is never followed by action is a proxy — but the literature's response to that concern is to make the acknowledgement substantive (read back the content, not "received") rather than to abolish acknowledgement in favour of output-scanning. The item's own proposed measure has a defect the literature avoids: searching a recipient's output for flagged content cannot distinguish "flag not received" from "flag received, considered, correctly rejected."
  Specific risks: If acknowledgement is abolished, the cheapest and fastest failure detector is removed. Under the item's measure the only signal arrives after the recipient's next output cycle, so a lost flag is invisible until then; a read-back would have caught it immediately. Second risk: output-scanning conflates non-receipt with informed rejection, and a recipient who correctly declines a flag is scored identically to one who never saw it — a metric that punishes judgement.
  Mitigations available: Take both measures rather than one. Require content read-back (recipient restates the flag substantively, not a bare receipt), which is the literature's actual recommendation and is what distinguishes a real closed loop from a checkbox; then separately run the item's output-scan as the effectiveness check. Add an explicit "received and declined, with reason" state so informed rejection is distinguishable from loss. Note the StatPearls boundary condition: this only works if read-back is a system norm, not an individual habit.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-486
    Strongest counterargument: Acknowledgement is not a decorative proxy — it is the core control in the domains that most care about handoff failure, and the item proposes deleting it. Readback/hearback is described in the patient-safety literature as the fundamental mechanism of closed-loop communication and an essential ground rule wherever small groups manage high-consequence processes; the simulated-emergency study found directed closed-loop communication associated with increased task completion, meaning acknowledgement tracked action rather than substituting for it; and system-wide I-PASS deployment moved handoff adherence from 41.6% to 70.5%. The item's real target is the degenerate form — a bare "received" with no content — and the discipline's answer to that has been settled for twenty years: require the recipient to read the content back, which cannot be produced without processing it. Replacing this with output-scanning is strictly worse on two counts: it delays detection by a full output cycle, and it cannot separate a flag that was never received from a flag that was received, weighed, and correctly declined. The right architecture is both — substantive read-back as the delivery check, output appearance as the effectiveness check — which is exactly the CAPA structure the batch reaches for elsewhere.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: That read-back is substantive (content restated) and normative (required of all agents, not adopted individually), and that a distinct "received and declined, with reason" state exists so that informed rejection is not scored as delivery failure.
    How to test: Run the item's 30-day enumeration of cross-agent flags, but classify each into four buckets rather than two: acknowledged and acted on, acknowledged and explicitly declined, acknowledged and silently dropped, never acknowledged. If the third bucket dominates, the item is vindicated and acknowledgement really is hollow here. If the fourth dominates, acknowledgement is the missing control, not the false one.
