    Strongest counterargument: The tacit-knowledge objection proves far too much and does not survive
      contact with this case. Tsoukas's argument is about *skilled performance* — how a craftsman knows
      which particulars to attend to — and the twenty cases are nothing like that. PREMISE-182 gives a
      closed-form identity for exactly the calculation the Summa run performed; PREMISE-174 states in
      plain words the thing ASSUMPTION-1309 proposed to violate; PREMISE-141 names the third terminal
      state that one run described as something it had to invent. These are not tacit judgements of
      relevance that a text cannot carry. They are explicit, indexed, directly-on-point statements that
      would have settled the question on sight, and the *only* reason they did not is that nobody opened
      the file. Against that, "recognition is tacit" is an elegant way of declining to do the cheap thing.
      The KM retrieval-cost account is if anything a *strengthening* of the item rather than a rival to
      it: "the repository was not searched because searching was costly" IS a propagation defect, stated
      in the vocabulary of the practitioner rather than the architect, and it points at the same fix. And
      the selection critique, while correct, does not touch the direction of the finding: twenty for
      twenty on a convenience sample cannot give a precise rate, but zero instances of a run citing its
      governing premise across twenty consecutive opportunities bounds the rate well away from "usually
      consulted" under any reasonable prior. The alert-fatigue objection is real but is an argument about
      *how* to propagate, not *whether* — and PREMISE-121's own value-weighted-triage clause says exactly
      that: raise the information value of each item routed, do not route fewer.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the remedy must deliver the *governing* premise
      and not the register, or PREMISE-121's override band applies to it on day one; (b) the twenty cases
      must be shown to be recognition-transparent — i.e. a fresh run given the text applies it — or the
      transport fix is the wrong build; (c) the rate must be re-derived on an enumerated window before it
      is cited again as a property of the estate; (d) whatever is built must have a named final element
      under PREMISE-173, and must not close the register's only contraction path under PREMISE-174.
      (c) and (d) cost nothing and are compatible with every word of the steelman.
    How to test: the five-case recognition discriminator described under Mitigations is the decisive
      experiment and it separates the steelman from the challenge cleanly. Run it BEFORE the denominator
      sweep, because if recognition fails the denominator measures the wrong quantity. Both are in-house,
      neither needs a new capability, and together they cost less than the propagation channel they would
