**Polarity note.** The count is a measurement and I do not contest it. The AGAINST direction tests three propositions the item carries. **(i) That "produced no deliverable" is a coherent defect category** — the item's own text concedes it is not homogeneous, since one of the nine "completed but wrote nothing by design." **(ii) That the remedy is to count deliverables and reconcile them against a manifest.** **(iii) That the indistinguishability of a silent zero from a completed run is a literature question.** I found a **strong challenge to (ii) in its natural form** — from audit sampling doctrine, which states in one line why counting produced artefacts cannot detect unproduced ones — and a **moderate challenge to (i)**, from the fact that the nine collapse at least four distinct states with different correct responses. (iii) I judge to be substantially an operating-system question rather than a literature one. The observation itself stands.

### Challenging evidence found: Yes (partial — against the defect category and against the artefact-counting remedy, not against the count)

### Sources

1. **PCAOB AU 350 / AS 2315, *Audit Sampling* — population appropriateness and the omitted-items illustration.** [verified this run via pcaobus.org; the standard's own worked example, quoted at snippet level from the retrieved page] — The single most relevant source in this file and a direct challenge to the remedy in its obvious form. The requirement: "the auditor should determine that the **population from which he draws the sample is appropriate for the specific audit objective**." The illustration, which is the fleet's exact situation: "**An auditor would not be able to detect understatements of an account due to omitted items by sampling the recorded items. An appropriate sampling plan for detecting such understatements would involve selecting from a source in which the omitted items are included.**" Counting deliverables — or reconciling deliverables against each other — is sampling the recorded items. The nine runs are the omitted items. The frame must be the *schedule*, which is authored independently of whether any run occurred.
2. **Batch control totals and reconciliation practice** (Tungsten/Kofax "Reconciling batch totals" documentation; standard IS-audit treatment of record count, hash total and dollar total). [verified this run — snippet level, vendor documentation and course material] — Establishes what the fleet does *not* have, and thereby challenges the item's implied fix. Batch reconciliation works because "the batch totals reconciliation compares the **computed** batch total with the **expected** batch total," where the expected total is declared *before* processing. There are "three kinds of batch totals: record count, hash total, and dollar total," and equality "provide[s] evidence that all transactions have been entered." The fleet has no per-run declared expected output, so a count of nine is not a control total; it is one observer's post-hoc classification, and nothing in the architecture can recompute it. Under this practice the item's own number is uncheckable by anyone but its author.
3. **ETL reconciliation practice** (Integrate.io, "Data validation in ETL," 2026; Datagaps ETL testing guide; Airbyte ETL test-case guide). [verified this run — snippet level, vendor documentation] — The same principle in the fleet's own idiom: "reconciliation checks that compare **source and destination** record counts can detect data loss, and these checks should run **automatically after each ETL job completes**." Note both conditions: a *source-side* count, and automatic execution. A daily rollup written by a run that itself may not finish satisfies neither.
4. **Errors of omission versus commission — detectability asymmetry** (Plutus Education comparative treatment; UX Magazine, "What are UX errors of omission and commission"; Coevolving Innovations, "Doing, not-doing"). [verified this run — snippet level, secondary/tertiary sources; **weak evidentiary quality, flagged**] — Corroborates the item rather than challenging it: "errors of omission are harder to identify as the transaction is entirely missing from records"; "it's easier to see something that is there than something that's missing." Included because it establishes that the item's diagnosis is the field's consensus and there is no serious literature on the other side.
5. **Parasuraman & Manzey (2010), "Complacency and Bias in Human Use of Automation: An Attentional Integration," *Human Factors* 52(3).** [verified this run — abstract and summary level via SAGE and the TU-Berlin deposit copy; **not read in full**] — Two findings bear on the remedy. Automation-related omission errors are attributed to "**cognitive vigilance decrements**" under multiple-task load, and both complacency and automation bias "**cannot be prevented by training or instructions**" and are "found in both naive and expert participants." The implication for C2A2 is unwelcome in both directions: a human reader of a nine-line daily omission report will habituate to it, and the report cannot be rescued by instructing the reader to attend. The reported omission-error rate of 55% in one aviation monitoring study [snippet level, **secondary attribution, not traced to primary**] is cited only to indicate the magnitude class.
6. **Deming — "Inspection does not improve the quality"; Point 3, "cease dependence on inspection"; the kp rule (all-or-none inspection for a process in statistical control).** (deming.org quotation pages; Rigdon et al., "Mood's Theorem, Deming's kp Rule, and the Death of Acceptance Sampling," *Quality Engineering* 8(1), 1995.) [quotations verified this run; the *Quality Engineering* paper **located but NOT read — title and venue verified via Taylor & Francis listing only**] — The economic challenge. Nine failures in twenty-five is a 36% defect rate. At that rate a detector is not the leverage point, and the kp rule says explicitly that partial inspection is never economically optimal for a stable process — it is all or none. If the fleet's response to a 36% no-output rate is a counter, it has chosen the option the quality tradition rules out.

### Strength of challenge: Moderate

### Summary

The measurement is sound and the diagnosis has no opposition in the literature — omissions are consensus-hard to detect, and "a silent zero and a completed run are indistinguishable" is a clean statement of the problem. Three challenges land beyond that. First and sharpest: the natural remedy — count what was produced, reconcile the counts — is disqualified by the doctrine the fleet is otherwise citing approvingly in ASSUMPTION-1077. AU 350 says you cannot detect omitted items by sampling recorded items and that the frame must be a source in which the omitted items appear. For C2A2 that source exists and is not the artefact tree: it is the **launchd schedule**, which declares what should have run and is authored independently of whether anything did. Second: "produced no deliverable" is not one defect. The nine collapse at least four states — crashed mid-flight; ran, found nothing to do, and was right; completed with no artefact by design (the item names this case itself); and blocked on an external dependency, where `c2a2-morning-chat-scrape` had a *contract* requiring it to write a failure note and did not. These have different correct responses, and a single counter that treats them alike will generate false positives on the legitimate cases and thereby train its reader to ignore it. Third: at a 36% rate, control-total practice and Deming both point away from detection. Batch reconciliation earns its keep because the expected total is declared in advance and the check runs automatically on completion; a hand-tallied figure in a rollup written by a run that may itself die satisfies neither condition, and the number nine, as things stand, is not recomputable by any process in the fleet. Finally, a scope caution: the rollup's unsourced "8 new proposals" is a *different* defect — an aggregate asserted without a read — and folding it into the omission finding risks a remedy aimed at neither.

### Specific risks

If the remedy is an artefact-side counter, the fleet builds an instrument that structurally cannot see its target population, and the AU 350 illustration says so in advance. It will report faithfully on the runs that survived to be reported on, which is the definition of the problem.

If the remedy is a per-run "you must produce a deliverable" rule, the fleet acquires false positives on every legitimate no-op — `summa-2026-daily-batch` first — and the standard response to a noisy detector with no reader is disregard. Parasuraman & Manzey's finding that this cannot be trained away means the disregard is not correctable by exhortation.

If the item is dismissed, the hazard is exactly as measured, and it is compounding in a specific way the item already shows: the day's rollup reported eight proposals as an accomplishment while two agents produced zero, so **the fleet's own summary layer is currently converting silent zeros into positive claims**. That is worse than not counting; it is counting the wrong sign.

A further risk: the number nine is currently a single-observer classification with no recomputable definition. If it is quoted forward — and the fleet's record shows counts being quoted forward — it will join the population described in ASSUMPTION-1072, where one measurand has four live values.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Reconcile against the schedule, not against the outputs.** Enumerate the launchd plists that should have fired in the window; for each, record fired / did-not-fire / fired-and-terminated. This is the frame AU 350 demands, it is owned outside the fleet's execution path, and it is a single directory read. Highest value per unit cost in this file.
(b) **Declare an expected output per task, in the task file, before the run.** This is the batch control total. It converts "no deliverable" from a judgement into a comparison, makes the count recomputable by anyone, and — critically — lets `summa-2026-daily-batch` declare `expected_outputs: none` so that it stops being a false positive.
(c) **Separate the four states.** `NO-OUTPUT-BY-DESIGN`, `NO-WORK-FOUND`, `BLOCKED-ON-DEPENDENCY`, `TERMINATED-INCOMPLETE`. Only the fourth is an incident; the third is a contract violation where the contract required a note (`c2a2-morning-chat-scrape`); the first two are healthy. A detector that cannot say which it saw will not be read twice.
(d) **Make the rollup cite its source.** "8 new proposals" should be a count computed from `inbox/proposals/pending/` at write time with the path and timestamp recorded, not a narrative claim. This is a different fix from (a)–(c) and addresses the second half of the item.
(e) **Then attack the rate.** Nine in twenty-five is a process-capability figure. Deming's position is that the counter will not move it and that effort spent on the counter is effort not spent on the timeouts, checkpointing and resumption that would.

### Recommendation: PARTIALLY-CHALLENGED

## STEELMAN

**Item:** ASSUMPTION-1069

**Strongest counterargument:** The item is right about the disease and about to prescribe the one treatment its own sister-item's literature disqualifies. ASSUMPTION-1077 asks the fleet to take PCAOB AU 350 seriously as a model for unattended validity. AU 350 contains a worked example that is, almost word for word, this item: *an auditor would not be able to detect understatements due to omitted items by sampling the recorded items; an appropriate plan would select from a source in which the omitted items are included.* Every artefact-side remedy — count the deliverables, reconcile the outputs, tally the proposals — samples the recorded items. The nine runs are, by construction, not in that population. There is exactly one frame in this architecture that contains them, and it is the launchd schedule: a declaration of what should have run, written before the fact, by something other than the fleet. That is a directory listing, not a project. And the framing beneath the remedy is also shakier than it reads. "Nine of twenty-five produced no deliverable" treats as one category a run that crashed after two tool calls, a run that correctly found nothing to do, a run designed to write nothing, and a run whose contract obliged it to write a failure note and which failed to do so — the last being a genuine and specific contract breach that the aggregate hides rather than exposes. Batch-control practice would not accept the number at all: control totals work because the expected figure is declared in advance and the comparison is mechanical, and here the expected figure does not exist, so nine is one reader's judgement that no process can reproduce. Set against all of this is a rate of 36%. Deming's kp rule is blunt about that regime: for a stable process, inspect all or none, never a sample; and inspection, whichever you choose, does not improve the thing inspected. A fleet losing a third of its runs does not have a counting problem.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** The reconciliation frame must be sourced from the scheduler rather than from the artefact tree, **and** each task must declare its expected output in advance so the reconciliation is mechanical rather than interpretive, **and** the four no-output states must be distinguishable so that legitimate silence does not train the reader to ignore the alarm. With all three, the item's hazard is closed and the remedy is cheap. With an artefact-side counter alone, the hazard is live and covered — the fleet will hold a daily figure that is structurally blind to the population it names.

**How to test:** **One, and it is decisive:** list the launchd agents scheduled in the 2026-08-14 window and compare against the 25 transcripts. If the schedule contains agents that produced no transcript at all, then the transcript-derived denominator of 25 is itself short, the "nine of twenty-five" figure understates the problem, and the frame argument is proved by measurement rather than by citation. This costs one directory read and needs no attendance. **Two:** re-derive the number nine from the artefacts alone, without the transcripts, and see whether it comes out as nine. If it cannot be re-derived, the figure is not a control total and should not be quoted forward. **Three:** check `c2a2-morning-chat-scrape` specifically — its contract required a note on failure and none was written. That is a single, provable contract breach with a named remedy, and it is a better first fix than any aggregate counter because it is falsifiable and closed in one edit.

## SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Affected items:** ASSUMPTION-1068, ASSUMPTION-1069, ASSUMPTION-1075, ASSUMPTION-1077 (peripherally ASSUMPTION-1070)
**Common vulnerability:** **Every instrument these four items propose draws its sample from the set of runs, artefacts or decisions that reached a recording surface — a frame generated by the very process the instrument is meant to audit.** 1068's watcher runs on the plane it watches; 1069 would count deliverables produced by runs that survived to produce them; 1075's checks report only on pairs where the check did not silently skip; 1077's override rate is computed over exactly the items some upstream filter routed to the human. In each case the failures of interest never entered the frame.
**Literature basis:** PCAOB **AU 350 / AS 2315** — "an auditor would **not** be able to detect understatements of an account due to **omitted items** by sampling the **recorded** items. An appropriate sampling plan for detecting such understatements would involve selecting from a source in which the omitted items are included" [verified this run]. **Priest & Klein (1984), "The Selection of Disputes for Litigation"** — outcome rates over a selected set are uninformative about the underlying population; the acknowledged corollary is that appellate reversal rate is "an imperfect proxy for quality" [verified this run, snippet level]. **LOPA / CCPS independence and common-cause rules** — credit only one layer where common cause applies [verified this run, snippet level].
**Risk level:** Critical
**Recommendation:** Before any of these four instruments is built, state its **sampling frame** and the **source from which the frame is drawn**, and check that the source is not produced by the audited process. For 1068 and 1069 the qualifying frame already exists and the fleet owns it: the **launchd schedule**. For 1077 the equivalent is **seeded defects** — without items of known status injected into the population, no override statistic has a denominator that means anything.
