### Sources

1. **Alpern, B. & Schneider, F.B. (1985), "Defining Liveness," *Information Processing Letters* 21(4):181–185.** [**VERIFIED this run — full paper fetched from cs.cornell.edu and read end to end**] — The decisive source, and it converts the item's claim from a fact about the world into a fact about C2A2's specification. Safety: *"P is a safety property if and only if... if the 'bad thing' happens during an execution, then there is an identifiable point at which it happens."* Liveness: *"A partial execution α is live for a property P if and only if there is a sequence of states β such that αβ ∈ P. A liveness property is one for which every partial execution is live"* — i.e. **no finite observation can refute a liveness property, because the good thing may still happen later.** "The instrument will eventually report" is a liveness property; that is precisely why C2A2 cannot tell silence from a clean result, and the item's intuition is formally correct *for that specification*. But Theorem 1 is the exit: *"Every property P is the intersection of a safety property and a liveness property."* **"The instrument reports by 06:00" is a safety property** — bounded liveness — and it is refuted at 06:01 by a finite observation with an identifiable point. Distinguishability is therefore not a fact about post-hoc epistemics; it is purchased by writing a deadline into the expectation. C2A2 has schedules and has not written the deadlines as assertions. That is a one-line change per task, and it makes the item's Critical proposition false by construction.

### Summary

This item states a proposition that the formal literature shows to be a property of C2A2's specification rather than of the world, and the item's own paragraph demonstrates it. Alpern and Schneider's characterisation, read in full this run, is exact: a liveness property — "the instrument will eventually report" — is one that **no finite observation can refute**, because every partial execution can still be extended to satisfy it; a safety property is one whose violation has "an identifiable point at which it happens" and is therefore refutable by a finite prefix. C2A2 has written its expectations as liveness properties, which is why silence looks indistinguishable from success. Their Theorem 1 supplies the exit — every property is the intersection of a safety and a liveness property — and the operational form of it is a deadline: "reports by 06:00" is refutable at 06:01 by looking. The distinction the item calls impossible costs one assertion per scheduled task. That the distinction is already being drawn is proved by the item itself, which counts four mutes, attributes one to `morning-system-health` dying after two `request_cowork_directory` calls, and notes the telemetry task stalled twice — all after the fact, all by comparing expected artifacts against actual ones. The real finding is that this is being done by hand by the presumption detector rather than automatically at the deadline. On the remedy, the search is less kind to the item's implied answer than the item expects: external heartbeats are standard, but Huang et al.'s gray-failure work names *differential observability* as the central pathology — the external observer reports healthy while the workload fails — and the practitioner literature adds that heartbeats pinged anywhere other than after verified completion reproduce the self-report defect one level up, that grace-period tuning generates false positives, and that "an untested dead man's switch is worse than none at all," which is precisely what the OpenStory telemetry contract turned out to be. Chandra and Toueg bound the whole enterprise — no failure detector is perfect in an asynchronous system — but that result does not transfer to a scheduled system with wall clocks and durable artifacts, and invoking it here would be a domain-transfer error. Finally, and most importantly for disposition: **this register already holds the remedy.** PREMISE-086 states the age-alarm dead-man's-switch pattern, states the perceived-liveness trap, and states the monitor-of-monitor requirement, with SRE and Nagios/Prometheus anchoring; PREMISE-100 states that a liveness signal is not evidence of correctness; PREMISE-053 stated the scheduled-job form earlier still. 819 is a deployment gap on validated premises, not a discovery — and framing it as a discovery is what has kept the deployment from happening.

### Specific risks

**The impossibility framing is self-fulfilling, and this is the main risk.** If "silence and a clean result are not distinguishable after the fact" enters the architecture as a structural finding, the correct response — write deadlines as assertions — looks like a partial mitigation of an intractable problem rather than the complete solution it is. Items framed as intractable get monitors; items framed as tractable get fixed. On the formal reading, this one is tractable.

**The heartbeat-as-answer risk.** Adopting external heartbeats without PREMISE-086's monitor-of-monitor conditional and without "ping only after verified completion" produces a green signal generated by an observer with a different vantage than the work. That is Huang et al.'s gray failure by construction and PREMISE-100's false-green by name, and it would make the fleet's self-assessment *worse* while appearing to fix it — because a green heartbeat is more persuasive than silence.

**The re-derivation cost.** Three ACTIVE premises already cover this. Minting 819 produces a fourth entry for one mechanism (PREMISE-138 bars it) and, worse, resets the clock: PREMISE-086 has been ACTIVE and undeployed, which is a *governance* finding about the gap between validation and implementation that a new premise would obscure rather than surface.

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** Clause (A) is correct, is not challenged by anything here, and is the item's genuine contribution: **the fix adopted twice was a clause instructing the failing component to report its own failure**, and that is structurally void for a component whose failure mode is not executing. The OpenStory telemetry contract is the proof — it declared it writes on PASS and FAIL both *because* "this is how a silent stall becomes visible," and it stalled silently twice. A contract clause is PREMISE-131's administrative control, the second-least-effective tier. Dismissing 819 would discard that, and it is the sentence the fleet most needs on the wall.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Convert every scheduled expectation from liveness to bounded liveness.** One assertion per task: "artifact X exists, is dated today, and carries an outcome value, by time T." This is Alpern–Schneider's safety form and it is the entire fix for clause (B).
(b) **Deploy PREMISE-086 rather than re-minting it** — age-alarm on time-since-last-PASS, alarm on age rather than display last value, plus the monitor-of-monitor.
(c) **Ping only after verified completion, never on entry**, and verify the *content* not the file's existence (PREMISE-115's effectiveness-check-at-content-level clause).
(d) **Mutation-validate the switch** (PREMISE-137's load-bearing condition): deliberately kill a task and confirm the alarm fires. The OpenStory contract failed exactly this test and nobody ran it.
(e) **Publish the denominator** (PREMISE-168): four mutes out of how many scheduled runs? The deadline assertions in (a) generate this figure automatically within a week and replace "an amount nobody can currently bound" with a rate.
(f) **Do not route correctness assurance through heartbeats** (PREMISE-100). Heartbeats bound silence only; 66/307 was a wrong-output failure and needs PREMISE-113's labelled corpus, not a watchdog.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-819

**Strongest counterargument:** The item asserts an impossibility that is actually a specification choice, and it demonstrates the choice is reversible in the same paragraph in which it declares it impossible. Alpern and Schneider settled the underlying question in five pages in 1985: a liveness property cannot be refuted by any finite observation, because "a liveness property is one for which every partial execution is live" — the good thing can always still happen. C2A2's expectations are written as liveness properties ("the run will report"), so of course silence is indistinguishable from success; that is what liveness *means*. But their Theorem 1 shows every property decomposes into a safety part and a liveness part, and the safety part is refutable at "an identifiable point." Write "reports by 06:00" instead of "reports," and at 06:01 the violation is a finite, locatable, mechanical observation. The distinction the item calls impossible costs one line per task. And the item already draws it: it counts four mutes, names `morning-system-health` as dying after two `request_cowork_directory` calls, and reports the telemetry refresh stalling twice — every one of those an after-the-fact distinction between silence and a clean result, made by comparing an expectation record against durable artifacts. What is actually true is narrower and more actionable: the distinction is currently drawn *by hand, retrospectively, by the presumption detector* instead of automatically at a deadline. On the remedy, the search cuts against the item's implied answer too. Huang et al.'s gray-failure work makes differential observability the central pathology — the external observer reports healthy while the workload fails — so an external heartbeat converts silence into a green signal from a different vantage, which is worse than silence because it is persuasive. The practitioner corpus adds that the modal implementation defect is pinging the switch anywhere but after verified completion (reproducing self-report one level up), that grace periods generate false positives, and that "an untested dead man's switch is worse than none at all" — which is exactly what the OpenStory contract was: it declared it writes on PASS and FAIL both *because* that is how a silent stall becomes visible, and then wrote neither, twice, because nobody ever killed it to check. Chandra and Toueg bound the ambition — no detector is perfect under asynchrony — but C2A2 is a scheduled system with wall clocks and durable files, not an asynchronous message-passing one, and importing the impossibility result here is a domain-transfer error. And the decisive point for disposition is in-house: **PREMISE-086 already holds the age-alarm dead-man's-switch pattern, the perceived-liveness trap, and the monitor-of-monitor requirement, validated with SRE and Nagios/Prometheus anchoring; PREMISE-100 already holds that a liveness signal is not evidence of correctness.** 819 is a deployment gap on validated premises. Framing it as a structural discovery is itself part of why it has not been deployed.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** Safety here requires four things, and they are all cheap. (i) Every scheduled expectation is written as a **deadline assertion over a durable artifact with an outcome value**, so that non-execution violates a safety property and is caught by a finite check. (ii) The check runs in a **different failure domain** from the thing it checks (PREMISE-089's shared-scheduler caveat), and has its own independent liveness check (PREMISE-086's conditional). (iii) The switch is **mutation-validated** — a deliberately killed task must produce an alarm — because an untested switch supplies false confidence and the fleet has one documented instance of exactly that. (iv) Heartbeats are scoped to **silence only** and never consumed as evidence of correctness; the 66/307 wrong-output class is routed to PREMISE-113's labelled-corpus discipline instead. Under those four the item's Critical grading is discharged and the residual risk is the irreducible Chandra–Toueg one, which is small in a system with wall clocks. Absent (i), everything the item says is true and the grading is right.

**How to test:** (1) **The one-week deadline experiment, and it settles the headline claim.** Write deadline assertions for all scheduled tasks; run for seven days; count violations. If the count is non-zero and each violation names a task and a time, clause (B) is refuted by construction and the fleet has, as a free by-product, the denominator the item says is unbounded. (2) **The kill test:** deliberately terminate the OpenStory telemetry refresh mid-run and confirm an alarm reaches a channel with demonstrated non-zero throughput (PREMISE-102). If no alarm fires, the switch was never a switch, and that — not indistinguishability — is the finding. (3) **The vantage test for gray failure:** have the heartbeat check assert a *content* invariant of the artifact (rows written, non-empty outcome field), not its existence, and re-run against the 66 zero-extraction transcripts. If the heartbeat goes green on files the guard could not read, differential observability is present in-house and clause (C) is confirmed challenged. (4) **The archaeology check, which is the cheapest and most uncomfortable:** date PREMISE-086's validation, and count how many of the day's four mutes would have been caught had it been deployed. That number is the cost of the intake gate defect, and it is a governance finding rather than an architectural one.
