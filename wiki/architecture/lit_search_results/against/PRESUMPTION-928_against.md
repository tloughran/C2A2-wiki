  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: I found no literature that treats deferral in an operating system as neutral, and several
  lines that treat it as a selection. The decision-avoidance review (Anderson 2003) classifies doing
  nothing as a structured behaviour with its own antecedents, grouping choice deferral with status quo
  bias, omission bias and inaction inertia rather than with the absence of a decision. Tversky and Shafir
  (1992) supply the specific mechanism the routed question asks about: enlarging or improving the option
  set raises deferral, so an escalation — which by construction enlarges the argument around a binary —
  makes non-resolution more likely rather than less. The closest operational analogue is the clinical
  alerting literature, and it is unambiguous: a flag that fires without resolution is overridden in 49%
  to 96% of cases, at 85–96% for the lower-severity classes, and its force decays measurably with
  exposure — one system's override rate rose from about 50% to 75% over five years, which van der Sijs
  et al. read as declining compliance. The safety-science literature names the end state: an
  acknowledged-but-unresolved signal, carried across repeated uneventful operations, is normalised into
  the accepted way of working. Applied to the item's own evidence, the four lit-search cycles executed
  under REVISE-436's unresolved binary are not four instances of waiting; they are four executions of
  limb (b), and they now constitute a precedent with a track record. The 08-25 binary at 13 days and a
  fourteenth consecutive escalation carry are the same pattern at a longer horizon. The escalation
  register, on this reading, is not a control that suspends practice; it is a label attached to practice
  that continues.

  Specific risks: (a) The incumbent option is being adopted without a decision record. If limb (b) is
  in fact wrong, the system has accumulated four cycles of output produced under it, and reversing the
  ruling later does not retract that output — the cost of the deferral is already sunk into the
  registers. (b) Precedent hardens: each additional cycle run under the unresolved binary makes limb (b)
  the thing the system does, and any later ruling for limb (a) becomes a change rather than a choice,
  which the status-quo literature says is systematically disfavoured. The deferral is therefore not
  neutral over time — it is actively loading the eventual decision. (c) The escalation counter is at
  risk of becoming the mechanism by which the item is *not* decided: it converts an unresolved question
  into a tracked, legible, apparently-managed state, which removes the discomfort that would otherwise
  force resolution. On the alerting evidence, repeated firing without resolution predicts declining
  responsiveness to the register itself, so the fourteenth carry is worth less than the first. (d) The
  register cannot currently distinguish "paused" from "running under the incumbent," so the system has
  no way to see this happening from inside. (e) This is the same question the 09-07 SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG
  raises one level down — whether a declared control is a control — which means the two items can
  reinforce each other's blind spot rather than catching it.

  Mitigations available: Make the incumbent explicit rather than implicit. Every open binary should
  record which limb the system is *currently executing under*, so that "no ruling" is displayed as
  "running under (b), undecided" rather than as a pause — this is the single change that would have made
  the four cycles visible as four selections. Attach a cost counter to each open binary: number of runs
  executed under it and volume of output produced, so the sunk cost of deferral is legible. Set an
  expiry rather than a carry: an escalation that reaches N carries either resolves or auto-resolves to a
  stated default that is written down in advance, which converts an unmanaged default into a chosen one
  (this is the standard remedy in the exception-management literature, though what I found there was
  grey literature rather than peer-reviewed work and is not cited above). Track the escalation
  register's own responsiveness — carries-to-resolution ratio over time — since the alerting evidence
  predicts it will decay. Note that the register-annotation mitigations are declarative and therefore
  fall under ASSUMPTION-1282's open question; the expiry rule is the only one with a mechanical consumer.

  Search scope: Preliminary — 4 queries plus 1 full-text retrieval. Covered: decision-avoidance

  reviewed study. That absence is itself reportable: the "escalation suspends practice" position appears
  to have no empirical literature behind it in what was searched.

STEELMAN:
  Item: PRESUMPTION-928
  Strongest counterargument: In a system that keeps running, there is no pause — there is only the
  question of which limb the running is happening under, and the answer is always the incumbent. Four
  lit-search cycles have now been executed under REVISE-436's unresolved binary, every one of them
  behaving as though limb (b) had been chosen; that is not a decision deferred, it is a decision taken
  four times and recorded nowhere. The psychology is against the presumption on both halves: doing
  nothing is a structured behaviour with its own causes rather than a null (Anderson), and adding
  argument to a conflicted choice increases deferral rather than resolving it (Tversky & Shafir) — so the
  escalation, which exists to force a ruling, is predicted to postpone one. The operational evidence is
  worse. Where a system raises a flag it cannot resolve, the flag is overridden in half to nearly all
  cases, and compliance decays with exposure — one cohort's override rate climbed from about 50% to 75%
  over five years. That is what a fourteenth consecutive carry looks like from the outside: not a
  preserved question, but a signal being trained out. And the safety literature names the terminus, where
  a known-open item carried across enough uneventful operations stops being an open item and becomes the
  way things are done. The escalation register is currently indistinguishable from a label on ongoing
  practice, and every additional cycle makes limb (b) harder to reverse, because reversing it will by
  then be a change to the status quo rather than a choice between two limbs.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The system would have to actually stop — no runs, no
  outputs, no downstream artefacts produced under either limb — while the binary is open. If work
  continues, neutrality is unavailable in principle, and the only remaining question is whether the
  incumbent is recorded. A weaker sufficient condition: the incumbent limb is named explicitly in the
  register, the outputs produced under it are marked as provisional and revocable, and the escalation
  carries an expiry that converts to a pre-stated default, so the default is chosen rather than
  accumulated.
  How to test: In-house and immediate. For each open binary, count (i) the number of runs executed since
  it opened, (ii) how many of those behaved consistently with limb (a) versus limb (b), and (iii) the
  volume of register content produced under it. If (ii) is lopsided — the item's own evidence says four
  of four went to (b) — the deferral has been a selection, and the count is the measurement. Second test,
  for the decay claim: plot carries-to-resolution over the escalation register's history. If the ratio of
  carried to resolved items has risen over time, the register is losing force exactly as the alerting
  literature predicts. Third: check whether any output produced under an unresolved binary has since been
  cited, built on, or promoted by a downstream agent — if so, the deferral has already been ratified by
  use, and the pending ruling is retroactive rather than prospective.
