## SEARCH-AGAINST-PRESUMPTION-790

**Original statement:** That when one artefact is measured several times and returns several numbers, the disagreement is a reporting inconsistency rather than a fact about the artefact. Four DB sizings and seven queue counts in one day, none reconciled, none treated as a finding.

**Polarity note (explicit inversion).** 14b's residual claim is that unreconciled disagreement should have been treated as a finding about the artefact, and that the system's contrary presumption is unsafe. The proposition I am challenging is **14b's residual claim**. The AGAINST case is unusually concrete here: for the specific quantities involved, the system's presumption is very often *literally correct* — disk-usage tools and distributed queue counters are documented to return different numbers for the same object by design, and metrology's own doctrine attributes such disagreement to an incompletely defined measurand rather than to the object. Note that this is a defence of the presumption's *conclusion*, not of the runs' *reasoning*: the runs reached a usually-correct conclusion without doing the work that would license it, and the work is small.

### Challenging evidence found: Yes

### Sources

1. **IBM Support, "Why Numbers from `du -s` and `df` Disagree"; Veritas Support article 100002717, "Discrepancy on the results from df and du and actual file size"; nixCraft, "Why command df and du reports different output?"; Tecmint, "Find Deleted Files Still Holding Disk Space in Linux."** [verified this run — vendor support documentation and established practitioner sources; not peer-reviewed.] — The direct empirical counterexample, and it is exactly the measurand C2A2 was arguing about. `df` and `du` are not two attempts at one number; they measure different things by construction. `df` reads the filesystem's own free-block and free-inode counters without walking directories; `du` traverses live paths and sums them. The documented divergence sources are (i) deleted-but-still-open files, whose blocks `df` counts and `du` cannot see because the path is gone — "the most common reason"; (ii) sparse files, where a 100 GB image may occupy 20 GB of blocks, so apparent size and allocated size differ by design; (iii) `--apparent-size` versus block accounting. The disagreement carries information about the *measurement method*, not about how big the database is.
2. **AWS, *Amazon SQS Developer Guide* / CloudWatch metrics documentation — `ApproximateNumberOfMessagesVisible` and related metrics.** [verified this run — AWS documentation summarised through docs.aws.amazon.com metric pages and corroborating practitioner sources including New Relic's SQS monitoring deep dive and an AWS re:Post thread on unexplained count decreases.] — The counterexample for the other measurand. The word "Approximate" is in the metric name because the count is approximate *by documented design*: SQS is distributed, counts are eventually consistent, and the service "samples a subset of its servers (based on a weighted random distribution)" when reporting. Seven readings of one queue producing seven numbers is therefore the specified behaviour of a correctly functioning system, and treating each divergence as a fact about the queue would generate a finding every time anyone looked.
3. **JCGM 200 (VIM3), *International Vocabulary of Metrology*, and JCGM 100 (GUM), *Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement* — the concepts of *measurand*, *definitional uncertainty*, and *metrological compatibility*.** [verified this run — BIPM-hosted VIM4 committee drafts, the JCGM VIM3 entry for "true quantity value," the ISO-hosted GUM definitions section, and NIST's hosted VIM copy; specific clause numbers not transcribed.] — The theoretical challenge, and it is the discipline whose entire job is this question. VIM3 redefines the measurand as the "quantity intended to be measured," obliging the analyst to state the definition before reporting. **Definitional uncertainty** is the uncertainty arising from incomplete definition of the measurand, and it is attributed to the *definition*, not to the object — which is, in metrology's own vocabulary, precisely the presumption 14b flags as unsafe. Metrology further holds that a measurand may be considered to have an "essentially unique" true value only when definitional uncertainty is negligible relative to other components; where it is not, disagreement between methods is expected and is not an anomaly.
4. **"Combining Results From Multiple Evaluations of the Same Measurand" and "Assessing Differences Between Results Determined According to the Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement" (NIST-affiliated, PMC4551221 and PMC4548867).** [verified this run — titles and PubMed Central identifiers confirmed; abstracts only, full texts not read.] — Supplies both the constructive machinery and the honest limit. Consensus-value methods for combining disagreeing determinations of one measurand are standard, and VIM3's **metrological compatibility** criterion gives the decision rule: results are compatible when their difference does not exceed the combined measurement uncertainty. The same literature notes that many results for one measurand in one matrix "do not seem to be equivalent, with stated measurement uncertainties being too small, possibly because definitional uncertainty is frequently omitted."

### Strength of challenge: Moderate-to-Strong on the general claim; Weak on the decision case

### Summary

For the two quantities actually at issue, the system's presumption is usually true, and the literature says so in unusually direct terms. Disk-usage disagreement between `df` and `du` is a documented consequence of the tools measuring different things — filesystem counters versus path traversal — with deleted-but-open file handles, sparse files, and apparent-versus-allocated accounting as the standard causes; the divergence is a fact about the instruments. Distributed queue depth is worse still: AWS names its own metric "Approximate" and documents that counts are eventually consistent and derived from a weighted random sample of servers, so seven readings yielding seven numbers is conformant behaviour, not a signal. Metrology generalises the point and supplies the vocabulary: definitional uncertainty is attributed to an incompletely specified measurand rather than to the artefact, and VIM3's response to disagreement is to *define the measurand*, not to open an investigation. So the runs' conclusion was, on the balance of evidence, correct. What the runs did not do is the small piece of work that licenses the conclusion, and here 14b's finding survives intact in a sharpened form. Metrology's compatibility criterion is not "disagreements are definitional"; it is "a disagreement is definitional *when it does not exceed combined uncertainty*, and is a finding when it does." No run stated its measurand, none stated an uncertainty, and none applied the criterion — so none of them was entitled to the conclusion they reached, even though it was probably right. And the decision case is exactly where the criterion bites: a gap between 4.65 GB and 3.2 GB free is not the kind of difference that `--apparent-size` versus block accounting produces at the margin, and 14b's observation that the three smaller numbers reached the human while the largest and latest did not is a reporting-selection problem that no amount of definitional uncertainty explains away.

### Specific risks

If the presumption is right in general but wrong on this instance, the exposure is exactly as 14b frames it: a capacity decision was made against numbers selected by recency and salience rather than by definition, and a genuine 1.45 GB shortfall could be sitting behind an assumption that "the tools always disagree." That is the specific hazard of a usually-true heuristic — it is most dangerous on the occasion when it fails, and it provides no signal that this is that occasion. If 14b's claim is adopted in its strong form, the counter-risk is a finding generated on every `df`/`du` comparison and on every pair of queue reads, which is a permanent stream of non-findings that will train readers to ignore the class — the PREMISE-110 degradation path, arrived at through a different door. There is a third risk peculiar to this item: because the presumption is *usually correct*, evidence of its correctness accumulates continuously, so the system's confidence in it will rise monotonically regardless of whether the one case that matters is being handled properly.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Define the measurand once, in writing, and name the instrument in every reading.** "Free space on volume X as reported by `df --output=avail` at time T" and "database file size in allocated blocks per `du --block-size=1`" are different measurands with different correct answers; once named, four sizings become four measurements of three quantities and the reconciliation is arithmetic. This is VIM3's entire prescription and it costs a string. (b) **Apply the compatibility criterion rather than the heuristic.** State an uncertainty, however crude, and treat a difference exceeding it as a finding — this preserves 14b's requirement without generating a finding on every routine divergence. (c) **On the specific decision case, resolve the known confounders directly:** `lsof +L1` for deleted-but-open handles, `du --apparent-size` versus block accounting for sparse files, and the filesystem's reserved-block percentage, which makes "available" smaller than "free" by a fixed fraction. These three account for most `df`/`du` gaps and can be checked in one command each. (d) **Fix the reporting-selection defect separately from the measurement question** — that the three smaller figures reached the human and the largest did not is a routing fact, unaffected by any metrological argument, and it is the part of 14b's observation that no source in this search touches. (e) **Note the overlap 14b asked about:** capture–recapture from disagreeing detectors, the Axis-2 estimator recommendation, is *not* applicable here. Capture–recapture estimates a hidden population from overlapping independent detections; `df` and `du` are not independent detectors of a common population but deterministic functions of different definitions, and applying an estimator to them would manufacture an uncertainty that is actually a definitional difference. That is a substantive warning against the previously-recommended remedy.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-790

**Strongest counterargument:** The presumption the item flags as unsafe is, for these measurands, the textbook answer. `df` and `du` disagree because they measure different quantities — filesystem block counters versus path traversal — and the vendor documentation of every major Unix explains the divergence in terms of deleted-but-open file handles, sparse files, and apparent-versus-allocated size. Amazon puts the word "Approximate" in the name of its queue-depth metric and documents that the count is derived from a weighted random sample of servers under eventual consistency, so seven readings of one queue yielding seven numbers is the system working. Metrology generalises this and has a term of art for it: definitional uncertainty, which is attributed to an incompletely specified measurand and not to the object, and which VIM3 addresses by requiring the analyst to state what quantity was intended to be measured before reporting anything. On that account, treating every disagreement as a fact about the artefact is not rigour — it is a category error that would generate a finding every time two differently-defined instruments were pointed at one object, which is continuously. The runs that declined to open an investigation reached the answer the discipline would have given them. **Where this stops, and it is a hard stop:** metrology's rule is not that disagreement is definitional but that disagreement is definitional *up to combined uncertainty* — the compatibility criterion — and beyond that it is a finding. No run stated a measurand, none stated an uncertainty, and none applied the criterion, so the correct conclusion was reached without entitlement, and a heuristic that is right most of the time and carries no signal about when it is wrong is the most dangerous kind to hold unstated. The 4.65 versus 3.2 GB case is where it matters: that is not a marginal accounting difference, and the fact that the three smaller figures reached the human while the largest and latest did not is a reporting-selection defect that no metrological argument reaches at all.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** Every reported figure names its measurand and its instrument, and every comparison between figures either resolves to "different measurands, reconciled by definition" or exceeds a stated compatibility bound and is escalated as a finding. Given those, the presumption is not merely safe but correct, and no investigation is warranted on routine divergence. Absent them, the system is holding a usually-true heuristic with no failure signal — and the one decision that mattered is on the wrong side of the bound.

**How to test:** Take the four DB sizings and, for each, record the exact command or API call that produced it. If they resolve to distinct measurands — available versus free, allocated versus apparent, file size versus page count — the presumption is vindicated for that day and the reconciliation is arithmetic. If two of them are the same command at different times returning materially different numbers, the presumption is refuted on its own terms and there is a fact about the artefact. Then test the 4.65-versus-3.2 GB gap directly against the three documented confounders: run `lsof +L1` for deleted-but-open handles, compare `du --apparent-size` against block accounting for sparse allocation, and read the filesystem's reserved-block percentage. If those three account for the gap, the decision was safe; if a residual remains, the residual is the finding. Separately and independently of all measurement questions, check the routing: establish whether the largest and latest figure was withheld from the human by a rule, by ordering, or by chance. That question has an answer in the record and it does not depend on resolving anything above.
