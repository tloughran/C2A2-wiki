### Summary

This is the item where the search direction mattered most, and the result is that the flagship instance does not survive contact with the literature it invokes. The claim that Scotist univocity is "the historic alternative" to Thomist analogy — such that a Rohr↔Stump convergence is dissolved by it — is the Radical Orthodoxy reading of Scotus, and it is disputed in print by the leading Anglophone Scotus scholar: Cross argues that Scotus advances a *semantic* theory about concepts and predication, not a metaphysics of being, in which case it is not the contradictory of a metaphysics of participation at all. Williams goes further and argues that all intelligible predication about God reduces to univocity, so that analogy presupposes rather than competes with it; and there is a live specialist literature on whether Scotus himself held analogy and univocity to be compatible. Three things follow that the item cannot absorb. First, its Critical grading is derived from a contested historiographical thesis treated as fact, which is exactly the shape PREMISE-129 requires a proof obligation for. Second, Scotus's own argument — read in full this run in Lyonhart's quotation of Ordinatio I, d.3, nn.39–40 — is the *mirror risk* in its original form: without a univocal core, nothing whatever can be inferred from creaturely concepts to divine ones, and "God is formally a stone" becomes as warranted as "God is wise." A policy of refusing shared vocabulary as evidence of shared commitment, applied consistently across 103 connections, is that argument's conclusion applied to a wiki. Third, and most damaging to the proposed method, Scotus states that theologians "observe the univocity of being in the way in which they speak, even though they deny it with their words" — declared doctrinal allegiance can be a *worse* guide to operative commitment than usage is, which inverts the item's assumption that the metaphysical check is the deeper one. Davidson supplies the theoretical caution about the field itself: the difference between Rohr's "being" and Stump's was stated in English, in one document, comparing two positions — which is enough translation to show they are disagreeing about a shared topic rather than inhabiting incommensurable schemes, and disagreement-in-a-shared-idiom is a substantive relation the schema also cannot record. And the decisive in-house fact: this register already holds, at High confidence and anchored on Manning et al. and Gentner, that lexical matching in this vault is high-precision and low-recall — that its characteristic error is **undercounting** real convergence. The item grades Critical on the opposite error profile without citing either premise. ### Specific risks

**The mirror risk, and it is the largest, because the system's primary output is on the other side of it.** If "shared vocabulary is not evidence of shared commitment" becomes an operating norm, the plausible outcome is not a cleaner network but a much smaller one — and PREMISE-042 predicts the discarded connections will be disproportionately the *real* ones, since genuine convergence here is analogical and already surfaces sparsely. Scotus's stone argument is the formal statement of the failure mode: raise the bar for what counts as a shared concept high enough and no inference across the divide survives. A network with 103 connections and unknown precision is a worse artifact than one with 30 and known precision, but it is a far better one than a network with 4.

If it is used to prune "several recorded Rohr↔Stump convergences," the pruning inherits the dispute, is invisible afterwards, and is not reversible — deleted edges leave no record of why.

**The self-declared field problem.** A lexical/substantive flag is a judgement about whether two thinkers mean the same thing — the hardest judgement in the system — recorded by the same layer that made the match. On the pattern documented in block 2 of today's systemic flag, it will be populated, stable, and uninformative.
