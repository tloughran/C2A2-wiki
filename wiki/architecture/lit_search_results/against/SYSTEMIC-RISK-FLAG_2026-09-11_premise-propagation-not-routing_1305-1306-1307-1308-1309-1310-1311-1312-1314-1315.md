  Why this matters more than the routing diagnosis:
    ASSUMPTION-1309 observes an 82% pre-answered rate (9 of 11) and concludes "routing defect." This
    batch reproduces the rate at 10 of 10 — and the additional structure it exposes is that the failure
    is upstream of routing. The items are not arriving at the search pipeline redundantly because 14a/14b
    routes badly; they are arriving because **the originating runs did not have the premise when they
    acted**. A pre-route grep placed in 14a/14b would suppress the downstream search while leaving the
    upstream runs exactly as uninformed as they are today. The 85-triplet ingest would still have been
    approved in one batch, the telemetry status would still have been a decorated PASS, and the Summa run
    would still have published 2-of-147 — because none of those runs read `validated_premises.md`.

    The routing remedy therefore does two harmful things at once: it does not touch the actual defect,
    and it removes the mechanism (search, and the possibility of a contradicting result) that is the
    register's only contraction path — making all 158 ACTIVE premises unfalsifiable. That is
    PREMISE-107's defining error of fault isolation: a remedy attached to an observation without being
    validated against the mechanism, which costs effort AND leaves the fault in place.

  Risk level: **Critical**

    Justification for Critical rather than High: the exposure is not bounded by this batch. If premises
    do not reach originating runs, then (a) every operational decision in the estate is being taken
    without the register, (b) the register's growth is not a health signal (PREMISE-174), and (c) the
    remedy currently under consideration would convert a correctable propagation gap into a permanent
    epistemic one. The condition is also self-concealing: the more premises accumulate, the higher the
    pre-answered rate climbs, and the more compelling the (wrong) routing diagnosis looks.

  Recommendation:
    1. **Measure the propagation gap before changing the routing layer.** One count, available today:
       across the last 30 days of run reports, how many cite a PREMISE-nnn identifier? If the answer is
       near zero, this flag is confirmed and ASSUMPTION-1309's diagnosis is refuted in-house without a
       search. This is the discriminating test PREMISE-107 requires and it is one grep.
    2. **Do not adopt the pre-route grep as a FILTER.** If adopted at all, adopt it as an ANNOTATOR at
       the point of *origination* — i.e. make the premise register a required read for the runs that
       take the actions, not a gate on the pipeline that reviews them. Bettenburg's answer applies:
       merge, do not suppress.
    3. **Run PRESUMPTION-948's count** — premises that have ever changed status after minting. If it is
       zero across 158, the register is already monotonic as a matter of fact, and the falsifiability
       cost of the grep is zero because the capability it would remove does not exist. That is a finding
       either way and it is one pass over the file.
    4. **Give PREMISE-182 a home where the runs that need it will read it.** The Summa nightly

    5. **Treat the 82% as a success signal until the propagation count says otherwise.** A register that
