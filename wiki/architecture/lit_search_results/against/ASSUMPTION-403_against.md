  Challenging evidence found: Yes

  Sources:
    1. Member-checking critiques (Thomas, 2017, "Member Checking: A Tool to Enhance Trustworthiness or Merely a Nod to Validation?", Qual Health Res). — Treating the participant as the arbiter of accuracy contradicts the relational, interpretive nature of understanding; respondent confirmation is not a definitive validity test and can mislead.
    2. Sandelowski; Morse — Validity is a property of the *interpretation and analysis*, not something the informant can singly certify; an informant may endorse or reject a reading for reasons unrelated to fidelity (social desirability, memory, changed views).
    3. Wimsatt & Beardsley, 1946, "The Intentional Fallacy." — The author's own verdict on whether meaning was captured is not authoritative over the meaning of what was said; author-ratification cannot be the sole criterion of correct interpretation.
    4. Grounding theory (Clark & Brennan). — Understanding in dialogue is *jointly* constituted by speaker and addressee acceptance; making the original claimant the *sole* judge unilaterally removes the addressee's constitutive role.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The strong, exclusive form of the assumption ("only ... sole judge") is challenged from several directions. Qualitative-methods scholarship warns that respondent validation is a fallible, non-definitive signal, not the seat of validity; interpretive theory denies the author authoritative control over captured meaning; and grounding theory makes understanding bilateral rather than author-owned. The challenge targets "sole/only," not the weaker claim that participant ratification is *a* strong criterion.

  Specific risks: If C2A2 treats author-ratification as the sole validity gate, it (a) discards independent semantic checks that could catch spurious "yes, captured" ratifications, and (b) inherits the known biases of member checking (social desirability, drift), potentially certifying understanding that did not occur.

  Mitigations available: Pair author-ratification with an independent semantic/third-party check (converging evidence) rather than treating either as sole; the study's own C0 gate (verdict-based) is a candidate cross-check.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-CHALLENGED

  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: For *understanding* specifically (as opposed to third-party "meaning"), the person whose claim it is has privileged, though not infallible, access to whether their point was captured; grounding theory even makes addressee acceptance constitutive. So author-ratification is a *legitimate and privileged* signal. But "privileged" is not "sole": the same theory requires the other party's uptake too, and interpretive scholarship shows author verdicts can be wrong. The safe form is "author-ratification is necessary/high-weight evidence," not "the sole judge."
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Ratification is used as high-weight but defeasible evidence, cross-checked against an independent semantic measure; disagreements between the two are surfaced, not overridden by author verdict alone.
    How to test: On cases where author-ratification and the C0 semantic verdict disagree, adjudicate by a blind third rater; measure how often the author verdict is the erroneous one.
