  Sources:
    1. Sloman, S.A., Rottenstreich, Y., Wisniewski, E., Hadjichristidis, C. & Fox, C.R. 2004. "Typical Versus Atypical Unpacking and Superadditive Probability Judgment." Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition 30(3): 573-582. - The sharpest challenge, because it attacks the sign of the effect rather than its size. Unpacking a description into components is standardly held to raise judged probability (subadditivity), which is what PRESUMPTION-577 implicitly assumes disaggregated filing would do for salience. Sloman et al. show the effect reverses when the components are atypical or unfamiliar: unpacking into atypical constituents produces *superadditivity*, i.e. the unpacked description is judged less likely than the packed one. Transferred to finding-filing, three separately tracked flags of unequal force - one central, two more peripheral - can plausibly be judged as a weaker whole than a single consolidated flag naming the same defect, because the reader's attention lands on the least typical member. The presumption therefore cannot assume that disaggregation is the salience-preserving baseline against which consolidation is a loss; whether it is depends on the typicality of the sub-flags, which nobody has assessed.
    2. Nisbett, R.E., Zukier, H. & Lemley, R.E. 1981. "The dilution effect: Nondiagnostic information weakens the implications of diagnostic information." Cognitive Psychology 13. - Supplies the mechanism by which separate filing can actively harm the strongest finding. Subjects given a mix of diagnostic and nondiagnostic information about a target made markedly less extreme predictions than subjects given the diagnostic information alone; the presence of individuating but nondiagnostic detail regresses judgment toward the base rate. If the three flags against 14b are of unequal diagnosticity - and the presumption's own framing, in which one is the headline, implies they are - then filing them as three peers surrounds the diagnostic finding with less diagnostic ones and predicts a *lower* assessed severity than filing the diagnostic one alone or filing a single flag that states the combined severity explicitly. The dilution literature thus turns the presumption's worry inside out: the risk to salience may lie in the disaggregated arrangement, not the aggregated one.
    3. Slovic, P. 2007. "'If I look at the mass I will never act': Psychic numbing and genocide." Judgment and Decision Making 2(2): 79-95. - Bears on the presumption's central assumption that a count of findings drives action. Slovic's synthesis is that response is driven by affect and by identifiability, and that it does not scale with the number of instances - the marginal response per additional case declines, and in some designs total response *falls* as the number rises (the "arithmetic of compassion"). The transfer is imperfect and should be stated as such: these are studies of affective response to victims, not of remediation of audit findings. But the structural claim - that N separately presented instances do not produce N times the response of one, and may produce less - is directly contrary to the assumption that separate filing preserves the force of three findings, and it is consistent with PREMISE-121's mechanism rather than with the presumption's doubt about it.
    4. Public Company Accounting Oversight Board. AS 2201, An Audit of Internal Control Over Financial Reporting That Is Integrated with an Audit of Financial Statements, paragraphs .62-.70. - Documented professional practice in which consolidation is the severity-*raising* operation, not a severity-hiding one. The standard requires the auditor to evaluate whether deficiencies, individually **or in combination**, constitute a material weakness, and states that multiple deficiencies affecting the same account, disclosure or assertion increase the likelihood of misstatement and may in combination constitute a material weakness even though each is individually less severe. Evaluating deficiencies only in isolation is a recognised implementation failure that causes late-stage severity escalation. This is the opposite of the presumption's model: in the standard's shape, three related HIGH findings filed as one flag with three sub-flags is the mandated form, and it exists to prevent the combined severity from being lost among individually survivable items.
    5. Lipe, M.G. & Salterio, S.E. 2000. "The Balanced Scorecard: Judgmental Effects of Common and Unique Performance Measures." The Accounting Review 75(3): 283-298. - The best available empirical support for PREMISE-121's mechanism, and therefore against the presumption's doubt about it. When evaluators were given scorecards containing both measures common to several units and measures unique to the unit's own strategy, they anchored on the common measures and effectively ignored the unique ones - despite the unique measures being the strategically informative ones. The finding is that adding separately tracked items does not distribute attention proportionally; it causes selective neglect, and what gets neglected is the item that does not match the reader's existing comparison frame. A newly filed, structurally unusual systemic flag is precisely a "unique measure" in this sense, so the study predicts that three such flags competing in one queue lose more attention than one flag consolidating them.
    6. Tversky, A. & Koehler, D.J. 1994. "Support Theory: A Nonextensional Representation of Subjective Probability." Psychological Review 101(4): 547-567. - Included because it is the presumption's strongest foothold and it should be stated at full strength: judged probability increases when the focal hypothesis is unpacked, and judgments are subadditive in the general case, so description form demonstrably changes assessed magnitude. That establishes the presumption's core claim that consolidation is not neutral. Two limits bound it. First, the dependent variable is judged probability of an event class, not the probability that a reader acts, and support theory says nothing about remediation. Second, source 1 above is a direct experimental demonstration by one of the same research programme's successors that the unpacking effect is not monotone and can reverse. So support theory establishes that a salience question exists without establishing which direction it runs.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The presumption is right that consolidation is not salience-neutral and right that retrievability via three DISPOSITION ids does not answer a salience question - support theory establishes that description form changes assessed magnitude, and nothing located refutes that. What the literature challenges is the assumed *direction*. Sloman et al. show unpacking effects reverse with atypical components; Nisbett, Zukier and Lemley show that surrounding a diagnostic item with less diagnostic ones regresses judgment toward the mean; Lipe and Salterio show that adding separately tracked items produces selective neglect of the structurally unusual ones rather than proportional attention; and Slovic's work indicates response does not scale with instance count. On this evidence three peer flags of unequal force are a plausible *loss* of salience relative to one consolidated flag that states the combined severity. AS 2201 then supplies the strongest single point: in the nearest professional analogue, combining related deficiencies is mandatory and is the operation by which severity is escalated, and evaluating them only in isolation is the recognised error. What survives the challenge intact is the process defect rather than the substantive one - no symmetry check is recorded, and the aggregation was applied by the same pipeline to findings adverse to its own sibling layer. That is an independence question the psychology and audit-standards literatures do not answer, and PRESUMPTION-577's settling quantity (remediation rate for n findings filed separately versus consolidated at matched severity) remains unmeasured in both directions.

  Specific risks: If the presumption is over-read and consolidation is treated as suspect per se, C2A2 acquires the failure mode AS 2201 was written to prevent: related findings are filed as individually survivable peers, no entry ever states their combined severity, and the reader is left to perform the aggregation that the standard makes the reporter's obligation - with the predictable result (dilution, selective neglect of the unusual item) that the strongest finding is discounted by proximity to weaker ones. A second over-read risk is procedural inflation: every consolidation now requires a defence, which raises the cost of reporting and, by PREMISE-121's own logic, reduces the number of findings that get reported at all. If the presumption is under-read, the real risk is not about salience mechanics but about who is doing the aggregating: a layer that may consolidate adverse findings against itself or its sibling, with no recorded symmetry check and no external ratification, has a discretionary lever whose exercise is unmeasured, and the specific failure mode is that the *count* of independent findings against the presumption-construction layer becomes a quantity the layer controls on the run where that count is the datum.

  Mitigations available: Adopt the AS 2201 shape rather than arguing about aggregation in the abstract: when related findings are consolidated, require the entry to state (a) each sub-finding's individual severity, (b) an explicit combined severity that may exceed any individual one, and (c) the count of independent sub-findings in the flag header, so consolidation cannot reduce the visible count. Impose the symmetry rule the presumption correctly notes is missing, as a standing convention rather than a case-by-case judgement: the same aggregation policy applies to findings favourable and adverse to any layer, and each consolidation records which policy was applied. Remove the discretion where it matters most by requiring that consolidations touching the aggregating layer or its sibling be flagged for external ratification, which is a cheap independence safeguard and does not require settling the salience question. Finally, make the settling quantity measurable prospectively: since remediation-rate comparison at matched severity requires a designed contrast, alternate the filing form for comparable low-stakes findings over the next N batches and record disposition-to-action latency - this is the only route to a signed estimate, and the literature above establishes that its sign genuinely cannot be assumed.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-577
    Strongest counterargument (against the presumption): The presumption assumes disaggregation is the neutral baseline and consolidation the intervention, but every relevant literature says the arrangement is a design choice whose effect on salience is not signed, and the best-evidenced direction runs against the presumption. Sloman et al. showed experimentally that unpacking into atypical components lowers rather than raises judged likelihood; Nisbett, Zukier and Lemley showed that placing a diagnostic item alongside less diagnostic ones regresses the reader's judgment toward the base rate; Lipe and Salterio showed that when items compete for attention, the structurally unusual ones - which is exactly what a novel systemic flag is - are the ones ignored. So filing three HIGH flags as peers is a live candidate for the arrangement that loses the strongest finding, and the consolidated form is a live candidate for the one that preserves it. In the closest professional analogue the matter is not left to judgement at all: PCAOB AS 2201 .62-.70 *requires* deficiencies to be evaluated in combination and treats isolated evaluation as the error, because combination is how severity is escalated rather than concealed. The presumption's substantive worry therefore has the sign backwards on current evidence, and what remains of it is a governance point about who holds the discretion, not a psychological point about what consolidation does.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (i) the consolidated entry states each sub-finding's severity and an explicit combined severity, and carries the independent-finding count in its header, so no reader has to reconstruct the aggregation; (ii) the aggregation policy is a standing convention applied identically to favourable and adverse findings, with the applied policy recorded; (iii) consolidations affecting the aggregating layer or its sibling are ratified outside that layer; and (iv) the pipeline does not treat the count of separately tracked items as a severity signal in either direction, since the literature shows count and assessed force are not monotonically related.
    How to test: The settling quantity requires a designed contrast, so build one. Over the next N batches, randomly assign comparable findings at matched severity to consolidated or separate filing, and record two outcomes: time from filing to first recorded action, and whether the strongest sub-finding is the one acted on. The presumption predicts higher action rates and better targeting under separate filing; the dilution and selective-neglect literatures predict the reverse, with the consolidated form outperforming when sub-finding diagnosticity is unequal. A second, cheaper test addresses the direction question directly and needs no new filings: take an already-consolidated flag and an already-separate set, present each to a reader cold, and ask for a severity rating and a next action - if the consolidated form rates higher, source 1's reversal is operating here. A third test is the symmetry audit and should be run first because it is nearly free: count consolidations over the pipeline's history, classify each by whether it aggregated findings favourable or adverse to a C2A2 layer, and compare rates. An asymmetry establishes the presumption's governance claim regardless of how the salience question resolves.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-CHALLENGED
