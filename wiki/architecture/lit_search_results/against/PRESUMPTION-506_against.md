  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: The structural observation is not contradicted: I searched for evidence that validated findings propagate to behaviour by default and found the opposite everywhere. The challenge is to the inference and to the risk statement. Audit and feedback — the closest and best-measured analogue of "a finding is fed back to the actor it governs" — does change behaviour, but by a median 4.3% (2012) to a mean 6.2% (2025 update) absolute improvement. That is small, and it means a single same-day recurrence is fully consistent with a propagation mechanism existing and performing at the literature's expected rate. The item's risk statement — that 110 premises, 236 flags and 457 monitors "have never altered an agent's conduct" — is a strong universal built on one observation and it is the part most likely to be false. Two further corrections follow from the same literature. First, the effect depends on identified moderators (repeated delivery, explicit targets, an action plan, a credible source), so simply adding a propagation step without them would predictably do very little. Second, the CAPA literature names what is actually missing more precisely than "propagation": an effectiveness check defined at diagnosis time and required before closure.
  Specific risks: If the item is right, the self-awareness layer is decorative and its cost is pure overhead. If the item is over-stated, the danger runs the other way: a propagation mechanism is built, produces the literature's 4–6% improvement, is judged a failure against an implicit expectation of near-total compliance, and is abandoned — discarding a working intervention for underperforming an unrealistic bar. Third risk: the propagation step is built without the moderators, performs below even 4%, and the whole approach is written off.
  Mitigations available: Run the item's own test but report it as a rate with a denominator, not as a binary: for each of the 110 premises, name the governed agent and count attributable changes, then compare against the 4–6% band as the prior. Build the effectiveness check rather than a generic propagation step: at validation time, record what evidence would show the premise changed conduct and by when, and do not close the premise until it exists — this is the CAPA remedy and it is more specific than the item's. Import the moderators deliberately: repeat the feedback, attach an explicit target and an action plan, and route it from a source the agent treats as authoritative. Set the success bar in advance at a literature-informed level so the mechanism is not judged against perfection.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-506
    Strongest counterargument: The observation is right and the expectation behind it is wrong, which makes the risk statement the weakest part. Audit and feedback is the most heavily studied instance of exactly this mechanism — a finding about an actor's conduct returned to that actor — and across 292 randomised trials the mean absolute improvement in desired practice is 6.2%, with the earlier 140-trial synthesis putting the median at 4.3%. That is the realistic ceiling for what any propagation step will buy. Under that prior, PREMISE-109's failure recurring the morning after validation is not evidence that no propagation exists; it is close to the expected observation even in a system where propagation works exactly as well as the best-evidenced interventions in medicine. So "110 premises have never altered an agent's conduct" is a universal claim resting on one instance, and the literature suggests the true figure is small but non-zero — which is a completely different diagnosis with a completely different remedy. The literature also specifies why generic feedback underperforms: it needs to be repeated, to come from a source the recipient credits, and to carry an explicit target and an action plan. A propagation step lacking those would be built, would deliver perhaps 2%, and would then be judged a failure — discarding the right idea because it was measured against the wrong bar. The CAPA literature names the actual missing artifact more sharply than "propagation" does: an effectiveness check, defined at diagnosis, required before closure. Its absence is the most common way correction programmes fail, and it is exactly what the PREMISE-109 record lacks.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: That the propagation mechanism is specified with the known moderators rather than generically, and that its success is judged against a pre-registered, literature-informed bar (single-digit percentage improvement) rather than an implicit expectation of compliance.
    How to test: Run the 110-premise sweep the item proposes, but pre-register the expected rate first. Record, for each premise, the governed agent and whether any attributable behavioural change appears in its subsequent output. A rate of literally zero across 110 premises would strongly support the item and would be well below what a working mechanism produces. A rate in the 4–8% band means propagation exists and is performing normally, and the correct response is to add the moderators, not to build a new layer.
