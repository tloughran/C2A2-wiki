  Sources:
    1. Searle, J.R. 1964. "How to Derive 'Ought' from 'Is'." The Philosophical Review 73(1): 43-58. - Attacks the presumption's foundational premise directly. Searle's argument is that the impossibility claim - that no evaluative conclusion follows from purely descriptive premises - has counterexamples once institutional facts are in view: from the descriptive fact that a person uttered certain words in a certain institutional setting (made a promise) it follows that they ought to do the thing. The derivation works because institutional facts are constituted by rules that are themselves normative, so describing an act *under an institutional description* already imports commitments. PRESUMPTION-586 treats the is/ought barrier as settled and self-evident and rests its whole force on it; Searle's paper, and the sixty years of literature it generated, establish that the barrier is at minimum contested where institutions are concerned - and the C2A2 pipeline is exactly an institution in this sense. The presumption's charge is therefore not a fallacy identification but a substantive position requiring argument it does not give.
    2. Hart, H.L.A. 1961 (3rd ed. 2012). The Concept of Law. Oxford University Press. - The standard jurisprudential account holds that the existence of the ultimate criteria of validity in a legal system - the rule of recognition - is a matter of social fact: it exists because officials converge in practice on treating certain criteria as authoritative and take a critical reflective attitude toward that convergence. There is no further delegation behind it; the practice is what its existence consists in. This is the strongest possible general challenge to the presumption's framing, because it says that at the foundation, authority acquired by practice is not a degenerate case of authority delegated - it is the only mode available, and asking "who authorised the rule of recognition?" is a question with no answer by construction. The residual is real and should be recorded: critics (e.g. the line of objection that Hart's factual account cannot ground the "ought" of obligation) press precisely PRESUMPTION-586's point, so the source establishes that the presumption is contestable rather than that it is wrong.
    3. International Law Commission. 2018. Draft conclusions on identification of customary international law, with commentaries. Report of the ILC, 70th session, UN Doc A/73/10. - The most directly useful source, because it both challenges and operationalises the presumption. It challenges it by codifying practice as a constitutive element of binding norms: a rule of customary international law exists where there is a general practice accepted as law. But it also gives the presumption its own best test, and gives it more precisely than the presumption states it: the two elements - general practice and opinio juris (acceptance as law) - must each be *separately ascertained*, and practice must be distinguished from mere usage or habit. That is exactly the checkable half 14b proposes, expressed as an established doctrinal test. Applied to C2A2: the fact that 14b now proposes intake rules is the practice element; what is missing is a separately ascertained record of the practice being accepted as authoritative. The right conclusion is not that practice cannot confer authority but that one of the two required elements is undocumented.
    4. Resnick, P. 2014. RFC 7282, "On Consensus and Humming in the IETF." IETF, Informational. - Documented standards-body practice in which practice-first legitimation is the explicit norm rather than a drift to be policed. The IETF's founding credo - "We reject: kings, presidents and voting. We believe in: rough consensus and running code" - places working implementation ahead of authorisation, and the RFC is itself an act of self-amendment: a body documenting and correcting its own decision procedure from within, on no authority but its own participants' consensus. Notably, the RFC's own complaint is the mirror image of the presumption's: it warns that the IETF has *drifted toward* vote-like procedures, i.e. toward the formalism the presumption prefers, and treats that drift as the degradation. This is a live counterexample to the claim that a monitoring or standard-setting body proposing its own intake criteria is per se an authority problem.
    5. Institute of Internal Auditors. 2024. Global Internal Audit Standards. IIA. - The nearest professional analogue, and it settles the framing question against the presumption's strong reading. Under the 2024 Standards the chief audit executive is responsible for the risk assessment, the internal audit strategy and the annual audit plan - that is, the auditor sets its own intake criteria - while the board reviews and approves the internal audit charter, the risk assessment, the plan and the resourcing, with senior management input that must not constitute pressure on the CAE. So the profession's answer to "may the auditor set its own intake rules?" is yes, subject to a recorded ratification step, and independence is protected by the ratification rather than by prohibiting the proposal. PRESUMPTION-586 conflates the two: what is anomalous at C2A2 is not that 14b proposed an intake rule for itself but that no equivalent of board approval is recorded. That is a narrower and more actionable finding than the one the presumption states.
    6. Sabel, C.F. & Zeitlin, J. 2008. "Learning from Difference: The New Architecture of Experimentalist Governance in the EU." European Law Journal 14(3): 271-327. - Describes and defends a governance architecture in which the monitored units revise the monitoring framework. Framework goals and metrics are set jointly, lower-level units get autonomy in how to pursue them in exchange for regular reporting and peer review, and - the load-bearing feature here - the framework goals, the performance measures and the decision procedures themselves are periodically revised by the participating actors, including new entrants. This is authority flowing from practice by design, with the legitimacy carried by the reporting-and-review obligation and by the periodicity of revision rather than by a prior delegation. It challenges the presumption's implicit ideal (authority must precede practice) by exhibiting a widely studied alternative in which it does not, while also naming the conditions that make the alternative defensible: transparency of the practice, peer review, and a scheduled rather than opportunistic revision point.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The presumption's diagnosis is over-stated and its remedy is vindicated, and the literature separates the two cleanly. The diagnosis - that offering a description of what the layer now does in the place where a normative entry belonged commits an is/ought error - depends on a barrier that Searle contested directly for institutional facts, that Hart's account of the rule of recognition makes unavailable at the foundation (where authority just is convergent official practice), and that the ILC's two-element doctrine and Sabel and Zeitlin's experimentalist architecture both treat as the ordinary mode by which norms come to bind. The IIA's 2024 Standards then dissolve the specific worry about auditors setting their own intake criteria: that is standard practice, and the safeguard is board approval of the charter, risk assessment and plan, not a prohibition on the auditor proposing them. What survives, and survives strengthened, is the checkable half. The ILC requires opinio juris to be *separately ascertained* from the practice and insists practice be distinguished from mere usage; the IIA requires a recorded approval; Sabel and Zeitlin require scheduled revision points and peer review. All three converge on the same missing artefact at C2A2 - a record of acceptance, distinct from the record of doing - which means the presumption's proposed measurement (how many intake or scoring rules in force at 14a/14b/15* were adopted by recorded sign-off versus by first use) is precisely the right instrument even though the framing that motivated it is philosophically shaky.

  Specific risks: If the presumption is over-read, C2A2 acquires a rule that no practice may be regularised without prior delegation, which is unsatisfiable in a system whose whole point is that the self-knowledge layer discovers what needs governing by doing the work - the RFC 7282 failure mode, where a body drifts toward procedural formalism and the substantive work is throttled by the authorisation queue, is the concrete version. A second over-read risk is that the presumption's argument form, being a general is/ought objection, applies to almost every entry the pipeline makes about itself, so it can be deployed to block any self-directed improvement, including improvements to independence. If the presumption is under-read, the risk 14b names is real and the literature sharpens rather than softens it: without a separately recorded acceptance step, the pipeline satisfies the practice element of authority and nothing else, and OPEN-138 closes by attrition - the specific failure mode is that in twelve months the intake rules in force will be describable only as "the ones that were used," with no artefact distinguishing a rule someone ratified from a rule that simply was not objected to, which is the ILC's "mere usage" case and is expressly not a source of authority.

  Mitigations available: Instrument the ILC's second element rather than arguing about the first: maintain a register of rules in force at 14a/14b/15* in which each entry records the date of first use, the date of recorded acceptance, and by whom - which makes the presumption's settling quantity a standing report rather than a one-off audit. Adopt the IIA's shape for the specific case: a layer may propose intake or scoring rules for itself, and such rules take effect only on recorded ratification outside the layer, which preserves the discovery function while restoring the independence safeguard the professional standard relies on. Borrow Sabel and Zeitlin's periodicity: fix a scheduled revision point at which rules adopted by use are either ratified or lapse, so drift is bounded in time by construction rather than by vigilance - this also directly answers 15c's warning about the graded reading of PREMISE-124 taking effect by drift. Keep OPEN-138 open explicitly and log the reason it stayed open, since the presumption's strongest observation is that an entry was not made; and record the normative half honestly as normative, since no literature can settle whether the layer *should* be advisory-only, only what conditions make either answer safe.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-586
    Strongest counterargument (against the presumption): The presumption's force comes entirely from treating the is/ought barrier as decisive, and in the domain that actually applies - institutional authority - it is not. Searle's counterexample shows that describing an act under an institutional description already carries commitments; Hart's account makes convergent official practice the *only* ground the ultimate criteria of validity have, so "authority acquired by practice" describes the normal case rather than a pathology; the ILC codifies general practice as one of two constitutive elements of binding custom; and Sabel and Zeitlin document a whole governance family in which the monitored actors periodically revise the monitoring framework and the legitimacy is carried by transparency and peer review rather than by prior delegation. The IETF goes further and inverts the presumption's preference outright, treating running code ahead of authorisation as the design principle and creeping proceduralism as the degradation. And on the specific charge - that an auditing layer proposing its own intake criteria is an independence problem - the IIA's 2024 Standards say the chief audit executive is *supposed* to own the risk assessment and audit plan, with the board's approval as the safeguard. So the presumption misidentifies the defect: the anomaly is not that the layer proposed a rule about itself, it is that no ratification is recorded, and stating it the presumption's way makes it unfixable in principle rather than fixable by adding one line to a register.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (i) the two elements are kept separate in the record - what the layer does, and a distinct artefact showing that doing it was accepted as authoritative; (ii) rules a layer proposes for itself take effect only on ratification recorded outside that layer; (iii) there is a scheduled revision point at which rules adopted by use either get ratified or lapse, so the window for drift is bounded by design; and (iv) the pipeline's transparency about its own practice is high enough that ratification is a real decision rather than a rubber stamp, which is the condition Sabel and Zeitlin's architecture and the ILC's opinio juris requirement both rest on.
    How to test: The checkable half is directly measurable and should be run as stated. Enumerate every intake, scoring, severity and disposition rule currently in force at 14a, 14b, 15a, 15b and 15c, and classify each as (a) adopted by recorded sign-off, (b) adopted by first use and later acknowledged, or (c) adopted by first use and never acknowledged. The presumption predicts category (c) dominates; the challenge predicts a substantial (a)+(b), and the split between (b) and (c) is the operative quantity because (b) is the ILC's "practice accepted as law" and (c) is its "mere usage." A second measurement discriminates drift from discovery: for each rule in (c), record the interval between first use and the present, and whether its scope has widened since first use - stable scope suggests a discovered convention, widening scope is the drift the presumption fears and is what the scheduled-lapse remedy targets. A third, cheap check tests the presumption's own symmetry: count how many rules adopted by first use *constrain* the adopting layer versus expand its remit. If the mix is roughly balanced the drift reading weakens considerably; if expansions dominate, the presumption is substantially vindicated on the checkable half regardless of how the is/ought question resolves.
