  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: The claim joins two markers (novel joint output; falling friction) under one word. Evidence shows they can dissociate: synchrony and cohesion rise while creative or complex joint output falls, and complex tasks favour complementary over synchronous coordination. Falling friction is the groupthink signature as much as a coherence signature.
  Specific risks: If C2A2 uses "friction falling" as a health signal for dyads or tradition dialogues, it would reward convergence that the literature associates with lost novelty. The "needs damping" clause is supported by this same evidence, so the challenge is to the markers, not to the damping.
  Mitigations available: Treat novel joint output (measured on the product) and falling friction (measured on the process) as separate indicators; require the first before reading the second as health. Wallot et al.'s complementarity framing gives a third indicator.

    Strongest counterargument: Coherence as experienced (cohesion, synchrony, smoothness) is not the same thing as productive coupling, and the best studies show they can run in opposite directions on complex creative tasks.
