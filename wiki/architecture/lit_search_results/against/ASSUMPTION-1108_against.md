### Polarity note — what I am challenging

The item is worded as a **finding**, and its implied corrective is that review must check *content* rather than *existence*. I searched against the finding and the corrective together, decomposed into four clauses:

- **(A) "Checking that an identifier exists is shallower than checking what it says."** Trivially true; searched and **found nothing against it**, and I do not pretend otherwise.
- **(B) "Existence-checking produces pass marks that do not survive re-performance."** **Challenged.** The inference treats re-performance as an oracle. The inter-rater-reliability literature says a second reading of the same object is a *noisy* instrument, not a gold standard, and the register already holds the same thing.
- **(C) The implied corrective: reviews must check what identifiers say.** **Partially challenged.** Bibliometric accuracy research divides reference errors into a **major** class (errors that prevent retrieval) and a **minor** class (errors that do not), and existence/retrievability checking targets the major class exactly. Audit doctrine (ISA 500) does not treat a procedure relevant to one assertion as defective; it treats it as **scoped**. The defect is claiming the wrong assertion, not running the cheap procedure.
- **(D) The inference from four days.** **Challenged.** Four instances, discovered by the audit that went looking for them, with no denominator reported: how many pass-marked days were re-performed and *survived*? Without that, "pass marks do not survive re-performance" is selection on the dependent variable.

### Summary

The item is right about the smaller thing and reaches too far with it. Nothing I found defends recording `pass` for an assertion the procedure never tested, and the register agrees in advance: PREMISE-142 holds that a binary vocabulary "forces the aggregator to render an absence as a positive assurance" and requires a three-valued output plus an evidence pointer. But the argument the item builds on that observation has two structural weaknesses. The first is the treatment of **re-performance as an oracle**. Bornmann, Mutz & Daniel's meta-analysis of 48 studies and 19,443 manuscripts puts the inter-rater reliability of content-level peer review at **ICC/r² ≈ .34 and κ ≈ .17** — barely above chance. Content review is precisely the deep mode the item's corrective prescribes, and two competent instances of it disagree most of the time. "Pass marks that do not survive re-performance" is therefore the *expected* output of running two such reviews over one artefact, and it does not by itself establish that the first mark was wrong. The register reaches the same place independently and more sharply: PREMISE-113 holds that a detector's findings are evidence about the detector until its precision is measured, and PREMISE-118's recursive caveat holds that where the judgement of defect came from the instrument, the response is **external measurement**. The four defective pass-marks were found by the same apparatus that issued them. The second weakness is the framing of verification as a **depth scale**. ISA 500 does not rank procedures by depth; it matches them to **assertions**, and states plainly that a procedure giving reliable evidence about existence gives no evidence about valuation — without calling it a bad procedure. On that reading the identifier-existence check is not shallow, it is *scoped*, and the defect is that its output was published under an assertion it does not support. That is a labelling fix, not a re-engineering. Bibliometrics supplies the same point from the other side: the field defines a **major** reference error as one that *prevents retrieval*, and an existence check is by construction a check on that class. It also supplies the item's best evidence, which I record at full strength: Walters & Wilder found that **43% / 24%** of *non-fabricated* LLM citations carry substantive metadata errors, which existence-checking passes. Both facts are true; the item states one. Finally, four instances discovered by the audit that sought them, with no denominator — how many pass-marked days were re-performed and survived? — is selection on the dependent variable, and PREMISE-168 already bars publishing a yield without one.

### Specific risks

**The oracle risk, and it is the main one.** If "did not survive re-performance" becomes an accepted defect verdict, the fleet has installed an unmeasured second reader as ground truth. At the reliability the literature reports for content review, that instrument will condemn a substantial fraction of *correct* first readings, and every such condemnation will be recorded as a defect against the cheaper check. The register's own PREMISE-114 says no arbitration rule can name a winner between two uncalibrated instruments; this item quietly names one.

**The depth-inflation risk.** Replacing existence checks with content checks everywhere raises the per-review cost by a large and unmeasured factor. The predictable outcome is fewer reviews, not better ones — and Google's programme reports that findings reviewers do not act on count as effective false positives regardless of whether the fault is real. A deep review nobody completes is worth less than a shallow one everybody does, truthfully labelled.

**The mislabelled-remedy risk.** The register already holds the cheap fix (PREMISE-142: three-valued output plus evidence pointer). If 1108 is adopted as "reviews must go deeper," the fleet buys a new instrument to solve a labelling problem, and the labelling problem persists inside the new instrument — because a content review that cannot reach a source will also need a NO-EVIDENCE value.

**The denominator risk.** Four is a numerator. Until the fleet reports how many pass-marked days were re-performed in total, the item's headline is uninterpretable, and PREMISE-168 already forbids publishing it in that form.

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** Walters & Wilder is a measured finding on a large sample and it says exactly what the item says: real sources carrying wrong metadata are common — 43% and 24% among non-fabricated citations — and an existence check passes every one of them. The sibling item ASSUMPTION-1112 is a live instance in this very vault. Dismissing 1108 because its inference is loose would discard a real and quantified exposure.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Relabel before re-engineering.** Adopt PREMISE-142's vocabulary for review outcomes: `verified-content` / `verified-existence-only` / `no-evidence`, each with an evidence pointer. This eliminates the false assurance at near-zero cost and is the register's existing holding, not a new commitment.
(b) **Measure the re-performer before trusting its verdicts,** per PREMISE-113: build a small corpus of known-good and known-defective review marks, run the re-performance procedure over it, and report precision and recall separately. Without this the four findings are evidence about the re-performer.
(c) **Report the denominator.** How many pass-marked days were re-performed on 08-03/08-04, and how many survived? One line, and it converts an anecdote into a rate.
(d) **Tier verification by assertion and by stake,** the ISA 500 structure: existence-only for low-stake identifiers, content verification for load-bearing citations (anything a premise rests on). This spends depth where the major content-error class (64.8% of content errors, per the quotation-accuracy literature) actually lives.
(e) **Adopt the bibliometric severity taxonomy explicitly** — retrieval-blocking errors versus non-blocking ones, and separately, source-does-not-substantiate errors. Three named classes, three checks, three costs; the current single `pass` conflates all of them.
(f) **Discharge PREMISE-118 with external measurement,** not with the review apparatus's own re-reading — an independent triage with inter-rater agreement, which is what the false-positive literature does and what PREMISE-113's challenge column already flags as the correct form.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** ASSUMPTION-1108

**Strongest counterargument:** The item's observation is sound and its inference is built on an instrument it has not calibrated. Start with the concession, because it is real: a `pass` that asserts content-verification when only existence was tested is a false particular, and the register said so twelve days earlier — PREMISE-142 holds that "a binary vocabulary forces the aggregator to render an absence as a positive assurance," and requires SUCCEEDED / FAILED / **NO-EVIDENCE** with an evidence pointer attached. Nothing in this file defends the current behaviour. But "produces pass marks that do not survive re-performance" makes re-performance the arbiter, and the meta-analytic evidence on what a second reading is worth is unambiguous and unflattering. Bornmann, Mutz & Daniel pooled 70 reliability coefficients from 48 studies covering 19,443 manuscripts and found mean **ICC/r² of .34** and mean **Cohen's κ of .17** — for exactly the deep, content-level review the item's corrective prescribes. At that reliability, disagreement between a first and second reading is the *modal* outcome for correct first readings, so "it did not survive re-performance" carries almost no information about the first mark until the re-performer's own precision is measured. C2A2 already holds this: PREMISE-113 says a detector's findings are evidence about the detector until its precision is known, and PREMISE-118's recursive caveat says that where the defect judgement came from the instrument, the answer is **external measurement** — "neither trusting nor discounting the self-report resolves it." The four defective marks were found by the same review apparatus that issued them. The second structural problem is treating verification as a depth ladder. ISA 500 does not have one. It matches procedures to **assertions** and states outright that inspection "may provide reliable audit evidence with respect to their existence, but not necessarily about... valuation" — and does not thereby call inspection defective. It is scoped. So the identifier-existence check is not a bad content check; it is a competent existence check whose output was filed under the wrong assertion, and the remedy is a label, not a new instrument. Bibliometrics converges: the field's own severity line is drawn at **retrievability** — a major error is one that "prevents retrieving the cited article" — so an existence check is precisely a check on the major class as that literature defines it. And depth is not free: Google's static-analysis programme counts a true finding the reviewer did not act on as an **effective false positive**, and CDS systems running at **49–96% override** produce a documented *paradoxical increase* in harm. A universal content-verification requirement predictably yields fewer reviews rather than better ones. Finally, four instances, surfaced by the audit that went looking for them, with no denominator reported, is selection on the dependent variable, and PREMISE-168 already bars publishing a yield without one. **What survives all of this, and survives well, is the narrower claim**: existence-checking cannot see the error class where the source is real and its metadata or its content is wrong — and Walters & Wilder measured that class at **43% (GPT-3.5) and 24% (GPT-4)** of *non-fabricated* citations. That is a large, quantified, live exposure, and the sibling ASSUMPTION-1112 is an instance of it inside this vault. The item is right that the gap exists; it is wrong that re-performance demonstrates it, and wrong that depth-everywhere is the remedy.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** Three conditions, and the first two are cheap. (i) **Every review outcome must name its assertion** — existence-verified is a legitimate, publishable result so long as it never appears as an unqualified `pass`; PREMISE-142's three-valued vocabulary with an evidence pointer discharges this entirely and requires no new verification capability. (ii) **The re-performing reviewer must have measured precision and recall** against a corpus with known-good and known-defective marks, per PREMISE-113, before any "did not survive re-performance" verdict is treated as a defect finding rather than as a disagreement; and per PREMISE-118's recursive caveat that measurement must be **external** to the apparatus under audit. (iii) **Content verification must be tiered by stake**, concentrated on identifiers that premises rest on, because the quotation-accuracy literature puts **64.8% of content errors** in the major class (source fails to substantiate, is unrelated to, or contradicts the assertion) and that is where depth buys the most. If all three hold, the item's exposure is closed and none of the challenges bite. If only the corrective is adopted — deeper review everywhere, re-performance as oracle — the fleet acquires an uncalibrated arbiter and a throughput collapse, and the labelling defect survives inside the new instrument.

**How to test:** (1) **The re-performer calibration run, and it is decisive.** Construct thirty review marks with known ground truth — fifteen genuinely defective, fifteen genuinely sound — shuffle them, and put them through the re-performance procedure blind. Report precision and recall. If recall is high and precision is high, "did not survive re-performance" is a defect verdict and the item stands as written; if precision is near the κ ≈ .17 band the literature reports, the four findings are disagreements, not defects. This is a day's work and it settles clause (B) outright. (2) **The denominator query.** Count how many pass-marked days on 08-03/08-04 were re-performed and how many survived. One number. (3) **The assertion-relabel A/B.** Ship PREMISE-142's three-valued vocabulary for review outcomes and re-run the same audit two weeks later. If the audit finds nothing further, the defect was labelling and the deep-review corrective was never needed. (4) **The stake-tiered depth trial.** Content-verify only identifiers that ACTIVE premises rest on; measure how many defects that finds per unit of reviewer time against a random-sample deep review of everything. If the tiered version finds more per hour — which the 64.8%-major-content-error figure predicts — the universal corrective is refuted on its own terms.

**Note on the systemic flag:** one of four items covered by the **SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG dated 2026-08-17** (batch A), at `lit_search_results/against/SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG_2026-08-17.md`. 1108's place in it: an existence-only check emitting `pass` is the same mechanism as 1106's inert guard, 1112's unread metadata and 1117's unexecuted search — a check that did not test an assertion emitting an outcome indistinguishable from one that tested it and found nothing. PREMISE-142's three-valued vocabulary plus evidence pointer is the single change that fixes all four; four separate remedies would fix the mechanism nowhere.
