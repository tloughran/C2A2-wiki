       Defect categories found: inconsistency between abstract and body, data present in the

    3. Quotation-error literature — "Accuracy of cited 'facts' in medical research articles: A

       dynamic in which a claim becomes treated as background knowledge. That is exactly what
       an unaudited PRS triplet becomes once it enters the vault and is linked.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The presumption fails on both of its components. On provenance: extraction from
    publishers' descriptions and host write-ups is a secondary-source route with measured error
    rates — abstract-versus-full-text major inconsistency at a 19% median (range 5-45%, and
    40-60% for RCT results in several specialties), quotation errors averaging around 22%, and
    improper secondary citation around 10% — so an unaudited object-level result on that route
    carries a materially higher error probability than the process artefacts that *are* being
    audited. On composition: the software-quality literature is explicit that process
    conformance does not license an inference to product quality, which is exactly the
    inference a 1,460-to-few ratio embodies. The quotation-error literature supplies the
    aggravating mechanism — authority by repetition — under which an unaudited triplet becomes
    background knowledge simply by being cited internally, at which point the original
    provenance is no longer visible to the reader. Disclosure of the route mitigates none of
    this on its own, because disclosure is only effective if something downstream conditions on
    it.

  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: The empirical question is not whether secondary sources contain
      errors but whether those errors change conclusions — and on that question the evidence
      is genuinely mixed and partly favourable. The Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 2025 case
      study found that including conference abstracts *rarely changed* systematic review
      conclusions; meta-epidemiological work has shown only minor differences between
      meta-analyses with and without grey literature; and the NIHR HTA work notes that in
      rapidly evolving fields abstracts were the only evidence available at 14% of time points,
      enabling analyses that would otherwise have been impossible. Applied to C2A2: publishers'
      descriptions may be perfectly adequate for extracting *identity-level* facts — who, what,
      which triplet — even if they are unreliable for effect sizes and conclusions, and the
      error literature's rates are dominated by exactly the numeric and inferential content
      that a PRS triplet may not depend on. There is also a resource argument the register's
      composition may reflect correctly: auditing machinery is cheap and generalises across all
      future output, whereas auditing each object-level result is expensive and generalises to
      nothing. A 1,460-to-few ratio might be the rational allocation, not a blind spot.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the extracted content is of the type
      that survives secondary sourcing — identity, existence, attribution — rather than the
      type that does not — magnitudes, conclusions, qualifications; (b) provenance tier is
      recorded as a machine-readable field on the item, not merely mentioned in prose, so that
      downstream consumers can condition on it and so that authority-by-repetition is blocked;
      (c) there is a sampling audit: some fraction of secondary-route items are verified
      against primary sources periodically, giving an empirical error rate rather than an
      assumed one; (d) the paywalled case is treated as a distinct and weaker tier again, since
      a host's write-up of a session is a tertiary description; (e) no item ever loses its
      provenance marker on being copied or linked. Condition (c) is the decisive one — without
      it, the steelman's claim that errors rarely matter is untested in this system
      specifically.
    How to test: Runnable directly. Take the PRS triplets extracted from publishers'
      descriptions, sample n of them, and verify each against the primary source; report the
      discrepancy rate with a confidence interval and compare it to the 19% median from the
      abstract-versus-full-report literature. If C2A2's rate is materially below it, the
      steelman is supported and the presumption is defensible for this content type; if it is
      at or above it, the presumption is refuted empirically in-system. Second test, on
      composition: classify every item in the register as machinery-directed or
      content-directed and confirm the ~1,460-to-few ratio, then compute the same ratio for
      *errors actually found* — if machinery audits are finding errors at a far lower rate per
      item than the content sample above, audit effort is demonstrably misallocated. Third:
      grep the vault for whether any object-level item carries a structured provenance field at
      all; if provenance exists only as prose disclosure, condition (b) fails by inspection.

  Specific risks: If object-level work does need auditing, C2A2's strongest results are its
    least verified, which inverts the usual risk gradient. Concretely: (i) the flagship result
    is the one most likely to be cited internally and externally, so an error in it propagates
    furthest before detection; (ii) authority by repetition means the disclosed provenance
    decays — after two or three internal citations the triplet reads as an established fact and
    the "from a publisher's description" qualifier is no longer adjacent to the claim; (iii)
    the composition asymmetry means the system has no baseline error rate for its own content,
    so it cannot even estimate how wrong it is, and any claim about content reliability is
    currently an assumption rather than a measurement; (iv) the machinery audits provide false
    assurance in the ISO 9000 sense — a well-audited process producing unverified products
    looks healthier than it is, and the very thoroughness of the machinery register may be what
    sustains the presumption; (v) the paywalled instance is unreproducible by a later auditor,
    which means that particular triplet cannot be re-verified at all without acquiring access,
    and its error status may be permanently indeterminate.

  Mitigations available: (1) Provenance tiering as a structured field on every object-level
    item — primary / secondary (abstract or publisher description) / tertiary (third-party
    write-up of an inaccessible source) — with the tier carried on every citation, which is the
    direct block on authority-by-repetition. (2) Sampling verification: audit a fixed fraction
    of secondary-route items against primaries each period and publish the observed error rate,
    converting an assumption into a measurement. (3) Content-type gating: permit
    secondary-route extraction for identity and existence claims, require primary sourcing for
    magnitudes, effect claims and conclusions — this is the distinction the abstract literature
    supports and it preserves most of the efficiency. (4) Duplicate independent extraction for
    high-salience results, the standard systematic-review control for extraction error. (5)
    Flag inaccessible-source items explicitly as unreverifiable so they can be excluded from
    load-bearing conclusions. (6) Rebalance the register deliberately: set a target ratio of
    content-directed to machinery-directed audit items, or at minimum instrument the current
    ratio so the asymmetry is visible rather than emergent.

  Search scope: Comprehensive for the biomedical evidence-synthesis framing — abstract accuracy
    (Pitkin et al.), abstract-versus-full-report discrepancy rates, conference-abstract
    inclusion effects on review conclusions, screening and extraction error rates — and for the
    citation-integrity framing (quotation error and improper secondary citation rates).
    Comprehensive-enough for the adjacent process-versus-product framing in software quality to
    support the composition argument, though that material came from teaching and overview
    sources rather than primary empirical studies. Preliminary on two areas that would sharpen
    it: publisher-metadata accuracy specifically (as distinct from author-written abstracts),

    descriptions in particular — the promotional register of a publisher blurb may have a
    different and possibly worse error profile than a scientific abstract, and that gap is not
    covered by any source cited here.
