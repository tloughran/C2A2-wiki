  Sources:
    1. Lakatos, I., methodology of scientific research programmes — via the *Stanford Encyclopedia of
       Philosophy* entry "Imre Lakatos," plus secondary expositions (qualityresearchinternational.com;
       Aeon, "Imre Lakatos and the philosophy of bad science"; PMC9643948, "Progressive and degenerative
       journals"). — **UNVERIFIED / SECONDARY** (SEP entry located in search results but **not fetched**;
       no primary Lakatos text read this run). The reported criterion, which is the one that matters here:
       a programme is **theoretically progressive** only if each successive modification **predicts novel
       unexpected facts**, and **empirically progressive** only if those predicted facts are at least
       occasionally corroborated; "once the reformulation of a theory results in no new testable
       predictions, the programme starts to degenerate." Degeneration is diagnosed by auxiliary hypotheses
       "cooked up after the event" to protect the core. **Why this challenges BOTH readings of 960:** the
       progressive/degenerating distinction is not settled by coverage. A programme that covers every case
       is progressive if it predicted them and degenerating if it accommodated them. **20-of-20 coverage
       is evidence for neither reading until you know whether the covering premise was minted BEFORE or
       AFTER the item surfaced.** That is a fact the estate already records — every premise carries a
       `Date validated` and every item a date — and nobody has computed it.
    2. Braun, V. & Clarke, V. (2021), "To saturate or not to saturate? Questioning data saturation as a
       useful concept for thematic analysis and sample-size rationales," *Qualitative Research in Sport,
       Exercise and Health* 13(2):201–216. Plus the saturation-critique literature: Hennink et al. (2017)
       on code vs meaning saturation; "Saturation controversy in qualitative research" (*Cogent Social
       Sciences* 2020); "Re-viewing the concept of saturation" (2025). — **UNVERIFIED** (all snippet
       level; the UWE repository record and Taylor & Francis abstract pages were located but **not
       fetched**). The reported content is directly on the intake's discriminator: saturation decomposes
       into **code saturation** (no new codes), **data saturation** (no new data) and **thematic
       saturation** (no new themes), and Hennink et al. specifically questioned the sufficiency of code
       saturation "because codes identify issues within segments of data, thereby lacking analytical
       depth." Braun & Clarke's own critique is sharper and is the one that bites: thematic saturation
       "turns a blind eye to the problematic conceptualization of themes," and the test is not how
       recurrent a theme is but **"whether the data enable the researcher to develop and test evaluative
       arguments."** Applied: "an ACTIVE premise covers this item" is a **code**-saturation observation.
       It is the weakest of the three and the one the methodological literature says is insufficient
       standing alone. The estate has measured code saturation and is reporting it as if it were

  Summary: Both candidate readings fail, and they fail for the same reason. Lakatos's criterion is that a
    programme is progressive when its modifications predict novel facts and degenerating when it
    accommodates facts after the event — so coverage alone distinguishes nothing, and the whole 20-of-20
    observation is uninformative until the premise dates are compared against the item dates. The
    saturation literature reaches the identical conclusion from the qualitative side: what the estate has
    measured is **code** saturation (every item has a covering premise), which Hennink and Braun & Clarke
    both identify as the insufficient form, and the sufficient form requires a defined population the
    estate has never enumerated. The register already holds the discriminating requirement as PREMISE-135
    clause (c) — one correct *advance* prediction about a kind not among the instances that produced the
    abstraction — and has held it since 2026-07-29 without applying it to itself. The genuine residue in
    960 is therefore neither the saturation reading nor the routing reading but a third thing: the
    observation is **currently uninterpretable, the discriminator is known and unrun, and the estate has
    already written the unfalsifiability reading into the register while continuing to treat the question
    as open.**

  Specific risks:
    - **A degenerating register looks exactly like a saturated one from the inside, and the estate is
      inside.** If premises are being matched to items post hoc, the pre-answer rate rises monotonically
      with register size and never falls, no matter how poorly the premises describe anything. Under
      PREMISE-174 there is no contraction channel to correct it. The failure mode is not that the estate
      gets a wrong answer; it is that it stops being able to get a wrong answer, which is worse.
    - **Monster-barring is the specific hazard and it is already named.** PREMISE-135's supporting
      evidence cites Lakatos's monster-barring — "the device by which a superseded generalisation is
      retrospectively made to look as though it had been right all along." A cycle that finds a covering
      premise for every item and mints nothing is performing monster-barring twenty times, and recording
      it as a health finding ("that zero is not an absence of work — it is this cycle's finding").
    - **Acting on either reading is worse than acting on neither.** Adopt saturation and the estate stops
      searching, closing the contraction path (PREMISE-174). Adopt the routing fault and it builds a grep
      that does the same thing (REVISE-449). Both remedies converge on the same harm from opposite
      premises, which is a strong signal that the question is upstream of both.
    - **The four measurements now in this series are same-instrument.** See PRESUMPTION-959: the 82%, the
      two 10-of-10s, the 20-of-20 and this cohort's 4-of-4 are one instrument's readings, so the apparent
      replication of the rate is not independent evidence that the rate is real.

  Mitigations available:
    - **Run the date test. This is the whole item and it is one query.** For each of the 20 items in the
      2026-09-11 cohort, compare the covering premise's `Date validated` with the date the item's
      underlying *situation* arose (not the date 14a/14b extracted it). Premise earlier → the register
      predicted it → progressive, and saturation is the live reading. Premise later, or the match audible
      only in hindsight → accommodation → degenerating, and the unfalsifiability reading is the live one.
      Both fields already exist in the files. Nothing needs to be built.
    - **Use the seven-verbatim split that is already recorded.** Seven of twenty were covered *verbatim*.
      Score those seven separately from the thirteen. If the progressive signal is concentrated in the
      verbatim seven and the thirteen are accommodations, the headline rate is 7/20 = 35%, not 100%, and
      the entire framing changes.
    - **Enumerate the population (PREMISE-135 clause a).** Name the kinds of architectural question this
      estate can raise. Without it, "no new kinds" is not a saturation claim, it is a sentence.
    - **Run one severe test (PREMISE-135 clause c).** Before the next cohort is extracted, have the
      register predict IN ADVANCE which premise will cover each item. Sealed predictions, opened after.
      A correct advance prediction is the only thing that distinguishes the two readings, and it converts
      the pre-answer rate from a tautology into a measurement. This is the single highest-value act
      available and it costs one file written before the run instead of after.
    - **Stop reporting the rate as a finding until one of the above has run.** Report it as UNCALIBRATED
      (PREMISE-124). A number that is consistent with both a healthy and a degenerate register is not
      evidence for either.

    Strongest counterargument: The degeneration charge is the wrong frame because the register is not a
      theory and pre-answering is not accommodation. Lakatos's criterion governs *empirical* programmes
      making claims about a world that pushes back; `validated_premises.md` is closer to a body of
      normative engineering standards, and nobody thinks a building code is "degenerating" because the
      next inspection finds it already covers the defect. For a standards corpus, high coverage of new
      cases is precisely what success looks like, and the novel-prediction test is a category error
      imported from a different kind of object. Moreover the premises were emphatically not cooked up
      after the event: PREMISE-182's closed-form identity, PREMISE-174's contraction argument,
      PREMISE-141's third terminal state — all were minted weeks or months before the items they covered,
      against entirely different source items, and each covered its 09-11 item *exactly*. That IS the
      advance prediction PREMISE-135 asks for; it has already happened twenty times and the estate simply
      has not counted it. On this reading the correct diagnosis is neither saturation nor unfalsifiability
      but the mundane one: the register is good, the runs do not read it, and the whole
      saturation-versus-degeneration debate is a sophisticated way of avoiding the cheap fix. The
      qualitative-saturation literature is also a strained transfer — it is about sampling human
      interviewees for thematic novelty, and the estate is not sampling anything.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the "minted before, covered after" claim must be
      verified rather than asserted — it is checkable from two date fields and has not been checked;
      (b) the covering match must survive a disjoint reader, or "the premise covers it" is the
      instrument's opinion of itself (PREMISE-096, and the calibration test in PRESUMPTION-959);
      (c) if the standards-corpus reading is adopted, the estate must then accept its corollary — a
      standards corpus is judged by whether conduct conforms, not by whether it grows, which routes
      straight back to ASSUMPTION-1321 and makes the growth statistic irrelevant rather than reassuring;
      (d) the population must still be enumerated, because even a standards corpus can have gaps and
      "covers everything we thought to ask" is not "covers everything."
    How to test: the sealed advance-prediction run. Before the next 14a/14b cohort is dispositioned, write
      down which ACTIVE premise will cover each item, seal it, and open it after. High concordance
      sustains the steelman decisively and ends the debate in one cycle; low concordance means the matches
      are being constructed at disposition time and the degeneration reading is live. Pair it with the
      retrospective date test on the 09-11 cohort, which is free and can be run today. Together these two
