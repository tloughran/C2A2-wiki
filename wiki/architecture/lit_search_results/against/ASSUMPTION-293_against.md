  Sources:
    1. Mosier, C., 1947. "A Critique of Face Validity." Educational and Psychological Measurement. — Classic argument that face validity is the weakest, most easily abused form of validity evidence; it cannot substitute for reliability or construct validation.
    2. AERA/APA/NCME, 2014. "Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing." — Validity requires multiple evidence sources; instruments whose ratings cannot demonstrate reliability are not considered valid tests. Single-rater designs preclude assessing inter-rater reliability and are explicitly discouraged (echoed in methodological reviews, e.g., ScienceDirect overview of interrater reliability; Ewha portfolio-assessment study, PMC 2025).
    3. Kahneman, D., Sibony, O., Sunstein, C., 2021. "Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment." William Collins. — Even within a single expert, occasion noise produces large judgment variability; N=1 confounds the construct with the rater's noise and bias with no way to separate them.
    4. Hallgren, K., 2012. "Computing Inter-Rater Reliability for Observational Data." Tutorials in Quantitative Methods for Psychology. — Reliability assessment is treated as integral to any research quantifying phenomena via raters; a sole rater leaves measurement error unquantifiable.
  Strength of challenge: Strong
  Summary: Psychometric consensus holds that face validity is the weakest form of validity evidence and cannot carry an instrument alone. A single-rater design makes it impossible to estimate measurement error, distinguish the rater's idiosyncrasies from the construct, or detect occasion noise — which Kahneman et al. show is large even within one expert. The special case claimed here (the expert IS the tradition's originator) actually worsens the problem: the rater certifying his own tradition's milestones is maximally exposed to self-confirmation, with no external check. Literature offers no recognized exemption for "founder authority" in measurement validation.
  Specific risks: PRS milestone certifications may encode one person's momentary judgment as durable fact; downstream artifacts (ISME claims, dyad ratifications) inherit unquantified error; external audiences can dismiss the whole milestone set as unvalidated.
  Mitigations available: Test-retest of the same milestones at intervals (intra-rater reliability as partial substitute); explicit written rationales per certification (auditability); periodic challenge by an independently-formed agent or human; treat certifications as provisional with confidence tags rather than authoritative.
  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: For a genuinely novel integrative tradition there is by definition no second qualified rater; demanding inter-rater agreement would make certification impossible rather than better. Expert-authored instruments (e.g., early FCI drafts) began as one or few authors' judgment and were validated later. Face validity plus documented rationale is a legitimate bootstrap stage, provided it is labeled as such and a validation path exists.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Certifications are explicitly provisional; rationales are recorded so future raters can re-derive them; a drift-detection/test-retest mechanism exists; the system never treats N=1 certification as terminal evidence.
    How to test: Re-present a sample of certified milestones to the MM-of-1 after 4-8 weeks, blind to prior ratings; measure intra-rater agreement. Low agreement falsifies the authority claim directly.
