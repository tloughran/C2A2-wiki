  Strength of challenge: Weak-Moderate

  Summary: The honest result is that the against direction found the presumption's defeater and then found
    that the defeater under-performs. There *is* a documented mechanism by which evidentiary qualifiers stay
    attached to the claims they govern — GRADE's per-outcome certainty rating is exactly that, and it is
    real, adopted, and a direct counterexample to a blanket claim that caveats never survive. But every
    assessment of it reached here reports inconsistent application and poor reproducibility of the process
    that produces it. So the challenge to PRESUMPTION-889 is: qualifier survival is achievable, not
    automatic, and it is achieved by per-claim attachment rather than by document-level declaration. That
    lands on the presumption's specific architecture — a header governing a body — rather than rescuing it.

  Specific risks: If the estate concludes from the for direction that caveats cannot survive, it may stop
    writing them, which is the worst available outcome. The GRADE precedent argues the opposite: write them
    smaller, attached to individual claims, and accept that the attachment is the work.

  Mitigations available: Move the qualifier to the claim. In this estate that means the access marking
    ([SNIPPET-ONLY], [authors unverified]) belongs on the source line, which is where these files already
    put it — and the run-level degradation statement should be repeated at each finding it degrades, not
    only in the header. This file is written to that standard; earlier files in the estate largely are not.

    Strongest counterargument: A header caveat is a reasonable economy in a corpus with one reader who reads
      whole documents. Repeating a degradation notice at every claim is noise that trains the reader to skip
      it — the alert-fatigue failure — and the estate has documented that failure elsewhere the same week.
      Header-scoping is not an oversight; it is a choice about where to spend the reader's attention.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: claims from these files would have to be consumed only
      by readers who saw the header — i.e. no claim would ever be quoted into another document without it.
    How to test: take five claims sourced from lit-search files and quoted elsewhere in the estate, and check
      whether the quoting document carries the source file's evidence-grade caveat. The 2026-08-26 run
      supplies a ready case: its 198 SNIPPET-ONLY markings, and whether anything citing those files repeats
      the marking.
