**Polarity note (explicit inversion).** 14b's wording states the presumption the system holds; 14b's own position is that the presumption is unsafe. The proposition I am challenging is therefore **14b's residual claim** — that a constant reading is uninterpretable because "no change" is indistinguishable from detector failure. The AGAINST direction is that this distinction is not merely decidable but is a *solved and standardised engineering problem*, cheap enough that whole industries mandate it, and that once the primitive is present a constant reading is a positive statement about the world rather than an absence of one.

### Sources

1. **NFPA 72, *National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code* — circuit supervision requirements (Class A/B pathways; trouble-signal annunciation).** [verified this run via multiple secondary sources: National Training Center pathway summaries; Reliable Fire Protection, "Fire Alarm Circuit Supervision Explained for Professionals"; code72prep, "NFPA 72 Class A Fire Alarm Wiring Explained". NFPA 72 primary text not read.] — The most direct refutation of the "undecidable" claim, and it is a legal requirement rather than a good practice. Every initiating circuit carries a small supervisory current; an open, short, or ground fault alters that current and is annunciated as a **trouble** condition, categorically distinct from **alarm** and from the quiescent no-alarm state. The secondary sources cite a 200-second annunciation requirement for open or ground-fault conditions. A fire panel that has reported "no alarm" for eleven consecutive months is making a substantive claim, precisely because the same wire that would carry an alarm is being continuously interrogated for liveness.
2. **Prometheus "Watchdog" / dead-man's-switch alerting pattern.** [verified this run: PromLabs training module "End-to-end watchdog alerts"; Bastos & Araújo, *Hands-On Infrastructure Monitoring with Prometheus*, Packt/O'Reilly 2019 — "Dead man's switch alerts" chapter; multiple practitioner writeups. Book authorship marked canonical, not re-verified.] — The monitoring field's standard answer to exactly 14b's question. An always-firing alert (`vector(1)`) is routed continuously to an *external* receiver; if the receiver stops seeing it, the alerting pipeline — not the monitored world — is declared failed. The complementary `absent()` function fires on a metric that has stopped arriving. The pattern exists because the profession agrees the failure mode is real, and it costs a few lines of configuration.
3. **Chandra, T. D. & Toueg, S., 1996. "Unreliable Failure Detectors for Reliable Distributed Systems." *Journal of the ACM* 43(2):225–267.** [canonical, not re-verified — the properties were returned in this run's search summaries (Aeron liveness-detection documentation; distributed-systems primers), the paper itself was not retrieved.] — Supplies the formal vocabulary that makes the item's question tractable rather than mysterious. Failure detectors are characterised by **completeness** (every crashed process is eventually suspected) and **accuracy** (how often live processes are wrongly suspected). These are two independent, separately measurable properties. The item's worry is a completeness worry, and completeness is a property one *designs and states*, not a fog one stands in.
4. **Data-freshness / staleness monitoring practice: max-timestamp-versus-SLA checks, heartbeat record injection, volume-anomaly detection.** [verified this run — practitioner and vendor sources: dbt source-freshness documentation via Paradime; DQOps timeliness-and-freshness check documentation; Elementary Data; RisingWave feature-pipeline observability. ### Strength of challenge: Moderate-to-Strong

### Summary

The item's observation is worth having, but its residual claim — that a constant reading is uninterpretable — is refuted by a large body of established practice in which exactly this distinguishability is engineered in as a matter of course. Fire-alarm codes mandate circuit supervision so that a quiet panel is asserting something rather than merely failing to assert; distributed-systems theory separates a detector's completeness from its accuracy so that each can be stated and measured; monitoring practice routes an always-firing watchdog to an external receiver so that silence in the pipeline is itself alarmed; data-quality practice injects synthetic heartbeats so that freshness is measurable when real volume is zero. The common structure across all of these is a **positive control**: a known-answer case that the instrument must continue to get right, run on the same path as the real reading. What the literature therefore says is not "14b is wrong to ask" but "14b's question has a cheap standard answer, and the correct finding is that C2A2's queue readers lack the control, not that the readers' conclusion is unknowable." The seven runs that read a structurally-held queue and reported exhausted work may well have been reporting accurately: a queue that is blocked by construction is a genuinely stable world, and reporting it as such is correct. What is missing is the one line that would let a reader tell the difference — evidence that the reader could still see an item if one were there.

### Specific risks

If 14b's residual claim is adopted at full strength, the operational consequence is that no unchanged reading may be acted on, which converts a routine and correct steady-state report into a permanently suspect one, and — since the review queue may legitimately remain held for weeks — produces a system that can never conclude anything about its own backlog. That is a worse epistemic position than the current one, and it will be resolved in practice by ignoring the requirement. If the concern is dismissed entirely, the hazard is the one the freshness literature names precisely: a reader that has silently lost its ability to see queue items reports the same "exhausted" string as a reader looking at a genuinely empty queue, and because the fleet has substituted other work on the strength of that string, the substitution volume grows exactly when the instrument is least trustworthy. Both failure directions run through the same missing artefact.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Inject a canary item.** Place one known, permanently-present sentinel item in the review queue that every reader must report seeing. A reader that reports "exhausted" without reporting the sentinel has failed, and this is detectable in the same read, at no extra cost. This is the fire-alarm supervisory current, transposed. (b) **Report the discriminating quantities, not the conclusion.** "Exhausted" is a conclusion; "read N items, M matched filter, queue file mtime T" is a reading, and a constant mtime across eleven readings is itself an observable. (c) **Externalise the liveness signal** — the watchdog pattern's essential feature is that the receiver is outside the pipeline being tested, so a reader's own claim to be alive cannot vouch for it. (d) **State each reader's completeness posture** (what it is designed to be unable to see) at construction, following the failure-detector vocabulary — this is the same recommendation PRESUMPTION-786's search produced from the soundiness literature, and it costs nothing when applied at build time.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-787

**Strongest counterargument:** Treating a constant reading as uninformative discards the most valuable output an instrument produces, because in a well-run system most readings *should* be constant and their constancy is the evidence that nothing is wrong. The distinguishability problem 14b raises is not open — it was closed decades ago, in codified form, by industries where getting it wrong kills people: NFPA 72 requires that every fire-alarm circuit be continuously supervised so that a broken wire annunciates as trouble within 200 seconds and is never confusable with the absence of an alarm. The software equivalents are cheaper still: an always-firing watchdog alert routed to an external receiver, an `absent()` rule, a synthetic canary that exercises the path when no real traffic does, a heartbeat record injected into a data pipeline so freshness is measurable at zero volume. Distributed-systems theory even gives the property a name and separates it from accuracy, so it can be stated and measured independently. Against that background, the eleventh identical reading is not epistemically empty; it is empty *only for an unsupervised instrument*, and the correct finding is a missing supervisory control, not an unreachable inference. The item as written escalates a five-line configuration gap into an epistemological impasse, and the escalation is costly: a queue that is legitimately held for months would, under the item's standard, generate an unresolvable doubt every single day.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** Each reader of the review queue must exercise, on the same code path as its real read, at least one case whose correct answer is known in advance — a sentinel item, a seeded record, or a self-reported observable such as the queue file's modification time and item count — and must report that case's outcome alongside its conclusion. Given that, "unchanged" is a positive assertion and the seven runs' inference is warranted. Absent it, 14b's worry is live, but what is missing is a control, not a proof.

**How to test:** Take the seven runs from the flagged day and ask, of each, whether any observable in its output would have differed had the queue reader been broken. If the answer is no for all seven, the instrument is unsupervised and the item is vindicated as a control gap. Then run the constructive test: place one sentinel item in the queue, re-run the readers unchanged, and count how many report it. Readers that report "exhausted" while a sentinel is present have a completeness defect that eleven days of constant readings could never have surfaced; readers that report the sentinel have demonstrated, in a single cheap experiment, that their constant output was substantive all along. A second measurement worth taking: record the queue file's mtime alongside each reading for a fortnight, and check whether "unchanged reading" and "unchanged file" ever diverge. A divergence is exactly the failure 14b fears, and it is directly observable.
