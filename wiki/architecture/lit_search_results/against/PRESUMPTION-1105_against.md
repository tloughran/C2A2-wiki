  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: Systematic-review methodology treats a priori criteria and documented, justified deviations as the safeguard against selection bias; discretionary exceptions are the classic route to it. LLM 'significance' judgments are unstable across runs, so the exception is not consistently applied. No study quantifies bias from LLM-discretionary inclusion exceptions specifically.

  STEELMAN: A discretionary exception triggered by the agent's own sense of 'significance' correlates with its priors (famous, confirmatory, recently salient sources) and is applied inconsistently. Unlogged, it cannot be detected or corrected. Aggregated, it shifts the corpus toward what the agent finds notable. 'No systematic bias' is an empirical claim that needs an audit; without one it is untestable, not true.

  Caveats: Bias not guaranteed; a rare symmetric exception could be benign; direction of bias not measured. Mostly by-principle evidence.
