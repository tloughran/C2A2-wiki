  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The presumption fails on theory and on measurement. In missing-data theory the distinction between a null result and an unattempted check is the distinction between two different missingness mechanisms, and a record that cannot represent it cannot state which mechanism downstream inference is assuming — the safety question becomes unanswerable rather than merely unanswered. Clinical informatics has named the specific case: informative-MNAR, where the absence of a measurement encodes a decision, with the standard example being a test not ordered because it was assumed negative. That is exactly C2A2's "no result recorded", and the methods literature built to recover from it — double sampling, specialised imputation — is expensive precisely because the cheap fix, recording the distinction at capture, was skipped. The closest domain analogue is decisive: systematic review methodology treats the search as the data-collection step and mandates structured search-provenance reporting through PRISMA-S, and meta-research measuring how well this survives in practice found that 95% of reviews do not report a reproducible search strategy. That is the presumption's empirical failure rate among trained humans writing under an explicit standard. The item's own evidence — four runs writing the distinction by hand because no field exists — is the same phenomenon at an earlier stage. The one qualification worth recording is that a schema field alone will not fix it: compliance studies find search-reporting standards are widely under-complied with even where mandated.

  Specific risks:
    - Unanswerable missingness class: no downstream analysis of the register can state whether its missing entries are MCAR, MAR, or MNAR, so no confidence figure computed over the register is defensible.
    - Systematic bias toward positive findings: undistinguishable nulls are free to omit, so the register drifts toward recording only what was found. This directly attacks the value of this agent's NO-CHALLENGE-FOUND results, which are its most fragile output.
    - Coverage overstated: an item with no recorded challenge looks searched. The 26 unsearched items from 2026-07-21 and the QUEUED backlog (cf. ASSUMPTION-1149) are the same ambiguity at register scale.
    - Prose is not preserved: 95% non-reproducibility among human reviewers is the base rate for exactly this notation strategy. Four hand-written qualifiers will not survive summarisation, migration, or a change of parser (cf. ASSUMPTION-1149 again — the parser does not read prose).
    - Expensive recovery: once the distinction is lost, recovering it requires re-running the searches, which is double sampling by another name and costs full search budget.
    - False comfort from adding a field: without enforcement the field will be left blank, and a blank field is a third undistinguished state.

  Mitigations available:
    - Add explicit register fields distinguishing at minimum: SEARCHED-FOUND-NOTHING, NOT-SEARCHED, SEARCH-ATTEMPTED-FAILED, and OUT-OF-SCOPE. Four states, not two, because "attempted and errored" is a third real case and "deliberately excluded" is a fourth.
    - Make the field mandatory and non-defaulting: a run that cannot fill it must fail, per the compliance findings. A nullable field reintroduces the ambiguity it was added to remove.
    - Record search provenance per item, PRISMA-S style: what was queried, where, when, and with what result count — not just the conclusion. This is what makes a null result auditable rather than merely asserted.
    - Backfill by re-search rather than by inference for the standing 26 + 163 + 13 items, and record the backfill as such.
    - Treat NO-CHALLENGE-FOUND as a first-class positive result with its own required evidence, so that reporting a null costs no less credit than reporting a finding. This is the direct file-drawer countermeasure.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-841
    Strongest counterargument: A null result and an unattempted check are two different missingness mechanisms, and once a register cannot tell them apart, nothing computed over that register can state what it is assuming — the question of bias is not answered badly, it becomes unanswerable. Clinical informatics named this case informative-MNAR and gives the exact analogue: a test that does not appear in a record because a clinician judged it unnecessary looks identical to a test that was never considered, and the difference determines whether the record can be analysed at all. The recovery methods, double sampling among them, are expensive because they re-collect data that the original capture step declined to encode for free. The closest domain to C2A2's own is more damning still: systematic review methodology decided this distinction was important enough to require a dedicated reporting extension, and meta-research on whether prose preserves it found that 95% of published reviews do not report a reproducible search strategy. Ninety-five percent is the measured failure rate of the exact strategy C2A2's four runs used. And the direction of the resulting bias is not neutral — undistinguishable nulls are cheap to omit, so the register will drift toward containing only positive findings, which means the first output to be corrupted is this agent's own NO-CHALLENGE-FOUND verdicts.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) Every downstream consumer would have to read the prose qualifiers and interpret them identically — testable, and contradicted by the fact that the queue's own parser does not read prose at all (ASSUMPTION-1149); (b) the four hand-written qualifiers would have to survive every subsequent summarisation and migration of the register, which prose does not; (c) nulls would have to be reported at the same rate as positive findings, or the file-drawer drift is already underway; (d) if a schema field is added, it would have to be mandatory and non-defaulting, since compliance research finds nominally-mandated search-reporting fields are widely left incomplete.
    How to test: Measure the loss directly. Take the four runs that wrote the distinction by hand and ask an independent reader — human or a differently-scaffolded agent — to classify each item as "searched, nothing found" or "not searched" using only the register as a downstream consumer would see it. The disagreement rate against the runs' actual intent is the presumption's local failure rate. Then measure the drift: count items in the register carrying an explicit null result versus items carrying a positive finding, and compare that ratio against the ratio in the underlying result files, where nulls are recorded in full. A gap between the two ratios is file-drawer bias in progress and is measurable today without any schema change.
