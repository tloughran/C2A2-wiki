  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: Since the 2026-04-13 validation, new work has strengthened the case that raw displacement-vector geometry is less trustworthy than it looks: calibrated-similarity results (Jan 2026) show anisotropy inflates similarity for unrelated pairs (exactly the false-positive mode that would generate spurious cross-tradition "structural similarities"), and semantic-shift results (Mar 2026) show long-text embeddings collapse in variance, which matters because C2A2 operates on passages rather than words. The 2025 emergence work is double-edged: it legitimizes linear analogy structure as a real phenomenon while showing offsets encode diffuse corpus statistics rather than specific relations. None of this overturns the assumption as stated, because the assumption already conditions usability on validation protocols; the challenge is that the validation protocols now have new, named failure modes they must cover (similarity calibration, length normalization, frequency correction, input-word exclusion) that the April validation may not have anticipated.

  Specific risks: Cross-tradition similarity claims in the wiki may include false positives generated by anisotropy (everything looks similar in a narrow cosine band) or by length artifacts (all long contemplative passages collapse toward each other), producing seemingly profound Levin↔Friston-style structural matches that are geometric artifacts; downstream triangulation then treats these artifacts as independent evidence.

  Mitigations available: Add calibration to the validation protocol (compare claimed matches against a null distribution of random-pair similarities from the same corpus rather than absolute thresholds); apply length-normalization or chunking before embedding; exclude input terms from nearest-neighbor results; require any load-bearing displacement match to replicate across at least two embedding models (structural claims that survive model change are less likely to be geometry artifacts).

STEELMAN:
  Item: ASSUMPTION-009 / PREMISE-002
  Strongest counterargument: The 2026 calibration literature shows that the embedding-space similarity scores underpinning displacement matching are systematically miscalibrated: unrelated pairs score high because the space is anisotropic, and long-text embeddings collapse toward one another regardless of content. A pipeline hunting for cross-tradition structural similarity is therefore running a detector whose false-positive rate is inflated in precisely the direction of its confirmation bias — it will find "deep resonances" between any two sufficiently long, sufficiently abstract contemplative texts. And the emergence results show that even genuine linear-analogy structure encodes diffuse corpus statistics, not the specific relational mapping the wiki's cross-tradition claims assert.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Validation protocols must include null-model calibration (random-pair baselines), length controls, and cross-model replication; displacement evidence must be labeled as suggestive, requiring textual confirmation before incorporation.
  How to test: For a sample of accepted cross-tradition matches, compute the similarity of each anchor against length-matched random passages from unrelated traditions; if the accepted matches do not clearly separate from the null distribution, the detector is finding geometry, not structure.
