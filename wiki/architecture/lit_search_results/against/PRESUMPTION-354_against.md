"Understanding is not achieved by translation." This challenges the adversarial-contest framing as too thin — the real relation is immersive and dialectical, not a head-to-head match.
    3. Normative-metaphor critique in visualization/framing (framing effects literature). — A "rival team / open seat" metaphor is not neutral: it imports zero-sum, winner-take-all, fixed-roster connotations that may misdescribe a relation that is partly synthetic/integrative (the project's own telos includes INTEGRATION across traditions — conscious realist monism — not just defeating rivals). The frame can prejudge the relation it is meant to describe.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The contest frame is partially challenged. Rivalry between traditions/paradigms is real (MacIntyre and Kuhn both affirm it — see 15a), so the frame is not wrong; but the SPECIFIC adversarial-sports version ("rival team," "open seat," implied shared scoreboard) is challenged on two fronts: Kuhnian incommensurability denies the neutral shared metric the metaphor presupposes, and MacIntyre's account locates the contest in immanent critique and epistemic crisis — an immersive, dialectical process — rather than head-to-head scoring. Moreover the project's integrative telos (CRM as a SYNTHESIS across traditions) sits awkwardly with a pure zero-sum rivalry frame. As a PRESUMPTION touching telos, the unexamined metaphor risks prejudging the relation.

  Specific risks: The adversarial frame could push the system toward a shared-scoreboard, defeat-the-rival design (e.g., a literal "open seat" or scoring view) that incommensurability says is ill-defined, and could crowd out the integrative work that is part of CRM's actual aim; framing effects then propagate into how rival paradigms are represented and engaged (as opponents to beat rather than traditions to immerse in and learn from).

  Mitigations available: Articulate the contest MECHANISM explicitly — rivalry via immanent critique / epistemic-crisis induction, not shared-metric scoring — so the frame inherits MacIntyre's actual model rather than the sports metaphor; hold the adversarial frame alongside an integrative/synthetic frame rather than letting "rival team" be the sole metaphor; avoid building a literal shared-scoreboard view that presupposes commensurability incommensurability denies.
