  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: Once an idea is absorbed its source link is lost; a later attribution is an inference, not a record. Merton named the opposite error, adumbrationism. Copied citations spread errors at scale. An agent that back-attributes without checking the source text risks all three.

  Specific risks: Claims credited to a source that does not say them; wrong attributions spread through wikilinks; inferred and recorded provenance become indistinguishable; credit concentrates on famous sources (Matthew effect, UNVERIFIED here).

  Mitigations available: Label back-attributions "inferred", separate from "cited by author". Require a passage-level quote or page reference. Grade the link ("anticipates" / "consistent with" / "states"). Human review of attributions to foundational sources.
