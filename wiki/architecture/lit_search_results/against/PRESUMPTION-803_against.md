**Polarity note.** The diagnosis — that eight proposals is reported without a denominator, and that volume has been standing in for coverage — is not challenged anywhere in what I found; it is a straightforward category error and the literature agrees. What the AGAINST direction attacks is the item's **implied remedy: aggregate the fleet's honest local partial-coverage statements into a coverage figure and report that instead of throughput.** Two independent bodies of evidence say this substitution is a downgrade, not an upgrade: coverage is empirically a weak predictor of the thing it proxies, and the moment it becomes the reported figure it becomes a target and stops being even that. A third line attacks the denominator itself: "fifteen traditions" is a frame the architecture chose, so coverage-of-the-frame is not coverage of the tradition space, and reporting it as such reproduces the original error one level up.

### Challenging evidence found: Yes (strong against the remedy; none against the diagnosis)

### Sources

1. **Inozemtseva, L. and Holmes, R. (2014). "Coverage Is Not Strongly Correlated with Test Suite Effectiveness." *Proceedings of the 36th International Conference on Software Engineering (ICSE 2014)*. ACM Distinguished Paper.** [**verified this run — primary PDF fetched and abstract read** at cs.ubc.ca/~rtholmes/papers/icse_2014_inozemtseva.pdf] — The central source. 31,000 test suites generated across five Java systems of up to 724,000 lines; statement, decision and modified-condition coverage measured; mutation testing used as the effectiveness ground truth. Finding, verbatim from the abstract: "there is a **low to moderate correlation** between coverage and effectiveness **when the number of test cases in the suite is controlled for**," and "**stronger forms of coverage do not provide greater insight** into the effectiveness of the suite." The second clause is the one that matters here: escalating to a more rigorous coverage definition does not rescue the metric. This is the largest study in the literature and it directly undercuts the assumption that a coverage figure would tell the fleet what its volume figure fails to.
2. **Goodhart's law applied to code coverage — the practitioner corpus.** [verified this run via de Pauw, "The Fallacy of the 100% Code Coverage" (ThinkingLabs, 2022); Wayne, "Goodhart's Law in Software Engineering" (Buttondown); and two further engineering-blog treatments] — The mechanism of the downgrade. "Code coverage began as a measure, became a target and then lost all effectiveness as either a measure or a target." The documented gaming pattern is specific and recognisable: teams write tests that touch lines without asserting anything, and hard-to-test units are *excluded from the measurement*. The reported case that generalises worst for C2A2: a team at 30% line coverage dropped to **3%** under mutation testing — an order-of-magnitude gap between "was executed" and "was actually checked." The fleet's analogue is exact: a tradition "covered" by an agent that ran and filed something versus a tradition actually surveyed.
3. **Risk-based auditing versus coverage-based auditing — professional-practice literature.** [verified this run via Wolters Kluwer/TeamMate "Practical guide to risk-based auditing", the IIA's *Internal Auditor* "Building a Better Auditor: The Case for Reasonable Assurance" (2025), and two further practitioner sources] — The audit profession made this exact move and made it in the *opposite* direction from the item's remedy: risk-based auditing "replac[es] coverage with prioritization, checklists with judgment." The stated failure mode is that "without discipline, teams often revert to checklist-driven procedures or to focusing on coverage, both of which can lack relevance," because "testing key controls provides meaningful assurance, while testing peripheral controls does not." A coverage figure weights a peripheral tradition and a load-bearing one identically. That is not a neutral property; it is the defect the profession abandoned coverage to escape.
4. **The streetlight effect, and its concrete instantiation in coverage-guided automated discovery.** [verified this run via the *Streetlight effect* survey material and current fuzzing literature: edge coverage is "a lightweight proxy for program behavior" that "captures path variations only weakly," so "many path-dependent bugs go undetected"; coverage-increasing inputs become rare as a campaign progresses; one reported evaluation had Jazzer exploiting only 14.8% of known vulnerabilities] — This is the item's own named test, and it returns against the remedy rather than for it. In the one field that has most seriously tried to use coverage as the steering signal for automated discovery, coverage is understood as a *cheap proxy chosen because it is measurable*, with acknowledged blind spots — and the field's own reviewers note that "the coverage collection and evaluation remain blind spots." Adopting coverage is adopting the streetlight, not escaping it.
5. **Reasonable assurance versus total assurance.** [verified this run via the IIA source above] — A framing challenge to the item's risk statement. The profession's stated position is that expectations are "gradually shifting from providing reasonable assurance to total assurance, a standard that is neither practical nor aligned with the principles of internal auditing." The item's risk clause — that the 636 PRS triplets and 103 cross-connections may be "a statement about which agents ran, not about the traditions" — is correct as stated but implies a total-coverage standard as the alternative. There is no third state in which those figures become statements about the traditions rather than about a sampling process; there is only a better-characterised sampling process.
6. **Guo et al. (2010), "Characterizing and Predicting Which Bugs Get Fixed" (Microsoft Research, ICSE 2010) and the wontfix literature.** [verified this run as search returns; abstracts read, primaries not read] — Included as a boundary note rather than a core challenge: in a domain with a genuinely enumerable denominator, the *proportion of the population ever addressed* is nonetheless low and predictably non-random, and this is treated as normal operation rather than as a coverage failure. A coverage figure in such a domain would be low, stable, and uninformative.

### Strength of challenge: Moderate

### Summary

Nothing I found defends volume-as-coverage; the diagnosis stands untouched and the missing denominator is a real defect. The challenge is entirely to the substitution. Inozemtseva and Holmes is the largest study of coverage-as-proxy in the literature — 31,000 suites, five systems, mutation testing as ground truth — and its result is that coverage correlates only low-to-moderately with the thing it proxies once suite size is controlled, and that stronger coverage definitions do not help. So a coverage figure would not be a good measure even before anyone started optimising it. The Goodhart corpus supplies what happens once someone does: coverage "began as a measure, became a target and then lost all effectiveness as either," with the reported 30%-to-3% gap between line coverage and mutation coverage giving the size of the gap between *touched* and *checked*. That gap is the fleet's central exposure, because "a tradition an agent ran against and filed something for" is line coverage, not mutation coverage. The audit profession has already run this experiment at institutional scale and moved the other way, replacing coverage with risk-based prioritisation on the explicit ground that testing peripheral controls does not produce assurance — and a coverage figure cannot express that, since it weights every tradition equally by construction. The item's own named test, the streetlight effect in automated discovery, returns against it: coverage-guided fuzzing is the field that most seriously adopted coverage as a steering signal, and it treats edge coverage as an acknowledged weak proxy with known blind spots. Finally there is the denominator problem the item does not reach. Fifteen traditions is a frame the architecture chose. A coverage figure computed against it would be a statement about the frame's exhaustion, not about the tradition space — the same inference the item is objecting to, relocated one level up and now wearing a percentage sign. Against all of that, the fleet's current practice looks better than the item allows: "read-verified by hand on 11 of the 20, grep-only on the other 9" is a *stratified* statement that preserves the quality distinction a single coverage number would destroy.

### Specific risks

If the remedy is adopted as stated, the fleet acquires a headline coverage percentage. Three things follow, all documented. First, the number will be a weak predictor of what it is taken to measure (Inozemtseva & Holmes). Second, once it is the reported figure, agents will optimise it — and the cheapest way to raise tradition coverage is to file *something* for every scheduled tradition, which converts today's honest zero-for-Carroll into a thin proposal that raises the metric and lowers the yield. This is the documented pattern, not a speculative one. Third, the aggregation itself destroys information: "11 read-verified, 9 grep-only" is strictly more informative than "20/20 covered", and the item's framing treats the failure to aggregate as the defect when the granular statements are the better artefact.

A fourth risk is specific to this fleet and is the one I would weight highest. A coverage figure would be computed against the architecture's own list of fifteen traditions. That list is a design choice, so the metric measures the fleet's progress through a self-set frame and reports it in the register of external validity. Because the frame is fleet-internal, no value of the metric can ever indicate that the frame is wrong — the streetlight is not merely where the fleet looks, it is what the fleet counts.

If the item is dismissed, the exposure it names is live and large: the 636 PRS triplets and 103 cross-connections are volume counts presented in a register that implies survey completeness, and the honest partial-coverage statements scattered through the corpus work are never brought into contact with them.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Keep the local, stratified statements and stop aggregating upward.** "11 of 20 read-verified, 9 grep-only" is the format the fleet already produces and it dominates any single percentage. The repair is to make such statements *mandatory* wherever a figure is reported, not to average them.
(b) **State the denominator and its provenance in the same sentence as any coverage claim** — "4 of the 15 traditions the architecture currently enumerates" — so the frame travels with the number and a reader can see that fifteen is a choice.
(c) **Report the zeros.** The single highest-value change available here costs nothing: today's rollup should have said "zero Carroll, zero Arkani-Hamed, both scheduled." A named-zero list is unfakeable in a way a percentage is not, and it is immune to the Goodhart pattern because it cannot be raised by filing thin work — filing thin work is visible as thin work.
(d) **Adopt the mutation-testing analogue rather than the coverage analogue.** The question worth asking is not "how many traditions were touched" but "if a tradition's material were silently corrupted, would any of the day's runs have noticed?" This is expensive and should be run rarely, on a sample — but it measures checking rather than touching, which is the gap the 30%-to-3% figure quantifies.
(e) **Weight by risk, as the audit profession did.** If a coverage figure is wanted anyway, compute it over the subset of traditions that the central claim actually depends on, and state that subset. Equal weighting is the property that made coverage useless for auditors.
(f) **Pre-register what the number would have to be.** Before computing any coverage figure, state what value would count as adequate and what action a low value would trigger. Without this the figure joins queue depth as a number that accumulates without ever forcing a decision — see PRESUMPTION-802, where exactly this happened to a different number.

### Recommendation: PARTIALLY-CHALLENGED

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-803

**Strongest counterargument:** The diagnosis is correct and the cure is a metric that software engineering spent two decades learning not to trust. Inozemtseva and Holmes generated 31,000 test suites across five systems up to 724,000 lines, used mutation testing as ground truth, and found only a low-to-moderate correlation between coverage and effectiveness once suite size was controlled — plus the finding that kills the obvious fix, that stronger forms of coverage give no greater insight. So a coverage figure is a poor proxy *before* anyone has an incentive to move it. Give it an incentive and the practitioner record is unambiguous: coverage "began as a measure, became a target and then lost all effectiveness as either a measure or a target," with hard-to-test units excluded from the denominator and one reported team's 30% line coverage collapsing to 3% under mutation testing. That order-of-magnitude gap between executed and checked is precisely the gap the fleet would be papering over, because "an agent ran against this tradition and filed something" is line coverage in every respect that matters. The audit profession ran this experiment institutionally and went the other way — risk-based auditing exists to replace coverage with prioritisation, on the stated ground that testing peripheral controls yields no assurance while testing key controls does, a distinction a coverage percentage cannot express because it weights every unit alike. Even the item's own chosen probe turns: coverage-guided fuzzing is the most committed attempt anywhere to steer automated discovery by coverage, and its own literature calls edge coverage a weak proxy that misses path-dependent bugs and calls coverage evaluation itself a blind spot. And beneath all of this sits the denominator the item never questions. Fifteen traditions is the architecture's own enumeration. Coverage against it measures the fleet's progress through a frame the fleet drew, reported in the register of having surveyed a space the fleet did not draw — which is structurally the same substitution the item is complaining about, now expressed as a percentage and therefore harder to notice. Meanwhile the artefacts the item cites as symptoms are the best epistemics in the building: "read-verified by hand on 11 of the 20, grep-only on the other 9" states a denominator, a numerator, and a quality stratification in fourteen words, and every one of those properties is lost the moment it is aggregated into a coverage figure. The defect is not that these statements were never aggregated. It is that the headline numbers were never held to their standard.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** Either every reported figure must carry its denominator and its verification stratum inline — so that "eight proposals" is never sayable without "across 4 of 15 scheduled traditions; 3 Levin, 2 McGilchrist, 2 Rohr, 1 Wright; zero Carroll, zero Arkani-Hamed" — or, if a single aggregate coverage figure is adopted, it must be risk-weighted, accompanied by a named-zero list, and paired with a periodic checking-quality probe (the mutation-testing analogue) that would detect the touched-but-unchecked case. With an unweighted aggregate and neither of those, the fleet has replaced a number that obviously overclaims with one that overclaims invisibly, and has added a gameable target to a system whose producers are the same agents that report the metric.

**How to test:** (1) **The Goodhart pre-test, one cycle:** announce a coverage target internally and see whether the next cycle's proposal count rises while median proposal depth falls. If it does, the mechanism is live in this fleet and the metric should not be adopted. (2) **The touched-versus-checked probe:** take three traditions the fleet would score as covered, silently introduce a plausible error into each tradition's source material, and re-run. The proportion detected is the fleet's mutation score; the gap between it and the coverage score is the fleet's version of the 30%-to-3% figure and is the single most decision-relevant number available. (3) **The frame test, free:** ask any two agents independently to enumerate the tradition space without being shown the architecture's list of fifteen. If the lists differ, the denominator is a choice and any coverage figure computed against it is a statement about the choice.
