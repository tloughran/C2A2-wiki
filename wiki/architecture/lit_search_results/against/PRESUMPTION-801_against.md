### Sources

1. **JCGM 100:2008 (GUM), §3.1.3 and Annex D; VIM definitional-uncertainty entries.** [verified this run via the BIPM PDF of JCGM 100:2008 and the ISO/JCGM HTML edition, §2 Definitions and Annex B/D] — The strongest and most directly contrary source. The GUM states that **"a measurand cannot be completely described without an infinite amount of information,"** and that incomplete definition of the measurand contributes a component of uncertainty which "may or may not be significant **relative to the accuracy required**." Its worked example is decisive for this item: a one-metre steel bar measured to micrometre accuracy must have its temperature and pressure specified; measured to millimetre accuracy, "its specification would not require a defining temperature or pressure or a value for any other defining parameter." Metrology therefore does not ask for a canonical measurand. It asks for a specification **sufficient to the tolerance of the decision being made**, and it treats residual ambiguity as a quantifiable uncertainty component rather than as a defect to be eliminated. The item's implicit demand — one definition, all counts agreeing — is a demand metrology explicitly declines to make.
2. **Campbell & Fiske (1959), "Convergent and Discriminant Validation by the Multitrait-Multimethod Matrix," *Psychological Bulletin* 56, 81–105.** [verified this run via Wikipedia's MTMM entry, the SAGE Encyclopedia of Research Design entry, Conjointly's Research Methods Knowledge Base, and multiple citation records; the 1959 paper itself **not** consulted in primary form] — The methodological challenge. The MTMM approach rests on **multiple operationism**: triangulation among two or more operational definitions, each "approximate, fallible, and independently imperfect." Disagreement among such measures is not a defect state; it is the raw material of the validity analysis. Convergent validity is *established* by examining how measures of the same construct relate, which requires that there be several of them and that they not be identical. On this reading the fleet's seven counts are not seven errors — they are an unusually rich multi-method dataset on a single construct, and the correct next step is to analyse their structure, not to suppress six of them.
3. **Metrics-governance / semantic-layer practice** (dbt Labs, "Semantic Layer: What it is and when to adopt it"; convergent vendor and practitioner guidance on metric standardisation). [verified this run via getdbt.com and several convergent accounts; vendor tier] — The applied version of the same challenge, and it is explicit that standardisation does not mean one number. The recurring formulation is that shared decisions get one approved enterprise version while **domain variants remain available under explicit names, and clear naming protects legitimate differences**; the anti-pattern named is *hidden or undocumented* divergence, not divergence as such. The diagnostic question the practice recommends is also directly usable here: *"under which realistic condition would two informed people still calculate this differently?"* That question does not resolve to one measurand; it resolves to an enumerated list of conditions, each of which may deserve its own named counter.
4. **Jones and the NESMA/software-metrics tradition** ("Troublesome software metrics cause errors in estimates," NESMA, 2014; convergent treatments of LOC counting rules). [verified this run via nesma.org and secondary summaries; practitioner/professional tier, and the specific Boehm-1981 vs Conte-1986 counting comparison is secondary-sourced] — Evidence that the fleet's condition is the industry's normal condition, not a local pathology. The stated finding is that **"almost every software metric has multiple definitions and ambiguous counting rules,"** and the canonical illustration is that lines of code has at least two defensible counting rules — physical lines including comments and declarations (Boehm 1981) versus non-comment non-blank lines (Conte et al. 1986) — so "a single software package could have two very different LOC counts depending on which counting method is used." The same literature reports the outcome of the standardisation attempt: LOC was standardised enough to be used widely and is nonetheless "not a valid metric for measuring economic productivity." That is the cost of premature standardisation in its purest form — the field agreed on a measurand and thereby entrenched the wrong one for a generation.
5. **Repeat-findings literature in internal audit and quality management** (PCAOB remediation guidance as described by JGA CPA, "Repeat Findings: Will the PCAOB Ever Be Satisfied?" and "Remediation in a World of Repeat Findings"; SimplerQMS, "Audit Findings"; AuditHink, "Addressing Recurring Audit Findings"; Audit Findings Roundup on follow-up gaps; a 2026 ResearchGate framework paper on reducing repeat findings in operationally intensive US enterprises). [verified this run via the named sites; professional/practitioner tier throughout, the ResearchGate item consulted by title and abstract only] — The response to the queue's explicit fourth-consecutive-day question, and it is unambiguous. A **repeat finding** is "an audit observation previously identified in an earlier audit that remains unresolved or has reoccurred despite claimed corrective action," and it is treated as a signal about the *organisation's remediation process*, not about the underlying defect: "repeat findings signal a breakdown in institutional learning: the organization sees the problem, documents the problem, and discusses the problem, but does not re-engineer the process conditions that produced it." Named root causes are inadequate root-cause analysis, ineffective CAPA implementation, and missing effectiveness checks. The prescribed response is that recurrence should **escalate the finding into formal root-cause analysis** rather than be re-reported at the same severity; repeat findings are described as risk multipliers. Four consecutive detections of one defect class with no closure is, by this literature's own criteria, a finding about the detection pipeline.
6. **Alert-fatigue and habituation literature** (PMC5387195, "Effects of workload, work complexity, and repeated alerts on alert fatigue in a clinical decision support system"; provider override rates of 49–96%). [verified this run via PMC and convergent secondary sources] — The mechanism by which source 5's failure mode operates here. Habituation is a documented response to repeated identical signals, with measured consequences. A defect class re-raised daily at unchanged severity is a repeated identical signal.
7. **Construct-validity literature on operationalisation** (Conjointly Research Methods Knowledge Base; Jacobs & Wallach-style measurement-and-fairness treatments, arXiv 1912.05511). [verified this run] — Supplies the standard framing: construct validity is "the degree to which inferences can legitimately be made from the operationalizations in your study to the theoretical constructs on which those operationalizations were based," it is "always a matter of degree, to be supported by critical reasoning," and it is "not a yes/no box to be checked." This directly contradicts the item's implicit binary — that either the measurand is defined or every count is definitionally correct and mutually incompatible.

### Strength of challenge: Moderate-to-Strong

### Summary

The diagnosis is correct and well supported: a count is a construction, the fleet's counters have undocumented selection predicates, and treating disagreement as a reporting error rather than a definitional one is a real mistake. Nothing found defends the presumption. But three separate literatures challenge what the item implies should follow.

Metrology is the sharpest. The GUM states that a measurand cannot be completely described without infinite information and that incomplete definition contributes an uncertainty component whose significance is judged **relative to the accuracy required** — its own example shows that the same measurand needs temperature and pressure specified at micrometre tolerance and needs neither at millimetre tolerance. So the request for "the measurand" is not the request metrology makes. The request it makes is: state the decision, state its tolerance, then specify the measurand only as far as that tolerance demands, and carry the residue as definitional uncertainty. Applied here, "the Summa review queue is 21–35 items, definitional uncertainty ±40%, dominated by disagreement over whether X counts" is a complete and honest measurement result. Whether it is *good enough* depends entirely on what decision the number feeds — and the item does not say, which is the gap this file would most want filled.

Measurement theory challenges the item's implicit binary. Campbell and Fiske's multiple operationism treats several approximate, fallible, independently imperfect operational definitions as the method rather than the failure, and construct validity is explicitly a matter of degree rather than a box to check. Seven same-day counts of one construct is, in that frame, a triangulation dataset. The disagreement is informative: it localises the definitional uncertainty and identifies exactly which predicate is contested. Suppressing six of the seven in favour of a canonical one discards that information and replaces a known uncertainty with an unknown one.

Applied practice agrees and adds the governance form. The semantic-layer literature's position is not one number but explicit naming — approved shared versions for shared decisions, domain variants preserved under distinct names, with the anti-pattern being *hidden* divergence rather than divergence. And the software-metrics tradition supplies the cautionary tale: almost every software metric has multiple definitions and ambiguous counting rules, LOC being the canonical case with two defensible rules and radically different results — and the industry's eventual standardisation on LOC produced a widely-used metric that is, in the same literature's judgement, not valid for what it was standardised to measure. Premature standardisation does not remove definitional uncertainty; it hides it inside an agreed number.

Finally, on the queue's explicit fourth-day question, the answer is yes and it is well evidenced. Repeat findings are a recognised category with a specific meaning: not that the defect is severe, but that the remediation process is ineffective. The literature's language is directly applicable — the organisation "sees the problem, documents the problem, and discusses the problem, but does not re-engineer the process conditions that produced it" — and its prescription is that recurrence should trigger escalation into formal root-cause analysis rather than another same-severity report. The habituation literature explains why re-reporting at unchanged severity degrades rather than accumulates. Four consecutive days is, on this evidence, a finding about 14b's own pipeline, and it is the item in this file with the clearest prescribed remedy.

### Specific risks

- **Over-specification stalls the fix.** Pursuing a canonical measurand for "the queue" without first naming the decision it serves is unbounded work — the GUM says the description never terminates. The likely outcome is a long definitional document, no decision-tolerance analysis, and counts that still disagree because the document did not anticipate the contested predicate.
- **Premature standardisation entrenches the wrong construct.** LOC is the worked example: agreed, widely counted, and invalid for its headline use. If the fleet picks one of its seven counters as canonical because it is convenient rather than because it matches a decision, it converts visible disagreement into invisible error, which is strictly worse for a fleet whose "only remaining tie to reality is quantitative."
- **Information loss.** Six discarded counts are six data points about where the definitional uncertainty lives. Under a multiple-operationism reading they are the diagnostic instrument, and collapsing them removes the fleet's only means of bounding what it does not know.
- **The repeat-finding risk is now the larger one.** By the internal-audit criteria this class has recurred four times with no closure, which is a documented indicator of ineffective corrective action and a risk multiplier. The specific hazard is habituation: a fifth identical report is likely to be read less carefully than the first, and the alert-fatigue evidence puts override rates for repeated interruptive signals at 49–96%.
- **If the item is dismissed entirely**, the risk is exactly as graded: the trend lines in `metrics/` are artefacts of which script ran, and with the qualitative gate dark twelve days there is no second channel to catch it. Nothing in this file reduces that.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Invert the order: state the decision and its tolerance first, then specify only that far.** This is the GUM's own procedure and it converts an unbounded definitional task into a bounded one. If no decision currently consumes the queue count, that is the finding, and no measurand work is warranted.
(b) **Publish the range with stated definitional uncertainty.** "21–35, definitional uncertainty dominated by predicate P" is a legitimate metrological result available today at zero cost, and it is exactly what the run that "declined to arbitrate" was reaching for. That run should be treated as having done the right thing.
(c) **Name the counters rather than unify them.** Semantic-layer practice: each script's selection predicate gets written down as prose and the counter gets a distinct name. Divergence then becomes documented rather than hidden, which is the actual anti-pattern the practice targets.
(d) **Run the two-informed-people test.** "Under which realistic condition would two informed people still calculate this differently?" — enumerating those conditions is cheaper than defining the measurand and produces the same information, in the form the fleet can act on.
(e) **Treat the seven counts as an MTMM dataset before discarding any.** Compare their selection predicates pairwise; the structure of the disagreement identifies the contested predicate directly.
(f) **Escalate the class rather than re-report it.** Per the repeat-findings prescription: the fifth occurrence should not be another PRESUMPTION at the same severity. It should be a root-cause analysis of why detections in this class do not close, with an effectiveness check attached. **This is the highest-value action in this file**, because it applies to the detection pipeline that produced all four instances rather than to any one of them.
(g) **Attach an effectiveness check to whatever is done.** The named root causes of repeat findings are inadequate root-cause analysis, incomplete CAPA, and *missing effectiveness checks*. A closure criterion stated in advance — e.g. "two consecutive days on which all reported queue counts agree or carry named predicates" — is what distinguishes remediation from documentation.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-801

**Strongest counterargument:** The observation is right and the demand it implies is one that metrology itself refuses to make. The GUM says plainly that a measurand cannot be completely described without an infinite amount of information, and that the significance of an incomplete definition is judged only relative to the accuracy required — its own steel-bar example specifies temperature and pressure at micrometre tolerance and drops both at millimetre tolerance. So "no run has stated the measurand" is not automatically a defect; it is a defect exactly to the extent that some decision needs a tolerance the current specification cannot support, and nobody has said what that decision is. Without that, defining a canonical measurand is unbounded work with no acceptance criterion. Measurement theory pushes the same way from the other side: Campbell and Fiske built the dominant validation method on multiple operationism precisely because every operational definition is approximate, fallible, and imperfect, and disagreement among several is what makes validity assessable rather than what makes it fail. Seven same-day counts of one construct is a triangulation dataset that localises the ambiguity; collapsing it to one canonical number converts a *measured* uncertainty into an *unmeasured* error. Applied practice says the same: metric governance protects legitimate variants under explicit names and treats hidden divergence, not divergence, as the anti-pattern — and software engineering supplies the cautionary case, where the industry did standardise on lines of code, with two incompatible counting rules quietly persisting underneath, and ended up with a universally-counted metric its own literature calls invalid for the thing it is used to measure. The run the item faults for declining to arbitrate was therefore behaving correctly; "21–35 with definitional uncertainty concentrated in one predicate" is a complete metrological statement, available today, and better than any single number the fleet could currently justify. And then the finding the item does not make about itself: this is the fourth consecutive day of the class with nothing closed. The internal-audit literature has a name for that, and it is not a name for the defect — a repeat finding means the organisation "sees the problem, documents the problem, and discusses the problem, but does not re-engineer the process conditions that produced it," with inadequate root-cause analysis and missing effectiveness checks as its standard causes and escalation into formal root-cause analysis as its standard remedy. On its own evidence the more urgent object of inquiry today is the pipeline that has now detected this four times.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** Some decision must actually consume the queue count, with a stated tolerance; the measurand must be specified only as far as that tolerance requires, with the residue carried explicitly as definitional uncertainty rather than resolved by fiat. Every counter must carry its selection predicate in prose and a distinct name, so divergence is documented rather than hidden. And the class must acquire a closure criterion stated in advance and an effectiveness check, because without those the fifth detection will read exactly like the fourth and will be attended to less. If no decision consumes the number, the safe action is to publish the range and stop — not to define a measurand for a quantity nothing depends on.

**How to test:** **(1) Trace the consumer.** For each of the seven counts, identify what downstream artefact or decision used it. If none did, the item's risk grading is over-stated and the cheap remedy (b) suffices; if one did, its tolerance sets the specification requirement and the definitional work becomes bounded and justified. **(2) Diff the predicates.** Extract the actual selection predicate from each of the seven scripts and diff them pairwise. This is a mechanical exercise, it takes an hour, and it will produce the enumerated list of contested conditions that a definitional document would take days to approximate — and it directly tests whether the counts are answers to different questions or the same question at different times (which is PRESUMPTION-797's mechanism and would change the remedy entirely). **(3) Test the temporal hypothesis explicitly:** run all seven counters within the same minute. If they still disagree, the cause is definitional; if they converge, it was schedule position and 797's as-of-stamp fix resolves this item too. This is the single most informative experiment available and it costs one run. **(4) Repeat-finding test:** take the three prior instances of this class and check each for a stated closure criterion and an effectiveness check. If none has either, the repeat-findings diagnosis is confirmed on the fleet's own record and remedy (f) should be actioned before any further detection in this class.

## SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG

**Date:** 2026-08-15

**Affected items:** PRESUMPTION-798, PRESUMPTION-799, PRESUMPTION-800, PRESUMPTION-801 — and, by continuity with yesterday's intake, PRESUMPTION-795 and PRESUMPTION-797.

**Common vulnerability — two, interlocking:**

**(A) The documentary reflex.** All four items resolve, in the form 14b states them, toward producing a *new descriptive artefact* — a run manifest (798), a record of what monitors do and do not cover (799), a clarified contract on escalation semantics (800), a measurand definition (801). In every case the literature's actual remedy is an **executable change on the execution path**: a positive completion record emitted by the run (798), an external heartbeat that alarms on silence (799), a version token carried by each disposition write (800), a named selection predicate attached to each counter (801). The documentary form of each remedy shares one failure mode — it sits beside the system rather than inside it, nothing enforces correspondence, and it drifts. This is the same hazard flagged against PRESUMPTION-795's register and PRESUMPTION-797's dependency record, now in its fourth and fifth instances. The danger is not that the documents are useless; it is that they read as though the problem were addressed.

**(B) Absence is being used as a signal by three of the four.** 798 infers non-execution from missing output; 799 infers monitor health from a written artefact; 800 infers current state from the absence of a contrary record. Chandra & Toueg (JACM 1996) establish that in an asynchronous system no detector can distinguish a crashed process from a slow one, and that practical detectors are unreliable in both directions, possibly infinitely often. Every remedy in this intake that relies on noticing something's absence inherits that bound, and the tuning that avoids false negatives lands in the alert-fatigue regime that CDS research measures at 49–96% override.

**Literature basis:** Chandra & Toueg, "Unreliable Failure Detectors for Reliable Distributed Systems," *JACM* 43(2), 1996; Rubin's missing-data framework and the MAR/MNAR indistinguishability result; IEC 61508-6 Annex D β-factor ranges (0.005–0.05, programmable electronics) via SINTEF A26922 and NTNU CCF notes; Koopman and Ganssle on watchdog kick placement and the supervisor pattern; PCAOB-derived repeat-findings guidance on ineffective CAPA; Ancker et al. on alert override rates (PMC5387195); configuration-drift practice (New Relic, Wiz, Elastic gap documentation).

**Risk level:** High

**Recommendation:** Three things. **First**, for each of these four items, require that the proposed remedy name the *line of execution* it changes; if it names only a file to be written, treat it as unremediated. **Second**, note that 798 and 799 are closed by the **same single instrument** — one external dead-man's-switch service with a named check per scheduled run, pinged on the success path only. That is one purchase, of the order of a configuration line and a few dollars a month, and it converts two Critical items into bounded-residual ones. It should be sequenced ahead of all documentary work in this intake. **Third**, and per source 5 of this file: this intake contains the fourth consecutive instance of the 801 class and the fifth instance of the off-execution-path remedy pattern. The internal-audit literature's prescription for recurrence is escalation into formal root-cause analysis with a stated closure criterion and an effectiveness check, not another same-severity report. The detection pipeline is working; the closure pipeline is the untested component.
