  Sources:
    1. Simons, D.J. (2014), "The Value of Direct Replication," *Perspectives on Psychological Science*
       9(1):76–80, doi:10.1177/1745691613514755. — **VERIFIED at abstract level** (publisher page
       retrieved and read 2026-09-12; full text paywalled and NOT read — the $35 access wall is recorded
       as the retrieval limit). This is the strongest published defence of direct replication in the
       literature, and **its own framing is the challenge**: "If an effect is reliable, any competent
       researcher should be able to obtain it when using the same procedures with adequate statistical
       power… **direct replication by multiple laboratories is the only way to verify the reliability of
       an effect**" [quoted verbatim from the abstract]. Simons requires replication *by other
       laboratories*, and the stated reason is to ensure an effect is not due to an idiosyncratic feature
       of the original lab. The pro-replication case therefore contains its own defeater for the routed
       presumption: the entity Simons requires to vary is precisely the entity C2A2 holds fixed. Same
       instrument = same lab. On Simons's own criterion, C2A2's replicates are not direct replications at
       all; they are re-runs.
    2. Stroebe, W. & Strack, F. (2014), "The Alleged Crisis and the Illusion of Exact Replication,"
       *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 9(1):59–71, doi:10.1177/1745691613514450. — **UNVERIFIED**
       (secondary summaries only; publisher page not fetched, Groningen and Utrecht repository records
       located but not retrieved). The reported argument attacks the presumption from the opposite side:
       identical operationalisations run at different times and on different populations may test
       *different theoretical constructs*, so a repeated phenomenon is not a rigorous test of the
       hypothesis, and reinstating the original circumstances is insufficient — the replicator must
       establish that the conditions realise the theoretical variable. **Recorded as a second,
       independent-in-direction challenge but carrying no weight in the rating**, because it is
       snippet-level and because the Replicability-Index critique of this very paper also surfaced in
       search and I did not retrieve either.
    3. Podsakoff, MacKenzie, Lee & Podsakoff (2003) and MacKenzie & Podsakoff (2012) on common method
       variance / mono-method bias. — **UNVERIFIED** (snippet level; neither paper fetched). The reported
       position: common method variance is systematic error variance stemming from the common method;
       method variance *biases the estimates of reliability and validity* of the underlying constructs;
       bias arises specifically when predictor and criterion are assessed with the same instrument from
       the same respondents, producing overestimated correlations. **No figure from this source is used.**
       The structural point is what transfers and it is the load-bearing one: common-method bias is a
       *systematic* term, and systematic terms do not average out under repetition — they accumulate
       identically in every replicate. Repetition shrinks the random component and leaves the systematic
       component untouched, so the interval narrows around a possibly wrong value. This is the formal
       version of PREMISE-120's "a systematic defect reproduces exactly as reliably as a correct
       measurement."
    4. Reliability-versus-validity, standard psychometrics: Cook & Beckman (2006), "Current Concepts in
       Validity and Reliability for Psychometric Instruments," *Am J Med* 119(2):166.e7–16,
       PMID 16443422. — **UNVERIFIED** (snippet level; abstract located, full text not fetched). The
       canonical statement is that reliability is **necessary but not sufficient** for validity: an
       instrument that is perfectly consistent can consistently measure the wrong thing. The scale that
       always reads five kilograms heavy is the textbook illustration. Same-instrument replication is a
       *reliability* procedure. It supplies no validity evidence at all, and reporting it as though it
       did is a category error rather than an overstatement.

  Summary: The presumption is refuted in its routed form and the refutation is already in the register at
    High confidence, so the finding here is not that 959 is wrong but that it was answered before it was
    asked. What the search adds beyond PREMISE-120 is the shape of the error: this is not a weak inference
    but a **conflation of reliability with validity**, and the strongest *defence* of replication in the
    literature turns out to require exactly the variation C2A2 does not have. Simons argues that direct
    replication is indispensable and, in the same breath, that it must come from other laboratories,
    because the point of the exercise is to rule out idiosyncrasies of the original lab. C2A2's replicates
    hold the lab fixed and vary only the day. The common-method-variance framing gives the mechanism:
    repetition shrinks the random term and leaves the systematic term exactly where it was, so the
    confidence interval tightens around whatever the instrument's bias is. The one thing the item gets
    right, and which PREMISE-120 already protects, is that this does not make re-running worthless — it
    catches nondeterminism and drift cheaply. It just cannot be reported as strengthening.

  Specific risks:
    - **Every rate this estate has ever reported about itself is affected.** The 82%, the 10-of-10, the
      20-of-20 and the 4-of-4 in this cohort are all same-instrument replicates. If they are being read as
      mutually strengthening, the estate's confidence in its own headline finding is inflated by an amount
      nobody has bounded. PREMISE-197's default — treat as approximately one observation — would reduce
      four cycles of evidence to roughly one.
    - **The failure is silent and directional.** Systematic bias does not announce itself by producing
      disagreement; it produces *agreement*, which is exactly what the estate has been reading as
      confirmation. The more cycles run, the more confident and the more wrong the record becomes. This is
      strictly worse than noisy measurement because noise is visible.
    - **The forbidden phrase is a live hazard.** PREMISE-120 forbids "independently confirmed" absent a
      different data path. Any cycle that writes "both directions independently reached X" without
      recording the four shared channels is in breach; PREMISE-111 makes the breach a substantive error
      and not a wording one.
    - **A wrong remedy is available and attractive.** The cheap response — "run it again next cycle" — is
      precisely the move the literature excludes, and it will produce a fifth agreeing replicate that
      makes the position look stronger while adding nothing.

  Mitigations available:
    - **Record the four channels on every claimed second check**, per PREMISE-120's binding requirement:
      code path, corpus, model, execution context. This is a one-line addition to each result file and it
      is currently not done. Where all four are shared, the file must say "single channel, two labels."
    - **Tag every self-measured rate UNCALIBRATED** until an external baseline or seeded denominator is
      attached (PREMISE-124). This costs a word and removes the inflation.
    - **Buy one genuinely disjoint replicate.** Under PREMISE-096 the corroborating layer must draw on a
      disjoint evidence source. The cheapest available disjoint reader here is a human: have Tom score
      five of the twenty pre-answer judgements independently. Five human scores against the pipeline's
      twenty is a calibration sample, and it is the only replicate in reach that varies the laboratory.
    - **Where a disjoint replicate is unobtainable, say the quantity is unreplicated rather than
      replicated.** "Measured four times by one instrument" is honest and is materially different
      information from "replicated four times."
    - **Do not let the concession be lost.** Re-running the same instrument still catches nondeterminism,
      drift and transcription error. That is a real and cheap defect class and the correct claim to make
      from a same-instrument replicate — the only one.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-959
    Strongest counterargument: The instrumentation objection, pressed to its conclusion, forbids the
      estate from learning anything at all, and that consequence should be suspected rather than embraced.
      There exists no second instrument. 14a/14b/15a/15b are the only apparatus available; a rule that
      same-instrument replication adds nothing is a rule that this system may never accumulate evidence
      about itself, which converts an epistemic caution into a permanent gag. Simons's "different
      laboratories" requirement is calibrated to a field where the threat is undisclosed analytic
      flexibility and file-drawering by a motivated original author — neither of which describes an agent
      re-running a register comparison on a new cohort. More pointedly, the four replicates are NOT
      re-runs of one measurement: each cycle drew a **different set of items** and compared them against a
      **register that had itself grown** between cycles. The data are new each time; only the reader is
      constant. That is closer to a single laboratory measuring a new sample each week — the ordinary
      condition of almost all field science — than to re-analysing one dataset four times, and no serious
      methodologist holds that a lab's second sample is worthless because it is the same lab. And the
      systematic-bias argument cuts both ways: if the instrument has a bias that manufactures
      pre-answering, that bias should be *nameable*, and nobody in either direction has named a mechanism
      by which 14a/14b would spuriously match items to premises. An unnamed bias is not a finding; it is
      the reference-class problem, which is universal to every inductive claim (PREMISE-135 already
      refuses this as a defeater by name: "an unspecified reference class is not a discriminating
      criticism of any particular replacement").
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the new-sample reading must hold — each cycle's
      items must be genuinely new and not re-derived from the prior cycle's residue, which is checkable
      in one pass and has not been checked; (b) a candidate bias mechanism must be named and tested, not
      merely feared, or the objection is the reference-class problem in a costume; (c) the *reader*
      constancy must be shown not to be load-bearing — the five-item human calibration does this; (d) in
      the meantime the quantity is reported with its channel disclosure and an UNCALIBRATED tag, which
      costs nothing and is compatible with every word of this steelman.
    How to test: two in-house checks, both cheap, and they separate the steelman from the challenge.
      **(i) Item-disjointness.** Verify that the four cycles' item sets are disjoint and that no item was
      re-surfaced from a prior cycle. If they are disjoint, the "new sample each week" reading is
      sustained and the challenge weakens to the reader-constancy limb alone. **(ii) Reader calibration.**
      Give five previously-scored items to a disjoint reader (Tom, or a model from a different family if
      one is reachable) with the covering premise withheld, and ask whether the register pre-answers each.
      Concordance near 5/5 bounds the instrument's bias small and the steelman holds; concordance near
      chance means the pre-answer rate is an artefact of the reader and four cycles of evidence collapse
      to zero. This is the single highest-value measurement named anywhere in this cohort and it takes
      under an hour.
