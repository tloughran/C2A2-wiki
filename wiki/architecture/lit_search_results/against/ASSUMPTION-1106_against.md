### Polarity note — what I am challenging

The item is worded as the **defective belief**. The proposition I searched AGAINST is 14a's implied corrective, which I decompose into four clauses because they do not stand or fall together:

- **(A) "A `pass` recorded by a check that did not execute is unwarranted."** Searched disconfirmatorily and **found nothing against it**. Laboratory validity-control doctrine says the same thing in stronger terms. I concede this fully and say so plainly.
- **(B) "Therefore the inexecutable check should FAIL."** **Challenged, and this is the load-bearing failure.** Every domain the brief named prescribes a *third terminal state* — invalid / indeterminate / error — with a repeat-or-repair path, and explicitly forbids collapsing it into either pole.
- **(C) "The pass/warn/fail severity schema is the defect."** **Partially challenged.** The measured instance's proximate cause is a splitter that returns zero sentences on unpunctuated ASR. That is an instrument defect with a cheap targeted fix; a schema change leaves it unrepaired and turns 66 days red without making one of them checkable.
- **(D) The inference from 66/307, and the claim that it "generalises."** **Challenged.** The measurement establishes *coverage*, not *harm*, and one guard on one corpus on one day does not license a general severity-schema finding.

### Challenging evidence found: Partial — none against clause (A), strong against clause (B), moderate against (C) and (D)

### Sources

1. **Laboratory validity-control doctrine — the invalid run is rejected and repeated, not reported.** [SNIPPET LEVEL — APHL *Laboratory Test Verification and Validation Toolkit* (qualitative assays); Sekisui OSOM package insert (POSITIVE / NEGATIVE / **INVALID** with internal procedural controls); Microbiologics technical note on positive controls; search returns read, primary standards not fetched this run] — "If no detectable positive control response is produced... the positive control is interpreted as failed, and the preceding diagnostic test would be considered **invalid**"; "if controls fail to produce the expected results, the run must be **rejected**, and the failure should be investigated"; invalid controls invalidate "all control levels tested in that control set," which are then "successfully retested prior to testing samples." **This is the strongest source in the file and it cuts both ways.** Against the item's *statement*: a `pass` under a failed validity control is categorically inadmissible, so clause (A) is right and I found nothing against it. Against the item's *corrective*: the prescribed disposition is a **named third state (INVALID) with a mandatory repeat**, not a failure verdict. Note the assay result sheets literally print three outcomes. The schema the item is arguing for exists in the reference domain, and it is not binary.
2. **IGRA indeterminate handling — indeterminate is explicitly not a positive, and the follow-up is risk-stratified.** [SNIPPET LEVEL — CDC "Clinical Testing Guidance for Tuberculosis: Interferon Gamma Release Assay"; systematic review/meta-analysis of indeterminate IGRA results (PMC10225525); PMC12279152 on indeterminate QFT-Plus during COVID-19; **CANONICAL — NOT re-verified**] — inadequate mitogen (i.e. positive-control) response accounts for ~89–96% of indeterminate results; the disposition is "high-risk patients should receive either a repeat QFT test with a new blood specimen or a tuberculin skin test, while **low-risk patients require no further testing**"; on repeat, "50% converted to a negative result within 30 days." Three challenges to clause (B) at once: (i) indeterminate results are *specifically not treated as positive*; (ii) the follow-up is **conditional on prior risk**, not uniform escalation; (iii) roughly half of indeterminates resolve benignly on repeat, so a fail-closed rule would have converted a large majority of them into false alarms.
3. **Sadowski, C., Aftandilian, E., Eagle, A., Miller-Cushon, L., & Jaspan, C. (2018). "Lessons from Building Static Analysis Tools at Google." *Communications of the ACM* 61(4).** [SNIPPET LEVEL — CACM landing page and Google SWE-book ch. 20 (Tricorder) summaries read; full text not fetched] — the operative threshold: code-review checks "should produce **less than 10% effective false positives**," and crucially, "if an analysis reports an actual fault but the developer did not understand it and took no action, that is considered an **effective** false positive." Analyzers above ~10% "were routinely dismissed or disabled." This is the direct cost argument against clause (B): converting the inert state to a hard failure on **21.5%** of the corpus puts the guard at more than double Google's disable threshold, on a signal that by construction carries no finding — the worst possible effective-FP profile, because there is nothing for a reader to act on.
4. **Bessey, A., Block, K., Chelf, B., Chou, A., Fulton, B., Hallem, S., Henri-Gros, C., Kamsky, A., McPeak, S., & Engler, D. (2010). The generalisation the item wants to draw — always fail on non-execution — is precisely the design Coverity rejected commercially, on evidence, over a decade of deployment.
5. **Alert-fatigue measurement in clinical decision support.** [SNIPPET LEVEL — AHRQ PSNet "Alert Fatigue" primer; Ancker et al. / JAMIA-lineage override studies; Wilson et al. 2026 systematic review PDF surfaced in search; **CANONICAL — NOT re-verified**] — documented override rates of **49–96%**, and the safety inversion stated outright: "a proliferation of alerts that are intended to improve safety actually results in a **paradoxical increase** in the chance patients will be harmed," because clinicians ignore "both the bothersome, clinically meaningless alarms **and** the critical alerts." Note this is the register's own PREMISE-131 lineage running the other way: 131 says an undelivered warning is worth zero; this literature says an over-delivered one is worth less than zero. Clause (B) trades the first failure for the second.
6. **pytest skip / xfail semantics — the "cannot execute" state is a deliberate non-failure in the reference test harness.** [SNIPPET LEVEL — docs.pytest.org "How to use skip and xfail to deal with tests that cannot succeed," multiple versions read via search returns] — "A **skip** means that you expect your test to pass unless the environment (e.g. wrong Python interpreter, missing dependency) prevents it to run"; skips and xfails are counted and listed **separately**, and "both XFAIL and XPASS don't fail the test suite by default." The brief asked whether the "not applicable" state exists in test harnesses. It does, it is universal, it is deliberate — and the design point that matters for C2A2 is the one the item is closest to being right about: the harness **counts and lists them separately**. The defect at C2A2 is not that skips exist; it is that a skip was folded into `last_qc_outcome: pass` instead of being counted in its own column.

The analogue for the guard is a splitter that falls back to token-window segmentation on zero-sentence input; that converts 66 inert days into 66 checked days, which no severity-schema change does.

### Summary

Searching against this item produced a split result of a familiar shape. The item's *observation* is correct and the reference domains are harsher about it than the item is: in clinical-laboratory practice a run whose validity control failed is **invalid** — rejected, investigated, retested — and reporting it as a result at all is a defect, so `last_qc_outcome: pass` on 66 transcripts is inadmissible and I found nothing anywhere that defends it. But the same sources refute the remedy the item implies. Every domain named in the search angle has already faced "the check could not execute" and has answered it the same way: a **named third terminal state** with a conditional follow-up path. Assay reports print POSITIVE / NEGATIVE / INVALID. IGRA guidance treats indeterminate as explicitly *not* positive and stratifies follow-up by prior risk, with roughly half of indeterminates resolving to negative on repeat. pytest counts skips and xfails in their own columns and by design does not fail the suite on them. None of these is "warnings do not fail" and none of them is "make it fail" — they are the third option the item's binary framing does not have room for. The quantitative case against fail-closed is also unusually specific: Google's Tricorder programme fixes the developer-trust threshold at **under 10% effective false positives** and reports that analyzers above it get disabled, while clinical decision support with override rates of **49–96%** produces a documented *paradoxical increase* in harm. Firing a blocking failure on 21.5% of inputs, carrying no finding, sits far outside both envelopes. Two further weaknesses are in the item's own evidence. First, the proximate cause is a sentence splitter that returns zero sentences on unpunctuated ASR — an instrument defect with a cheap targeted repair, and a severity-schema change leaves it in place while turning 66 days red without making one of them checkable. Second, 66/307 measures **coverage**, not harm: nothing in the item establishes that any of those 66 transcripts contained a fabrication, and until the base rate is known the finding is "the guard was inert," not "the days were unprotected in a way that mattered." Finally — and this is the file's largest finding — the register pre-check the intake declares it skipped returns the answer already minted, in three parts. **PREMISE-142** (2026-08-05) holds that "the output vocabulary **must be three-valued: SUCCEEDED / FAILED / NO-EVIDENCE**, and every status claim must carry an evidence pointer," because "a binary vocabulary forces the aggregator to render an absence as a positive assurance" — which is 1106's finding verbatim, twelve days earlier, from a different instrument, with the third state already named. **PREMISE-118** supplies the follow-up obligation (contain / assess impact / fix cause / verify, with retrospective impact assessment) and its bound: "the obligation is **assessment, not automatic invalidation**." **PREMISE-131** supplies the warning-is-not-a-control doctrine. The general claim is therefore already held on all three axes; the measurement is the genuinely new content, and it is worth more than the doctrine wrapped around it.

### Specific risks

**The alarm-inversion risk, and it is the main one.** If clause (B) is adopted and inexecutable checks become failures, the fleet acquires a red signal on 21.5% of a corpus that carries no finding. The clinical evidence is that this does not produce vigilance; it produces override, and the overrides generalise to the alerts that *do* carry findings. PREMISE-131's own lineage warns about the delivery stage; this is the same channel destroyed from the other end.

**The unrepaired-instrument risk.** A schema change is a governance action that feels like a fix and is not one. After it, the splitter still returns zero sentences on unpunctuated ASR, the anti-fabrication guard still checks nothing on those 66 days, and the fleet has traded a false `pass` for a true-but-uninformative `fail`. Coverage is unchanged at 78.5%.

**The base-rate risk.** The item's headline generalises from a coverage measurement to a claim about protection. If the fabrication base rate on unpunctuated-ASR days is near zero — plausible, since those transcripts are the ones with the least fabricable structure — then the realised harm from the inert guard is near zero and the Critical grading is mis-set. If it is high, the item is understated. Neither is known, and the item does not say so.

**The generalisation risk.** "This is the day's lead finding and **generalises**" is asserted from one guard, one corpus, one audit. The register already holds (PREMISE-011 family) that thresholds are set under stated conditions; a severity-schema change is a fleet-wide threshold change licensed here by n=1 instrument.

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** The pytest evidence contains the item's real point and I want it recorded without hedging: harnesses **count skips separately**. Folding a non-execution into `pass` is not a defensible design in any domain I searched, and 66 days of it is a genuine, measured, currently-live defect. Dismissing 1106 because its remedy is wrong would discard a correct finding.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Add the third state rather than changing the polarity of the second.** `last_qc_outcome` takes a fourth value — `inapplicable` / `invalid` — and every reported `pass` rate is published with its denominator, per PREMISE-168's yield-without-denominator holding. This is the assay-report design, the pytest design, and the register's own PREMISE-047 "surface staleness explicitly" design, all agreeing.
(b) **Repair the splitter first and re-measure.** Token-window fallback on zero-sentence input; re-run the guard over the 66 transcripts; report how many fabrications it then finds. That single number decides whether the Critical grading survives, and it is the cheapest experiment available.
(c) **Stratify the follow-up rather than uniformly escalating it**, per the IGRA pattern: an inert guard on a low-risk artefact is logged; on a load-bearing artefact it blocks. This preserves the fail-closed intuition exactly where it earns its cost.
(d) **Set an explicit inertness budget with an alarm on the rate, not on the instance.** One inert day is a log line; 21.5% is an incident. This puts the signal where the information actually is and keeps the per-item channel clean, consistent with the <10% effective-FP envelope.
(e) **Do not mint 1106 as a new premise; apply PREMISE-142 to the QC field.** The three-valued vocabulary already exists in the register and was minted for exactly this failure mode. `last_qc_outcome` takes SUCCEEDED / FAILED / **NO-EVIDENCE** plus an evidence pointer (which transcript, which timestamp, which check). Where 1106 adds anything, add it as a clause to PREMISE-131 (a warning is not a control, *and an inexecutable control is not a control either*), not as a standalone near-duplicate. PREMISE-138 bars re-minting.
(g) **Discharge PREMISE-118 explicitly.** The obligation on discovering an out-of-tolerance instrument is contain / assess impact / fix cause / verify, with a retrospective impact assessment over every result since last known-good calibration — and the bounding clause says the obligation is *assessment, not automatic invalidation*. Mitigation (b) above is that assessment. Until it is run, both "the 66 days are fine" and "the 66 days are compromised" are unsupported.
(f) **Record the OPEN-153 gate defect once.** This is the fifth cohort item whose remedy was drafted against a register that already held the answer. That is now a pattern, not an incident.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** ASSUMPTION-1106

**Strongest counterargument:** The item has diagnosed a real defect and then reached for the one remedy its own reference domains have already tried and rejected. Concede the diagnosis first, because it is not small and nothing in this file touches it: clinical laboratories treat a run whose validity control failed as **invalid** — rejected, investigated, retested — and reporting such a run as a result is itself the error, so 66 transcripts carrying `last_qc_outcome: pass` under an inert guard is inadmissible on any account. But look at what those same laboratories actually print on the report. Three outcomes: POSITIVE, NEGATIVE, **INVALID**. Not two. The IGRA guidance is more explicit still — an indeterminate result is specifically *not* treated as positive, the follow-up is conditioned on the patient's prior risk rather than applied uniformly, and about half of indeterminates convert to negative on a simple repeat, so a fail-closed rule would have manufactured false alarms out of most of them. pytest, which the brief nominated as the test-harness reference, has had this exact state since its beginning: a skip means "the environment prevents this from running," skips and xfails are **counted and listed separately**, and by deliberate design they do not fail the suite. The convergence across three unrelated domains is the argument: none of them says "warnings do not fail" and none of them says "make it fail" — all three say *name the state and give it its own column and its own follow-up path*. The item's framing has no room for that third option, and so it is forced into the binary that the evidence rejects. The cost side is quantified and it is severe. Google's Tricorder programme sets developer trust at **under 10% effective false positives**, where an "effective" false positive includes a true finding the reader did not act on, and reports that analyzers above that line get disabled outright; clinical decision support running at **49–96% override** produces a documented *paradoxical increase* in harm, because the ignoring generalises from the noise to the signal. A blocking failure on 21.5% of a corpus, carrying no finding by construction, is worse than either envelope allows. And the proximate cause is not the schema at all: a sentence splitter returns zero sentences on unpunctuated ASR. Change the schema and the splitter is still broken, the guard still checks nothing on those days, and coverage is still 78.5% — the fleet will have converted a false `pass` into a true `fail` and learned nothing new about a single transcript. Two further gaps sit in the item's own evidence. 66/307 is a **coverage** measurement; it establishes that the guard was inert, not that any of those days contained a fabrication, and without that base rate the Critical grading is unanchored in either direction. And "this generalises" is asserted from one guard on one corpus on one day. Finally, the register already holds the doctrine: **PREMISE-131**, ACTIVE, externally anchored, High confidence — a warning is not a control, an undelivered warning has *zero* effect rather than reduced effect, and the admissible mitigations are elimination or an interlock, not a notice. The item's general claim is a re-mint of a premise that also prescribes a different remedy than the item implies.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** Three things, and they are separable. (i) The **third state must exist and be counted** — `inapplicable`/`invalid` as a first-class outcome, with every published `pass` rate carrying its denominator, so that "the guard did not run" can never again be read off as "the guard found nothing." (ii) The **escalation must be conditional, not uniform** — blocking where the artefact is load-bearing, logged where it is not, with the rate rather than the instance carrying the alarm; this keeps the channel inside the <10% effective-FP envelope that determines whether anyone still reads it. (iii) The **instrument must be repaired**, because a severity change that leaves the splitter broken does not increase coverage by one transcript. If all three hold, the item's concern is fully addressed and the literature's objections do not bite. If only the schema changes, the fleet has taken on the alert-fatigue failure mode in exchange for the silent-inertness one, and the reference domains say that is a bad trade.

**How to test:** (1) **The base-rate run, and it is decisive.** Repair the splitter with a token-window fallback, re-run the anti-fabrication guard over the 66 previously-inert transcripts, and count the fabrications found. Zero means the realised harm was zero and the Critical grading is mis-set; non-zero gives the exact cost of fifteen days of inertness. Hours of work, and it settles the item's severity in one number. (2) **The override experiment.** Ship the third state in log-only form for two weeks and measure how often a human reads an `invalid` line before acting. If the read rate is near zero, the state is decorative and only an interlock will do — which is PREMISE-131's own conclusion arrived at empirically. (3) **The generalisation check.** Enumerate every other check in the fleet and measure, for each, the fraction of inputs on which it cannot execute. If 1106 is a general finding, other guards will show non-trivial inertness rates; if only this one does, it is a splitter bug wearing a doctrine's clothes. (4) **The denominator audit.** Grep every published QC rate for a stated denominator. Any rate without one is already in violation of the register's existing yield-figure holding, independent of this item.

**Note on the systemic flag:** this item is one of four covered by the **SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG dated 2026-08-17** (batch A), filed at `lit_search_results/against/SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG_2026-08-17.md`. Common vulnerability 1: ASSUMPTION-1106, 1108, 1112 and 1117 are **one mechanism on four instruments** — an instrument that did not perform its check emits an outcome indistinguishable from one that performed it and found nothing — and PREMISE-142 already contains the mechanism, the three-valued vocabulary and the reason.
