  Challenging evidence found: Partial

  Sources:
    1. APA Dictionary of Psychology, "criterion contamination"; CASRAI/Simply Psychology,
       "Criterion Validity" — establish the general psychometric principle that a validation is
       only as good as the independence between predictor and criterion; a criterion that shares
       the predictor's contamination source invalidates the validation. This is directly
       consistent with (not against) the claim — it is the orthodox mainstream position, not a
       counter to it.
    2. Common Method Bias literature (Podsakoff et al., summarized in Annual Review of
       Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior, "Common Method Bias: It's Bad,
       It's Complex, It's Widespread, and It's Not Easy to Fix," 2022) — notes that method bias
       is present in nearly all measurement to some degree, and that validity is treated in
       practice as a matter of degree (convergent/discriminant/incremental validity assessed
       across multiple imperfect indicators) rather than a binary "validated / not validated"
       property. This offers the closest available push-back: instruments are routinely used
       and trusted despite known, uncorrected shared-method limitations, provided triangulating
       evidence exists — so "not validated" (binary, absolute) overstates what the field
       actually does with imperfect control sets.

  Strength of challenge: Weak

  Summary: This claim is close to a definitional/methodological truism in measurement theory
  (construct validity, criterion contamination) rather than an empirical claim, so there is
  little genuine literature that contradicts it directly — most sources found reinforce it. The
  only available push-back is pragmatic rather than theoretical: real-world validation practice
  treats "validity" as continuous and repairable via triangulation (multiple partially-independent
  indicators), not as an all-or-nothing property that a single shared blind spot destroys
  outright. The claim's use of "is not validated" is stronger/more binary than how the
  measurement literature actually operationalizes validity failure.

  Specific risks: If C2A2 treats any discovered shared blind spot as fully invalidating a probe
  (all-or-nothing), it may over-discard instruments that are still partially informative via
  other validated dimensions, when the correct response (per common-method-bias literature) is
  to add an independent triangulating check rather than discard the instrument.

  Mitigations available: Reframe "not validated" as "validity claim does not extend to the
  blind-spot region" — narrow the scope of trust rather than zeroing it out — and add a
  triangulating control drawn from a genuinely independent source/mechanism.

  Search scope: Preliminary — 1 targeted search plus reliance on general construct-validity/
  common-method-bias knowledge. This claim is largely a restatement of orthodox measurement
  theory (Cronbach & Meehl 1955 construct validity; Campbell & Fiske 1959 multitrait-multimethod)
  and a genuine disconfirming search is unlikely to surface much more without exhaustive
  psychometrics literature review, which was not performed here.

  Recommendation: NO-CHALLENGE-FOUND

STEELMAN:
  Item: ASSUMPTION-1600
  Strongest counterargument: Even granting the logic is sound, "not validated" is too absolute:
  measurement science treats validity as continuous and repairable through triangulation, and
  in practice near-total independence between predictor and criterion is rarely achievable, so by
  a strict reading of this claim almost no real-world instrument would ever count as "validated."
  The claim proves too much if taken literally.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The claim would need to be read narrowly — as
  "the validation does not extend to cases within the shared blind spot" rather than "the
  instrument is globally invalid" — and C2A2 would need a mechanism to add independent
  triangulating checks for the specific blind-spot region rather than discarding the probe
  wholesale.
  How to test: Identify a second, mechanistically independent control set that does not share
  the original blind spot and check whether the probe's verdicts still hold there; if they do,
  the instrument retains partial validity outside the blind spot even though the claim, read
  literally, would call it invalidated.
