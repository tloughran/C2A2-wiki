  Sources:
    1. Boyd, R. 1979. "Metaphor and theory change: What is 'metaphor' a metaphor for?" In A. Ortony (ed.), Metaphor and Thought, Cambridge University Press, ch. 21. — The direct challenge to the presumption's ordering. Boyd argues that some metaphors are theory-constitutive: they are essential to the statement of a scientific theory and are introduced precisely where no antecedent theoretical vocabulary exists, in order to provide epistemic access to a phenomenon whose structure is not yet known. Their referential imprecision is not a defect peculiar to metaphor but a general feature of referring terms, and it is resolved by subsequent inquiry rather than required in advance. On this account, borrowed vocabulary legitimately bears theoretical weight before the structural correspondence is established - the correspondence is what the borrowing is for. The presumption's rule inverts the documented order of scientific concept formation.
    2. Humphreys, P. 2004. Extending Ourselves: Computational Science, Empiricism, and Scientific Method. Oxford University Press; and Humphreys, P. 2019. "Knowledge transfer across scientific disciplines." Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part A 77: 112-119. DOI 10.1016/j.shpsa.2017.11.001. — What transfers across domains is often not a model but a template: a mathematical or computational form whose construction assumptions can be stated explicitly. Humphreys argues specifically that template application "can be made explicit without relying on similarity relations and tacit knowledge" - which is a direct challenge to the presumption's framing, since it means a transfer can be licensed by shared formal structure even where the domain-level similarity check that PREMISE-134 demands would fail or be inapplicable. "Reference frame" is not a bare word: it names a coordinate system plus a transformation rule. If PRS triplets are in fact indexed and composed the way a frame is, the transfer is template-licensed and the shared term is a symptom of the shared form, not a substitute for it.
    3. Knuuttila, T. & Loettgers, A. 2014. "Magnets, Spins, and Neurons: The Dissemination of Model Templates Across Disciplines." The Monist 97(3): 280-300. — The canonical empirical counterexample. The authors trace the Ising model from magnetism into the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick spin-glass model and then into the Hopfield model of associative memory, showing that basic ideas and mathematical methods transferred successfully from magnetic systems into neuroscience despite the absence of any object-level similarity between magnets and neurons. If a domain-similarity gate of the kind PREMISE-134 imposes had been applied to Hopfield, it would have blocked one of the most productive transfers in computational neuroscience. This is a documented case where borrowing preceded and enabled a genuine correspondence, which is exactly the pattern the presumption treats as a warning sign.
    4. Dunbar, K. 1995. "How scientists really reason: Scientific reasoning in real-world laboratories." In R.J. Sternberg & J. Davidson (eds.), The Nature of Insight, MIT Press, pp. 365-395. — In vivo observation of working molecular biology and immunology laboratories, coded sentence by sentence. Analogical reasoning is not an occasional heuristic but the dominant mode: local analogies are used continuously to solve experimental problems and regional analogies to build theory, with analogical reasoning occupying the majority of the coded reasoning in laboratory meetings. The descriptive base rate challenges the presumption's implicit cost model: a rule that treats analogy-routed inference as presumptively defective until a structure-mapping check is passed would, applied at the observed frequency of analogy use, suppress most of the reasoning that empirically produces results.
    5. Chalmers, D.J., French, R.M. & Hofstadter, D.R. 1992. "High-level perception, representation, and analogy: A critique of artificial intelligence methodology." Journal of Experimental and Theoretical Artificial Intelligence 4(3): 185-211. — Attacks the methodology PREMISE-134 rests on, and names the Structure-Mapping Engine as its target. Their argument is that SME and comparable models start from hand-coded representations and therefore presuppose the very thing at issue; in real cognition, representation-building and analogical mapping are interleaved, and representations "appropriate to a given context" are built up flexibly during the mapping attempt rather than fixed before it. This is a specific problem for the presumption's in-house adjunct, which asks that alignable relations and non-alignable differences be named BEFORE the Hawkins claim is treated as load-bearing. If the critique is right, no such pre-mapping representation of a PRS triplet or a cortical reference frame exists to be compared; the comparison is what would produce it. Applying the check as a gate is therefore question-begging in a way that applying it as ongoing bookkeeping is not.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The presumption's caution is real and nothing found refutes it: a term borrowed years ago can harden into an identity claim, and a thalamic-transform mechanism result does not automatically bear on a Problem-Research-Solution triplet. What the literature challenges is the inferential rule the presumption applies. Boyd's account of theory-constitutive metaphor holds that borrowed vocabulary is introduced precisely before the correspondence is established, to provide epistemic access, with reference fixed by later inquiry - so "routed through a shared term rather than an established structural correspondence" describes the normal order of scientific concept formation rather than a diagnosis of failure. Humphreys' template account shows that transfer can be licensed by shared formal structure with the construction assumptions stated explicitly and without appeal to domain similarity at all, which means the PREMISE-134 check is not the only available warrant; and "reference frame" carries formal content (coordinate system plus transformation rule) that could be the thing transferring. Knuuttila and Loettgers supply the counterexample that makes this concrete: the Ising template moved from magnets to spin glasses to Hopfield associative memory, and a domain-similarity gate would have blocked it. Dunbar's in vivo data show analogy is the majority mode of reasoning in productive laboratories, so a presumptive-defect rule applied at that frequency has a large suppression cost. Finally, Chalmers, French and Hofstadter attack structure-mapping methodology directly on the ground that it assumes fixed pre-mapping representations, which is the assumption the presumption's "name the alignable relations first" adjunct requires. The net result: the demand to distinguish borrowed vocabulary from established correspondence is sound as a grading exercise and unsupported as a precondition.

  Specific risks: If the presumption is over-read into a gate, C2A2 acquires a rule that no claim from a source tradition may bear weight until a structure-mapping check has been passed - a rule that the Hopfield case shows would have blocked a genuinely productive transfer, that Dunbar's data show would apply to most of the reasoning the system actually does, and that Chalmers/French/Hofstadter argue cannot be executed in the order it demands because the representations it compares do not exist prior to the comparison. The system would also be forced into inconsistency: the reference-frame vocabulary is the architecture's central organising metaphor, so a gate strict enough to block the thalamic-transform inference is strict enough to invalidate the architecture's own descriptive vocabulary, which it cannot abandon. If the presumption is under-read, the named hazard is live: a mechanism claim about thalamic transforms is imported as architectural warrant on the strength of a term the project itself chose, the falsifier offered tests the Hawkins claim rather than the transfer, and the reflexive scope-extension of PREMISE-127 is violated on the day it was adopted.

  Mitigations available: Convert the check from a gate into graded bookkeeping, which is what the analogy literature actually prescribes. (a) Apply Hesse's positive/negative/neutral partition rather than a pass/fail alignment test: record which relations align between a cortical reference frame and a PRS triplet, which are known not to align, and which are undetermined - and require that the neutral set be non-empty and revisited, rather than requiring the negative set be empty before the claim may be used. (b) Ask the template question instead of the similarity question, per Humphreys: state the construction assumptions under which a PRS triplet would be a frame - is there a coordinate-like index, a composition rule, a transformation that preserves something? If those can be written down, the transfer is template-licensed and the shared term is downstream of shared form; if they cannot, the presumption is vindicated on grounds stronger than terminology. (c) Grade the load-bearing claim rather than admitting or excluding it: mark the thalamic-transform inference as template-unverified and let it inform without warranting, which preserves the productive use Dunbar documents while keeping the reification risk visible. (d) Test the transfer directly, not just the source claim: the current falsifier is a prediction about thalamic activity, which tests Hawkins and is silent on C2A2. A transfer-level falsifier is needed - some prediction about PRS behaviour that follows from the frame reading and not from the triplet's ordinary properties.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-572
    Strongest counterargument (against the presumption): Scientific vocabulary is routinely borrowed before the correspondence that justifies it is established - Boyd's theory-constitutive metaphors exist precisely to give epistemic access to a structure not yet characterised, with reference fixed by later inquiry - so "shared term, not established correspondence" is a description of how theoretical concepts are formed rather than evidence that an inference is bad. Moreover, similarity is not the only licence for transfer: Humphreys shows that formal templates move between domains with their construction assumptions made explicit and no similarity relation invoked, and Knuuttila and Loettgers document the Ising model travelling from magnets to spin glasses to Hopfield associative memory, a transfer that a domain-similarity gate would have refused and that turned out to found a research programme. Empirically, Dunbar's in vivo studies find analogical reasoning to be the majority mode in laboratories that produce results, so treating it as presumptively defective has a large and unpriced suppression cost. And the check itself is contested: Chalmers, French and Hofstadter argue that structure-mapping models presuppose hand-coded representations, and that representation and mapping are built together - which means the demand to name alignable relations before the comparison asks for something that only the comparison can produce.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: either (i) the construction assumptions under which a PRS triplet counts as a reference frame can be written down explicitly - an index, a composition rule, a preserved transformation - in which case the transfer is template-licensed and does not depend on the shared word; or (ii) the thalamic-transform claim is marked as informing but not warranting, so it shapes hypotheses without entering as architectural justification; and in either case (iii) a transfer-level falsifier exists, distinct from the falsifier that tests Hawkins.
    How to test: write the two representations side by side and attempt the template statement before attempting the similarity judgement. Specify what a cortical reference frame does formally (coordinate assignment, transformation under movement, composition of frames) and ask whether the PRS triplet supports each operation. If all three map, the shared term was tracking shared form and the presumption's terminological objection dissolves. If one or two map, record the residue as negative analogy and downgrade the claim from load-bearing to heuristic. If none map, the presumption is confirmed on structural grounds, which is a stronger result than confirming it on the grounds that the term was borrowed. Separately, derive one prediction about PRS behaviour that follows from the frame reading and not from the triplet's ordinary properties, and check it - a transfer that generates no distinctive prediction is decorative regardless of how the vocabulary arose.
