  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The literature does not refute the claim, but it does refuse to grant it the settled
    status the brief's phrase "externally corroborated" implies. Three distinct problems surface.
    First, there is a live and undecided rival account — Bird's epistemic view — on which progress
    just is the accumulation of knowledge, i.e. exactly the "banked facts" the claim demotes; the
    Bird/Rowbottom exchange shows this is contested, not resolved. Second, both source traditions
    are criticised on the specific ground the brief anticipated: Laudan's model founders on the
    individuation-and-counting of problems (SEP, Scientific Progress), and Lakatos's demarcation
    admits no instantaneous rationality and no definitive attribution of degeneration, so the
    criterion is retrospective and in that sense untestable in the moment it would be needed;
    Branahl 2024/2025 goes further and argues the dichotomy has definitional gaps requiring a third
    category. Third, there is a counterexample class: the string/multiverse programme generates
    questions prolifically and is nonetheless assessed as degenerative for producing no novel
    testable content, and Chu & Evans show at scale that a field's volume of activity predicts
    ossification rather than turnover. The most consequential finding is negative: I found no
    empirical study operationalising question-generation as a measured predictor of field
    productivity, so the empirical leg of this claim appears to be absent rather than contested.

  Specific risks: If the claim is false, or true only in a weak non-exclusive form, then the PRS
    Problem slot is measuring the wrong thing. A C2A2 register that scores a research programme
    highly because it emits many open questions will (a) reward ad hoc proliferation, which in
    Lakatos's own terms is the signature of degeneration rather than progress, and (b) systematically
    under-credit consolidation phases where a programme is converting questions into stable results
    — precisely the episodes Bird's account counts as progress. Because the criterion is only
    retrospectively applicable, any live scoring the pipeline produces is unfalsifiable at the time
    it is produced, which means the slot can never be shown to have been wrong within the run.
    Secondary risk: the claim is currently carried as "externally corroborated" when what exists
    externally is an open philosophical dispute plus an empirical gap.

  Mitigations available:
    - Drop the exclusive disjunction. State the claim as "question-generation is one marker of
      programme health, alongside knowledge accumulation," which is compatible with both Laudan and
      Bird (Dellsén, "Scientific Progress: Four Accounts").
    - Add the novel-prediction qualifier Lakatos actually required: count only questions whose
      answers would be independently testable, not questions generated by protective-belt repair
      (SEP, "Imre Lakatos").
    - Adopt Branahl's third category. Allow the register a "stagnant" verdict so that low
      question-yield is not forced to read as degeneration (arXiv:2404.18307).
    - Normalise any question-count by field size to avoid the Chu & Evans scale artefact
      (PNAS 118(41)).
    - Downgrade the provenance label from "externally corroborated" to "contested in the source
      literature; no empirical operationalisation located."

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-1164
    Strongest counterargument: Question-generation cannot do the work the claim assigns it because
      it is not independently measurable and not monotonically related to programme health. The
      problem-solving tradition has never solved the individuation problem — there is no principled
      way to say how many questions a programme has generated, so any count is an artefact of
      description grain. Worse, the failure mode of a degenerating programme is precisely the
      generation of new questions: each anomaly absorbed by an ad hoc auxiliary spawns fresh
      questions about the auxiliary, so a raw question-count is maximised by the pathology it is
      supposed to detect. The string/multiverse case and the Chu & Evans finding that large, highly
      active fields ossify rather than turn over are the empirical shape of this. And because
      Lakatos concedes there is no instantaneous rationality, the criterion delivers verdicts only
      in hindsight, which makes it unable to guide a live pipeline at all.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (1) The Problem slot counts only questions that
      are answerable-in-principle and testable, not questions generated by defensive repair.
      (2) The slot is treated as one input among several, with a knowledge-accumulation term
      present, so that consolidation phases are not penalised. (3) The register accepts a neutral
      "stagnant/undetermined" verdict rather than forcing progressive-or-degenerating. (4) Any
      cross-programme comparison normalises for programme size and age. If all four hold, the
      challenges above are boundary conditions rather than refutations.
    How to test: Yes, empirically testable. Take a historical corpus of research programmes with
      independently-assessed outcomes (e.g. the fundamental-physics cases in Branahl 2024, or
      Cochrane-style retrospective assessments in biomedicine). Operationalise "questions generated"
      at time t (open-problem statements in review articles, "future directions" sections,
      registered-report question counts). Test whether question-yield at t predicts an independent
      outcome at t+k (novel confirmed predictions, method adoption, canon turnover per Chu & Evans),
      controlling for field size and funding. The claim predicts a positive partial correlation. The
      degeneration objection predicts an inverted-U or a null once ad hoc questions are excluded.
      Discriminating those two would settle the slot's design.
