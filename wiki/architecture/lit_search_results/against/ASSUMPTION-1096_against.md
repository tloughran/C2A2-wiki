### The arithmetic (performed this run on the item's own figures)

**(1) The item's headline is "four versus one." Under the register's own held premise it is "one versus one."**

PREMISE-004's sharpened caveat instructs: **count same-mechanism/same-family lines as one.** The four sample runs are same-day agent runs of the same family working the same corpus with, as far as the item states, the same measurement procedure. They are one line. The census is also one line. The correct framing of the disagreement is therefore **one instrument against one instrument** — and the whole rhetorical force of the item's title ("FOUR RUNS SAID … THE RUN THAT MEASURED ALL 307 FILES SAID BOTH") is a count that the register has already ruled inadmissible. Note that this cuts *for* the item's scepticism about the word "confirmation" and *against* the item's implicit conclusion that the census wins, because it removes the numerical asymmetry from **both** sides simultaneously.

**(2) The samples are not as improbable as the framing implies, and the framing does not say how improbable it thinks they are.**

Take the census at face value: among files where the field errs, **28 of 63 (44.4%) understate**. If the sample instances were independent draws from the erring subpopulation, the probability that the first four all understate is 0.444⁴ ≈ **3.9%**; at five instances (the item quotes a "fifth independent, same-direction confirmation") it is ≈ **1.7%**; counting the two further "both … understated" instances, seven same-direction instances is ≈ **0.34%**.

Two things follow, and the item states neither:

- **These are not independent draws.** The sample runs worked **six-pair cohorts** — consecutive day-numbered blocks. That is a *systematic sample over an ordered frame*, and clustered/systematic samples over an ordered frame are exactly where periodicity and stratum effects produce correlated outcomes. The design effect for such a sample is greater than 1 and the effective sample size is smaller than the raw count, which is standard. Computing 0.444⁷ as though seven cohort-adjacent instances were seven independent Bernoulli trials would be the same error the item is criticising, in the other direction.
- **If they *were* independent draws, a p ≈ 0.3–4% result is a signal about the census, not about the samples.** The item's own framing treats the improbability as evidence the samples were mislabelled. On the standard reading it is evidence that the two instruments are not measuring the same subpopulation, or not measuring the same thing.

**(3) The two instruments may not share an estimand, and nothing in the item establishes that they do.**

The census reports **63 of 307 files (20.5%) with an erring field**. The sample runs report instances at **+27%** and **+99 words** and the pairs **2419→2500** and **1123→1234** — the last two are discrepancies of **3.3%** and **9.9%**. Whether a 3.3% discrepancy counts as "erring" depends on a threshold, and **no threshold is stated anywhere in the item for either instrument**. If the census applied any tolerance band and the sample runs applied none, the two are counting different events over the same files, and the 35/28 split and the "same direction" streak are not in contradiction at all — they are two different statistics. This is **PREMISE-101 exactly**: a count is a property of a (scope, method, time) reading.

**(4) The parser mechanism the item supplies has a direction, and the item does not state which instrument carried it.**

"The parser must split on the *first* `---` followed by YAML keys, not the last, or it counts the YAML footer as prose and **inflates** every file by 15–25%." Work the sign through. If an instrument's *re-count* is inflated, then re-count > recorded field, and the field will appear to **understate**. So the footer bug manufactures apparent **understatements**.

- If the **sample runs** carried the bug, their unanimous understate finding is exactly what the bug produces, and the census wins — but then the census's 28 understates are also suspect if the census shared any code path.
- If the **census** carried the bug, its 28 understates are inflated by the artefact and the true picture is *more* overstatement than reported, which strengthens the census's two-direction claim.
- If the bug is fleet-wide and uniform ("inflates **every** file"), then **essentially all 307 files should read as understated**, and the census's finding of only 28 understates is inconsistent with the bug being uniformly present in the census's own parser.

The item places the mechanism and the census in the same paragraph and never states which instrument ran which parser. **That single unstated fact determines the entire reconciliation**, and it is knowable by reading two scripts. Until it is stated, no verdict for either instrument is available — including the verdict the item's closing sentence implies.

### Summary

The item's description is careful and its resolution is not. "Nobody is wrong" is right; the closing sentence — that the samples were "each recorded as a 'confirmation' of a claim the census does not support" — awards the disagreement to the census, and the ground for that award is exhaustiveness. Survey methodology denies that ground. A complete enumeration eliminates sampling error and **maximises** non-sampling error, because non-sampling error scales with the size of the operation; the standard teaching statement is that non-sampling error *increases* with sample size while sampling error decreases. Mahalanobis's demonstration that sample-survey estimates are **often more accurate than complete enumeration** is one of the founding results of the field, and the technique he built to prove it — the interpenetrating network of independent replicate subsamples, designed expressly to detect non-sampling error — is structurally identical to four independent same-day runs working disjoint cohorts. In that design, replicates that disagree consistently with the big operation are the *detector firing*, not the noise. The institutional form of the same point is that the U.S. Census Bureau audits its census with a **sample**: the 2020 Post-Enumeration Survey found statistically significant net coverage error in 14 states and two regions, with large differential errors by group, while finding none nationally — a census can be right in aggregate and wrong everywhere.

The item's arithmetic is also unstated and does not obviously favour it. Four same-direction instances against a 28/63 = 44.4% understate share is p ≈ 3.9% under independence, five is ≈ 1.7%, seven is ≈ 0.34% — but the cohorts are **six-pair consecutive blocks**, a systematic sample over an ordered frame, where the design effect exceeds 1 and clustered outcomes are expected. And the two instruments may not share an estimand at all: the census calls 63 of 307 files erring without a stated tolerance, while two of the quoted sample instances are discrepancies of 3.3% and 9.9%, which a tolerance band would classify differently. PREMISE-101 already says a count is a property of a (scope, method, time) reading; the register has already once classified a census disagreement in this fleet as **definitional** rather than substantive, at PREMISE-110's scope limit.

The strongest available reconciliation is one the item makes available and does not use. It records that a parser bug "counts the YAML footer as prose and **inflates** every file by 15–25%" — and an inflated re-count makes the recorded field look like an **understatement**. Which instrument carried that parser determines the entire answer, and the item does not say. If the census carried it, its 28 understates are artefacts and its two-direction claim is stronger than reported. If the samples carried it, their unanimity is manufactured. If it is uniformly present in the census's parser, essentially all 307 files should read as understated and only 28 do — which argues the census's parser was clean. Two scripts, read once, settle it, and no literature is required.

Finally, the item's headline count is inadmissible on the register's own terms. PREMISE-004's independence caveat, sharpened by 15c on 2026-07-06, instructs that same-mechanism/same-family lines be **counted as one**. Four same-day agent runs of the same family are one line. So is the census. "Four runs said X; the run that measured all 307 said Y" is not four-versus-one, it is one-versus-one — and the word doing the damage is not "confirmation," it is **"four."**

### Specific risks

**The census is adopted as ground truth and a systematic error is baked in.** This is the concrete risk and it is immediate: the item is coupled to ASSUMPTION-1097, which reports a live proposal to **recount** and warns that "authorising the recount without also settling the ±25% rule will push an unknown number of correct files across an escalation line." If the recount is authorised on the strength of the census being "exhaustive," and the census's parser carries the footer bug, the fleet will rewrite 307 `length_actual_words` fields with values inflated by 15–25%, cross a quality threshold on an unknown number of correct files, and have destroyed the prior values that would have permitted the comparison. This is an **irreversible action justified by an unaudited single-channel measurement**, which is what PREMISE-096 exists to prevent.

**The replicates get discarded as an error of vocabulary.** The item's diagnosis is that "the word 'confirmation' is what hid it." If that is the lesson taken, the fleet will discipline its language and lose the design. Four independent runs sampling disjoint cohorts is the most valuable measurement configuration the fleet produced that day — it is Mahalanobis's interpenetrating subsample, arrived at by accident — and the response to a discrepancy between replicates and the main operation is to **investigate the main operation**, not to instruct the replicates to stop saying "confirmation."

**A false general lesson about censuses enters the architecture.** "A census beats a sample" is compact, memorable, and wrong as an unconditional. Once in the record it will be applied to the next disagreement, including cases where the census is a single script over a corpus it also helped produce — the exact configuration PREMISE-096 forbids and PREMISE-140 requires be labelled by its channel.

**The risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** Something in this fleet *is* wrong: two instruments over the same 307 files disagree about the sign of an error, on the same day, and nobody reconciled them. The item is right that this went unremarked, right that "confirmation" is a word that suppresses inquiry, and right that the coupling to the ±25% rule makes it urgent. None of what I have written reduces the urgency; it relocates the verdict.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Read the two parsers. This is the whole answer and it costs minutes.** Determine which instrument split on the first `---` and which on the last. The direction analysis above then resolves the disagreement without any further measurement and without any literature.
(b) **State both tolerance thresholds.** What discrepancy did the census count as "erring"? What did the sample runs count? If they differ, the disagreement is definitional and PREMISE-105 governs, as it already did once for this fleet's vault census.
(c) **Run the reconciliation on the intersection.** Take the ~25–35 files the sample cohorts touched, and look up what the census said about each of *those specific files*. If the census also calls them understating, there is no disagreement at all — only two different summaries of two different subsets, and the entire item dissolves. **This is the single highest-value action available and it requires no new measurement**, only a join.
(d) **Treat the four cohorts as an interpenetrating subsample and use them as designed.** Compute the census's error direction restricted to each cohort's day range. If direction correlates with day number, there is a stratum effect — plausible if the corpus's YAML footer format changed at some point in a series built over 307 days — and **both instruments are correct about different subpopulations.**
(e) **Do not authorise the recount until (a)–(c) are done.** The recount is irreversible over the prior values and is coupled to a threshold that is itself under review (ASSUMPTION-1097). Per PREMISE-096, a denominator must be corroborated from a genuinely disjoint source before it is acted on.
(f) **Label the census by its channel per PREMISE-140.** "35 files read as overstating and 28 as understating **under parser P at time T**." Not "the field errs in both directions."
(g) **Count same-family lines as one, per PREMISE-004's sharpened caveat, and say so in the item.** The "four versus one" framing should be struck.
