A documented-known-issue is a legitimate governance state provided it is owned, scored and revisitable. That is a set of conditions to check, not grounds to reject the disclaimer.
    3. UK Parliament research briefing SN/SG/2607, "Changes in crime recording practices"; and the register-based official-statistics accuracy literature (e.g. "Scalable Generalised Accuracy Estimation for Multisource Register-based Official Statistics," arXiv:2502.10182). — Directly challenges the presumption's inferential move about direction. When two series are produced under DIFFERENT COUNTING RULES (unit of count, inclusion criteria, deduplication, treatment of multiples), the resulting divergences routinely go in opposite directions across categories: a rule that deduplicates raises one category and lowers another, and a rule that reclassifies moves items BETWEEN categories, which mechanically produces one increase and one decrease from a single cause. Non-uniform direction is thus a normal signature of a single rule difference, not evidence against one. In the reported figures 511/165/54 vs 609/132/110 the totals (730 vs 851) and the opposite-signed middle term are consistent with reclassification between categories plus differing inclusion scope - one artifact, two directions.
    4. "Datasheets Aren't Enough: DataRubrics for Automated Quality Metrics and Accountability" (2025). — The current critique of self-reported documentation is that it lacks standardised rubrics and is inconsistently applied and enforced - i.e. the remedy proposed in the literature is to ADD a rubric to self-report, not to discard self-report as untrustworthy.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The presumption's core caution - a file's own disclaimer is not a reconciliation - is sound and nothing located refutes it. But two of its supporting moves are challenged. First, self-certified known-limitation documentation is the field standard (Gebru et al. 2021) and documented acceptance of a known issue is a governed control rather than a failure mode, provided it is owned, scored and revisitable; the correct response is to check those conditions, not to withdraw trust. Second, and more substantively, the argument from non-uniform direction does not work: in official statistics, divergences between series produced under different counting rules routinely run in opposite directions across categories, because reclassification moves items between buckets and deduplication cuts one way while scope changes cut the other. The reported figures - differing totals plus one opposite-signed term - are consistent with a single rule difference. What remains valid is the conclusion, reached by a different route: only a recomputation under a stated counting rule settles it.

  Specific risks: If the presumption is over-read, C2A2 distrusts its own documentation layer generally, which would delegitimise the datasheet-style disclosures that are currently its only visible quality signal, and it forgoes the correct diagnostic (identify the rule difference) in favour of a generalised suspicion. If under-read, a canonical counter is trusted on the strength of a self-issued disclaimer with no stated counting rule, no owner and no review date, and every downstream figure - including any progress metric per PRESUMPTION-563 - inherits an unreconciled base.

  Mitigations available: Do the reconciliation, which both the presumption and the literature agree is the settling test (OPEN-112 / ASSUMPTION-560). Before that, convert the disclaimer into a governed accepted-risk entry: state the counting rule for each series, name an owner, score the residual exposure, and set a review date - which is what the risk-register literature requires and what turns a disclaimer into a control. Add a DataRubrics-style checklist to the canonical counters so the self-report is scored rather than merely present. Concretely, test the reclassification hypothesis first: check whether the 165-vs-132 and 54-vs-110 differences net out against a category boundary, which would identify a single rule difference and dissolve the direction objection.

    Strongest counterargument (against the presumption): Self-reported known limitations are the field standard for data documentation - Gebru et al. build datasheets on exactly this, on the grounds that only the creator can assess known limitations - and a documented accepted risk with an owner and a review date is a recognised control precisely because it converts inertia into a visible decision. More importantly, the presumption's evidential lever fails: divergences between series counted under different rules very commonly run in opposite directions across categories, because reclassification shifts items between buckets while scope or deduplication changes shift the total. One rule difference is entirely capable of producing 165 -> 132 alongside 54 -> 110, so non-uniform direction is a normal signature of a single artifact rather than evidence against one. The suspicion is therefore unearned, even though the demand for reconciliation is right.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: the disclaimer names the counting rule that produced each series, has an owner and a review date, and a recomputation under a single stated rule reproduces the canonical line.
    How to test: recompute PRS/CROSS/FINDING counts under one explicitly written counting rule and reconcile against the master-wiki canonical line. Then check whether the residual differences are attributable to a single rule (category boundary or inclusion scope). If one rule accounts for all three divergences, the presumption's direction argument is refuted and the disclaimer was accurate; if not, multiple independent counting errors exist and the disclaimer understated the problem.
