  Challenging evidence found: Yes

  **Direction of challenge, stated explicitly.** The brief offered two possible AGAINST directions: (i) evidence that terminological priming does not drive coding convergence, which would challenge ASSUMPTION-937's worry; or (ii) evidence that it strongly does, which would challenge the wiki's 90+ CROSS entries. **I searched for (i) and did not find it. What I found supports (ii).** So I am not challenging ASSUMPTION-937 — I am challenging the CROSS corpus that ASSUMPTION-937 puts at risk, and I am reporting that the worry is better founded than the wiki's single instance of self-restraint suggests.

  Sources:
    1. "Anchoring Bias in Large Language Models: An Experimental Study." arXiv:2412.06593 [author list not confirmed this session]. — Finds anchoring bias "widely prevalent" in LLMs, that strong models are readily influenced by "expert" opinions presented in the prompt, and — most damagingly for C2A2 — that Chain-of-Thought, Thoughts of Principles, Ignoring Anchor Hints, and Reflection all fail to reduce it: "none of these simple mitigating strategies can effectively reduce anchoring bias in LLM responses." This is the direct challenge. If a C2A2 detector is given a candidate term ("second-personal") in the prompt and asked whether two traditions converge on it, the anchoring literature predicts it will find the term, and predicts that instructing it to be careful will not fix this.
    2. "Source framing triggers systematic bias in large language models." *Science Advances*, doi:10.1126/sciadv.adz2924 (preprint: "Source framing triggers systematic evaluation bias in Large Language Models," arXiv:2505.13488) [author list not confirmed this session]. — Shows high inter- and intra-model agreement across topics that **breaks down once source framing is introduced**, so agreement scores shift systematically with attribution rather than content. Bears directly on C2A2's practice of attributing a proposition to a named thinker and asking whether another thinker agrees: the attribution itself moves the verdict. Note the compounding implication — high baseline agreement between LLM judgements is *not* evidence of a real signal, because it is precisely what the framing manipulations then override.
    3. "How Does Prompt Anchoring Affect Large Language Model Outputs?" *Publications* (MDPI) 14(3), 43 [author list not confirmed this session]. — Reports that prompt anchoring "consistently reconfigures the conceptual distribution of outputs across all models," i.e. the effect is not a marginal bias on a verdict but a shift in which concepts the model produces at all. For a cross-tradition detector whose output *is* a set of concepts, this is the failure mode named in ASSUMPTION-937, measured.
    4. "Bias in, Bias out: Annotation Bias in Multilingual Large Language Models." arXiv:2511.14662 [author list not confirmed this session]. — Proposes a framework distinguishing **instruction bias**, annotator bias, and contextual/cultural bias, and argues that bias from task framing distorts model outputs. "Instruction bias" is exactly the C2A2 case: the framing of the detection task determines what counts as a convergence.
    5. Gentner, D., & Markman, A. B. (1997). "Structure Mapping in Analogy and Similarity." *American Psychologist* 52(1). — The structure-mapping literature's central empirical finding is that **retrieval is driven by surface similarity while structural similarity is what predicts success**. Shared vocabulary between two traditions is a surface feature. A detector that retrieves candidate pairs by shared terminology is therefore operating on exactly the cue the literature identifies as a poor guide to genuine relational correspondence — and Gentner's work shows this dissociation holds even for careful human reasoners. Also relevant from the same corpus: training that emphasises structural alignment over superficial resemblance improves analogical reasoning quality, which is the shape of the available mitigation.
    6. Jingle-jangle literature: Lawson, K. M., & Robins, R. W. (2021), "Sibling constructs"; "Extrinsic Convergent Validity Evidence to Prevent Jingle and Jangle Fallacies," *Multivariate Behavioral Research* 56(1), doi:10.1080/00273171.2019.1707061; "Uncovering jingle and jangle fallacies: A systematic review mapping future directions," *Zeitschrift für Psychologie* 233(4); "Escaping the Jingle-Jangle Jungle: Increasing Conceptual Clarity in Psychology Using Large Language Models," *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, doi:10.1177/09637214251382083 (Wulff & Mata, 2026). — Establishes that a single label attached to distinct phenomena (jingle) is a well-documented and pervasive source of false convergence *within* one discipline, where terminology is comparatively standardised. C2A2 detects convergence *across* fourteen traditions with no shared terminological discipline at all, which makes the jingle exposure strictly worse. The Wulff & Mata paper is doubly relevant: it proposes using LLM semantic embedding to detect construct overlap, which is close to what C2A2's detector does — and it is offered as a tool for *diagnosing* jingle-jangle, not as evidence that LLM-detected semantic proximity is itself substantive convergence.
    7. Qualitative-methods literature on coding-scheme circularity and coder drift (surveyed via methodological sources rather than a single canonical paper; treat as directional): creating a coding framework before analysis is documented as inviting confirmation bias, "as researchers may only look for predefined themes"; **definitional drift** — data coded differently at the start of a corpus than later — is a recognised failure requiring re-examination of already-coded material; and coder bias appears as differing marginal distributions in code use. Also: "Evaluating and Tracking Qualitative Content Coder Performance Using Item Response Theory," PMC10691860, which exists precisely because coder performance drifts and must be tracked. Challenges the assumption that a detector's coding scheme is a neutral instrument over a long-running corpus. C2A2 has been running for months with an evolving vocabulary; definitional drift is not a hypothetical.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: **I am challenging the 90+ CROSS entries, not ASSUMPTION-937.** The literature offers no support for the exculpatory reading — I found nothing showing terminological priming fails to drive coding convergence — and it offers a great deal of support for the incriminating one. The most direct evidence is from the LLM-annotation literature: anchoring bias is prevalent and, critically, is not removed by chain-of-thought, self-reflection, or explicit instructions to ignore the anchor; prompt anchoring reconfigures the conceptual distribution of outputs, not just the final verdict; and source framing systematically shifts agreement judgements while leaving high inter-model agreement intact, which means the detector's confidence and consistency carry no evidential weight against the artefact hypothesis. Alongside this, Gentner's structure-mapping results show that retrieval by surface similarity — and shared vocabulary is surface similarity — is a poor guide to relational correspondence even in careful human reasoners, and the jingle-jangle literature shows that a single label attached to distinct phenomena produces false convergence routinely within single disciplines, let alone across fourteen. The wiki's own instance is the model case: two of three Rohr sources used "second-personal" in the proposal's voice. That the network caught this once, by hand, on one pair, does not bound the rate at which it happens; it establishes only that the rate is greater than zero and that the existing detection method is manual and unaudited. The correct inference is that the CROSS corpus has an unmeasured artefact rate and that the burden is now on C2A2 to measure it.

  Specific risks:
    - **Unbounded artefact rate across 90+ CROSS entries.** One caught case gives a lower bound of one and no upper bound. Nothing currently distinguishes CROSS entries found by shared structure from those found by shared vocabulary the network supplied.
    - **Mitigations that do not work.** The anchoring literature specifically reports that instructing the model to ignore the anchor, to reason step by step, or to reflect, all fail. So the natural C2A2 fix — "add a prompt line telling the detector not to import terminology" — is the fix the literature says does not work. Any mitigation must be structural, not instructional.
    - **Agreement is not evidence.** Convergent verdicts across runs or across models are exactly what the source-framing paper finds under conditions where framing is nonetheless driving the result. C2A2's practice of corroborating a finding by independent runs (used elsewhere in the same night's work) does not discriminate artefact from signal here.
    - **Definitional drift over a months-long corpus.** Terms minted early become the vocabulary later runs search with. This is a positive feedback loop: each new CROSS entry expands the seeded lexicon that the next detection pass uses.
    - **Voice contamination is currently caught only by hand.** The Rohr × Stump refusal was a judgement call by one reviewer on one night. There is no automated check for whether a term appears in a source's own voice versus a C2A2 proposal's paraphrase — and this is mechanisable (quotation-level provenance), so its absence is a gap, not a limit.
    - **Downstream propagation.** CROSS entries feed the master index, the pattern detector, the synthesis files, and the narration graph's shared-reference edges. An artefact in the CROSS layer is not contained to that layer.
    - **The refusal itself may be a misleading positive.** ASSUMPTION-937 was logged as an act of self-restraint. Read adversarially, one refusal in ninety-plus mints, arrived at by chance inspection of source voice, is a very low audit rate presented as a governance success.

  Mitigations available:
    - **Quotation-level provenance as a hard requirement.** Every CROSS entry must record, for each side, a verbatim quotation from the source in the source's own voice containing the convergent term or its documented equivalent, with a locator. Entries that cannot supply this on both sides are marked PROVISIONAL and excluded from counts. This is the direct fix for the voice contamination that ASSUMPTION-937 identified, and it is structural rather than instructional, which the anchoring literature says is necessary.
    - **Blind re-detection audit.** Sample n CROSS entries; strip C2A2's own vocabulary from the source excerpts; give a fresh detector only the excerpts and ask it to state what, if anything, the two share, without seeing the CROSS entry. The proportion of entries whose convergence survives is the measurable artefact rate. This is the single highest-value action available and it is cheap.
    - **Seeded-term negative controls.** Deliberately inject a term into the prompt that is NOT present in either source and measure how often the detector reports convergence on it. A nonzero rate calibrates the corpus.
    - **Vocabulary quarantine.** Maintain an explicit list of terms C2A2 minted itself and forbid the detector from using minted terms as retrieval keys. This directly breaks the drift feedback loop.
    - **Structure-over-surface criterion (Gentner).** Require every CROSS entry to state the *relational* correspondence — what plays the role of what — and not merely the shared label. Entries whose entire content is "both use word W" fail.
    - **Jingle-jangle check.** Before minting, require an explicit statement of what would make the two uses of the term *different* phenomena, and why that is ruled out. Lawson & Robins' sibling-construct guidance is the template.
    - **Drift tracking.** Periodically re-code an early sample with the current scheme and measure disagreement, per the item-response-theory coder-tracking approach. The wiki already runs a drift-since-written check; extend it from claim drift to *coding-scheme* drift.
    - **Report CROSS counts with an error bar.** Until the audit rate is known, "90+ cross-connections" should not be presented as a headline metric without a stated artefact-rate caveat.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-937
    Strongest counterargument: The anchoring and framing literature establishes that a *biased* detector can be produced, not that C2A2's CROSS corpus is one. C2A2 does not simply ask "do these two converge on term W?" — a large fraction of CROSS entries are minted from independently ingested source material by different agents on different nights, and the wiki's own record shows the network repeatedly declining pairs and downgrading entries on source testimony (CROSS-008 was downgraded on Stump's own words; Rohr × Stump was refused; Fredrickson came back as a boundary test rather than a convergence twice in three days). A system that manufactures convergence does not routinely produce disconfirmations against its own headline metric. Moreover, the seeded-vocabulary worry has a benign reading: paraphrasing two traditions into a common idiom is what synthesis *is*, and the objection, taken to its limit, would forbid any cross-disciplinary comparison whatsoever, since no two fields share primitive vocabulary. The real question is not whether C2A2 supplies terminology — it must — but whether the relational structure survives when the terminology is removed, and that is a testable question the network has not yet been asked.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) The blind re-detection audit must have been run, with a measured survival rate, and that rate must be published alongside the CROSS count. (b) Every CROSS entry must carry own-voice quotations on both sides, or be marked provisional. (c) The detector must not use C2A2-minted terms as retrieval keys. (d) Each entry must state a relational correspondence, not just a shared label. (e) The observed disconfirmations must be shown to be systematic rather than incidental — i.e. the refusal rate must come from an audit process, not from whichever reviewer happened to inspect source voice that night. (f) It must be accepted that inter-run agreement does not corroborate a CROSS entry, since framing effects survive high inter-model agreement.
    How to test: (1) **Blind re-detection**, as above — the decisive experiment. Sample 20–30 CROSS entries stratified by age and by tradition pair; strip C2A2 vocabulary; measure survival. (2) **Negative-control seeding**: inject absent terms and measure false-convergence rate; this gives the detector's specificity directly. (3) **Own-voice provenance sweep**: for all 90+ entries, mechanically check whether the convergent term appears in a verbatim source quotation on both sides, or only in C2A2's paraphrase. This is a text-matching job and can be run tonight; its output is an immediate triage of the corpus. (4) **Held-out tradition test**: give the detector two traditions it has never paired, with and without a seeded term, and compare the number and content of convergences found. (5) **Drift measurement**: re-code the earliest 10% of the corpus with the current scheme and report disagreement, to establish whether the coding scheme itself has moved.
