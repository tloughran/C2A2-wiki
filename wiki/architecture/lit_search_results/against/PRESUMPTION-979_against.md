  Summary: The presumption is challenged by a randomised controlled trial that tested almost exactly
    it. Yavchitz et al. randomised 300 expert readers — corresponding authors of PubMed-indexed RCTs —
    to systematic-review abstracts with and without a PRISMA-standard limitations section, and found no
    effect on confidence in the results (0.19 [−0.37, 0.74]), on confidence in the conclusions' validity
    (0.07 [−0.49, 0.62]), or on judged benefit (0.12 [−0.42, 0.44]); the authors note that expert
    readers should if anything perform better than a general readership. Obar and Oeldorf-Hirsch's
    experimental survey adds the ceiling case: 98% of participants missed prose clauses assigning their
    first-born child as payment, in a document they had formally agreed to. The canonical
    corrective-action hierarchy classifies warnings — which is what a prose qualification is — in its
    weakest tier, on the explicit ground that they rely on humans remembering, and the automation-bias
    literature reports that instruction and training do not repair this class of failure. Two things
    cut the other way and are recorded: the estate's own six-day instance was a **red status field**,
    not prose, which means the item's baseline assumption is itself shaky and the remedy is not simply
    relocation; and the alarm-fatigue percentages that would have quantified the neglect rate could not
    be verified and are excluded by name.

      load-bearing, it must be expressible as a condition that alters the status field; if it cannot

      converts an unread control into a read one by construction rather than by exhortation.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-979
    Strongest counterargument: The RCT does not measure what the item needs it to measure, and reading
      it as decisive is a category error. Yavchitz et al. asked whether a limitations section changes a
      reader's *confidence rating on a Likert scale* — a global affective judgement made in seconds by
      a participant with no stake in the outcome and no subsequent action to take. That is not what a
      caveat in a C2A2 run report is for. The estate's qualifications exist to be **retrieved later, by
      an agent, at the moment a downstream claim depends on them** — they are annotations on a durable
      record, not persuasion aimed at a reader's momentary confidence. A null effect on Likert
      confidence among busy experts is entirely compatible with a prose scope limit doing its whole job
      six weeks later when a different agent greps the premise and reads the clause. The Obar study
      points the same way and is even further off: it measures whether people read a document they have
      every incentive to skip, in a context engineered to make skipping frictionless, and finds they
      skip it — which says nothing about a corpus whose readers are retrieval systems that do not skim.
      Indeed the estate has direct evidence in the opposite direction from this very run: **prose scope
      limits and load-bearing clauses inside premises were found, read and acted on by 15b tonight** —
      PREMISE-111's standing discount, PREMISE-026's "remediation requires owner assignment,"
      PREMISE-141's third-state clause — none of which is a status field, all of which were prose, and
      each of which changed a verdict. That is four caveats read and acted on in one run. The honest
      generalisation is not that prose is unread; it is that prose is unread **by humans skimming a
      daily report** and read perfectly well by agents doing retrieval, and the correct remedy is
      therefore not to move content into status fields but to be clear about which audience each
      artefact serves.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the agent-retrieval channel must actually be
      reliable, and tonight's run is evidence **both ways** — I read four prose clauses and acted on
      them, and the intake's pre-checks **missed three covering premises whose text was in front of
      them**, so the same channel succeeded and failed in the same cycle; (b) the human channel must
      not be load-bearing, which is false — the metabolism freshness gate, the four run reports, and
      every outbound synthesis are read by Tom, and PRESUMPTION-974's whole finding is that the only
      actor with an effector is human; (c) "read later by an agent" must be a real mechanism rather
      than a hope, which requires that something *triggers* the retrieval — and for the six-day red
      gate nothing did; (d) the steelman must survive its own best example, and it does not cleanly:
      PREMISE-111's standing discount was prose, was read by me tonight, and has still **not been
      applied anywhere in the record**, which is the item's claim in its strongest form — the caveat
      was read and had no effect. The steelman's real contribution is (a): it correctly identifies that
      the audience split matters and that a blanket "prose is unread" is too strong.
    How to test: **The estate has the counter-example and can run the differential in-house.** Take the
      last N daily run reports. Classify every load-bearing qualification as either (i) prose beneath a
      headline or (ii) built into a status row, as the scheduler check did. For each, record whether any
      subsequent artefact — a later run, a disposition, a premise entry — **acted on it**, with
      denominators per PREMISE-168. If the act-on rate for (ii) materially exceeds (i), the scheduler
      check's pattern is validated by the estate's own data and should be mandated; if the rates are
      both near zero, the item is right in a stronger form than it claims and the channel is not the
      variable — the forcing function is, which routes to PRESUMPTION-962. Second, cheaper test for the
      steelman's audience split: count how many prose clauses in `validated_premises.md` marked
      "load-bearing" have a traceable downstream application. PREMISE-111's discount is one known
      instance of zero. If that generalises, the agent-retrieval channel is not doing the work the
      steelman credits it with, and the finding is much worse than the item states.
