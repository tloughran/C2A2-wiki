  Sources:
    1. "Maintaining Stable Personas? Examining Temporal Stability in LLM-Based Human Simulation." CHI 2026 Extended Abstracts, DOI 10.1145/3772363.3799334. — New and directly on point. Persona drift, inconsistency and exaggerated trait expression compromise both realism and validity of simulated behaviour. Unlike humans, LLMs lack consistent personality-like behaviour because training on diverse text produces models that simulate many personalities simultaneously. A "tradition-as-agent" is a persona; the persona is not stable; therefore the "behavioral data" is not attributable to the tradition.
    2. "Assessing the Reliability of Persona-Conditioned LLMs as Synthetic Survey Respondents." arXiv:2602.18462 (Feb 2026). — Critical finding: LLM-generated personas introduce systematic biases downstream, and simulated populations drift *further* from real outcomes as more attributes are specified. C2A2 specifies heavily-elaborated tradition personas — precisely the regime where divergence is worst. This inverts the intuition that richer personas are more faithful.
    3. "Persistent Instability in LLM's Personality Measurements: Effects of Scale, Reasoning, and Conversation History." AAAI 2026, AI Alignment track (arXiv:2508.04826v3). — Instability persists across model scale and reasoning capability and is affected by conversation history. Scaling up or adding reasoning does not fix it, and long C2A2 sessions actively worsen it.
    4. "Stable Personas: Dual-Assessment of Temporal Stability in LLM-Based Human Simulation." arXiv:2601.22812 (Jan 2026). / "LLM-Based Educational Simulation: Evaluating Temporal Student Persona Stability." arXiv:2605.06307 (May 2026). — Nuanced result: self-reported traits stay stable at high intensity, but *observer-rated behavioural expression* shows within-conversation drift in unscripted dialogue. This is the damaging split for C2A2 — the agent will continue to *say* it is reasoning as the tradition while its actual output drifts. Self-report cannot be used as a validity check.
    5. "Eval4Sim: An Evaluation Framework for Persona Simulation." arXiv:2603.02876 (Mar 2026). — The existence of a dedicated evaluation framework establishes that persona simulation requires validation to be treated as data. C2A2 runs no such evaluation; its agent outputs are ungrounded by the field's own current standard.
    6. Construct-validity critique in the persona-simulation literature (2026). — Adaptations of human instruments to LLM subjects "cannot achieve the same construct validity as the originals." Calling agent output "behavioral data" is a construct-validity claim, and the literature says it does not transfer.
    7. "On the Limits of LLM-as-Judge for Scientific Novelty Assessment." arXiv:2606.12071 (June 2026). — LLM novelty judgements diverge inconsistently from expert judgement; the methodology cannot self-certify its own outputs as novel or genuine.
    8.   Evidence trajectory (challenging): growing

  Summary: The methodological claim is now challenged by a coherent and rapidly growing 2026 subfield on LLM persona simulation validity. Three findings are individually sufficient to defeat the assumption as stated: personas drift behaviourally within a conversation while self-report remains stable (so the agent will appear in-character while not being in-character); richer persona specification increases divergence from the real target rather than decreasing it; and construct validity does not transfer from human instruments to LLM subjects. Together these mean that even a perfectly non-hallucinating agent would not be producing data about the tradition — it would be producing text conditioned on a tradition label whose behavioural fidelity is unmeasured. The field has responded to this problem by building evaluation frameworks (Eval4Sim); C2A2 uses none, so by the current standard of the field its outputs are unvalidated simulation, not empirical data.

  Specific risks: If false, C2A2's central methodological claim — its claim to be doing something new and empirical rather than generating structured commentary — is unsupported, and every "finding" attributed to a tradition is instead a finding about a base model's priors under a label. This is the most externally-visible risk in the batch: the methodology claim is what would be scrutinised first by any outside reader, and the 2026 literature is exactly what they would cite. Internally, the risk is that drift is invisible — the agent keeps asserting its identity while its outputs converge toward the base model's generic voice, so the 14 traditions gradually become one voice with 14 names, and cross-tradition "signals" become artefacts of that convergence (interacting with PRESUMPTION-014 and ASSUMPTION-021).

  Mitigations available: (a) Stop calling it empirical data — call it structured elicitation, which is defensible and loses little; (b) run a distinctiveness test: blind-classify agent outputs by tradition and measure accuracy; if a rater cannot recover the tradition above chance, the personas are not differentiated; (c) measure drift directly using the observer-rated (not self-report) protocols from arXiv:2601.22812 / arXiv:2605.06307; (d) use scripted prompts with explicit task framing, which arXiv:2605.06307 reports eliminates within-conversation drift — a cheap and effective structural fix; (e) cap conversation length, since history worsens instability (AAAI 2026); (f) validate against ground truth — check agent claims about a thinker against that thinker's actual published positions on a sample.

  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: The persona-simulation validity literature is aimed at a specific and different use case — substituting LLM personas for human survey respondents in order to estimate population-level quantities, where fidelity to real human distributions is the whole point and drift is fatal. C2A2 makes no such claim: it does not assert that its Levin agent predicts what Michael Levin would say, only that conditioning on a tradition's corpus and commitments produces a usefully different search bias over an idea space than an unconditioned model does. That is a claim about generative diversity, not about behavioural fidelity, and it survives persona drift entirely — indeed the "traditions collapse into one voice" worry is directly testable and would be visible in the outputs. Reframed as instrumented idea-generation with tradition-conditioned priors, the methodology is modest, honest and arguably genuinely new as *practice* even if not as *measurement*.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (1) The word "empirical" and the phrase "behavioral data" are dropped or heavily qualified in all artefacts; (2) tradition distinctiveness is measured rather than assumed — this is the one condition the steelman itself depends on; (3) no claim is made about what any real thinker holds without checking against primary sources; (4) drift is monitored with observer-rated rather than self-reported measures.
    How to test: Blind distinctiveness test. Take 40 agent outputs across the 14 traditions, strip identifying labels and vocabulary, and have a rater (human, or a held-out model) assign each to a tradition. Above-chance accuracy supports the weak steelman version; at-chance accuracy falsifies both versions and indicates persona collapse. Secondary: ground-truth spot check — take 10 substantive claims each agent has made about its thinker and verify against primary sources. Both tests are day-scale and neither has been run.
