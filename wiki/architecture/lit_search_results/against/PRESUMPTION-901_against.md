  Sources:
    1. Popper, K. 1959 (orig. 1934). *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*. (Accessed snippet-level via
       Thornton, "Karl Popper," and "Popper, Karl: Philosophy of Science," Internet Encyclopedia of
       Philosophy, iep.utm.edu/pop-sci/.)
       — Names the failure mode directly: *conventionalist stratagems*, later relabelled by Hans
       Albert as *immunizing stratagems*. Popper enumerates four, of which the first is precisely
       the behaviour at issue: introducing ad hoc hypotheses that render the refuting evidence
       irrelevant. The defining property is that the modification *reduces testability*. This is a
       named, specified failure mode for "adopt the challenge into the statement," and it predates
       the presumption by roughly ninety years.
    2. Lakatos, I. 1970. "Falsification and the Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes."
       In Lakatos & Musgrave (eds.), *Criticism and the Growth of Knowledge*. Cambridge UP.
       (Snippet-level via plato.stanford.edu/entries/lakatos/ and secondary summaries.)
       — Names the failure mode at programme level: the *degenerating problemshift*. Amendment is
       permitted, but each successive modification must be theoretically progressive (predict a
       novel fact) and empirically progressive (that fact occasionally corroborated). A sequence of
       adjustments that only accommodates known anomalies, without excess empirical content, is
       degenerating. Content-reducing ad hoc modification in the face of anomaly is explicitly
       disqualifying. This is the sharpest available statement of the exact risk: unfalsifiability
       by accretion — a claim that survives every challenge because it has absorbed every challenge,
       and thereby forbids nothing.
    3. Hansson, S.O. "Science and Pseudo-Science." *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
       (plato.stanford.edu/entries/pseudo-science/); and "Pseudoscience and the Demarcation Problem,"
       *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (iep.utm.edu/pseudoscience-demarcation/).
       — Confirms the failure mode is standard in the demarcation literature, and simultaneously
       supplies the *second-order* challenge: it is widely agreed that falsifiability fails as a
       usable demarcation criterion precisely because ad hoc assumptions can always save a theory
       (the Duhem–Quine problem) and because a workable characterisation of "ad hoc" is lacking.
       There are degrees of ad hocness, contested even among practitioners.
    4. Laudan, L. 1983. "The Demise of the Demarcation Problem." (Accessed secondary, via SEP/IEP
       and Aeon essay "Imre Lakatos and the philosophy of bad science.")
       — The criterion that names the failure mode is itself contested. Laudan dismisses demarcation
       as a pseudo-problem on the grounds that no agreement on criteria has been reached since
       Aristotle. Secondary commentary notes that Lakatos buys sophistication at the cost of a sharp
       or practicable demarcation criterion — i.e. the Lakatosian test is real but not mechanically
       applicable.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The presumption is straightforwardly false, and this is the clearest result in the
  intake. The failure mode for adopting every challenge into the statement is named twice over in
  the canonical philosophy of science — as Popper's conventionalist/immunizing stratagem and as
  Lakatos's degenerating problemshift — and both name the same discriminating property: the
  amendment reduces the claim's testable content. So 14b's inference that "no failure mode is
  named" reports a gap in the pipeline's *own* vocabulary, not a gap in the literature. The NOVELTY
  candidacy fails. One genuine complication survives and should be carried forward: the criterion
  is contested as a *demarcation* tool. Ad hocness admits of degrees, the Duhem–Quine underdetermination
  means no amendment is decisively ad hoc in isolation, and Laudan's dismissal of the demarcation
  project is influential. The criterion is therefore serviceable as a *diagnostic heuristic within*
  an inquiry and unserviceable as a *bright-line gate*. C2A2 should adopt it in the former role only.

  Specific risks: If the presumption is left in place, C2A2 has no stopping rule and no way to
  distinguish a statement that has been strengthened by challenge from one that has been hollowed out
  by it. The predicted end state is a corpus of statements that are locally unobjectionable and
  globally uninformative — each has survived every challenge because each has been widened until it
  forbids nothing. Because unfalsifiability by accretion is monotonic and cumulative, this failure is
  invisible per-amendment and only detectable across the sequence; a pipeline that audits amendments
  one at a time will never see it.

  Mitigations available: Adopt the Lakatos test as an explicit amendment gate, in its property form
  rather than as a demarcation verdict: an amendment is admissible only if the amended statement is
  independently testable and forbids at least as much as the prior statement. Track per-item
  amendment *chains* rather than individual amendments and compute content trajectory across the
  chain. Retain the pre-amendment statement so content loss is measurable. Given Laudan and the
  degrees-of-ad-hocness problem, use the gate to trigger review, not to auto-reject.

  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: Popper's and Lakatos's criteria were built for empirical theories with
    predictive content; C2A2's items are largely architectural and methodological claims about a
    documentation pipeline, which do not "forbid observations" in the required sense. On that reading
    the failure mode is named for a different object, and 14b's presumption might be defensible as
    the narrower claim that no failure mode is named *for this kind of claim*. Against that: the
    degenerating-problemshift pattern is defined over content, not over empirical content
    specifically, and the pipeline's items do have content — they license and forbid pipeline
    behaviours. The narrower reading survives only by conceding that C2A2's items are unfalsifiable
    to begin with, which is a worse position.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: that some amendments are rejected as
    content-reducing, that the pipeline retains pre-amendment statements, and that at least one
    dimension of each item's content is specified precisely enough for reduction to be detectable.
    How to test: Reconstruct the full amendment chain for the ten most-amended items. For each chain,
    ask of the first and last version: what does version 1 forbid that version n permits? If the
    answer is non-empty for a majority of chains, the degenerating pattern is present and confirmed.
    Cheap, retrospective, requires no new instrumentation beyond version retention.
