    1. Paluck, Green & Green 2019 Behavioural Public Policy, 'The contact hypothesis re-evaluated' (title seen; findings from background recall): few randomised studies with delayed outcomes. Intergroup-dialogue reviews (Dessel & Rogge 2008) stress structured confrontation of difference, and say the evidence is mostly weak designs.
  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: Dyadic warmth in committed couples does not straightforwardly scale to groups. Rigorous contact effects are smaller than assumed, and harmony can suppress the disagreement the model is meant to protect.
  STEELMAN: The strongest form of the challenge: the couple finding predicts health, not disagreement quality, and the couples are pre-selected by decades of commitment. Using it as a model for traditions imports the outcome (warmth) and drops the conditions that made it possible. The intergroup literature names a failure mode, harmony masking asymmetry, that this model would not detect.
