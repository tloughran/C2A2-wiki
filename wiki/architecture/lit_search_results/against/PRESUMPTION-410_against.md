Event-time semantics / "birth event" ambiguity. - When a relationship becomes "real" is genuinely contested; authoring a proposal is an act of one party, whereas a CONNECTION arguably forms at mutual engagement or at approval - choosing authoring privileges proposal-time without justification.
    2. Survivorship/selection bias in dating by proposal. - Proposals that were authored but never engaged/approved would be dated as "formed," inflating/mis-timing the connection population if authoring is the anchor.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: Anchoring formation to proposal-authoring is a convenient but contestable choice. A cross-tradition CONNECTION plausibly comes into being at mutual engagement or at approval, not when one side drafts a proposal; dating by authoring can misplace the connection's birth and even count never-realized proposals as formed. This is the semantic counterpart to ASSUMPTION-381's dual encoding: the encoding is fine, but the chosen valid-time-begin event is questionable.

  Specific risks: Mis-timed or over-counted connections; analyses keyed to "formation" skewed earlier than reality; never-approved proposals dated as connections.

  Mitigations available: Carry engagement and approval dates alongside authoring; define "formation" explicitly and justify it; sensitivity-check analyses under alternative formation events.
