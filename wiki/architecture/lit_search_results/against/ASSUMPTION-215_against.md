  Sources:
    1. MacIntyre (1981) "After Virtue"; (1988) "Whose Justice?": a tradition is "an historically extended, socially embodied argument." — A freshly constructed corpus is neither historically extended nor socially embodied, so on the project's own authority it may not be a tradition at all.
    2. MacIntyre (1990): traditions develop rationality through generations of internal debate and crisis. — Tradition-constituted rationality cannot be authored de novo; the Summa-2 would lack the credentialing the Thomist Summa earned over centuries.
    3. Adversarial-collaboration caveats (Kahneman & Klein 2009): contests are only evidential if neither side is the referee. — Conscious-Realist-Monism is the designer's own position, so the head-to-head risks home-team / experimenter bias.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The deepest challenge is self-applying: by MacIntyre's own definition (the project's chosen mentor) a tradition is historically extended and socially embodied, which a constructed Conscious-Realist-Monist corpus is not — so calling it a "genuine rival" to a 700-year tradition is a category mismatch. The contest also has a refereeing problem: the new summa is the designer's own view, inviting confirmation bias in both construction and scoring. Together these mean the head-to-head can produce the APPEARANCE of evidence while being structurally tilted.

  Specific risks: An experiment that "shows" Conscious-Realist-Monism competitive with Thomism, but whose result is an artifact of (a) building the rival to win and (b) comparing an immature corpus against a stress-tested canon.

  Mitigations available: Have a disinterested party (or a distinct agent) construct/steelman Summa-2; pre-register the comparison criteria and the conditions under which Thomism would win; treat Summa-2 as a candidate synthesis, not a "tradition," and report the asymmetry explicitly.

  Recommendation: CHALLENGED (strong)

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-215
    Strongest counterargument: The project's own MacIntyrean commitments deny that a tradition can be constructed: traditions are historically extended, socially embodied arguments whose rationality is earned across generations of crisis and repair. A built-in-a-session corpus authored by the proponent of the view it represents is therefore not a genuine rival but a steelman of the designer's position, and a head-to-head between it and Thomism measures the designer's construction skill, not the relative strength of two traditions.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Either redefine the experiment's claim from "tradition vs tradition" to "constructed synthesis vs canonical tradition (asymmetry acknowledged)," or build Summa-2 adversarially so its proponent is not its referee.
    How to test: Pre-register win conditions; have an independent agent attempt to make Thomism defeat Summa-2 on Summa-2's own best ground. If the contest cannot be lost, it is not evidential.
