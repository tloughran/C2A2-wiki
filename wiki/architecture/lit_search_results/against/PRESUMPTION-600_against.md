  Sources:
    1. Statistical power / small-sample objection — the decisive challenge, and it is arithmetic rather than bibliographic. The item's own settling quantity is a comparison of INCORPORATE rates between an 8-item cohort with 1 conversion and a 4-item cohort with 0. A two-proportion comparison at 1/8 versus 0/4 has no discriminating power whatever: the exact test's p-value is 1.0, and a single additional conversion in either arm reverses the sign. The item names a measurement that cannot, at these cohort sizes, distinguish a filter effect from noise — so the presumption is not merely untested but, on its stated instrument, untestable. This challenges the item at the point where it is strongest elsewhere (it names a settling quantity) by showing the named quantity is not yet measurable.
    2. Deduplication literature in systematic review: McKeown, S. & Mir, Z.M. (2021), "Considerations for conducting systematic reviews: evaluating the performance of different methods for deduplicating references," and the 2024 follow-up in *Research Synthesis Methods* 15(1) (doi 10.1002/jrsm.1736); Rathbone, J. et al. (2015), "Better duplicate detection for systematic reviewers: evaluation of Systematic Review Assistant-Deduplication Module," *Systematic Reviews* 4:6; PRISMA-S (Rethlefsen et al. 2021, *Systematic Reviews* 10:39). — Challenges the framing directly. Redundancy filtering at intake is not merely permitted in the closest applied practice, it is *mandated* and separately reportable under PRISMA-S. The literature's stated concern about deduplication is exclusively false positives — erroneously removing unique eligible records — and the remedy prescribed is measuring and reporting the filter's error rate, not abandoning the filter. Applied here: the objection to the pre-check is not that it depresses yield but that its false-exclusion rate is unmeasured and unreported. That is a much narrower and more tractable finding than the item's.
    3. Little, J.D.C. (1961), "A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = λW," *Operations Research* 9(3):383-387, with the WIP-limit literature built on it (Kanban/lean practice; Anderson 2010). — Challenges the frame in which "yield per item" is the figure of merit. Admission control is the standard remedy for a queue whose service rate is the binding constraint, and its documented effect is to *reduce cycle time and raise completion rate* by limiting arrivals ahead of the bottleneck. C2A2's own diagnosis (PRESUMPTION-553 / MONITOR-484: a ~174-item backlog with an observed drain rate of zero) is exactly the condition under which the literature prescribes admission control. Judging such a filter by the residual cohort's per-item conversion rate is judging it by the wrong statistic; the relevant one is items dispositioned per unit of scarce reviewer capacity.
    4. Sequential-filter recall results, read against the item (e-discovery/TAR tradition). — The compounding-recall argument that supports the item also bounds it: composed recall is the product of stage recalls, so the size of the effect is governed by the pre-check's false-exclusion rate specifically, not by how many items it removed. A filter removing 9 of 13 with a 2% false-exclusion rate costs almost nothing; one removing 2 of 13 with a 50% false-exclusion rate costs a great deal. The item reasons from the removal fraction (9/13, salient) rather than from the error rate (unmeasured, decisive) — the same substitution of a rate for an effect that C2A2's own PRESUMPTION-588 file identified in Groves & Peytcheva.
    5. Wang, Z. et al. (2020), "Error rates of human reviewers during abstract screening in systematic reviews," *PLOS ONE* 15(1):e0227742 (PMC6959565). — Cuts both ways and is cited here for the direction that challenges. The measured total error rate at abstract screening (~10.8%) is the *combined* false-inclusion and false-exclusion rate for an unaided human screener. A filter with a 10% error rate operating on a 13-item cohort is expected to misroute roughly one item — which is not nothing, but is far below the magnitude the item's framing implies, and is comparable to the error rate C2A2 already tolerates at every other stage.
    6. Base-rate / Berkson-type objection, inverted. — Berkson's bias arises when conditioning on admission induces a *spurious association* used for inference about a population. The pre-check is not being used to infer anything about a population; it routes items to different treatment. Selection bias is a defect of *estimators*, and the INCORPORATE rate is being used here as an operational statistic, not an estimator of an underlying propensity. On that reading the item imports a statistical concept into a throughput setting where it has no target parameter to bias.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-600
    Strongest counterargument: The pre-check is doing exactly what the literature prescribes for C2A2's actual pathology, and the item is measuring it by a statistic chosen so as to make it look harmful. The system's disclosed condition is a backlog of roughly 174 items with an observed drain rate of zero for 22 days; under Little's Law the only levers are raising service rate or limiting arrivals, and admission control is the standard, well-evidenced application of the second. Judging it by residual per-item INCORPORATE rate rewards *removing* the filter, which would restore the arrival rate that produced the backlog. Worse, the comparison the item specifies — 1/8 against 0/4 — cannot separate signal from noise at any conventional standard; one conversion flips it. So the item proposes to indict a throughput measure on an underpowered efficiency statistic. And the deduplication literature, the nearest practice with a mandated redundancy filter, does not treat such filters as yield-depressing at all: its entire concern is false exclusion, whose remedy is to measure and report the filter's error rate, not to remove the filter. The honest residual finding is much smaller than the item: the pre-check's false-exclusion rate is unmeasured and unreported, and PRISMA-S-style reporting would require it. Everything else the item asserts is either unpowered or frame-dependent.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (i) the pre-check's false-exclusion rate is measured and is low — i.e. GOVERNED-CANDIDATE items, if forced through the full pipeline, would not have converted; (ii) the figure of merit is agreed in advance and is throughput-relative (dispositions per unit capacity), not per-item yield, or if per-item yield is retained, the cohorts are large enough to detect a difference; (iii) the filter's operation is reported per cycle, as PRISMA-S requires, so the removal is visible rather than inferred by a reader relating two facts held in one document.
    How to test: The decisive test is not a cohort comparison. Take a sample of GOVERNED-CANDIDATE items — the 9 removed on 07-29 are available — and run them through 15a/15b/15c blind to the pre-check's verdict. The conversion rate among items the filter removed IS the false-exclusion cost, measured directly, at n=9 rather than n=4, and it requires no matched cohort and no assumption about comparability across days. Report it alongside the removal count each cycle.

  Specific risks: If the item is acted on as stated — the pre-check weakened or removed because the residual cohort's yield looked low — C2A2 restores the arrival rate into a queue whose drain rate is zero, worsening the condition MONITOR-484 already flags, on the basis of a comparison with no power. The secondary risk is methodological and recurs across this batch: adopting a settling quantity that cannot be estimated at available cohort sizes gives the appearance of a testable claim while guaranteeing an inconclusive test, which then reads as "no effect found."

  Mitigations available: (a) Substitute the direct test above (run the removed items) for the cohort comparison; it is better powered, needs no matching, and answers the question the deduplication literature says matters. (b) Adopt PRISMA-S-style reporting of the pre-check as a standing convention: count in, count removed, criterion, and measured false-exclusion rate. (c) State the figure of merit before measuring. (d) Do not weaken the pre-check on the current evidence.

  SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG:
    Date: 2026-07-31
    Affected items: PRESUMPTION-591, PRESUMPTION-592, PRESUMPTION-600 (all three items in this batch)
    Common vulnerability: UNDERPOWERED SELF-MEASUREMENT. Each item is well-formed in that it names a settling quantity by type, per the REVISE-252 amendment — partition fraction, false-green rate, yield comparison. In all three cases the quantity is a rate or a proportion, and in all three cases the available denominator is single-digit: one observed still-running task (n=1), two cohorts of 8 and 4, and an UNCALIBRATED population whose size is not stated but whose per-item bound computation is a per-item judgment. A rate estimated on a single-digit denominator cannot discriminate the hypotheses the items pose, so each item is positioned to return "no effect found" for reasons of power rather than of fact — and a null returned for want of power is indistinguishable, in the register, from a null returned on evidence. This is a defect in the REVISE-252 convention as currently amended: naming a quantity by TYPE is a real improvement over naming it by value, but it does not require that the quantity be estimable at available sample sizes, and none of today's three are.
    Recommended systemic action: extend the REVISE-252 convention with a feasibility clause — a settling quantity must be accompanied by the denominator currently available and, where that denominator is single-digit, either a route to a larger one or an explicit acknowledgement that the item is not currently decidable. The alternative route is usually available and cheaper: for all three items in this batch, a direct per-item test on the existing population (attempt the bound; compare write-time to terminate-time; run the removed items) has a larger effective n than the cohort comparison the item specifies. Note this is the second batch-level instance of measurement design being the binding constraint (cf. REVISE-253, where both directions refuted the item's own remedy).

  Recommendation: CHALLENGED
