  Sources:
    1. Co-citation false positives (MacRoberts & MacRoberts critiques of citation analysis). - Shared references are often perfunctory, contrastive, or boilerplate; shared-id overlap does NOT entail conceptual integration.
    2. Construct-validity gap (Borgman & Furner). - Bibliometric overlap measures relatedness/co-occurrence, a weaker construct than 'functioning as one'.
    3. Shared-resource overlap can arise from common background citations (canonical references everyone cites) rather than genuine joint function.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: Partially challenged: while shared references proxy intellectual relatedness, the literature warns that the proxy has substantial false positives - citations/identifiers are often perfunctory or merely conventional, so two modules sharing a canonical id may not be 'functioning as one' in any integrative sense. The PASS-direction construct validity (does a shared-id match MEAN integration?) is exactly what is unexamined, and the bibliometric critiques suggest the false-positive rate could be high, especially for canonical/background resources cited by everyone.

  Specific risks: The integration detector could PASS on perfunctory/background-citation overlap, over-reporting 'modules functioning as one' and inflating synthesis coverage.

  Mitigations available: Measure false-positive rate against hand-labeled integration; down-weight canonical/background shared ids; require corroborating signals beyond a single shared id.
