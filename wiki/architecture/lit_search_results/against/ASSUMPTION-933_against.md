  Sources:
    1. Bruineberg, J., Dołęga, K., Dewhurst, J., & Baltieri, M. (2022). "The Emperor's New Markov Blankets." Behavioral and Brain Sciences. — The canonical "Markov blankets are not what you think" paper. Distinguishes "Pearl blankets" (an epistemic/instrumental device inside a modeller's already-specified probabilistic model) from "Friston blankets" (a purported physical/metaphysical boundary between agent and world), and argues that treating the latter as licensed by the former relies on unfounded assumptions. Challenges the ORIGINAL bridge claim (a phenomenal boundary read as a physical/statistical one) directly; it also undercuts the wiki's negative closure, because the closure adjudicates the same instrumental-vs-ontological question the paper says is routinely conflated, without ever specifying the model relative to which the blanket would or would not exist.
    2. Biehl, M., Pollock, F. A., & Kanai, R. (2021). "A Technical Critique of Some Parts of the Free Energy Principle." Entropy 23(3), 293 (arXiv:2001.06408). — Verified directly from the arXiv record. Proves that "various definitions of the 'Markov blanket' proposed in different works are not equivalent"; that the rewriting steps used in the free energy argument "are not generally correct without additional (previously unstated) assumptions"; and by counterexample that the original free energy lemma "when taken at face value, is wrong." Direct challenge to any yes/no verdict about whether some boundary "is" a Markov blanket: the predicate is not univocal, so a negative closure that names one reading and retires a claim on it is a closure against one definition among several.
    3. Raja, V., Valluri, D., Baggs, E., Chemero, A., & Anderson, M. L. (2021). "The Markov blanket trick: On the scope of the free energy principle and active inference." Physics of Life Reviews 39, 49–72. — Argues that the FEP's reach depends on a permissive move by which Markov blankets can be located almost anywhere by suitable coarse-graining, so their presence carries far less ontological information than claimed. Cuts both ways against ASSUMPTION-933: it deflates the original identity claim, and it means "not a Markov blanket" is a claim that requires an explicit dynamical specification the wiki never gave. Generated a large peer commentary set (PubMed 35963034, 35930909, 36150310), i.e. the question is live, not settled.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: I am challenging in two directions and they are not symmetric. **Primary direction — against the original bridge claim (FPD-009 and the Arkani-Hamed/Wolfram homology).** The literature is strong and adverse here: Bruineberg et al. show that reading a Markov blanket as a real boundary between a system and its world is exactly the move that the formalism does not license; Biehl et al. prove the definitions are not even equivalent across the FEP corpus; Chalmers and Albahari show the dissociation relatum is itself an unexplained primitive; and positive geometry is defined only for a supersymmetric toy theory while the ruliad is criticised as retrofittable and untested. Both bridges were weakly founded to begin with. **Secondary direction — against the negative closure's warrant.** The closure "it is not a Markov blanket, because dissociation is phenomenal inaccessibility rather than conditional independence" adjudicates on one of several inequivalent definitions, and does so without ever writing down the state space, the partition, or the dynamics relative to which conditional independence would be assessed — which is the precondition Raja et al. and Biehl et al. both insist on. The correct epistemic state is therefore not "closed negatively" but "not well posed as asked," which is a different and less comfortable position: the wiki now records a resolution where the literature supports only a dissolution. Nothing found rehabilitates the identity claim; the challenge is that a four-month HIGHEST-priority question was retired by a route that cannot deliver a verdict of either sign.

  Specific risks:
    - **Closure laundering.** A question retired as "answered negatively" is removed from the queue and stops attracting review, whereas "ill-posed, needs a specified formalism" would keep it live. The wiki has converted an open formal question into a settled fact on one run's reading, which is the precise failure mode 14a flagged.
    - **Definitional single-pick.** "Conditional independence" was treated as *the* Markov-blanket criterion. Biehl et al. show at least several inequivalent definitions are in circulation; a verdict against one is not a verdict against the predicate.
    - **Asymmetric burden.** The negative closure was accepted on a lower evidential standard than the positive claim ever was. Retirements are edits to the wiki's belief state and should carry the same burden as assertions.
    - **Non-comparability mistaken for opposed comparability.** Saying two restrictions "run in opposite informational directions" presupposes a shared informational space. Positive geometry (planar N=4 SYM amplitude positivity) and ruliad observerhood (computational-boundedness sampling of a rewriting limit) have no established common domain. The wiki has stated a relation between objects for which no relation is defined — and then reported the statement as a resolution.
    - **Downstream dependency.** FPD-009 stood at "Priority: HIGHEST" since April. Anything that was written while it stood, and anything written since on the strength of its retirement, inherits an unreviewed premise in one direction or the other.
    - **Authority borrowed from a contested framework.** Using "Wolfram's own new observerhood criterion" as the deciding test imports whatever unfalsifiability the criterion carries into the wiki's verdict.

  Mitigations available:
    - Reopen FPD-009 with status **ILL-POSED** rather than RETIRED, and record the closure and its reversal both as unreviewed single-run readings.
    - Before any further Markov-blanket claim (positive or negative), require the entry to name: (i) which definition of Markov blanket is in use, with citation; (ii) the state space and partition; (iii) the dynamics; (iv) whether the claim is instrumental (about a model) or ontological (about a system). Reject entries that omit any of the four. This is directly implementable as a template check.
    - Add a standing wiki convention distinguishing **Pearl blankets** from **Friston blankets** (Bruineberg et al.) and forbid inference from the first to the second.
    - Apply a **retirement burden rule**: retiring a HIGHEST-priority claim requires the same adversarial review as minting one. Retirements currently pass on a single reading.
    - For the Arkani-Hamed/Wolfram pair, downgrade to **NOT-COMPARABLE (no common domain established)** rather than "equivocation," and annotate that no literature relates the two frameworks in either direction.
    - Record explicitly in the Kastrup pages that the dissociation model faces the decombination problem and that its DID evidence base is contested, so the relatum's own standing is flagged wherever it is used in a bridge.

    Strongest counterargument: The wiki did not claim to settle a question in the physics or philosophy literature; it claimed only that a bridge *it had itself asserted* no longer holds up on inspection, which is an internal bookkeeping act and needs no external warrant. On that reading the closure is a self-correction and should be counted as a governance success: an unsupported claim that had sat at HIGHEST priority for four months was withdrawn rather than defended. Moreover, the direction of the closure is the conservative one — it removes a cross-tradition identity claim rather than adding one — so even if the reasoning is under-specified, the resulting belief state is strictly safer than the one it replaced. Requiring a fully specified state space and partition before permitting a *retraction* would make the wiki harder to shrink than to grow, which is the wrong incentive for a system already worried about manufactured convergence.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) The wiki's records must distinguish "we retract our claim" from "the claim is false," and 933's entries must read as the former, not the latter. (b) Retirements must not be treated as settled facts by downstream agents — i.e. nothing may be built on "dissociation is not a Markov blanket" as a positive premise. (c) The four-item Markov-blanket template above must be enforced going forward, so no future entry can restate either polarity without a specified formalism. (d) The Arkani-Hamed/Wolfram pair must be marked as having no established common domain, so that neither the homology nor its denial can be cited. (e) A retirement-burden rule must exist so that the asymmetry between minting and retiring is a deliberate policy rather than an accident of who ran that night.
