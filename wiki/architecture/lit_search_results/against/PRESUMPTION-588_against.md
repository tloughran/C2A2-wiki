  Sources:
    1. Manski, C.F. 1990. "Nonparametric Bounds on Treatment Effects." American Economic Review 80(2): 319-323. - The direct methodological refutation of the claim that a matched comparison is required before anything can be entered. Manski's worst-case bounds identify an interval for an average treatment effect using only the observed data plus the logical range of the outcome variable, imputing the extremes for the unobserved counterfactual - no assumption about the missingness mechanism, no matched arm, no untestable model. The bounds are typically wide, and that is the point: the honest entry for a quantity like "items attended to with versus without Chat context" is not UNCALIBRATED but an interval whose width is itself informative, because a wide interval that still excludes the decision-relevant threshold settles the question, and one that does not tells you exactly how much more instrumentation is needed. PRESUMPTION-588's settling quantity is a difference in an outcome under a present and an absent input, which is the canonical partial-identification object; a bound exists by construction from the outcome's logical range.
    2. Groves, R.M. 2006. "Nonresponse Rates and Nonresponse Bias in Household Surveys." Public Opinion Quarterly 70(5): 646-675. - Challenges the inference from "seven days of missing input" to "material degradation." Reviewing the empirical record, Groves concludes that nonresponse can but need not induce bias, that there is no minimum response rate below which estimates are necessarily biased, and that surveys with higher response rates have not been shown to produce different estimates - additional effort and funds buy higher response rates without demonstrated reductions in bias. The transferable structural finding is that the *quantity* of missing input is a poor predictor of the *magnitude* of distortion in the output, because the distortion depends on the covariance between missingness and the quantity of interest, not on the missingness rate. Applied here: the fact that the inbound channel has been dark since 07-24 licenses no inference about the size of the effect on what agents attended to, in either direction, and the presumption's framing treats duration as though it were severity.
    3. Molenberghs, G., Beunckens, C., Sotto, C. & Kenward, M.G. 2008. "Every missingness not at random model has a missingness at random counterpart with equal fit." Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B 70(2): 371-388. - Bounds the presumption's own remedy, and does so sharply: for any model in which the unobserved values influence the missingness process, there exists a model in which they do not that fits the observed data equally well. The mechanism is therefore not identifiable from observed data, which means the matched comparison PRESUMPTION-588 asks for would not settle the question either - even with one day's workload run with Chat context and one without, the inference from that contrast to "what the outage cost" requires unverifiable assumptions about whether the days were exchangeable. The discipline's response is not measurement but sensitivity analysis: state the assumption set, report how the conclusion moves across it, and treat the range as the finding. This converts the item from "we cannot measure it, so record UNCALIBRATED" into "no measurement can identify it, so the deliverable is a stated range over assumptions" - a stronger and more honest position than either the original entry or the presumption's proposed correction.
    4. VanderWeele, T.J. & Ding, P. 2017. "Sensitivity Analysis in Observational Research: Introducing the E-Value." Annals of Internal Medicine 167(4): 268-274. - Supplies a ready-made method for bounding the influence of something you cannot observe, which is precisely the narrowed question. The E-value is the minimum strength of association an unmeasured factor would need with both the exposure and the outcome to fully explain away an observed association. Inverted for this case, the analogous computation is: how strong would the Chat-context input have to have been - how many additional items attended to, or how large a redirection of the day's agenda - for its absence to have changed the day's dispositions. That number is computable from the day's recorded output alone, requires no counterfactual day, and yields a falsifiable claim of the form "the missing input would have had to redirect more than k percent of attended items to matter." This is exactly the calibration the presumption says is unavailable, obtained without the comparison it says is necessary.
    5. Liu, N.F., Lin, K., Hewitt, J., Paranjape, A., Bevilacqua, M., Petroni, F. & Liang, P. 2024. "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts." Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics 12: 157-173. - Challenges the presumption's unstated premise that the missing input had positive expected value. Across multi-document question answering and key-value retrieval, performance varied substantially with the *position* of relevant information in the context and degraded when relevant content sat in the middle of long inputs; models did not robustly use information in long contexts. Since the missing input here is prior-day conversational context prepended to agents that already receive substantial file-based context, the sign of its contribution is not given: added context can displace attention from the material that carries the day's task, and the effect is empirically position- and length-dependent rather than monotonically beneficial. The presumption is right that the cost is unmeasured; it is not entitled to assume the unmeasured quantity is a loss rather than a mixed or negligible term.
    6. Chandra, T.D. & Toueg, S. 1996. "Unreliable Failure Detectors for Reliable Distributed Systems." Journal of the ACM 43(2): 225-267. - Refutes the structural claim that "an input that never arrives leaves no trace." Failure detection in asynchronous systems is a formalised problem with a standard solution shape: a detector is characterised by completeness (every crashed process is eventually suspected) and accuracy (correct processes are not permanently suspected), and Chandra and Toueg establish which detector classes suffice for consensus even when the detector makes infinitely many mistakes. The relevant lesson is that absence becomes detectable the moment something is instrumented to expect a signal - a heartbeat, a sequence number, a timeout - and that the resulting detector need not be perfect to be useful. The inbound channel's silence left no trace only because nothing on the receiving side asserted an expectation; a per-run assertion that a dated context artefact was present, logged with its date, converts seven invisible days into seven logged omissions at negligible cost, and turns the presumption's structural claim into a solved engineering problem.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: On the narrowed question the challenge is strong and largely dispositive: methods for bounding the cost of an input that never arrived are numerous, established, and none of them requires the matched comparison the item names as the settling quantity. Manski's worst-case bounds give an interval from the outcome's logical range alone; the E-value approach yields a falsifiable threshold statement - how consequential the missing input would have had to be to change the day's dispositions - computable from existing output; and Chandra and Toueg's failure-detector framework shows that undetectable omission is a property of uninstrumented receivers, not of omission. Two sources cut further and complicate the presumption's premises rather than just its remedy: Groves shows that the amount of missing input is a poor predictor of the size of distortion, so seven dark days licenses no magnitude claim; and Liu et al. show that added long-context input is not monotonically beneficial, so the sign of the missing term is not given either. Molenberghs et al. then bound the presumption's own proposed fix, since the missingness mechanism is not identifiable from observed data and the matched comparison would still rest on an unverifiable exchangeability assumption. What survives the challenge is the item's core observation, and it survives cleanly: nothing was in fact instrumented, so the record does contain no bound, and "the file is the deliverable" does not address the inbound direction. What falls is the conclusion - UNCALIBRATED is not the honest entry, because a bound is cheaply obtainable and the honest entry is that bound plus its width.

  Specific risks: If the presumption is over-read, C2A2 adopts a standard under which any unmeasured input counts as an unbounded cost, which is not conservatism but a systematic upward bias on the severity of anything not yet instrumented - and because the pipeline generates its own items, that bias is self-amplifying: every uninstrumented channel becomes a HIGH-risk entry, the queue fills with unbounded costs, and the genuinely large ones lose their distinctiveness. A specific version of this is over-crediting the missing input: if the pipeline concludes that seven days of output were produced without necessary steering, it may discount or re-do sound work on the basis of a quantity nobody bounded, and Liu et al. make it live that the missing context's contribution could have been neutral or mildly negative. If the presumption is under-read, the failure mode is the one it names and it is real: nothing on the receiving side asserts that a dated context artefact arrived, so an omission that recurs indefinitely produces no entry, and the pipeline's daily summaries will continue to report no degradation because no observable is defined whose value could differ - the specific cost is that the *duration* of any future inbound outage becomes unknowable after the fact, since there is no per-run record of what was present.

  Mitigations available: Instrument the receiver first, because it is the cheapest and it forecloses recurrence: each agent run asserts at start whether a dated inbound context artefact was present and logs the date, which yields a per-run presence series and makes any future outage self-documenting - this is the Chandra-Toueg heartbeat applied at negligible cost and it does not require the outage to be fixed. Then bound the historical cost without waiting for restoration, using two computations that need only existing records: a Manski-style worst-case interval on the day's primary output measure (items attended to, or dispositions made), imputing the extremes for the counterfactual; and an E-value-style threshold statement of how large a redirection of attention the missing input would have had to cause to change any recorded disposition. Record the result as a bound with its width and the assumption set, per Molenberghs et al., not as a point estimate - and explicitly not as UNCALIBRATED. If the matched comparison is later run, pre-specify the outcome measure and the exchangeability assumptions before the comparison day, since Groves's finding implies the contrast will be uninformative about magnitude unless the outcome is chosen in advance; and pre-register the possibility of a null or negative effect so that Liu et al.'s prediction is testable rather than unaskable.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-588
    Strongest counterargument (against the presumption): The narrowed claim is that without a matched comparison the cost of a missing input cannot be bounded and the honest entry is UNCALIBRATED, and that claim is false as a matter of established method. Partial identification gives a worst-case interval from the outcome's logical range with no assumption about the missingness mechanism and no counterfactual arm; the E-value construction gives a falsifiable threshold - how consequential the absent input would have had to be to overturn a recorded conclusion - computable from output already in hand; and the failure-detector literature shows that absence is detectable as soon as a receiver asserts an expectation, so "an input that never arrives leaves no trace" describes a missing assertion rather than an epistemic barrier. Worse for the presumption, the matched comparison it names as the settling quantity would not settle it: Molenberghs et al. prove the missingness mechanism is unidentifiable from observed data, so the contrast between a day with context and a day without still rests on an unverifiable exchangeability assumption, and the terminal deliverable is a sensitivity range either way. Meanwhile Groves shows the amount of missing input predicts the magnitude of distortion badly, and Liu et al. show that additional long-context input is not monotonically helpful - so the presumption simultaneously assumes the missing term is a loss and that its size scales with duration, and neither assumption is supported. Recording UNCALIBRATED where a bound is cheaply available is not epistemic humility; it is a refusal to compute, and in a self-auditing pipeline it converts every uninstrumented channel into an item of unbounded severity.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (i) a per-run presence assertion exists for inbound context, so omissions are logged rather than inferred; (ii) any item claiming a cost states either a bound or the method by which a bound would be obtained, and UNCALIBRATED is not accepted as a terminal entry where a worst-case bound is computable; (iii) if a matched comparison is run, its outcome measure and exchangeability assumptions are pre-specified and a null result is admissible; and (iv) the pipeline records the *sign* of the missing input's expected contribution as an open question rather than assuming it is a loss.
    How to test: Three tests, in ascending cost. First, compute the worst-case bound now from existing records: pick the day's primary countable output (dispositions made, items attended to), impute best and worst plausible counterfactual values, and report the interval - if it excludes any threshold that would change a decision, the item is settled without further work. Second, compute the threshold statement: identify the day's dispositions and ask what fraction of attended items would have had to be different for any disposition to flip; that number is the calibration the item says does not exist, and it is derivable from the output alone. Third, and only if the first two leave the question open, run the matched comparison - but run it as a three-arm contrast, not two: a day with inbound context, a day without, and a day with deliberately *inflated* inbound context, because Liu et al.'s finding predicts a non-monotonic response and a two-arm design cannot detect it. A fourth check is free and should be done first: retrospectively grep the seven dark days' outputs for items whose content plausibly required prior-day conversational context, and count them. Zero such items would substantially challenge the presumption; a nonzero count would give it a lower bound rather than an unmeasured cost, which is precisely the entry it should have had.
