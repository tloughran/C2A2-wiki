### Specific risks (of adopting the corrective as framed)

1. **Building response capacity for unvalidated signals.** If detection investment is cut while precision is unmeasured, the fleet builds closure throughput for a stream whose per-item value is unknown. PREMISE-121 measures the consequence in the nearest analogue: override rates of 49–96%, with degradation concentrated on **low-information items**, and desensitisation that **generalises** — true positives discounted alongside false ones. Adding closure capacity to a low-precision stream trains the human to discount everything.
2. **Blinding the fleet mid-repair.** The three dead launchd agents were invisible until a better *detector* was written. PREMISE-086 was ACTIVE and unenforced on that surface, and only a detection improvement revealed it. Deprioritising detection while such enforcement gaps exist means the fleet stops discovering the ones it has not found — and it has no estimate of how many that is.
3. **Skipping *Exploit* and going straight to *Elevate*.** The cheapest available moves — admission control, TTL sweeps, a one-off drain pass, raising per-item information value — are all named in the register and none is in 812's remedy space. Building closure capacity before exhausting them is the expensive option chosen first.
4. **A misdirected target from a category error.** If the 1,105:78 ratio is accepted as a measure of misallocation, the implied goal is a decision per detection. That target is achievable by lowering the decision bar, which converts a knowledge register into a rubber stamp — the exact failure PREMISE-121's override-rate literature describes, and Goodhart's law applied to the closure pipeline.
5. **Register duplication and false independence.** 812 restates PREMISE-102 and a weaker form of PREMISE-106. Minting it separately risks the pipeline citing them later as independent corroboration (PREMISE-111, PREMISE-120).

Theory of Constraints begins with *Identify the Constraint* — identification is measurement, and measurement is detection — and it puts *Exploit* before *Elevate*: extract more from the closure capacity you already have, by prioritisation and admission control, before building more of it. 812 skips exploit entirely. Under TOC, detection is not the rival of closure; it is the instrument that aims it.
