  Sources:
    1. Sikorski, T. & Hammer, D., 2010. "A critique of how learning progressions research conceptualizes sophistication and progress." ICLS Proceedings. — Learning progressions impose linear ladder structure on knowledge that is contextual and dynamic; ladder-implied milestones can misrepresent what competence actually looks like.
    2. Shavelson, R. & Kurpius, A., 2012 (in "Learning Progressions in Science"). — Learning progressions are conjectural models requiring empirical validation; curriculum-derived sequences frequently fail empirical recovery, with students not occupying the hypothesized levels.
    3. "Empirical recovery of learning progressions through the lens of educators." Journal of Mathematical Behavior, 2020 (ScienceDirect). — Even educator-endorsed progressions show imperfect empirical recovery; expert plausibility of a ladder does not establish that its milestones are real stages.
    4. Achieve, 2015. "The Role of Learning Progressions in Competency-Based Pathways." — Distinguishes empirically validated progressions from curriculum-coherence artifacts; warns that curriculum-implied milestones reflect instructional convention, not necessarily the structure of the domain.
  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: The learning-progressions literature supports using ladder tools as hypothesis generators but challenges treating their implied milestones as well-formed candidates without acknowledging two failure modes. First, curriculum-derived milestones encode instructional convention and authorial assumption; empirical validation studies regularly fail to recover hypothesized levels. Second, the ladder format itself (linear, discrete, monotone) is contested — critics argue real conceptual development is contextual and non-linear, so ladder-implied milestones can be artifacts of the representation rather than of the domain. Since ASSUMPTION-296 only claims candidacy ("candidate PRS-elements" subject to dyad ratification), the challenge is to adequacy of the filter, not the sourcing: ratification by the same intelligence that authored the ladder (per ASSUMPTION-293/295 problems) is a weak validation step for milestones already shaped by that intelligence's conventions.
  Specific risks: PRS inherits the ladder's linearity and granularity assumptions wholesale; dyad ratification rubber-stamps representation artifacts as milestones; later empirical work (or external critics) finds the milestone set unrecoverable.
  Mitigations available: Tag PRS-elements with provenance (curriculum-derived vs independently evidenced); require at least one non-ladder source of support before promotion past candidate status; allow non-linear/partial-ordering structure in PRS rather than forcing ladder order.
  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: Every validated learning progression in the literature started as exactly this — an expert-conjectured sequence later refined empirically. Using ladder tools to generate candidates is the standard, recommended first step (hypothesis, then validation), and the claim explicitly stops at "candidate," with ratification as a separate gate. The alternative (no structured candidate source) is worse.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Candidacy is genuinely defeasible in practice (some candidates actually get rejected at ratification); the ladder's ordering assumptions are marked as conjecture; the PRS schema can represent non-linear structure.
    How to test: Track the rejection/modification rate of ladder-derived candidates at dyad ratification. A near-100% pass rate indicates the ratification gate is not functioning and the ladder is being transcribed, not tested.
