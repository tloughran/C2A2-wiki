### Summary

Searching against this item produced an unusual result: the metrology standard supports the transfer the brief asked me to attack, and then undermines the conclusion drawn from it. VIM3 §2.1 NOTE 2 states outright that measurement "implies comparison of quantities **or counting of entities**," so measurand doctrine does cover counts of discrete records and 14b's transfer is legitimate — I searched for the boundary and did not find it. But VIM3 §2.27 shows what the doctrine then requires. Under-definition is not a licence for plural answers; it is a *floor on achievable uncertainty*, and "any change in the descriptive detail leads to another definitional uncertainty" — the prescribed move is to add detail until the floor is below target, which for a deterministic line-count over a frozen file drives it to zero. The standards prescribe the definitional work that yields a single number; they do not stop at "both correct." This vault has already reached the same place independently: PREMISE-114's exit clause says that where a quantity is deterministic over a frozen snapshot the disagreement is definitional, the remedy is to write the counting rule and re-derive, and **convergence is the expected outcome**. The queue counter counts lines in a file. It is that class of quantity. So the clause "the demand for a single number is malformed" is contradicted, for the item's own flagship instance, by an ACTIVE premise. The item's evidence points the same way once read as a defect report rather than a taxonomy: neither series counts items, one under-counts by 506, the other returns 2,352 occurrences against 2,175 lines and is "not even a stable line count." Two instruments both failing to measure the target construct, one of them internally incoherent, is a defect finding — and it was produced *by* the reconciliation attempt the item says cannot succeed. Data-reconciliation practice treats count divergence as a routine first-class control with a differential diagnosis attached, and ISA 530.13's anomalous-misstatement provision supplies the governance form: a "not representative of the population" disposition is available but carries a high burden of demonstration, and asserting it is not discharging it. The unfalsifiability worry is real and Popper gives it a usable edge — auxiliary moves should be judged by whether they *increase testability*. "Different measurands, here they are in writing" predicts both numbers exactly and is checkable in one run. "Different measurands" as a bare disposition explains every discrepancy equally well and predicts nothing. **What survives, and survives well, is the triage question the item raises but does not answer:** PREMISE-161's binding scope limit (b) already holds that a live growing quantity is not deterministic over a frozen snapshot and that forcing such readings to one definition destroys information. That is exactly right, and it is the criterion the four remaining disputes need — not a blanket both-correct doctrine.

### Specific risks

**The unfalsifiable-escape risk, and it is the main one.** If "two undefined measurands, both correct" enters the architecture as a general disposition rather than as a conclusion earned per-instance, the fleet acquires a response available to every future numerical disagreement, at zero cost, with no prediction attached. The register already has the antidote and it would be bypassed: PREMISE-161 requires an unarbitrated disagreement to be **RECORDED AS A FINDING** and treats a decision taken across it as **UNDECIDABLE, not conservative**. A both-correct doctrine converts "undecidable" into "resolved," which is the precise inversion 161 was minted to prevent. Note the item is currently well-behaved on this — it declares its own partial resolution — but the *statement* generalises further than the *evidence*, and statements are what propagate.

**The lost-defect risk.** In the queue-counter instance the both-correct framing would have retired two genuine instrument faults as definitional variety. A series returning more occurrences than there are lines is broken by inspection. If the four remaining disputes are dispositioned by frame rather than by inspection, faults of the same kind go unrepaired and the affected figures keep being published under PREMISE-117's publish-then-revise licence with no correction ever issued.

**The register-conflict risk.** If 815's statement is promoted as written it stands in direct tension with PREMISE-114's exit clause for frozen-snapshot quantities. Two ACTIVE items giving opposite guidance for the same quantity class is worse than either alone, because the next reader will apply whichever they grep first. PREMISE-138 bars re-minting; this needs reconciling into 114 rather than standing beside it.

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** The diagnosis is right and it is not small. Five consecutive days of same-population-different-count reports, and **no run stated the measurand** on any of them — that is the finding, it is uncontested by anything in this file, and it is the reason the queue-counter instance took five days to resolve rather than one. PREMISE-161's scope limit (b) also means the item is straightforwardly *correct* for any time-varying quantity in the disputed set, and at least one of the four (the review-queue dispute, 21..35, spanning days) is a prime candidate. Dismissing 815 wholesale would discard a correct rule along with an over-general one.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Triage the four remaining disputes by PREMISE-161's scope-limit criterion before dispositioning any of them.** For each: is the underlying quantity deterministic over a frozen snapshot, or time-varying? Frozen → PREMISE-114's exit (write the definition, designate it the reference, re-derive; expect convergence). Time-varying → PREMISE-165's as-of stamp and 14b's both-correct reading. This is the item's real contribution and it is a half-hour of work.
(b) **Require a written measurand before any "both correct" disposition is accepted** — the ISA 530.13 burden in operational form. Both definitions in writing, each shown internally consistent, each shown to reproduce its own figure on a frozen snapshot. Under this rule the loose queue series fails immediately (2,352 > 2,175) and is correctly classified as a defect.
(c) **Adopt Popper's testability criterion as the acceptance test for the disposition**, because it is cheap and it discriminates: a both-correct claim is admissible only if it makes a checkable prediction. If it predicts nothing, it is an immunizing stratagem and must be refused.
(d) **Do not promote 815 as a standalone premise.** Reconcile it into PREMISE-114 as a scope note — 114's exit holds for frozen-snapshot quantities; 161's limit (b) holds for time-varying ones — rather than minting a third item that contradicts the first.
(e) **Repair the two queue-counter instruments rather than retiring them as measurands.** Both are defective by the item's own evidence; neither counts items; the fleet needs one that does.
(f) **Record the five-day cost.** PREMISE-161 already requires unarbitrated disagreements to be logged as findings. Five consecutive days with no measurand stated is itself the finding, and it should be recorded once rather than rediscovered a sixth time.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-815

**Strongest counterargument:** The standards the item appeals to say the opposite of what it concludes, and the vault has already worked this out for exactly this class of quantity. Start with the concession, because it is real and it is the item's: VIM3 §2.1 NOTE 2 says measurement "implies comparison of quantities or counting of entities," so measurand doctrine genuinely does reach counts of discrete records, and the objection that metrology does not transfer to counting does not survive contact with the text. But VIM3 §2.27 is the clause that decides the matter. An under-defined measurand does not produce two correct answers; it produces a **floor on achievable uncertainty** — "the practical minimum measurement uncertainty achievable in any measurement of a given measurand" — and "any change in the descriptive detail leads to another definitional uncertainty." The standard's remedy for under-definition is to *add detail*, which lowers the floor, and for a deterministic line count over a frozen file the floor goes to zero. The doctrine the item invokes prescribes precisely the definitional labour that terminates in a single number. It never says stop at "both correct." This vault reached the same conclusion by a different route three weeks ago and wrote it down: PREMISE-114's load-bearing exit holds that where the quantity is deterministic over a frozen snapshot the disagreement is almost certainly definitional, that the procedure is to write the counting rule, designate it the reference and re-derive, and that **convergence is the expected outcome and arbitration is not needed.** A queue counter counts lines in a file; a file is frozen; so for the item's own flagship instance an ACTIVE premise says a single number is reachable, which is the one thing "the demand for a single number is malformed" denies. Read the item's evidence as a defect report rather than a taxonomy and it says the same thing: neither series counts items, the strict series misses 506 lines, the loose series returns 2,352 occurrences against 2,175 lines and is "not even a stable line count." An instrument that finds more of a thing than there are places for it to be is broken, not differently-scoped, and the thing that exposed both faults was the reconciliation attempt the item says cannot succeed. Data-reconciliation practice runs row-count checks after every load as a matter of routine and carries a differential diagnosis — counts matching but totals not matching points at transformation faults rather than missing rows — so the field the item's brief expected to be silent has in fact worked out how to distinguish the cases. ISA 530.13 supplies the governance form: an auditor *may* declare a misstatement anomalous and unrepresentative, but must obtain a high degree of certainty it is truly isolated. "Different measurands" is that move, and it carries that burden; asserting it is not discharging it. And the unfalsifiability worry has a sharp criterion rather than just a name — Popper's advice is that auxiliary hypotheses be judged by whether they *increase testability*. Written measurands increase it, because they predict both figures exactly and can be checked in one run. An unwritten both-correct disposition decreases it, because it fits every discrepancy that will ever arise and forbids none. The register's own guard, PREMISE-161, requires an unarbitrated disagreement to be recorded as a finding and a decision across it to be called UNDECIDABLE rather than conservative — and a both-correct doctrine silently converts UNDECIDABLE into RESOLVED, which is the exact inversion 161 exists to prevent. **What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** The "both correct" disposition is safe exactly where the measured quantity is **not deterministic over a frozen snapshot** — a live growing corpus, a count taken at different times, a sampled or eventually-consistent metric — and only when both measurands have been **written down**, each shown internally consistent, and each shown to reproduce its own figure on a fixed input. Where the quantity *is* frozen and deterministic, PREMISE-114's exit governs, one definition must be designated the reference, and the disagreement is a defect until re-derivation converges. So safety requires (i) the frozen-versus-time-varying triage to be performed per dispute and recorded, (ii) a written-measurand precondition on every both-correct disposition, (iii) the disposition to make a checkable prediction, and (iv) 815 to be reconciled into PREMISE-114 rather than standing beside it, since two ACTIVE items giving opposite guidance for one quantity class is worse than either.

**How to test:** (1) **The one-run reproduction test, and it is decisive.** Freeze the queue file. Write both counting rules explicitly. Re-derive. If both definitions reproduce their figures exactly, they are genuine distinct measurands and 14b is right for that instance; if either fails to reproduce — and the loose series will, since it already returns more occurrences than lines — it is a defective instrument and the both-correct framing is wrong for it. Minutes of work, and it discriminates the two hypotheses the register is arguing about. (2) **Triage the four open disputes** on the frozen/time-varying criterion and record the classification before dispositioning. (3) **Apply the testability filter**: for each proposed both-correct disposition, state one observation that would refute it. Any disposition with no such observation is an immunizing stratagem by Popper's own criterion and should be refused. (4) **Build the instrument nobody has**: a counter that counts *items* against a written definition, and check whether it converges with either existing series. If it converges with neither, both are defects and the item's fifth-day framing was the wrong lens throughout.
