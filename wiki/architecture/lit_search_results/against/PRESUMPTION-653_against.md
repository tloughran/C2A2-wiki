  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The governance literature does support the challenge — its standard remedy
    for conflicting figures is to assign an owner and a canonical definition, and it
    explicitly frames recurring divergence as an accountability failure rather than an
    arithmetic one. The semantic-fragmentation point is the sharpest: reconciling the
    number without reconciling the definition guarantees the divergence returns, which
    is consistent with ten figures appearing for five quantities in one day rather than
    one figure drifting slowly. However, the evidence base is largely practitioner-grade
    — vendor blogs, consulting guidance, one book chapter — rather than empirical study,
    and a substantial dissenting strand argues that multiple context-dependent truths are
    legitimate and that forcing a single number destroys information. The challenge is
    therefore real but not conclusively evidenced: the literature says ownership is the
    right frame, but does not empirically demonstrate that unowned duplicate metrics
    diverge over time in the way the item implies.

  Specific risks: If the quantities are genuinely unowned, reconciliation is a treadmill:
    each day produces new pairs of figures, each is settled by whoever is looking, and
    the settlement has no durability because no role is responsible for maintaining it.
    Institutionalising the disagreement is worse than it looks, because it converts a
    detectable defect into an accepted feature — downstream consumers can no longer tell
    whether a discrepancy is the known one or a new one, so the anomaly detector for this
    class of problem is permanently disabled. Any downstream decision, guard threshold, or
    trend that consumes one of the two figures is silently sensitive to which one it got.
    The ten-in-one-day rate suggests the generation process is ongoing, so the number of
    affected artifacts grows monotonically.

  Mitigations available: (1) Name an owner per quantity — a single agent, file, or
    function that is the authoritative producer — and make every other site of that
    number a derived reference rather than an independent computation. This is the
    literature's actual prescription. (2) Publish the definition alongside the number
    (what is counted, over what window, with what filters); most divergences resolve
    into two different definitions once written down. (3) Instrument divergence rather
    than accepting it: emit a check that recomputes each quantity by both paths and
    records the delta over time. If the delta is stable, the two definitions differ; if
    it wanders, at least one path is broken. (4) Set a policy that an unresolved
    divergence expires — a disagreement that cannot be closed within a fixed window is
    escalated as an ownership gap, not filed as a standing condition. (5) Count the
    divergence rate as a first-class health metric; ten per day is itself the signal.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-653
    Strongest counterargument: Multiple figures for one quantity are frequently correct
      and informative — different windows, different filters, different inclusion rules
      answer different questions, and the dissenting literature explicitly warns that
      forcing a single source of truth discards legitimate contextual variation and
      creates a false authority. In a small system, appointing an owner for every
      quantity is heavy governance overhead that will not be sustained by a single
      operator, and the practical result of ownership-by-decree is that the owner rubber
      stamps whichever number is convenient. Reconciliation by declaration, plus a
      recorded note of the disagreement, is a cheap and honest response that preserves
      the information that the two paths differ — which a forced single number would
      erase.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) The divergences are explicable
      by definitional differences that are written down, so a reader can tell which
      figure answers which question. (b) The number of divergent quantities is stable
      rather than growing. (c) No downstream artifact silently consumes one figure while
      another artifact consumes the other. (d) The institutionalised disagreements are
      individually enumerated and dated, not aggregated into a general caveat.
    How to test: Take the five quantities. For each, write down the exact computation
      behind both figures. If both computations are recoverable and differ in a way that
      explains the gap, the reconciliation frame is adequate. If either computation
      cannot be located or reproduced, the ownership frame is correct. Second test:
      recount the divergence rate a week later; a rising count falsifies the "one-off
      reconciliation" reading.
