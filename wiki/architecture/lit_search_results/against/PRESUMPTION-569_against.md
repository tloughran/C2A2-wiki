  Sources:
    1. Levitt, S.D. & List, J.A. 2011. "Was There Really a Hawthorne Effect at the Hawthorne Plant? An Analysis of the Original Illumination Experiments." American Economic Journal: Applied Economics 3(1): 224-238. — Recovered and analysed the original illumination data, long thought destroyed and never formally analysed, and found that "existing descriptions of supposedly remarkable data patterns prove to be entirely fictional." All lighting changes fell on Sundays, the only off day; output was high on Mondays whether or not a change had occurred. The canonical reactivity finding was a day-of-week artefact misread as reactivity. Only weak evidence of subtler Hawthorne effects survived. Directly challenges reactivity-under-observation as a default explanation for a post-intervention shift.
    2. McCambridge, J., Witton, J. & Elbourne, D.R. 2014. "Systematic review of the Hawthorne effect: new concepts are needed to study research participation effects." Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 67(3): 267-277. — Nineteen purposively designed studies (8 RCTs, 5 quasi-experimental, 6 observational). Concludes there are small but statistically significant effects, with "wide inconsistencies in this evidence making it difficult to draw clear conclusions," and that the Hawthorne label itself is too imprecise to be useful. The evidential base for the presumption's alternative explanation is weak and heterogeneous, not a reliable prior.
    3. Jonsson, A. & Svingby, G. 2007. "The use of scoring rubrics: Reliability, validity and educational consequences." Educational Research Review 2(2): 130-144. (Review of 75 empirical studies.) — Reliable scoring of performance assessments is genuinely enhanced by explicit rubrics, "especially if they are analytic, topic-specific, and complemented with exemplars and/or rater training," because rubrics establish a relatively common interpretation and add restrictions to judgement. An immediate change in judgement rates after an explicit criterion is introduced is the predicted and documented effect of introducing the criterion — the favourable reading is a live empirical hypothesis, not a mere self-flattering assumption. The review's second finding is the constraint: rubrics do not thereby produce *valid* judgement, and validity has to be established separately.
    4. Spiegelhalter, D.J. 2005. "Funnel plots for comparing institutional performance." Statistics in Medicine 24(8): 1185-1202. — The standard treatment of exactly this inferential situation: apparent large ratios between small counts fall inside control limits and carry no signal, which is why funnel plots plot the estimate against its precision and "avoid spurious ranking into league tables," and why overdispersion adjustment is needed before any unit is called an outlier. A move from 1 to 5 on counts of this size sits well within Poisson variation. This challenges the *shared framing* of the note and the presumption: "fivefold rise" is not a fact about the pipeline, it is an artefact of dividing small numbers.
    5. Lopez Bernal, J., Cummins, S. & Gasparrini, A. 2017. "Interrupted time series regression for the evaluation of public health interventions: a tutorial." International Journal of Epidemiology 46(1): 348-355. — Sets out what is required to attribute a level change to an intervention at a defined point in time: adequate pre- and post-intervention observations, explicit modelling of the underlying secular trend, and control for seasonality and co-occurring events, with level (intercept) and slope changes distinguished. A single post-intervention observation cannot separate a level change from the pre-existing trend or from noise. Neither the note's causal reading nor the presumption's application-effect reading is licensed by n=1 after the change.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The presumption is right that the note's causal reading is unearned, but its own alternative fares no better and its shared premise fails first. The reactivity explanation the presumption gestures at is the weakest available default: Levitt and List found the founding Hawthorne evidence to be an artefact of the day of week, and McCambridge et al.'s systematic review finds only small, wildly inconsistent effects and recommends abandoning the label. Meanwhile the note's own explanation is a documented mechanism, not wishful thinking — the rubric literature finds that explicit, topic-specific criteria measurably change and tighten judgement, immediately, which is exactly the shape of the observation. The decisive problem is that neither reading is licensed by the data. On counts this small, a 1-to-5 movement sits inside ordinary Poisson variation (Spiegelhalter's whole point in recommending funnel plots over league tables), and attributing a level change to an intervention requires pre/post series with the secular trend modelled, which a single post-change run cannot supply (Lopez Bernal et al.). So the challenge to the presumption is that it grants the number more evidential status than it has, in order to contest the direction of the inference — when the correct finding is that the number is not yet evidence in either direction. What survives, and is not challenged by anything located, is the structural point: the run in question was itself under judgement on whether it can adopt premises about its own reliability, and self-assessed effectiveness of a self-imposed bar on that run is not independent evidence.

  Specific risks: If the presumption is over-read, C2A2 acquires a reflex of discounting any favourable post-change movement as reactivity or self-serving application — an explanation the empirical literature says is small, inconsistent, and in its founding case fictional. That reflex would make the system unable to register that any of its own corrections ever worked, which is a failure mode symmetrical with the one being guarded against, and would suppress a mechanism (explicit criteria tighten judgement) that has 75 studies behind it. If the presumption is under-read, a run-to-run count movement that is statistically indistinguishable from noise is filed as evidence that a self-imposed bar functions, on the very run in which the pipeline's competence to adopt premises about its own reliability was the question at issue — and that unearned validation then licenses further self-referential adoptions. The common risk to both readings is treating an n=1 count difference as a finding at all.

  Mitigations available: Do the in-house adjunct already specified — apply the external-referent bar retrospectively to the last 20 MONITOR dispositions and count how many would now clear it. That converts a two-point comparison into a retrospective series with an unchanged decision context, which is the closest available approximation to the pre-intervention arm Lopez Bernal et al. require, and it is blind to the current run's incentives. Before that, stop calling it a fivefold rise: plot the INCORPORATE count against its denominator with control limits (Spiegelhalter) and record whether the point is inside them; if it is, the anomaly does not exist and neither explanation is needed. If a series is accumulated, model the underlying trend before attributing any level change. Separately, apply the Jonsson & Svingby constraint: a criterion that raises inter-rater consistency has not thereby been shown valid, so test whether the items admitted under the bar have better external referents on independent inspection, not merely whether more of them were admitted. And decouple the self-referential loop: have the bar's effect assessed on a run where the pipeline's own reliability is not the item under judgement.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-569
    Strongest counterargument (against the presumption): The presumption asks us to prefer a reactivity or self-serving-application reading over the plain mechanistic one, but that preference has the evidence backwards. The founding Hawthorne result dissolved on reanalysis into a day-of-week artefact, and the best systematic review finds effects that are small, inconsistent, and conceptually too vague to carry weight — so "they behaved differently because they were being judged" is the weaker hypothesis, not the sceptical default it is usually treated as. The rival hypothesis, by contrast, is well documented: making a criterion explicit changes and tightens judgement immediately, across 75 studies. And the presumption's own framing concedes too much to the number it is contesting — describing 1 to 5 as a "fivefold rise" treats small-count noise as a signal, which is exactly the error funnel plots were designed to prevent, and attributing any level change to an intervention from a single post-change observation is inadmissible under standard interrupted-time-series requirements. The presumption is thus over-reading the data in order to under-read the explanation.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the count movement is shown to lie outside control limits for its denominator, so that there is an anomaly to explain at all; (b) the bar's effect is estimated over a series rather than a single post-change run, with the underlying trend in INCORPORATE rate modelled; (c) the retrospective application of the bar to prior MONITOR dispositions produces a pass rate consistent with the current run's, indicating the bar is doing the same work when applied blind to the current run's incentives; (d) admitted items are checked for actual external-referent quality, not just for having been admitted under a criterion that names external referents; (e) the assessment of whether the bar works is made on a run where the pipeline's own reliability is not the matter under judgement.
    How to test: First, the noise test — compute the INCORPORATE rate with its denominator for each of the last N runs and plot against precision with Spiegelhalter control limits. If the current run is inside the limits, the item resolves as "no anomaly", and both the note's explanation and the presumption's alternative are unnecessary. If it is outside, run the discriminating test: apply the bar retrospectively, blind, to the last 20 MONITOR dispositions. If roughly 25% of them clear it, the bar has a stable threshold and the current run's 5 is consistent with threshold placement rather than favourable application; if far fewer clear it, the bar is being applied more permissively on the run where permissiveness was self-serving, which is the presumption's prediction. Third, a validity check independent of the rate: sample the 5 INCORPORATE items and verify each stated external referent against the source, since Jonsson & Svingby show a criterion can raise consistency without raising validity.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-CHALLENGED

  Search scope note: Moderate search. Four literatures probed (reactivity/Hawthorne, rubric and criterion effects, small-count institutional comparison, interrupted time series) and all yielded directly applicable material; the strongest findings came from the small-n and ITS methodological literatures rather than from the reactivity literature the item's search targets pointed at. No source addresses self-assessment by automated reasoning pipelines directly. Not located and worth a broader search: empirical work on gatekeeping-threshold drift when the gatekeeper's own output is the item being gated.

SYSTEMIC-RISK-FLAG:
  Date: 2026-07-29
  Affected items: PRESUMPTION-568, PRESUMPTION-569 (and cf. PRESUMPTION-565)
  Common vulnerability: Both items infer a motivated-reasoning explanation from a *direction-of-favourability* pattern — six defects all classified in the direction that preserves the disposition (568); a rate moving in the direction that vindicates a self-imposed bar (569) — without establishing a null model or base rate for how often that pattern arises from a legitimate single mechanism or from ordinary variation. PRESUMPTION-565 failed on the same structure in the opposite direction (arguing from non-uniform direction). The pattern-of-favourability heuristic is real and is codified in audit practice (ISA 450's qualitative bias indicator), but it is a *prompt to investigate*, not a finding, and it requires a denominator before it carries weight.
  Literature basis: ISA 450, "Evaluation of Misstatements Identified during the Audit" (pattern of individually immaterial items favouring one outcome as a bias indicator — an indicator, not a conclusion); Spiegelhalter, D.J. 2005, Statistics in Medicine 24(8): 1185-1202 (small-count variation misread as institutional signal); Levitt, S.D. & List, J.A. 2011, AEJ: Applied 3(1): 224-238 (canonical reactivity finding dissolved into an artefact); McCambridge, J. et al. 2014, J Clin Epidemiol 67(3): 267-277 (reactivity effects small and inconsistent).
  Risk level: High
  Recommendation: Before a 14b presumption rests on a favourable-direction pattern, require (i) a stated null model — how often would this pattern occur if the mechanism were benign, and what is the denominator; and (ii) an explicit check for a single legitimate mechanism that would produce the whole pattern. Absent both, file the observation as a prompt to investigate rather than as an inferred presumption. Without this, the presumption stream risks systematically converting ordinary variation and normal criterion effects into imputations of motivated reasoning, which is itself a directional bias — and one the pipeline has no independent check against, since it is the same pipeline being assessed.
