STEELMAN:
  Item: ASSUMPTION-1164
  Strongest counterargument: Generating questions is cheap and universal — nearly every published
    paper does it — so it cannot be what distinguishes a living research programme from a dying one.
    Laudan, whose problem-centred view the claim borrows, treats unsolved anomalies and conceptual
    problems as costs and measures progress by significance-weighted problems SOLVED; Bird measures it
    by knowledge gained. On neither view does a programme that generates many questions and banks few
    answers count as healthy. The claim therefore inverts the tradition it cites as external defence.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: the PRS Problem slot is read as structured,
    weighted ignorance that is expected to be discharged, and programme health is scored on
    discharge as well as generation.
  How to test: across traditions, compare Problem-slot count with the rate at which Problem slots are
    later resolved; if count does not predict resolution, count is not a health signal.
