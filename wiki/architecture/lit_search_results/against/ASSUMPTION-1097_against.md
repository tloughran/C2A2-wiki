### The arithmetic (performed this run on the item's own figures)

**(1) The overruns are 14–26% beyond the escalation line, not marginal.**

A ±25% rule admits 0.75–1.25 of tier target. Five of six files sit at **1.42–1.57**. Expressed against the escalation line itself, they are at **1.14–1.26 of the threshold** — that is, 14% to 26% past the point at which escalation is mandated. These are not files sitting on the boundary where a rounding convention would decide. They are files that would still breach a **±50%** rule (1.57 > 1.50) in at least one case. Any "the threshold is slightly too tight" reading is unavailable on these numbers; the gap between the rule and the practice is roughly a factor of two on the tolerance half-width.

**(2) The measurement change is the same size as the entire tolerance half-width. This is the quantified form of the item's "multiplicative interaction," and it is worse than the item says.**

ASSUMPTION-1096 records the parser mechanism as inflating counts by **15–25%**. The gate's tolerance half-width is **25%**. So:

- **A file currently at 1.05 of target (comfortably passing) moves to 1.21–1.31 after a re-measure — i.e. it may cross the escalation line on no change in content whatsoever.**
- A file at 1.20 moves to 1.38–1.50: a certain breach.
- A file at 1.42 moves to 1.63–1.78.

The third run's warning — "authorising the recount without also settling the ±25% rule will push an unknown number of correct files across an escalation line while nobody writes a word" — is exactly right, and the number it calls unknown is bounded above: **every file currently between 1.00 and 1.25 of target is at risk**, which on any plausible distribution is a large fraction of the corpus.

**(3) In measurement-system terms the gauge is unfit, and that changes what the correct remedy is.**

Standard measurement-systems analysis assesses a gauge by the share of the **tolerance** its variation consumes. The AIAG MSA acceptance bands are conventionally %GRR **< 10% acceptable, 10–30% marginal / acceptable depending on application and cost, > 30% unacceptable** [**CANONICAL** — AIAG *Measurement Systems Analysis* reference manual, cited from established knowledge, NOT verified this run]. **The gauge is unfit for the gate it feeds, by a wide margin, on the item's own numbers.** This reframes the whole dispute: the ±25% rule is not primarily mis-set, it is being read off an instrument that cannot resolve it. ### Summary

The item is right that a length gate penalising the day's principal repair work is a perverse incentive, and right that the recount and the threshold interact. It is wrong about what six consecutive exceptions demonstrate, and its proposed remedy is the one move the record most strongly warns against.

"Six consecutive days can't each be an exception" is a general claim about thresholds and the economics of rules denies it. Rules are set ex ante and are therefore systematically over- and under-inclusive; that over-inclusion is the price paid for cheap application and ex ante guidance, and exceptions render a rule standard-like without making it defective (Kaplow 1992). Whether to keep this rule is a cost comparison — six escalations a human must read, against every run adjudicating its own length with no fixed referent — and the item asserts the conclusion of a comparison nobody ran.

The remedy is worse than the inference. "Exclude the Bridges section from the count" is a scope waiver, proposed by the runs producing the overruns, on a gate that has fired six consecutive times. The Rogers Commission's finding on Challenger is the same object with the same count: a launch constraint imposed after O-ring erosion, "imposed, and regularly waived, by the Solid Rocket Booster Project Manager," with **six consecutive waivers prior to 51L**. The register already holds this mechanism twice (PREMISE-146 clause 3; the policy/mechanism premise). It also already holds the detection consequence: threshold loosening suppresses detection, converting a false-positive problem into a silent false-negative one, and after the exclusion "no escalations" and "the gate no longer sees the thing it was built to see" are the same record. And Goodhart cuts both ways here — the item invokes it correctly against the rule and then proposes redefining the measure's scope so the activity stops counting, which is the canonical gaming response to a binding target.

On the coupling, the item understates the problem and I have quantified it. The parser discrepancy is 15–25% of measured value; the gate's tolerance half-width is 25%. **The measurement error is the same size as the tolerance half-width**, consuming on the order of 30–50% of the total tolerance from a single known bug — which by conventional MSA acceptance bands (>30% unacceptable) makes the gauge **unfit for the gate it feeds**. Every file currently between 1.00 and 1.25 of target can cross the escalation line on a re-measure with no change of content. This reframes the dispute: the primary defect is not the threshold's value, it is that the threshold is being read off an instrument that cannot resolve it.

That yields the direct answer to the last search question, and it is not the answer either proposal assumes. Coupling a threshold change to a measurement change **is** recommended — the limit is a function of the measurement system and must be revised when the system changes — but **sequentially and in one order only**: establish gauge capability first; fix the parser; re-measure; extend the existing limits over the post-fix data; recalculate only from post-change subgroups, once impact is evidenced. Not simultaneously, and not by negotiation between the parties producing the breaches. The alarm-management standards say the same thing in their own vocabulary: the response to a standing alarm is a documented **rationalisation** in which every alarm is individually justified against a written philosophy under formal management of change — while also confirming that a limit set at commissioning and never revisited is itself the documented failure, which is PREMISE-011's uncommitted tuning cadence.

The Rogers Commission's specific finding was that neither the constraint, nor the reason for it, nor the six waivers **were known to** Moore or Thomas. The variable separating a functioning gate from control erosion is not the exception rate; it is who grants the exception. Six-for-six escalation to a named human with zero self-granted waivers is a gate doing exactly what gates are for. The correct reading of this day is not "the rule is broken." ### Specific risks

**The exclusion is adopted and the gate goes dark.** This is the concrete near-term risk. If the Bridges section is excluded from the count, escalations will drop, and per PREMISE-118's already-held finding the resulting quiet is uninterpretable: it is equally consistent with the problem being solved and with the detector no longer detecting. The prior state — six escalations to a named human — was informative. The successor state will not be, and the fleet will have traded a costly signal for a cheap silence and recorded it as an improvement.

**The recount is authorised on an unfit gauge and the threshold is renegotiated in the same act.** Given the arithmetic, this is the highest-consequence path. A 15–25% instrument shift against a ±25% band will move an unknown but large number of currently-compliant files across the escalation line on no content change, and if the threshold is simultaneously loosened to absorb that, nobody will be able to say afterwards which files moved because of the instrument and which because of the rule. **The two changes are confounded by construction if made together**, and the prior values are destroyed by the recount, so the confound is not recoverable. PREMISE-105 already forbids reading the series across it.

**The precedent generalises to gates that matter.** "A rule breached six times is a broken rule" is compact and portable and will be applied next to a gate whose over-inclusion is protecting something. Kaplow's point is that over-inclusion is what rules *do*; a fleet that treats each instance of it as a defect will convert its rules into standards one at a time, and standards are the expensive regime — every run adjudicating for itself, no fixed referent, and PREMISE-146's clause (4) signal degradation arriving anyway from the other direction.

**Risk of doing nothing, recorded at full strength.** The perverse incentive is real and PREMISE-011's anchoring challenge predicts precisely this outcome: "tune later" becomes "never tune" without a committed cadence, and no cadence was committed. Six escalations of the same kind is a genuine cost, it is being paid by a human whose review queue the register elsewhere records as the binding constraint, and PREMISE-146 clause (4) is correct that once every run breaches, "breached" stops discriminating a 2% overrun from a 200% one. **Nothing in this file argues for leaving the rule alone.** It argues that the rule is second in the queue, behind the gauge.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Fix the parser first, alone, and change nothing else.** This is the whole recommendation compressed. The gauge consumes 30–50% of the tolerance; no threshold decision made on it means anything. Fix, re-measure, and do not touch the rule in the same change.
(b) **Then set the threshold from post-fix data, per SPC practice.** Extend the current ±25% band over the re-measured corpus, observe the distribution, and recalculate only from post-change subgroups once impact is evidenced. This is the sequential coupling the literature endorses and it is available at no cost beyond patience.
(c) **Do not exclude the Bridges section.** If the section genuinely should not count, that is a **rationalisation decision** requiring a written justification against a stated philosophy under change control (ISA-18.2 / EEMUA 191), made by someone other than the runs producing the overruns. Made in-line as "the cheapest fix," it is a scope waiver and the record it will leave is indistinguishable from Mulloy's.
(d) **Apply PREMISE-146 clause (6)'s three obligations, which the register already validated and which the item does not use:** label the ceiling **soft** with a stated tolerable breach ratio, so "breached" is not read as a fault report; **publish the mandatory floor** (the fixed cost of the Bridges section and any other non-discretionary content) so every run knows its discretionary headroom before it starts; and **report breach relative to the floor**, not relative to zero. That addresses the perverse incentive without moving the line, and it is already validated practice in this register.
(e) **Commit the tuning cadence PREMISE-011 condition (b) required and nobody set.** The absence of a cadence, not the value of the threshold, is the defect that produced six consecutive escalations with no revision path. This is the cheapest durable fix in the list.
(f) **Record the escalation behaviour as the finding it is.** Six breaches, six escalations to a named human, zero self-granted waivers. That is the variable on which the Challenger comparison turns and the fleet is on the right side of it. It should be in the item, because if it ever stops being true, *that* is the moment the normalisation-of-deviance reading becomes correct — and there will be no way to notice unless the baseline is written down now.
(g) **Pre-register the recount's effect.** Before re-measuring, state how many files are expected to cross the escalation line on instrument change alone. Then re-measure and compare. This is the only way to keep the two changes distinguishable, and it costs one sentence written in advance.

Establish gauge capability, fix the parser, re-measure, extend the existing limits over the post-fix data, and recalculate only from post-change subgroups once impact is evidenced. Never simultaneously, and never by negotiation between the parties producing the breaches; the alarm-management standards say the same thing when they require a documented rationalisation, individually justifying every alarm against a written philosophy under formal management of change. Finally, the fact the item reports and does not use. Its Context field says the matter was "escalated to Tom by name in two runs; neither acted unilaterally." The Rogers Commission's specific finding was that the waivers were granted inside one office and that Moore and Thomas **did not know**. The variable that separates a functioning gate from control erosion is not the exception rate — it is who grants the exception. Six breaches, six escalations to a named human, zero self-granted waivers: that is a gate doing exactly what gates exist to do, which is to force a deviation out of the run and into a channel where someone with authority can see it. The honest reading of the day is not "the rule is broken." It is "**the rule cost six escalations and produced six correct handoffs, on an instrument that cannot measure the thing the rule is about**" — and the repair queue is gauge, then floor, then threshold, in that order, with the scope exclusion not in the queue at all.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** The parser must be fixed and the corpus re-measured **before** any threshold decision, because the gauge currently consumes 30–50% of the tolerance and no limit read off it is meaningful. The two changes must be sequential and separately recorded, with a pre-registered prediction of how many files cross the line on instrument change alone; if they are made together the confound is permanent, because the recount destroys the prior values. If the Bridges section is genuinely to be excluded, that must be a written rationalisation against a stated philosophy, authored by someone other than the runs producing the overruns, under change control. PREMISE-146 clause (6)'s three obligations — label the ceiling soft with a tolerable breach ratio, publish the mandatory floor, report breach relative to the floor — must be applied, because they remove the perverse incentive without moving the line and they are already validated in this register. And the escalation baseline must be written down: six breaches, six escalations, zero self-granted waivers. If that ratio ever moves, the normalisation-of-deviance reading becomes correct, and there will be no way to detect the transition without the baseline.

**How to test:** (1) **Compute the crossing count before re-measuring.** Take the current distribution of files by ratio-to-target, apply a uniform +15% and a uniform +25% shift, and count how many currently-compliant files cross 1.25. That number is the item's "unknown number of correct files," it is knowable today, and it decides whether the coupling is a marginal concern or the dominant one. (2) **Gauge study.** Re-measure a sample of 20 files with both parsers and report the discrepancy distribution against the 50% tolerance width. If the discrepancy is confirmed at 15–25%, the gauge is unfit and the threshold discussion is premature; this is the discriminating test and it is an afternoon's work. (3) **The escalation-authority audit,** which is the Challenger-differentiating variable: across all six exceptions, record who granted each one. Six-for-six escalation to a human is the safe state; the first self-granted exception is the state change and should be alarmed on. (4) **Pre-register the post-fix threshold.** Before the recount, state what ±band the fleet expects the corrected distribution to require, then re-measure and compare. A large gap between the pre-registered band and the fitted one is evidence the threshold was always about something other than length. (5) **Cost the alternative.** Estimate the human cost of six escalations against the cost of standard-like case-by-case adjudication. This is Kaplow's comparison, it is the one the item skipped, and it is the only thing that actually settles whether the rule should exist.
