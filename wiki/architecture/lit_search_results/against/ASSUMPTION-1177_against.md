       Homeostasis is a setpoint criterion. Nothing in the framework rewards arriving somewhere
       unoccupied; it rewards arriving at the target from arbitrary starting points and after arbitrary
       perturbation. ABSTRACT-ONLY (OpenAlex).
    4. Fields, C., & Levin, M. 2022. "Competency in Navigating Arbitrary Spaces as an Invariant for
       Analyzing Cognition in Diverse Embodiments." Entropy, 24(6):819. DOI 10.3390/e24060819. — The
       most explicit statement of the navigation formalism the assumption invokes. Novelty appears in
       it, but in a specific and different place: "the capacity to handle novelty ... to thrive and
       adapt to new circumstances." That is novelty in the *perturbation* and in the *route*, with the
       goal held fixed — the William James criterion of reaching the same goal by multiple means. The
       assumption reads a novelty-of-destination criterion off a novelty-of-path framework.
       ABSTRACT-ONLY (OpenAlex).
    5. Friston, K., & Frith, C. 2015. "A Duet for one." Consciousness and Cognition, 36:390-405.
       DOI 10.1016/j.concog.2014.12.003. — The Friston-side attribution is substantially more accurate
       than the Levin-side one, and this is the paper that grounds it: "the infinite regress induced by
       modelling another agent — who is modelling you — can be finessed if you both possess the same
       model," producing "a narrative that is shared by agents who are exchanging sensory signals" and
       "a generalised synchrony between internal (neuronal) brain states." So an overlap condition is
       genuinely there. But note its logical role: shared model is a *sufficient condition for
       communication to be tractable*, offered as a way out of a regress. It is not proposed as a
       measure of success, and nothing in it says more overlap is better. ABSTRACT-ONLY (OpenAlex).
    6. Friston, K., Lin, M., Frith, C. D., Pezzulo, G., Hobson, J. A., & Ondobaka, S. 2017. "Active
       Inference, Curiosity and Insight." Neural Computation, 29(10):2633-2683.
       DOI 10.1162/neco_a_00999. — Shows that the second, supposedly rival criterion is already inside
       the first. "Minimizing (expected) variational free energy leads to active sampling of novel
       contingencies," closing "explanatory gaps in generative models"; structure learning then yields
       abduction and Bayesian model reduction with "all the hallmarks of 'aha' moments." Novelty-seeking
       is a derived consequence of free-energy minimisation, not its opposite. If both criteria are
       terms in one objective, adopting both is not incoherent — it is the objective.
       ABSTRACT-ONLY (OpenAlex).
    7. Bruineberg, J., Dołęga, K., Dewhurst, J., & Baltieri, M. 2022. "The Emperor's New Markov

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The contradiction the assumption names does not survive contact with the source literature,
    and it fails at the Levin end decisively. Friston and Levin are co-authors on the formal statement
    of morphospace navigation (Friston, Levin, Sengupta & Pezzulo 2015; again in Kuchling, Friston,
    Georgiev & Levin 2019), where morphogenesis is modelled as variational free-energy minimisation, the
    mechanism is cells that "share a common (genetic and epigenetic) model of organismal form," and the
    success criterion is arrival at "the target morphology." Overlap and target, on the Levin side, in
    Levin's own co-authored formalism. Levin's independent statements agree: TAME's operative notion is
    anatomical homeostasis, a setpoint criterion, and Fields & Levin (2022) locate novelty in the
    perturbation and the route while holding the goal fixed. The "emptiness criterion" therefore appears
    to be a misattribution, and by the assumption's own logic a misattribution at either end dissolves
    the contradiction. The Friston-side attribution is better founded — Friston & Frith (2015) do
    finesse a modelling regress by having both agents "possess the same model" — but even there, shared
    model is a tractability condition for communication, not a scored objective, and Friston et al.
    (2017) show that free-energy minimisation *generates* novelty-seeking as a derived term. The two
    alleged rivals are terms in one objective function, not incompatible criteria. What remains true is
    a real but differently-located problem: the FEP's own falsifiability is contested (Colombo & Wright;
    Bruineberg et al.), so C2A2 may indeed have no way to fail — but because of a defect in one
    imported framework, not because of an incompatibility between two.

  Specific risks: The risks run in an unusual direction here, because the challenge finds the vault
    safer than it thought on one axis and less safe on another. If ASSUMPTION-1177 is wrong as stated,
    then (i) PRESUMPTION-858 — "two success criteria adopted from two tracked traditions are presumed
    compatible" — is not falsified but *vindicated*, since in this instance the two are not merely
    compatible but jointly formalised by the two named authors, and its Critical risk rating should be
    revised; (ii) any remediation queued against 1177 (auditing cross-connections for criterion
    conflict) would be work performed against a phantom; (iii) more seriously, the vault's paraphrase of
    Levin appears to invert his criterion from target-seeking to emptiness-seeking, and if that
    paraphrase has propagated it corrupts every Levin entry that depends on it — a scoping problem of
    exactly the shape ASSUMPTION-1178 raises for Hawkins and Hoffman; (iv) the genuine unfalsifiability
    exposure is unaddressed, because it sits in the FEP itself and would not be found by looking for
    conflicts between criteria.

  Mitigations available:
    - Read the joint literature before treating two thinkers as rivals. Friston, Levin, Sengupta &
      Pezzulo (2015) and Kuchling, Friston, Georgiev & Levin (2019) exist and are directly on point; a
      co-authorship check is a cheap standing test before any "incompatible formalisms" claim is filed.
    - Distinguish tractability conditions from success criteria when importing a formalism. Bruineberg
      et al. (2022) supply the vocabulary (Pearl blanket vs Friston blanket; inference *with* a model vs
      *within* a model) and the warning.
    - Address the real falsifiability exposure on its own terms. Colombo & Wright (2018) is the entry
      point for what it costs to use FEP as a first principle, and it is a per-framework question, not
      a cross-framework one.
    - Re-derive the Levin criterion from primary sources and correct downstream entries if the emptiness
      reading has propagated.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-1177
    Strongest counterargument: The assumption may be right about the disease and wrong about the
      aetiology, and the disease is the serious part. An accelerator that scores itself on
      cross-tradition connection has a genuine structural problem: connection-finding is nearly costless
      and nearly always succeeds, so a criterion phrased in terms of it cannot be failed. That worry is
      untouched by my finding — indeed the Friston-Levin co-authorship makes it worse, since if the two
      formalisms are one formalism then the vault has one criterion where it thought it had two, and a
      single criterion of the form "did we find overlap" is more trivially satisfiable than two in
      tension. It is also fair to say that a co-authored paper does not by itself prove conceptual
      unification; Friston has applied variational free energy to a very large number of domains, and
      Colombo & Wright's worry is precisely that a principle applicable to everything constrains
      nothing. On that reading the 2015 paper is evidence for the unfalsifiability charge rather than
      against the incompatibility charge, and the assumption's conclusion — "no way to fail" — stands
      even though its stated reason does not.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the accelerator's success criterion is stated
      independently of both imported formalisms, in terms the project can fail — a prediction, a
      falsifiable claim about the world, or a pre-registered expectation about what a cross-connection
      should let someone do that they could not do before; (b) the Levin paraphrase is corrected at
      source so that no entry scores "landed in an empty region" as success; (c) the FEP is used
      descriptively, to model what the vault is doing, rather than normatively, to certify that it
      worked. Under (a)-(c) neither formalism is load-bearing for the project's falsifiability and the
      contradiction is moot.
    How to test: Three checks. First, textual: locate the vault passages that state each criterion and
      compare them against Friston, Levin, Sengupta & Pezzulo (2015) and Fields & Levin (2022). If no
      vault passage cites a primary source for the emptiness criterion, the misattribution is
      established. Second, bibliographic: the co-authorship check is a one-query test and it has now
      been run — the answer is that the two authors have published together on exactly this. Third, and
      most important, behavioural: take a sample of entries the vault has scored as successful
      cross-connections and ask whether any *could* have been scored as failures. If the answer is none,
      the unfalsifiability is demonstrated directly and does not depend on adjudicating either
      attribution.
