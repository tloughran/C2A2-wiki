On its own terms the source tradition warns against the cross-level inference the item performs.
    3. Kuncheva and Whitaker's finding, as reported in "Balancing accuracy and diversity in ensemble learning using a two-phase artificial bee colony approach," *Applied Soft Computing* (ScienceDirect S1568494621001356, retrieved 2026-07-20): no strong correlation exists between ensemble accuracy and diversity, and if diversity increases without bound the ensemble's accuracy **decreases**. Also "Trade-Off Between Diversity and Accuracy in Ensemble Generation" (Springer, 10.1007/3-540-33019-4_19). Challenges the monotone preference: preserved local optimization is not automatically good; there is an interior optimum, and a scoring rule that never penalises non-convergence sits at the wrong end of it.
    4. Alasdair MacIntyre, *Three Rival Versions of Moral Enquiry* (Notre Dame Press) and "MacIntyre on Tradition, Rationality, and Relativism" (retrieved 2026-07-20). MacIntyre's position is that incommensurable schemes do **not** entail relativism and that a tradition in epistemic crisis can be shown, by its own standards, to be rationally defeated by a rival — an adjudication with a convergence-shaped success signature. The item recruits MacIntyre against convergence scoring; MacIntyre's actual claim is that rational resolution between traditions is possible and is the point.
    5. Brand, C. et al., "The Ideological Turing Test: A Behavioral Measure of Open-Mindedness and Perspective-Taking," *Cognitive Science* (2025), doi 10.1111/cogs.70126. The test is operationalised as passing when opponents **agree** with the produced argument at or above the rate they agree with in-group arguments, and the paper's own limitation section notes this conflates producing convincing arguments with genuine understanding. The instrument proposed to measure the alternative signature scores agreement.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The item's central intuition — that maximal agreement can destroy the diversity that made an ensemble useful — is supported in the ensemble literature, but the literature's actual finding is a **tradeoff with an interior optimum**, not a case for abandoning convergence as a criterion: Kuncheva and Whitaker found no strong diversity–accuracy correlation and that unbounded diversity degrades accuracy. Three further challenges land. The transfer condition flagged by 14b is real: Levin's collective-intelligence framework is under active philosophical criticism in its home domain, and Levin himself states that intuitions at one level do not transfer reliably across levels, so a bioelectric collective's virtual-governor behaviour is not evidence about a set of research traditions. The MacIntyre citation runs the other way: MacIntyre's project is to show that rival traditions can be rationally adjudicated, one defeating another on the other's own terms, which is a convergence-shaped outcome. And the replacement instrument is compromised: the best-validated operationalisation of understanding-without-agreement, the ideological Turing test, scores understanding by measuring opponents' agreement.

  Specific risks: Removing convergence from Rung-2 scoring without a validated alternative leaves the rung unscored in practice, since the proposed alternative's only instrument reduces to agreement. Worse, a rule that never penalises non-convergence is unfalsifiable as a success criterion: any dialogue outcome, including two traditions talking past each other indefinitely, satisfies "preserved local optimization." Since P-500 identifies convergence as the accelerator's target function, replacing it with an unmeasurable criterion converts a possibly-wrong objective into no objective.

  Mitigations available: Score both criteria and report them as a pair rather than replacing one with the other — this is the ensemble literature's own recommendation and is the item's stated in-house test. Treat the Levin result as a generative analogy with no transfer warrant until a transfer condition is stated and checked. Where the ITT is used, report the agreement-rate confound explicitly rather than treating the score as an understanding measure. Take MacIntyre's actual criterion seriously as a third scoring option: can tradition A state tradition B's position in B's own terms and identify B's unresolved epistemic crisis?

STEELMAN:
  Item: ASSUMPTION-475
  Strongest counterargument: The assumption imports a normative conclusion from a domain whose transfer conditions are unstated and whose originator explicitly denies cross-level intuition transfer, uses it to remove the only operational scoring criterion the rung has, and proposes a replacement whose leading instrument measures the very thing it was designed to exclude. A bioelectric collective and a set of research traditions differ on every dimension that made the virtual-governor result meaningful: the cells share a physical coupling medium, have no symbolic representations, no capacity to misdescribe each other, and no standard by which one could be shown wrong. Meanwhile the ensemble literature — the closest formal analogue that actually concerns aggregating diverse local optimisers — reports that diversity and accuracy trade off with an interior optimum and that unbounded diversity is harmful, which supports scoring on *both* axes rather than deleting one. And the philosophical authority the item leans on argues the opposite of what it is recruited for: MacIntyre's whole point in *Three Rival Versions* is that rival traditions are rationally adjudicable and that a tradition can be defeated on its own terms. The strongest version of the challenge is therefore not "convergence is good" but "the item has removed a measurable criterion in favour of an unmeasurable one on the authority of a disanalogy."
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: A stated and checked transfer condition from bioelectric collectives to symbolic research traditions; a validated instrument for mutual registration that is demonstrably not a proxy for agreement; and evidence that the two scoring criteria actually rank dialogues differently.
  How to test: Score the same set of existing dialogues under (a) a convergence measure, (b) a mutual-registration measure, and (c) an ITT-style measure. If (b) and (c) correlate strongly with (a), the alternative signature is agreement wearing different clothes and the assumption fails empirically. If (b) and (a) rank dialogues differently, record which dialogues the two disagree about and inspect them by hand — that disagreement set is the entire evidential content of the assumption.
