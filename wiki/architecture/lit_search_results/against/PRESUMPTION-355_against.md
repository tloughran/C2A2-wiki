    1. Goodhart's law / output-vs-outcome (Goodhart 1975; software-metrics gaming literature) — birth-rate (count of new artifacts) is a classic gameable output proxy that diverges from value; optimizing creation rewards quantity over quality/use and is a textbook metric-gaming failure mode.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate-Strong

  Summary: The exclusive birth-rate framing is challenged across all three relevant literatures: Goodhart warns a creation count optimizes into quantity-gaming; software-evolution work shows revision/survival/use carry the real signal (high birth + high churn = churn, not yield); scientometrics weights for impact because raw counts are weak value proxies. Creation is a legitimate LEADING indicator (15a), but "yield = birth-rate, lifecycle irrelevant" discards the very events (validation, use, retirement) that distinguish durable output from artifact-spam.

  Specific risks: A birth-rate "yield" rewards producing many short-lived triplets; the system congratulates itself on volume while quality/survival silently degrade; couples REVISE-115 (count doing silent valuation) and the metabolism-view proxy cluster.

  Mitigations available: Report birth-rate as ONE leading indicator beside survival (how many created triplets persist/are used/are validated) and a churn/retirement rate; never optimize birth-rate; quality-weight or at least survival-weight the headline.

  STEELMAN:
