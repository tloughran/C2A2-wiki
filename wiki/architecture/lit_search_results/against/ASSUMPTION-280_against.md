  Sources:
    1. Web-scraping research ethics for identifiable groups (Brown et al. 2025; AoIR). — For IDENTIFIABLE groups, transparency is necessary but explicitly NOT sufficient; the guidance escalates toward consent/opt-out as identifiability and harm rise. Challenges any reading of "disclose" as the COMPLETE response.
    2. Notice-vs-consent critique (privacy-self-management critique, e.g., Solove "Privacy Self-Management and the Consent Dilemma"). — Notice/disclosure regimes systematically fail to protect because users/affected parties rarely read or can act on notices; disclosure can become a liability-shield that does little real work. Challenges disclosure-as-protection.
    3. Group-privacy literature. — Harms here accrue to communities as collectives, for whom individual-style notice is a poor fit; in-product disclosure does not give the listed community any agency. Challenges adequacy.

  Strength of challenge: Weak-Moderate

  Summary: The challenge does not dispute that disclosing provenance and not implying endorsement is correct — it is. The challenge is that 280, as stated, risks being read as the SUFFICIENT response when the literature treats disclosure as a NECESSARY MINIMUM for identifiable groups, with consent/opt-out indicated as harm rises. Notice-vs-consent critiques further warn that disclosure can become a box-ticking shield. So 280 is right as far as it goes but under-specifies the obligation; the stronger "disclosure discharges the obligation" reading is squarely refuted (and is the separate PRESUMPTION-313).

  Specific risks: If "must disclose" is treated as the whole duty, the tool lists identifiable communities scraped without consent while believing the ethics are settled — the exact over-confidence PRESUMPTION-313 flags; group-level harms (misrepresentation, unwanted exposure) remain unaddressed and the listed communities retain no agency.

  Mitigations available: Adopt 280's disclosure as the floor, not the ceiling; pair it with an opt-out/takedown path and consider opt-in for higher-sensitivity communities (the alternatives 313 says were never raised); treat group privacy explicitly (community-level, not individual-style, notice).

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-280
    Strongest counterargument: Disclosure is the easy, cheap part of the obligation and doing it can create a false sense of ethical closure. For identifiable groups listed without consent, the live questions are agency (can a community decline?) and group harm (misrepresentation at scale), neither of which a popover addresses. The research-ethics literature it leans on actually escalates beyond notice as identifiability rises — so citing that literature to justify disclosure-only inverts its thrust.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: That disclosure is held as the necessary minimum AND paired with at least an opt-out/takedown mechanism (and opt-in for sensitive cases), with group-level harm considered — i.e., 280 is treated as the floor while 313's sufficiency overclaim is rejected.
    How to test: Check whether a listed community that objects has any in-product recourse; if not, disclosure is operating as a shield, not a safeguard.
