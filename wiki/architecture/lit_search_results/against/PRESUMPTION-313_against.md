  Sources:
    1. Notice-vs-consent critique (Solove, "Privacy Self-Management and the Consent Dilemma," 2013). — Disclosure/notice systematically fails as a substitute for consent: affected parties rarely read or can act on it; transparency ≠ agreement. Direct challenge to "transparency cures the consent gap."
    2. Web-scraping ethics for identifiable groups (Brown et al. 2025; AoIR). — As identifiability/harm rise, guidance escalates from notice toward opt-out/consent; identifiable communities are exactly the case where notice-only is judged inadequate. Challenges sufficiency.
    3. Group-privacy / collective-consent literature (Taylor, Floridi & van der Sloot, "Group Privacy," 2017). — Groups have privacy interests not reducible to individual notice; listing identifiable communities engages collective interests that in-product disclosure does not address, and for which opt-in/opt-out is the relevant mechanism. Challenges the omission of alternatives.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The presumption is strongly challenged. The consent literature is explicit that notice/disclosure is NOT a substitute for consent — Solove's consent-dilemma shows notice regimes fail precisely as a discharge mechanism, and web-scraping and group-privacy ethics escalate to opt-out/consent for identifiable groups, which these communities are. Critically, the presumption's structural flaw is the SUPPRESSED ALTERNATIVE: don't-list and opt-in were never raised, so "disclose" was treated as the whole option space when the literature names at least three (disclose / opt-out / opt-in / don't-list). Disclosure does ethical work but does not cure the consent gap.

  Specific risks: The tool lists identifiable communities scraped without consent while treating the ethics as settled; affected communities have no agency or recourse; potential for misrepresentation, unwanted exposure, and reputational harm at the group level — and a reputational/ethical liability for the project (and for Tom/Notre Dame) if a listed community objects.

  Mitigations available: Reopen the option space — add an opt-out/takedown path at minimum, opt-in for higher-sensitivity communities; treat disclosure (ASSUMPTION-280 / PREMISE-052) as the necessary floor, not the discharge; document the don't-list criterion (which communities should never be listed); route the value/scope decision to Tom.
