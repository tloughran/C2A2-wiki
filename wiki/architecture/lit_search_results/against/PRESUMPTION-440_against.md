  Challenging evidence found: Yes

  Sources:
    1. Dragioti et al., 2015, "A systematic appraisal of allegiance effect in RCTs of psychotherapy" (PMC4570291). — Researcher allegiance to a preferred outcome inflates effect sizes in its favor; a non-financial conflict of interest / optimism bias. A study framed as seeking evidence "against pessimism" carries exactly this allegiance.
    2. Rosenthal — observer-/experimenter-expectancy effect. — Expecting (and wanting) a result subconsciously biases treatment of participants and interpretation of data; strongest when the measure is subjective (as "understanding" is).
    3. Holman et al., 2015, "Evidence of experimental bias ... need for blind data recording" (PMC4496034). — Preferred-direction expectations bias measurement itself; blinding is the standard remedy — implying an un-blinded, direction-committed design is a live bias.
    4. Nickerson, 1998 — confirmation bias. — A sought conclusion drives selective search, weighting, and interpretation of evidence toward the desired direction.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The presumption (that framing one direction as desirable is benign) is strongly challenged. Allegiance, expectancy, blinding, and confirmation-bias literatures converge: a preferred, "desirable" outcome direction — here, evidence against MacIntyrean pessimism — is a documented source of inflated, direction-favoring results, especially with subjective measures and no blinding. The framing does not merely describe a hope; it is a channel through which the sought direction can contaminate design, measurement, and interpretation.

  Specific risks: The study's headline (understanding rises under listening; evidence "against pessimism") is the allegiance-favored direction, so its credibility is discounted absent bias controls; compounds with same-model non-independence (P-442) and unvalidated construct (P-437).

  Mitigations available: State hypotheses in direction-agnostic form; preregister the desired-outcome-neutral analysis (ties to A-407); blind raters/agents to the study's framing and to condition; report the allegiance risk as a limitation.

  Recommendation: CHALLENGED

  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: Having a motivating hypothesis (even a hoped-for one) is normal and not itself a defect; the entire apparatus of blinding, preregistration, and the study's own C0 gate and adversarial 14/15 pipeline exists precisely to wall the hope off from the result. If those controls hold, the "desirable direction" is inert.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Condition- and framing-blinding for raters/agents; direction-agnostic preregistered hypotheses; allegiance declared and independently audited (the 15b adversarial role is itself a partial control).
    How to test: Re-run scoring with raters blind to framing/condition; compare to un-blinded scoring. A framing-dependent gap quantifies the smuggled direction.
