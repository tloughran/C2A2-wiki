### Summary

The item has found a real defect and named it wrong, and the misnaming points the remedy in a direction the literature does not support. Concede first: a tick that emitted "stable, no new sources" without executing a search recorded nothing about the monitored item, and PREMISE-142 already holds that such a claim is inadmissible — the output vocabulary must be three-valued with an **evidence pointer** attached, because "a binary vocabulary forces the aggregator to render an absence as a positive assurance." That is exactly what happened, seventeen times out of seventeen. But "a monitoring cadence tick records nothing about the monitored item," stated generally, is false, and the reason is a theorem rather than an opinion. Chandra and Toueg's result is that in an asynchronous system a crashed process cannot be distinguished from a slow one, and the standard construction is **periodic heartbeats**: the absence of an event is not itself an event, so a purely event-driven monitor cannot observe silence and cannot tell "nothing happened" from "I stopped watching." The operational literature the brief asked for says the same in plainer words — silent failures are expensive precisely because "nothing crashes, nobody gets paged," and internal error signals "disappear exactly when needed," which is why an external periodic ping whose *absence* is the alert is the standard remedy. In that literature the answer to a monitor that reported nothing is **more cadence, not less**. The maintenance evidence is likewise more equivocal than the slogan. Nowlan and Heap's 89%-not-age-related finding is cited for condition-based monitoring and is real; the other 11% is real too, and the same study reports that many components have **no effective form of scheduled maintenance** — a claim about what to do, not about whether time is informative. And condition-based monitoring has a stated precondition that the item's remedy does not check: "**no P-F interval, no predictive maintenance**." Where a failure has no detectable precursor, there is nothing to trigger on and the clock is the only available trigger. Several of C2A2's monitored objects have no event channel at all. In the domain the brief nominated for re-check intervals — systematic-review updating — the answer is explicitly a clock: living systematic reviews screen "e.g., monthly," gated by a judgement about how fast the field moves. And Shojania's survival analysis exists because time carries information; its whole design turns on the distinction the item collapses, between new evidence existing and evidence "sufficiently important to warrant updating." That is the flaw in 17/17. The endpoint is "had new material once searched," and for living authors publishing weekly the prior on that is near 1. A test with a positive rate of ~1 by construction cannot discriminate between "the cadence is wrong" and "the search never ran" — and the second hypothesis is the one the item's own root cause supports. Finally, the register settles it in-house: `validated_premises.md` runs on Monthly and Quarterly re-check stamps, and those clock-triggered re-checks produced **DISPOSITION-408** (new caveats on per-space similarity calibration and embedding collapse) and **DISPOSITION-409** (the correlated-LLM-errors proviso, citing Kim et al. ICML 2025). A clock fired, an agent searched, the register learned. If a cadence tick recorded nothing about the monitored item, those dispositions could not exist. What failed at 17/17 was not cadence; it was the search step inside it — which makes 1117 a second instance of ASSUMPTION-1106, not an independent finding.

### Specific risks

**The silence-blindness risk, and it is the main one.** If "the variable is the search, not the time" is adopted and monitoring moves to event-driven triggers, C2A2 loses the only mechanism by which a *dead monitor* is detectable. This is not a theoretical concern: the vault has already logged five consecutive dark syncs on a notification channel (referenced in PREMISE-131's applicability line), and a dark channel is invisible to an event-driven design by construction. The failure mode being traded into is strictly worse than the one being traded out of, because a wrong report is at least a report.

**The no-event-channel risk.** Several monitored objects — a theologian's meditations, a philosopher's lecture schedule, an unindexed institutional page — have no notification channel, no feed, and no precursor signal. For these the P-F precondition fails outright and condition-based monitoring is not available at any price. A remedy that assumes an event channel will silently drop coverage of exactly the items whose publication is least predictable.

**The wrong-endpoint risk.** Optimising for "find new material" rather than "find material that changes a recorded claim" will drive the cadence toward continuous search at rising cost with no measured improvement in decisions. Shojania's design is the corrective, and the LSR literature's gating criterion — "new evidence is likely to **change the findings**" — is the operational form of it.

**The generalisation risk from 17/17.** The item reports a numerator with no denominator and two draws whose randomness is not established. If the sampled items were drawn from the reported-stable pool by an auditor hunting failures, the design is selection on the dependent variable and the rate is uninterpretable. PREMISE-168 already bars publishing a yield in this form.

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** Seventeen consecutive false "stable" reports is a severe, measured, live defect, and it is worse than 1106's inert guard in one respect: an inert guard emits a *neutral* pass, whereas this monitor emitted a **positive assurance about the world** — "no new sources" is a claim about the literature, not about the run. PREMISE-142 calls that a false particular and observes that "a false particular is harder to unwind than a silence." The item is right that something must change, and right that it is urgent.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Fix the search step, not the trigger.** The measured failure is that the search did not execute; the remedy is to make execution observable. Under PREMISE-142 every monitoring report carries an **evidence pointer** — which query, which corpus, which timestamp, how many results — and a report without one is `NO-EVIDENCE`, never `stable`.
(b) **Keep the clock and add the events.** These are complements, not alternatives, and every domain searched says so. Event triggers where a channel exists (RSS, arXiv listings, publisher feeds); a periodic tick as the floor and as the liveness proof. The heartbeat literature's rule applies directly: the *absence* of a tick is itself an alert.
(c) **Change the endpoint from "new material exists" to "new material changes a recorded claim,"** per Shojania and per the LSR gating criterion. Then measure the base rate of conclusion-changing updates per item per month. That number sets the cadence empirically and is the only thing that can.
(d) **Stratify cadence by measured volatility**, which is what living systematic reviews do: fast-moving items screened monthly, stable ones quarterly, with the classification recorded and revisited. This is the register's own existing pattern (Monthly / Quarterly re-check stamps) applied to tradition monitoring.
(e) **Report the denominator and the sampling frame** for the two draws before the 17/17 figure is used to justify an architecture change.
(f) **Do not mint 1117 separately from 1106.** They are the same defect — an instrument that did not run emitted an outcome indistinguishable from an instrument that ran and found nothing — and PREMISE-142 governs both. Minting two premises for one mechanism inflates the register and splits the fix. See the systemic-risk flag filed for this batch.

**Strongest counterargument:** The measurement is damning and the diagnosis inverts it.
