  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The empirical premise survives intact and is if anything better supported than the item states: correlated errors across frontier models are documented at ICML level, correlation does rise with capability, and the nine-judges/two-effective-votes result is a real paper. The challenge is to the inference. Ladha's correlated-vote extensions of the Condorcet theorem establish the crucial point the item skips — correlation degrades aggregation gain continuously and can break the asymptotic theorem, but the group remains more reliable than the individual, so two effective votes is a discount, not a nullity. The de-entanglement literature goes further and repairs the estimator directly, reporting gains over majority voting once contributions are reweighted by inferred independence. The Kish figure the item leans on is itself an approximation valid for exchangeable votes with common pairwise correlation, which a seven-family panel does not satisfy, and its bias direction under heterogeneity is not established. And the diversity and debate literatures show that effective independence is an engineerable quantity rather than a fixed ceiling. Finally, the reflexive limb — that this invalidates 15a/15b concordance — is a transfer from same-direction judge panels to adversarially-assigned opposite-direction searchers that no located source licenses. The correct conclusion the literature supports is: unweighted concordance is badly biased and must be reweighted or replaced; it is not that concordance carries no information.

  Specific risks:
    - Over-correction: discarding concordance entirely removes a signal that Ladha shows is still above the single-judge baseline, replacing a biased estimator with none. Mid-run changes of disposition method made under an "invalidated" verdict may be a net loss of information.
    - Uncalibrated ρ: acting on ~2 effective votes as if it were measured for *this* system imports an exchangeability assumption. C2A2 has not measured its own ρ between 15a and 15b, and the Kish formula's error under heterogeneous dependence is not known to be conservative.
    - Unverified citation propagating: "Kohli 2026" could not be author-verified. The paper exists; the attribution has not been checked. If this citation is repeated across the register it will become load-bearing without ever having been confirmed.
    - Foreclosed remedies: reweighting, role/prompt diversification, and debate-style aggregation are all documented responses. A verdict of "invalidated" is likely to stop the search for them.
    - The reflexive claim is unfalsified in both directions: nothing located shows 15a/15b agreement *is* independent either. The honest status is unmeasured, not invalidated.

  Mitigations available:
    - Measure ρ locally rather than importing it. Run 15a and 15b on a set of items with known dispositions and compute their error correlation directly. This is the single highest-value experiment available and it converts the entire question from citation to measurement.
    - Adopt de-entangled reweighting (arXiv:2604.07650) rather than abandoning concordance: weight each searcher's contribution by inferred independence.
    - Diversify structurally: different prompt scaffolds, different model families, or different role framings for 15a and 15b, per the diversity literature. C2A2 already has the strongest available decorrelation lever — opposite search direction — and should quantify what it buys.
    - Report confidence as an interval derived from an estimated N_eff, not as a scalar derived from agreement count.
    - Verify the "Kohli 2026" attribution before any further propagation, or re-cite as arXiv:2605.29800 with title only.

  Search scope: Moderate-to-comprehensive on the ML side, moderate on the social-choice side.   STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-1150
    Strongest counterargument: The item is right about the world and wrong about what follows. Correlated error among frontier models is real, rises with capability, and does mean that nine agreeing judges are worth about two — but "worth about two" is the finding, and two is not zero. Ladha's correlated-vote extensions of Condorcet make this precise: as correlation rises, majority-rule effectiveness declines continuously and the asymptotic theorem can fail outright, yet the group stays more reliable than any individual member. Meanwhile the literature that produced the alarming number has already produced the repair — reweighting ensembles by inferred independence beats majority voting — so the correct response to a biased estimator is recalibration, not abolition. The reflexive limb is weaker still: the correlated-error results are measured on panels of judges asked the same question in the same direction, whereas 15a and 15b are assigned opposite directions and blinded to each other, which is the strongest structural decorrelation available short of changing model families. Extending the finding to 15a/15b concordance is an assertion about a design the cited work did not study. And a system that declares its confidence metric invalidated on the strength of a single unverified citation, mid-run, has substituted one uncalibrated procedure for another.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) The measured error correlation between 15a and 15b on this vault's items would have to be materially lower than the ~0.6 same-direction figure — plausible given opposite assignment, but currently unmeasured in either direction; (b) confidence would have to be reported through a reweighted or interval-valued estimator rather than raw agreement count, so that a non-zero but discounted N_eff is representable; (c) the "Kohli 2026" attribution would have to be verified, or the claim re-grounded on arXiv:2605.29800 by title; (d) the disposition method change made mid-run would have to be shown not to have discarded more information than the biased metric contained.
    How to test: Directly and cheaply. Take a set of items whose true disposition is independently known or can be adjudicated. Run 15a and 15b on all of them. Build the 2×2 table of their errors and compute the phi coefficient — that is ρ for this pipeline, measured rather than imported. Then compute N_eff = 2 / (1 + ρ) and compare against the imported ~2-from-9 figure. Separately, run the same items through 15b twice under different prompt scaffolds and measure the within-role correlation; the gap between within-role and cross-role correlation is exactly what the opposite-direction assignment is buying, and it is currently an unknown that the item treats as zero.
