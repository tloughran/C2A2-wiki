  Sources:
    1. Boudry & Braeckman. "Immunizing Strategies and Epistemic Defense Mechanisms." Philosophia. — The decisive theoretical objection, and new to this file. Immunizing strategies are moves that protect a belief system from disconfirmation; appealing to paradigm incommensurability to explain why no one has published your result is a textbook instance. Under this analysis ASSUMPTION-019 is not a finding about science but a device that converts every possible negative search result into confirmation, which makes it unfalsifiable.
    2. Kirkland et al. "Anti-vaccine activists, Web 2.0, and the postmodern paradigm." Vaccine (2012). / related work on paradigm-shift rhetoric in the microbiome debate. — Empirical documentation that "we are in a paradigm shift, that's why the establishment literature is silent" is the standard rhetorical move of positions later shown to be wrong. Base-rate argument: the prior on paradigm-shift appeals being correct is low.
    3. "What Was the 'Paradigm Shift'?" The New Republic (2024), and the surrounding Kuhn-reappraisal literature. — Reports the professional consensus bluntly: the language of paradigms and paradigm shifts is ubiquitous *except among historians and philosophers of science*. The framework C2A2 leans on has been largely abandoned by the specialists who would have to license the inference.
    4. Popper's response to Kuhn; Laudan (1977), "Progress and Its Problems"; Hoyningen-Huene (1993). — Cycle-1 baseline: strict incommensurability is not accepted; theory change is far more continuous than the Kuhnian picture implies, so "expected silence" during transition is not a well-supported empirical regularity.
    5. Unresolved-anomaly problem (standard Kuhn critique, restated in 2026 reviews). — Kuhn never specified how many anomalies induce a shift or what makes evidence anomalous. Without those criteria, "we are in a paradigm shift" is not a testable diagnosis, so it cannot license an inference about literature absence.
    6. "Back to the basics: guidance for designing good literature searches." PMC, 2024 (PMC11149711). — Literature-search bias results from poor keyword selection, poor strategy, and failure to include unpublished/hard-to-reach work. Database bias arises when searches are limited to specific databases. Every C2A2 search in this pipeline is a web search from a single interface — precisely the configuration this guidance warns produces false absence.
    7. Nature Human Behaviour (2019). "The importance of no evidence." — Absence of evidence must be distinguished from evidence of absence; the distinction requires an estimate of search power, which C2A2 does not compute.
    8. PNAS (2025). "Evaluating interdisciplinary research: disparate outcomes for topic and knowledge base." — Interdisciplinary work faces systematically different review outcomes; relevant work may exist but be dispersed across venues and vocabularies rather than absent. Explains apparent silence without invoking paradigm shift.
    9. Catalogue of Bias, "Publication bias" (Oxford CEBM); PMC 2024 funnel-plot review. — Positive results are preferentially published; silence in the literature is systematically *biased* silence, which cuts against reading it as evidence of anything.

  Summary: This assumption is challenged more sharply than the cycle-1 file recorded, but not by anything published since April. The strongest objection is structural rather than empirical: as stated, ASSUMPTION-019 makes literature absence unfalsifiable, since a positive result confirms the work and a negative result is explained away as paradigm silence. That is the definition of an immunizing strategy, and it has a poor track record — it is the standard rhetorical position of views that turn out to be wrong. Separately, the methodological literature supplies at least four mundane explanations for absence (keyword failure, database limitation, interdisciplinary dispersion, publication bias) that must be excluded before the Kuhnian reading is even a candidate, and C2A2 has excluded none of them. Compounding risk: ASSUMPTION-017's finding that AI literature search misses 68–96% of relevant studies means C2A2's absences are very likely search artefacts.

  Specific risks: If false, C2A2 systematically converts its own search weakness into a claim of originality. Every "no prior work found" becomes "we are ahead of the field," and the project builds a body of purportedly novel findings that are in fact unlocated prior art. The reputational and epistemic cost is asymmetric: publishing a claimed novelty that turns out to be a 2019 paper in a journal the pipeline never indexed is far more damaging than having found the paper and cited it. There is also a motivational risk — the assumption makes negative results feel like progress, removing the corrective pressure that would otherwise improve search quality.

  Mitigations available: (a) Reverse the default — treat absence as search failure until search power is demonstrated; (b) run at least one absence-check through a scholarly index (Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, Google Scholar) with citation-chaining, not web search alone; (c) forward/backward citation-chase from one known-relevant anchor paper per claim; (d) vary vocabulary across traditions deliberately, since interdisciplinary dispersion is the leading benign explanation (PNAS 2025); (e) ask a domain expert "who else works on this?" — a five-minute check that dominates any amount of automated searching; (f) strike the Kuhnian framing from artefacts entirely and replace it with an explicit statement of search scope and known limitations.

  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: The assumption is not being used to *assert* novelty; it is being used to prevent a specific error in the opposite direction — treating literature absence as automatic disconfirmation and abandoning a line of work because no one has published on it. That defensive use is legitimate and well-founded: genuinely interdisciplinary syntheses do fall between indexed fields, incommensurable vocabularies do impede retrieval, and requiring prior publication before pursuing an idea is a recipe for never pursuing anything new. Kuhn's descriptive claim that communities cluster around shared frameworks and that cross-framework work is under-published survives even Laudan's and Hoyningen-Huene's corrections to the strong incommensurability thesis.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (1) The assumption is used only defensively (do not abandon on absence) and never offensively (do not claim novelty from absence) — and the artefacts must be checked for offensive uses; (2) mundane explanations for absence are actively excluded before invoking it; (3) search power is stated with each absence claim; (4) no downstream finding rests on an absence.
    How to test: Take five existing "no prior literature found" claims. For each, spend 30 minutes with a scholarly index and citation-chaining from one anchor paper. If prior work is found for even two of the five, the assumption is being used to launder search failure and should be retired. This is a two-hour test with a decisive outcome and should be run before cycle 6.
