  Strength of challenge: Strong (against "entropy is an objective ordering principle"); Moderate (against the weaker claim that the two ladders are worth comparing)

  Summary: The source cell offers entropy, simplicity→complexity, and cause→effect as if they were one objective ordering. The located literature shows they are three, and that they disagree. Entropy is monotone in closed systems, but complexity is non-monotone (Aaronson, Carroll & Ouellette 2014; LMC), so an entropy ladder and a complexity ladder put the interesting middle levels in different places. Complexity itself has no agreed measure (Ladyman et al.; Lloyd 2001), so "simplicity to complexity" is not objective until a measure is fixed, and different measures rank levels differently. Cause→effect fails as an ordering where causation is bidirectional across levels (Noble; Craver & Bechtel). Potochnik & McGill argue discrete levels of any kind are problematic and should be replaced by scale. The claim that the earlier ladder "reaches for an objective ordering principle" is therefore true as a description of intent but the principle reached for does not exist in the form assumed.

  Specific risks: (a) If III.2.0 adopts the entropy ladder as spine, the rung order will contradict a complexity ordering and Carroll's own in-vault tradition. (b) "Objective" may be doing rhetorical work: the entropy ladder could be preferred over the disciplinary ladder (rows 247-250) on the grounds of objectivity it does not possess. (c) Any comparison of the two ladders that assumes they are "the same order under two descriptions" will fail if the orderings are genuinely different partial orders (see PRESUMPTION-909).

  Mitigations available: State which measure is meant (thermodynamic entropy? algorithmic complexity? statistical complexity?) before using it to order rungs. Treat the entropy ladder as one perspective among several (Wimsatt) rather than the objective spine. Use Potochnik & McGill's "scale" as the comparison axis, which the two ladders may actually share, rather than entropy or complexity.

STEELMAN:
  Item: ASSUMPTION-1258
  Strongest counterargument: There is no objective ordering principle here to compare against. Entropy orders closed systems monotonically but says nothing about levels of organisation (a cell and a rock at the same entropy are not on the same rung); complexity, the thing the cell actually wants, is non-monotone in entropy and has no unique measure; cause-to-effect is bidirectional across levels in every mature account. The earlier ladder is not more objective than the later one; it is a different subjective ladder wearing a physics vocabulary. Comparing the two on the assumption that one is objective will import a false tie-breaker into III.2.0.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The corpus fixes a specific measure (e.g. statistical complexity or scale) and shows that both ladders are monotone in it; or the outline uses the entropy ladder only as an authored perspective and does not claim objectivity for it.
  How to test: Take the rungs of both ladders (A, I, S, N, B, P, C, S and the row-292 ladder), assign each a scale (length/time) and, if possible, an entropy-production and a complexity estimate; check whether the three rankings agree. If they do not, the "objective ordering" claim is falsified for this corpus.
