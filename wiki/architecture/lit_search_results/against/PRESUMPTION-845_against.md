  Original statement: That a pipeline which discovers its own invalidation can act on that discovery. The run that filed REVISE-350 completed its cohort, minted three premises, and routed the remedy to a human who has not replied in eleven days.

  Reading challenged: The presumption itself — that discovering an invalidating finding confers the ability to act on it. The organisational-safety literature is unambiguous that disclosure and action are separate capacities, that disclosure without a corresponding authority is the standard configuration preceding accidents, and that unanswered escalation converts voice into silence.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The presumption fails on the clearest available evidence. Columbia is the canonical demonstration that a correct discovery, made by qualified people and formally escalated through a legitimate channel, produces no action when the authority to act sits elsewhere and declines — the imagery request was denied without debate. Discovery and the capacity to act are separate, and conflating them is the standing precondition for accidents rather than an unusual failure. Vaughan's normalisation-of-deviance account supplies the dynamic that makes the item's specific situation worse over time: operating past a known invalidation without consequence is precisely the pattern that converts a violated constraint into an accepted practice, and the pipeline has now been doing this for eleven days while continuing to mint premises under the method it declared invalid. The voice-and-silence literature adds the second-order cost: repeated escalation without response teaches the escalating party that escalation is futile, so the eleven-day silence degrades not only this remedy but the disposition to file the next one. And the constructive finding is that the missing control has a name and an established form. Safety-critical industries grant stop-work authority — the right to halt, immediately, without the approval of the party being halted — and C2A2 has escalation without it. The run that filed REVISE-350 could report; it could not stop. Its completing the cohort afterward is not a lapse but the direct and predictable output of a system built that way.

  Specific risks:
    - Continued production under a known-invalid method: three premises were minted after the invalidation was recognised. Every artefact produced in the eleven days inherits the invalidation, and nothing marks which ones.
    - Normalisation: the longer operation continues past the finding without consequence, the more the finding's status changes from "blocking" to "noted", and this transition is not recorded anywhere.
    - Voice extinction: unanswered escalation predicts reduced future escalation. The next invalidating discovery is less likely to be filed, and its absence will be indistinguishable from there being nothing to find (which is PRESUMPTION-841 again).
    - Escalation into the bottleneck: the remedy was routed to the same human actor who is the review constraint identified in PRESUMPTION-844. The escalation path terminates in the congestion it is meant to relieve, so escalation volume cannot be the remedy.
    - No stop state: the lifecycle has no representation for "suspended pending resolution", so the only available behaviours are continue or fail. A pipeline with no pause has, in practice, no brake.
    - Reflexivity: REVISE-350 concerns concordance-weighted confidence (ASSUMPTION-1150), which this very pipeline uses to disposition items — including, potentially, this one. The pipeline is producing judgements with an instrument it has itself declared invalid.

  Mitigations available:
    - Institute a stop-work state in the lifecycle: a finding of self-invalidation should place affected work in SUSPENDED, not merely file a remedy request. This is the direct analogue of the control the safety industries use and it does not require the human's involvement to take effect.
    - Set an escalation timeout with a default action. Eleven days with no reply should trigger something automatically — suspension, downgrade of affected outputs, or a second escalation route — rather than nothing.
    - Tag every artefact produced after an unresolved invalidation with the invalidation's identifier, so that the affected set is enumerable when the remedy arrives.
    - Provide an escalation path that does not terminate at the review bottleneck, since routing a remedy into a congested queue is a null operation (cf. PRESUMPTION-844).
    - Measure escalation-response latency and filing rate together. Per the voice literature, a falling filing rate following a rising response latency is the signature of voice extinction and is detectable before it completes.
    - Distinguish, in the register, "remedy filed and pending" from "remedy filed and abandoned" — an eleven-day silence currently reads as the former and may be the latter (again PRESUMPTION-841).

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-845
    Strongest counterargument: Knowing and being able to act are different capacities, and systems that conflate them fail in a characteristic and well-documented way. At Columbia, engineers correctly identified the hazard, correctly escalated it through a legitimate channel, and formally requested the imaging that would have confirmed it — and the request was denied without debate by the managers who held the authority. The discovery was not the problem; the routing of the discovery into a party with the power to ignore it was. Vaughan's account explains what happens next: each interval of continued operation past a recognised constraint, with no immediate consequence, makes the next such interval easier, until the constraint has been renegotiated rather than resolved. C2A2 has now run eleven days past a self-declared invalidation while continuing to mint premises under the invalidated method, which is not a delay in remediation but the normalisation mechanism executing on schedule. The voice literature supplies the compounding cost — escalation that goes unanswered teaches the escalator not to escalate — so the eleven days are also purchasing a lower future rate of exactly the discoveries this system depends on. And the missing control is not exotic: safety-critical industries grant every participant stop-work authority, the right to halt without the approval of the party being halted, precisely because escalation alone has been shown insufficient. C2A2 has escalation and no brake, so the run that filed REVISE-350 and then completed its cohort was not failing to follow its design; it was following it exactly.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) The escalation target would have to be responsive within a bounded time, and a bound would have to exist — eleven days with no defined timeout means the escalation has no failure mode, only an indefinite pending state; (b) the escalation path would have to terminate somewhere other than the actor who is already the system's bottleneck, or escalation volume simply adds to the congestion; (c) the lifecycle would have to carry a SUSPENDED state so that a self-invalidation can halt affected work without requiring the unavailable human; (d) artefacts produced during the pending period would have to be tagged with the invalidation, so that a late remedy can still be applied to them; (e) the pipeline's filing rate for invalidating findings would have to be monitored, since the voice literature predicts it will fall and the fall will look like an absence of problems.
    How to test: Three things, all measurable now. (1) Escalation latency distribution: for every remedy routed to a human in the past ninety days, record time-to-response. If the distribution has a long unbounded tail, REVISE-350 is not an outlier and the pathway is structurally non-functional rather than temporarily blocked. (2) Contaminated-output count: enumerate every artefact minted since REVISE-350 was filed that depended on the invalidated method. That number is the cost of having no stop state, and it grows daily. (3) Voice-extinction check: plot the rate at which runs file invalidating findings against the rolling escalation-response latency over the past several months. A negative relationship is the predicted signature and would show the second-order cost is already being paid — and it would mean the current low rate of such findings is evidence of suppression, not of health.
