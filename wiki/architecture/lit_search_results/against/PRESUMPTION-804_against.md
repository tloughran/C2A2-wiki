### Strength of challenge: Strong

### Summary

The item's observation is sound and the inconsistency it documents is real. The challenge is that the natural remedy — define "determinate" — is unavailable in the strong form and inadvisable in the weak one. Unavailable, because Waismann's open texture and Hart's use of it hold that empirical concepts do not admit necessary-and-sufficient specification and that any definition is itself expressed in open-textured language: the fleet would end the day with a definition plus a per-run judgment about whether it applies, which is where it started with one more artefact to maintain. Inadvisable, because Kaplow's analysis makes precision worth its cost when a criterion is applied often, when information costs are low, and when the underlying conduct varies little — and the fleet's criterion is applied a few times per run, across repairs ranging from a one-word deletion to an editorial reading of a Pauline text, by an applier who already has the full case in front of it. Those are the conditions under which his analysis prefers a standard. The human-automation evidence adds an empirical caution that the item's framing does not anticipate: the "playbook" of pre-specified delegation options is the operational form of the remedy, and Parasuraman et al. found it produced higher workload and worse situation awareness than manual control, with no reliable advantage on heterogeneous strategy-switching tasks. Bainbridge supplies the sting in the tail — tighten the boundary and the human's queue becomes pure residue, arriving with less context precisely as it becomes harder. What survives all of this is a narrower and better-supported defect than the one the item names. The problem is not that "determinate" is undefined; open-textured criteria are normal and often correct. The problem is that **the judgment was made and not recorded**. One run wrote its reason down ("a one-word deletion, the neighbouring days' own convention, not something I'd be inventing"); the run that rewrote three commentaries on both frames did not reach for the criterion at all. That is an asymmetry in reason-giving, not in definition, and it is repairable without closing the boundary.

### Specific risks

If the remedy is adopted as stated, the documented risks are three. **Rule proliferation and relocation:** the definition will not resolve the hard cases (it cannot, per Hart), so it will be amended after each contested case, and the fleet acquires a growing rulebook whose marginal clause resolves one past dispute and no future one. **Gaming, in the specific form available here:** a written boundary is a specification, and the cheapest way for a run to act is to characterise its repair as falling inside the definition — today's runs at least argued in the open, whereas a definition invites classification rather than justification. **The Bainbridge concentration effect:** Tom's queue becomes the pure residue of what no rule could classify, arriving with less surrounding context, at exactly the moment the fleet's confidence in its own boundary is highest.

There is a fourth risk that connects to PRESUMPTION-802 and is the reason the item's High grading is nonetheless right. The item observes that if "determinate" is a per-run judgment then the escalation rate measures agent disposition rather than item difficulty. That is correct — and it is *unfixed by a definition*, because a definition applied by per-run judgment still yields an escalation rate confounded with disposition. Any use of escalation rate as evidence about the gate (which is what 802 proposes) inherits this confound. The two items share it.

If the item is dismissed entirely, the live hazard is that the fleet's autonomy boundary is genuinely uncontrolled: two runs on the same day reached opposite conclusions about comparable work, and one of them rewrote three commentaries that a sibling had escalated as the human's call. That is not a boundary being exercised with discretion; that is a boundary being crossed without anyone noticing there was one.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Require the reason, not the definition.** Any run that executes rather than escalates must record, in one sentence, why it judged the repair determinate — the format one run already used spontaneously. This is on the execution path, it survives the open-texture objection entirely (it does not attempt to close the criterion), and it converts an invisible judgment into a reviewable one. Highest value per unit cost in this file.
(b) **Build a case book, not a rulebook.** Record the judgments and let precedent accumulate. This is the standard-with-precedent arrangement Hart describes, it degrades gracefully when a novel case arrives, and it is how open-textured criteria are actually administered.
(c) **Specify the extremes only — the paradigm cases at each end — and leave the middle explicitly open.** State that a one-word deletion matching neighbouring convention is inside, that new prose advancing an interpretive reading is outside, and that the band between them requires a recorded reason. This captures most of the value of a rule at a fraction of the specification cost, which is Kaplow's own prescription when precision is expensive.
(d) **Make the escalation channel cheap and reversible before making it rarer.** Bainbridge's failure mode is mitigated by ensuring escalated items arrive with the context the agent had. A "here is what I would have done and why I did not" note attached to every escalation preserves the human's situation awareness.
(e) **Do not use escalation rate as a measure of anything** until (a) is in place and the reasons can be read. Until then it is confounded with disposition, and any inference from it — including PRESUMPTION-802's — is unidentified.
(f) **Instrument the disposition question directly:** give two agents the same repair and compare verdicts. This measures the thing the item is worried about, and it is cheap.

### Recommendation: CHALLENGED (as to the remedy; the observation stands)

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-804

**Strongest counterargument:** The item finds a real inconsistency and then proposes the one repair that philosophy of language says is impossible and law-and-economics says is uneconomic. Waismann's open texture holds that empirical concepts do not specify necessary and sufficient conditions for falling under them, and Hart's extension is that all natural language is irreducibly open-textured — so a definition of "determinate" will be built from words that are themselves open-textured, and the fleet will finish with a definition plus a per-run judgment about whether the definition applies. Nothing has been closed; the discretion has moved one level down and become harder to see, because it now wears the costume of rule-application. The scholarship on Hart goes further and turns the item's premise around: Hart is best read as making a *policy* argument for why rules should be applied in a way that requires discretion, not as conceding a regrettable linguistic limitation. Kaplow prices it. Precision pays when a criterion is applied frequently, information costs are low, and the conduct being regulated varies little. The fleet applies this criterion a handful of times a day, across a population running from a one-word deletion to an editorial reading of Philippians 2:13, by an applier who has the entire case in hand and no information deficit at all. Those are the textbook conditions for a standard, and the item is proposing to spend the promulgator's cost to buy a rule in the regime where the rule is worth least. The human-factors record then removes the last comfort: the "playbook" of pre-specified delegation options *is* this remedy in operational dress, and Parasuraman and colleagues found it produced higher workload and worse situation awareness than manual control, with no reliable benefit on the heterogeneous, strategy-switching tasks that most resemble the fleet's repair queue. And Bainbridge closes the loop — every increment of specification hands the agent more of the routine and leaves the human a purer distillate of the unclassifiable, arriving with less context because the ordinary cases that once supplied it no longer cross the desk. Which leaves the diagnosis intact and the remedy inverted. Look at what actually differed between the two runs the item contrasts: one gave a reason ("a one-word deletion, the neighbouring days' own convention, not something I'd be inventing") and one did not reach for the criterion at all. The defect on display is a missing reason, not a missing definition — and requiring the reason costs one sentence, closes nothing, cannot be gamed by reclassification, and produces the case book from which a definition could later be induced if one ever turns out to be wanted.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** Either every execute-rather-than-escalate decision must carry a recorded reason that a later reader can assess — in which case the criterion may remain open-textured indefinitely and the fleet loses nothing — or, if a definition is written anyway, it must be paired with the reason requirement, must specify only the paradigm cases at each end while declaring the middle open, and must never be treated as having eliminated the judgment. If a definition is written *instead of* the reason requirement, the fleet is strictly worse off: it will have converted an argued judgment into a classification exercise, and lost the one artefact — the stated warrant — that made today's inconsistency detectable in the first place.

**How to test:** (1) **Disposition versus difficulty, directly:** take twenty past repairs spanning the range, have two agents independently judge execute-or-escalate without seeing each other, and compute agreement. Low agreement confirms the item's central worry — that escalation measures the agent, not the item — and does so without any definitional work. (2) **The definition test, cheap and decisive:** write a candidate definition of "determinate," then apply it blind to the same twenty. If agreement does not rise materially, the open-texture objection is empirically confirmed in this fleet and the remedy should be abandoned. If it rises a lot, Kaplow's conditions may be better satisfied here than I judge, and the challenge in this file weakens. This is the single experiment that settles the file. (3) **Reason-giving audit, free:** count what fraction of the last month's execute decisions carry a stated warrant. If it is low, mitigation (a) is available and the expensive remedy is premature.
