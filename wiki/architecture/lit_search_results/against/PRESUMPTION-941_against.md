  Summary: The item's inference runs from "damage is nil" to "no item." The precursor literature
  defines a near miss as an event whose causal chain is identical to the accident's up to a terminal
  step that differed in minor or non-robust ways — that is, in luck. On that definition the phrase in
  the record, "damage is nil, but by luck," is not a reason to close; it is the diagnostic criterion
  for opening. The decision literature adds the harm: recovered failures are systematically encoded as
  successes, perceived risk falls while statistical risk is unchanged, and where an outcome was
  spared by luck no accountability attaches and neither the actor nor the organisation learns. Vaughan
  supplies the longitudinal form of the same loop, and the OIG's finding that 61% of uncaptured harm
  was "not perceived as reportable" shows that the binding constraint on such systems is perception of
  reportability rather than willingness to report — again, exactly this item's mechanism. But the
  finding that should be reported first is not from the literature at all. **The estate already holds
  the corrective, and applied it in the same sentence in which it closed the item.** PREMISE-143 has
  been ACTIVE since 5 August carrying Dillon & Tinsley's conclusion that near-miss risk attenuation
  "is prevented only by framing the CHANCE element in the recovery." The run wrote "damage is nil, BUT
  BY LUCK." It named the chance element explicitly — performed the literature's one named prophylactic
  perfectly — and then used the sentence as a closing rather than an opening. That is a more
  uncomfortable result than a simple miss, because it shows the defect is not ignorance: the estate can
  produce the correct framing and still route on the outcome. Finally, the honest counterweight, which
  PREMISE-143 itself requires me to carry: every source above concerns human cognition and human
  organisations. Risk attenuation after a near miss is a psychological mechanism; agents do not have
  the complacency the studies measure. What survives the transfer is not the psychology but the
  INFORMATION-THEORETIC limb — the precursor definition — which holds regardless of who is doing the
  reading, and the STRUCTURAL limb: a system that filters records by realised severity has fewer
  records, whoever reads them.

  Mitigations available:

          it is one line: an item is opened by the CHAIN, not by the OUTCOME. Operationally — any
          event where a destructive or irreversible operation executed outside its intended scope
          opens an item, and the damage assessment is a FIELD on that item rather than a filter on
          whether it exists. This is cheap and it is the only mitigation that addresses the mechanism

  Strongest counterargument: The presumption routes on the one variable the safety literature says is
  least informative, and the estate has already written down why. Take the formal definition first: a
  near miss is a precursor whose ingredients "differ in only minor or non-robust ways from those
  necessary for a consequential event" — the causal chain is the accident's chain, and what differs is
  the last link. So when the record says "damage is nil, but by luck," it is asserting that the chain
  completed and the terminal link was decided by chance. On the literature's own definition that
  sentence is not a reason to close an item; it is the criterion for opening one. Closing on it
  discards a record that is informationally equivalent to the accident up to the point where luck
  intervened, and buys nothing, because the luck is not a property of the system and will not be there
  next time. Now the harder limb. Dillon and Tinsley's result is that recovered failures are encoded
  as successes: perceived risk falls while statistical risk is unchanged, and where the outcome was
  spared by good fortune, nobody is held to account for the decision and neither the individual nor
  the organisation learns. Vaughan's Challenger is the longitudinal version — deviation that survives
  becomes the standard. And the OIG's hospital data give the mechanism a number from the capture side:
  61% of uncaptured harm consisted of events staff did not perceive as reportable. Under-reporting was
  a perception failure, not a willingness failure, which is precisely what an "averted harm is not an
  event" rule institutionalises. Then the sharpest point, and it is the estate's own. PREMISE-143 has
  been ACTIVE since 5 August carrying the finding that near-miss attenuation is prevented ONLY by
  framing the chance element in the recovery. The run framed it. It wrote "but by luck". It executed
  the one prophylactic the literature names — and then filed the sentence as a closing. The defect is
  therefore not that the estate lacks the concept; it is that the concept is present in the prose and
  absent from the routing. That is a worse failure than ignorance, because no amount of further
  learning fixes it: it needs a rule that reads the words and changes what happens to the item.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Two conditions, one of which is a genuine defence.
  (1) The transfer condition must fail — and it partly does. Every source here measures human
  cognition: complacency, accountability, perceived risk. Agents do not become complacent, and an
  agent that reads a record next week has no memory of having got away with it. If the estate's items
  are read only by agents, the psychological limb of the challenge does not transfer, and PREMISE-143
  already carries exactly this caveat. What survives regardless is the structural limb: whoever reads
  the register, a register that filters by realised severity contains fewer precursor records, and no
  reader can learn from a record that was never written. So the safe condition is not "agents are
  immune"; it is "the estate opens the item anyway, and lets agents be the ones who read it without
  attenuation." (2) The estate would have to be able to show that the luck is a property of the
  system rather than of the occasion — that the regeneration path which made this over-deletion
  harmless is guaranteed for the next one. If that can be shown, "damage is nil" is a statement about
  a designed safeguard and closing is correct. Nothing in the record shows it, and the fact that the
  named repair is one narrowed glob suggests the opposite: the safeguard being relied on is not the
  glob.
