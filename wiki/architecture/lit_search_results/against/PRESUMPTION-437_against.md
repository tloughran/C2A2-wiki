  Sources:
    1. Reading-comprehension dimensionality literature (multidimensional IRT of comprehension; sub-construct validation, Language Testing). — Comprehension/understanding is standardly modeled as multidimensional, not unitary; a single composite can conflate distinct sub-abilities.
    2. Cronbach & Meehl, 1955, "Construct Validity in Psychological Tests." — A construct claim requires a nomological net (factorial, convergent, discriminant evidence); asserting a single valid score without this is unsupported by definition.
    3. Messick, 1995. — Validity is a property of *interpretations* of scores, established through multiple lines of evidence; an unexamined single-number "understanding" fails the modern construct-validity standard.
    4. Measurement-and-fairness literature (arXiv 1912.05511). — When a construct has multiple defensible operationalizations, no single operationalization fully captures it; treating one number as "understanding" reifies a measurement choice.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The presumption is directly challenged: the mainstream measurement literature treats understanding/comprehension as multidimensional and holds that a single score is valid only after construct-validity work (dimensionality, convergent/discriminant checks) — work the item flags as unperformed. Until that work is done, the numeric "understanding" is a plausible index but not a demonstrated valid measure of a single quantity, and effects on it (A-404's +0.086) inherit that ambiguity.

  Specific risks: The headline effect and the robust/directional/null sort (P-439) rest on a metric of unknown construct validity; a change in the score may reflect one sub-dimension (e.g., lexical overlap) rather than "understanding," which would misdirect every downstream conclusion, including the MacIntyre bearing claim (A-408).

  Mitigations available: Perform construct validation (factor structure; convergent validity vs author-ratification and vs blind human raters; discriminant validity vs surface lexical overlap — the study's own C0 fidelity-limb finding that lexical overlap "does no real work" is a start); report sub-scores, not just a composite.

  Recommendation: CHALLENGED

  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: A single score can be a perfectly serviceable *proxy* for a latent understanding dimension if it correlates well with author-ratification and blind human judgment, even absent a full factor model; demanding complete construct validation before any use would halt most applied measurement. The presumption is a real gap, but a remediable one, not a fatal flaw.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The metric is shown to load on a dominant single factor and to converge with independent understanding judgments; sub-dimensions are reported when they diverge.
    How to test: Factor-analyze the metric's components; correlate composite and sub-scores with author-ratification and blind human ratings; check discriminant validity against pure lexical overlap.
