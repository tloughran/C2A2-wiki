  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The literature does not deny that citation/co-occurrence records are useful signals, but it consistently shows that such records are themselves interpretive artifacts — subject to perfunctory citing, negative/refutational citing, incomplete indexing, and proxy-based attendance inference — rather than unmediated evidence of "contact." This weakens the sharp categorical distinction ASSUMPTION-825 draws between "external co-presence" (treated as raw fact) and "inferred cross-connection" (treated as interpretation): both are inferential, differing in degree of mediation and in whose inference is doing the work, not in kind. Whether CROSS-049 (the actual source) exhibits perfunctory, negative, or substantive citation was not verified in this search and would need item-level checking to weigh the challenge's applicability.

  Specific risks: If C2A2 treats CROSS-049-style co-presence as intrinsically higher-confidence than its own cross-connection inferences, it may over-weight a citation/co-mention that is perfunctory, incidental, or even oppositional, while under-weighting a well-grounded internal inference — inverting the intended confidence ordering for that specific pair of programs.

  Mitigations available: Yes — inspect the actual citation/co-mention context in CROSS-049 for engagement depth (substantive vs. perfunctory/negative) before treating it as a stronger evidential class; treat "external co-presence" as raising a floor on evidential weight rather than establishing a categorical ceiling over inference-based links.

STEELMAN:
  Item: ASSUMPTION-825
  Strongest counterargument: Scientometrics research shows that citation, co-authorship, and co-attendance records are not neutral observations of "contact" but are themselves shaped by citation politics, perfunctory/negative citing practices (up to half of citations show little engagement per some studies), incomplete database coverage, and proxy inference (e.g., inferring attendance from authorship lists). Because these external records are produced by the same kind of interpretive, error-prone human process that C2A2's own cross-connection inference is, elevating "external co-presence" to a categorically distinct and more trustworthy evidential class risks conflating provenance (came from outside vs. inside the system) with reliability (is actually informative about substantive intellectual contact). A citation can register two programs in the "same breath" for reasons entirely orthogonal to genuine exchange of ideas — a shared session, a rhetorical aside, a critique — while a careful internal analogy-based inference might correctly identify deep structural contact the citation record never captured.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The distinction survives if C2A2 uses "external co-presence" only as a floor-raising signal (independent corroboration) rather than a rank-ordering rule, and if it spot-checks CROSS-049 for the nature of the citation/mention (substantive engagement vs. perfunctory/negative) before treating it as evidentially privileged over inference.
  How to test: Retrieve the actual text/context of CROSS-049 and classify the citation type (substantive, perfunctory, negative/refutational, incidental co-mention); compare confidence calibration on a small sample of other cross-program links where both an external co-presence record and an internal inference exist, checking whether the external record in fact predicts independently-verified contact better than the inference does.
