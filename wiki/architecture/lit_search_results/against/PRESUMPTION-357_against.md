Discriminant validity (Campbell & Fiske 1959) — measures of DIFFERENT constructs should diverge; treating divergent numbers as estimates of one quantity to reconcile is a construct conflation. 269 (network footprint), 264 (cumulative production, retirees retained), 262 (surviving on-disk census) plausibly answer three different questions.
    2. Construct validity (Cronbach & Meehl 1955) — "reconcile to one number" presupposes one construct; if the operationalizations define different populations, reconciliation is a category error, not a measurement task.
    3. Stock-vs-flow distinction — cumulative production (a flow) and surviving census (a stock) are definitionally different and SHOULD differ by exactly the retired/reused set; "reconciling" them to one value erases a real, meaningful gap.

  Summary: The single-quantity presumption is strongly challenged. The three numbers map onto three recognizable, distinct constructs — graph footprint, cumulative production (flow, retirees kept), and surviving census (stock) — and discriminant validity plus the stock/flow distinction say these SHOULD differ; the gaps are informative, not error to be reconciled away. The reconciliation impulse is the category error: the task is to DISAMBIGUATE the three constructs and label each, not to force convergence to one "real" PRS number.
