  Date searched: 2026-08-24
  Original item: PRESUMPTION-858
  Original statement: Two success criteria imported from two traditions are presumed compatible because both are inside the paradigm being assembled. Friston-derived: success = belief overlap. Levin-derived: success = landing in an empty region. Maximal overlap is the least novel region; adopting both leaves the accelerator with no failure condition.

  Reading challenged: Not the observation that two criteria were imported without a compatibility check — that is real and the check was not done. What is challenged is the inference from "two criteria pull in opposite directions" to "therefore no failure condition." Several mature literatures hold novelty and consensus jointly as *conjunctive* success criteria with sharp, computable failure conditions, and have done so for decades. The absence of a failure condition here does not follow from the presence of two criteria; it follows from the absence of a stated aggregation rule. That is a specification defect, not an incommensurability. Separately, 15b found no literature supporting the crux as posed — there is no body of work showing that multi-tradition synthesis systems *characteristically* acquire unfalsifiable success conditions.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The item's central inference — two criteria pulling in opposite directions implies no failure condition — is contradicted by several independent literatures in which exactly this pair is held jointly and scored. The consensus definition of creativity has been bipartite and *conjunctive* since 1953: novelty and effectiveness are both necessary, so a conjunction has more ways to fail than either criterion alone, not fewer. Multi-objective quality-diversity optimisation is a running engineering answer: MOME keeps a Pareto front per novelty cell and reports a single MOQD score that can decrease, which is a failure condition. March's exploration/exploitation frame names two opposite failure modes for the same pair. Lakatos, invoked by the item's own search angle, in fact holds that an unfalsifiable hard core is normal and that the failure condition lives at the programme level — where "repeated corroboration of very similar ideas" is his definition of degeneration and is also an exact description of maximal-overlap optimisation. What the accelerator actually lacks is not a failure condition but an **aggregation rule**: nobody wrote down whether the two criteria are conjunctive, Pareto-dominated, or disjunctive. Only the disjunctive reading — pass if *either* criterion is met — is unfalsifiable, and nothing in the record shows that reading was chosen; it was simply never chosen against. Finally, 15b found a stronger and different objection than the item's: in morphospace theory, empty regions are empty because of functional, developmental and structural constraint. "Landing in an empty region" is therefore not merely in tension with overlap — it is inverted relative to its own source discipline, and would be a defective criterion even if the Friston-derived criterion were deleted.

  Specific risks:
    - Misdiagnosis: if this is dispositioned as an incommensurability, the remedy sought will be dropping one tradition. The literature says the remedy is writing one line specifying the aggregation rule. Dropping a tradition is expensive and does not fix a disjunctive gate.
    - The disjunctive default: if no rule is stated, implementations drift toward "count it as a success if it scores on either axis," which is the one reading that genuinely has no failure condition. This is the live hazard and it is not the one the item names.
    - Inverted novelty criterion (unflagged): rewarding empty regions imports the sign error from the morphospace metaphor. Regions of the cross-tradition space with no existing connections are the most likely to be empty because the connection is unsound. This risk is orthogonal to the overlap conflict and survives any resolution of it.
    - Asymmetric unfalsifiability: the FEP-derived half carries a documented unfalsifiability problem that the Levin-derived half does not. Treating the two criteria as symmetric partners hides where the risk actually sits.
    - Lakatosian degeneration is unmonitored: the accelerator has no measure of whether its output over time is novel content or "repeated corroboration of very similar ideas." That measure exists, is cheap, and would be the failure condition.

  Mitigations available:
    - State the aggregation rule explicitly and prefer conjunctive or Pareto. A candidate passes only if it clears a novelty floor *and* a coherence floor; report the pair, never a max.
    - Adopt an MOQD-shaped score: partition the cross-tradition space into descriptor cells, keep a Pareto front per cell, and report one scalar (hypervolume sum) that is allowed to go down. This is the literature's direct answer to "two criteria, no failure condition."
    - Replace "empty region" with "sparse but reachable region," and require an explicit reason-for-emptiness check before scoring a region as opportunity: is it empty because unexplored, or empty because constrained? McGhee's three constraint types (functional, developmental, structural) give a usable checklist.
    - Add a programme-level degeneration monitor per Lakatos: track the ratio of novel content to restatement across cycles. If the ratio falls monotonically, the programme is degenerating regardless of what either criterion says.
    - Score the two criteria against their own traditions before use — this is the task ASSUMPTION-1177 was raised for, and its outcome determines whether either criterion is correctly attributed at all. Do not resolve 858 before 1177.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-858
    Strongest counterargument: Holding novelty and consensus together is not a contradiction that a system stumbles into by importing carelessly from two traditions — it is the *definition* of the thing the accelerator exists to produce, and it has been operationalised with hard failure conditions for decades. The standard definition of creativity has been bipartite and conjunctive since the 1950s, and a conjunction is strictly harder to satisfy than either conjunct. Multi-objective quality-diversity optimisation already ships the exact architecture the item says is impossible: a Pareto front per novelty cell, aggregated into one score that can fall. March named the two opposite failure modes — the failure trap and the competency trap — in 1991. And Lakatos, whom the item's own search angle invokes, held that unfalsifiable cores are normal and located the failure condition at the programme level, where his description of degeneration ("repeated corroboration of very similar ideas") is a word-for-word description of what maximal-overlap optimisation produces. So the failure conditions exist, are well known, and are cheap. What is actually missing is one unwritten line: the aggregation rule. The item has diagnosed a missing specification as a philosophical impasse, and the two remedies are very different in cost. Worse, the framing distracts from a defect that is both larger and unmentioned: in the discipline that supplies the morphospace metaphor, empty regions are empty because of functional, developmental and structural constraint. "Landing in an empty region" is a sign error relative to its own source, and it would be a bad criterion even in a system that had never heard of Friston.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) The aggregation rule must be written down and must not be disjunctive — a candidate passes only by clearing both floors, or by sitting on a Pareto front, never by maxing one axis; (b) the novelty criterion must include a reason-for-emptiness check, so that constraint-empty regions are not scored as opportunity-empty; (c) a programme-level degeneration monitor must exist, tracking novel content against restatement over cycles, so that the failure condition is observable even when neither criterion is individually falsifiable; (d) the FEP-derived criterion's own unfalsifiability problem must be acknowledged as an asymmetric risk rather than folded into a symmetric "two traditions disagree" story; (e) ASSUMPTION-1177 must resolve first, because if either criterion is misattributed to its tradition then the whole conflict is an artefact of the import step rather than a property of the traditions.
    How to test: Take the last N accelerator outputs and score each on both axes independently. Plot them. Three observables settle it. First, is the accepted set the union of the two axes' top performers (disjunctive — unfalsifiable, the item is right in substance if not in reasoning), the intersection (conjunctive — falsifiable, the item is wrong), or a Pareto front (falsifiable, the item is wrong)? Second, compute the MOQD-style score across cycles and check whether it has ever gone down; a score that has never declined across many cycles is either a genuinely monotone system or an unfalsifiable one, and the tie is broken by deliberately injecting a known-bad candidate and confirming it is rejected. Third, for every output scored as "landed in an empty region," ask whether the region is empty because unexplored or because constrained — if the constrained fraction is high, the novelty criterion is inverted and that is a bigger finding than the conflict.
