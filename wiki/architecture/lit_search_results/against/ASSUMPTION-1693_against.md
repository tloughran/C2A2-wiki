  Strength of challenge: Weak-to-Moderate

  Summary: The replication literature broadly supports treating small non-preregistered studies
  with low confidence, so the direction of the grade is not contradicted. The challenge is to the
  grading rule. Sample size and preregistration are coarse proxies. Within the non-preregistered
  class, the strength of evidence (p-value, effect-size plausibility, independent replication)
  predicts replicability. Preregistration labels can be wrong or silently departed from, and
  studies can fail for reasons neither variable captures (analytic or modelling errors). A fixed
  "Speculative" grade can be too generous for flawed or withdrawn findings. It can also be too
  harsh for small studies with very strong evidence or independent replications.

  Specific risks: Uniform "Speculative" labels erase distinctions C2A2 needs for cross-tradition
  weighting. Contested or withdrawn Fredrickson-lineage findings could sit at the same grade as
  sound pilot data. Preregistered work could get unearned upgrades when its preregistration is
  nominal.

  Mitigations available: Use a multi-factor grade, e.g. GRADE-style downgrade/upgrade factors:
  risk of bias, imprecision, inconsistency, indirectness, publication bias, plus a replication
  count. Add an explicit "Contested/Withdrawn" tier below "Speculative". Check the
  preregistration actually matches the reported analysis before crediting it.

STEELMAN:
  Item: ASSUMPTION-1693
  Strongest counterargument: "Small and not preregistered" is a two-bit summary of a
    many-dimensional reliability question. The best-known evidence that preregistration drives
    replicability was itself retracted for misstating preregistration. Replication success
    tracks the strength of the original evidence more than labels do. The Fredrickson tradition
    includes a high-profile finding that failed for reasons that have nothing to do with sample
    size or preregistration. A rule that maps those two bits to one grade will be wrong in both
    directions often enough to matter.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: "Speculative" is used as a ceiling, not a fixed
    grade, with separate downgrade paths for known errors or withdrawal and upgrade paths for
    independent replication.
  How to test: Take the study behind 2598543c, check for independent replications, published
    critiques or corrections, and the strength of the reported evidence. Check whether the grade
    changes under a multi-factor rubric.
