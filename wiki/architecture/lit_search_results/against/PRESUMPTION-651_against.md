  Sources:
    1. Cortes, C. & Lawrence, N.D., 2021. "Inconsistency in Conference Peer Review:
       Revisiting the 2014 NeurIPS Experiment." arXiv:2109.09774. — Re-analysis of the
       2014 duplicate-committee experiment: 49.5% of papers accepted by one committee
       were rejected by an independent committee reviewing the same papers.
    2. Beygelzimer, A., Dauphin, Y., Liang, P. & Wortman Vaughan, J., 2023. "Has the
       Machine Learning Review Process Become More Arbitrary as the Field Has Grown?
       The NeurIPS 2021 Consistency Experiment." arXiv:2306.03262. — Larger replication:
       committees disagreed on 23% of accept/reject calls, ~50.6% of one committee's
       accepts were rejected by the other, and roughly half the accept list would change
       on a rerun. Disagreement was highest for the most selective dispositions.
    3. Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality, 2012. "Validity and Inter-Rater
       Reliability Testing of Quality Assessment Instruments." NCBI Bookshelf NBK92293.
       — Establishes that an instrument's inter-rater reliability must be re-established
       after revision, and that validity and reliability are independent: an instrument
       can be reliable and still measure the wrong construct.
    4. (2014). "Approximate measurement invariance in cross-classified rater-mediated
       assessments." PMC4274900. — In rater-mediated measurement, a substantial share of
       total variance is attributable to the rater and the occasion rather than to the
       object rated; models that omit this term systematically misattribute instrument
       variance to the items.
    5. Measurement-invariance literature (overview, ScienceDirect Topics; see also
       longitudinal invariance reviews, e.g. PMC11092300). — Invariance is the formal
       precondition for treating any score or score distribution as a statement about
       the object. Where the instrument is being revised between or during
       administrations, invariance is not established and comparisons are uninterpretable.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The peer-review consistency experiments are the closest available analogue
    and are damaging: with the same items and two independently constituted expert
    review processes, roughly half of the positive dispositions do not survive a rerun.
    That is an upper bound on how much of any single run's disposition mix can be
    attributed to the items. The psychometric literature supplies the formal statement
    of the problem — measurement invariance is the precondition for reading a score
    distribution as a property of what was scored, and an instrument that has declared
    its own arrangement insufficient has by its own testimony not established
    invariance. The AHRQ guidance adds the operational rule that reliability must be
    re-tested after revision, which has not happened here. Notably, the item's own
    internal evidence — the run declaring its arrangement insufficient — is itself
    the strongest single piece of disconfirming evidence, and the literature's role is
    to say that this declaration should have downgraded the dispositions, not merely
    accompanied them.

  Specific risks: If the disposition mix is instrument-attributable, then 6 REVISE out
    of 7 says the review arrangement is severe, not that the corpus is weak. Acting on
    it generates six revision cycles of work that address no real defect, consuming the
    review queue (compounding PRESUMPTION-656) and injecting churn into stable artifacts.
    Worse, the dispositions were issued at full authority, so nothing downstream records
    that they were provisional; a later reader cannot distinguish a REVISE grounded in
    the item from a REVISE generated by a mis-set instrument. If the instrument is
    subsequently revised, there is no marked cohort to re-adjudicate. The failure also
    corrupts any meta-statistic — a "REVISE rate" computed across runs with different
    instrument states is not a measurement of anything.

  Mitigations available: (1) Retro-label: mark every disposition issued by that run
    with the instrument state at time of issue, so the cohort is enumerable later.
    This is the single cheapest and most valuable action. (2) Downgrade authority to
    match self-declared sufficiency — a run that reports its arrangement insufficient
    should emit PROVISIONAL dispositions, and the system should have a distinct
    disposition value for this rather than reusing REVISE. (3) Run a duplicate-committee
    check in miniature: re-review two or three of the six under a deliberately different
    arrangement and measure agreement. Any disagreement above roughly one in three
    reproduces the NeurIPS finding and settles the question. (4) Establish a baseline
    expected disposition mix; a run whose mix departs sharply from baseline should
    trigger instrument inspection before item action. (5) Do not compute or report
    rates across runs whose instrument state differs.

  Recommendation: CHALLENGED

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-651
    Strongest counterargument: A high REVISE rate is exactly what one should expect
      from a corpus that has never been systematically reviewed before — first-pass
      review of unreviewed material legitimately produces mostly-negative dispositions,
      and inferring instrument bias from a skewed mix is itself a base-rate error. The
      peer-review experiments measure disagreement between two committees, not bias in
      one; they show that dispositions are noisy, not that they are uninformative, and
      a REVISE that is 50% likely to be reproduced still carries real information. And
      an instrument declaring its own arrangement insufficient is evidence of an
      unusually candid instrument, which is weak grounds for discounting its output —
      the alternative instruments in the system make no such declaration and are not
      thereby more trustworthy.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) The specific insufficiency the
      run declared is unrelated to the dispositions it issued — i.e. it concerned scope
      or throughput rather than judgment criteria. (b) The dispositions are individually
      justified by item-specific reasons that a reader can check without re-running the
      instrument. (c) Acting on a false REVISE is cheap and reversible, so the cost of
      instrument-attributable variance is bounded. (d) The disposition mix is comparable
      to a prior baseline from a differently-arranged run.
    How to test: Re-review three of the six REVISE items under a different arrangement
      (different prompt, different ordering, or a second agent) and compare dispositions.
      Also read the six REVISE justifications and count how many cite an item-specific
      defect versus a generic or process-level concern; a preponderance of generic
      reasons is direct evidence of instrument attribution.
