### Polarity note — what I am challenging

The item's *defective* belief is that a review verdict is durable — a fact about the day. **14b's corrective — the proposition I searched AGAINST — has three parts, and I searched all three: (1) a pass mark is a record of which questions one reviewer asked; (2) therefore the 307-pair corpus has no known quality level at all; (3) therefore the remedy is to record the METHOD, not merely the scope.**

- **(A) A verdict is a property of the reading, not of the object.** Searched disconfirmatorily; **found nothing against it**, and it is already held in this register in almost these words. Correct.
- **(B) "The corpus has no known quality level at all."** **Challenged, Strong.** A clean inspection of n items licenses a computable upper bound on the defect rate *for the defect classes the check had power to detect*. The correct statement is "a known bound for some classes and none for others," which is narrower, actionable, and immediately measurable.
- **(C) The remedy — record the METHOD.** **Challenged, and this is the clause the brief asked me to attack directly.** A self-declared method field is a self-report about process and inherits every documented failure mode of self-reported process compliance. The evidence that a recorded method predicts detection is weak even when the method is *measured* rather than declared.
- **(D) The four counterexamples were not randomly drawn.** **Challenged.** They were produced by targeted re-examination, so they establish existence, not rate.

### Sources

1. **Hanley, J.A. & Lippman-Hand, A. (1983), "If Nothing Goes Wrong, Is Everything All Right? Interpreting Zero Numerators," *JAMA* 249(13):1743–1745; the "rule of three."** [SNIPPET LEVEL — Hanley's own course PDF at jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca, Wikipedia and HandWiki entries on the rule of three, Beach 2015 *Anaesthesia* and Uzoigwe 2015 *Anaesthesia* follow-ups; **primary JAMA article NOT fetched**] — The direct refutation of clause (B). "If a certain event did not occur in a sample with n subjects, the interval from 0 to 3/n is a 95% confidence interval for the rate of occurrences in the population," and the paper's point is precisely that "observing zero events does not justify concluding that the underlying risk is zero" — one should quote the **interval**, not the zero. Applied here: nineteen consecutive clean static reports over a 307-pair corpus do not say the corpus is clean, but neither do they say nothing. They bound the rate of *the defect classes the static check can see* at roughly 3/n with 95% confidence, and the bound tightens as the streak lengthens. "No known quality level at all" throws away a computable number. The correct and far more useful formulation is two-sided: **a bounded rate for detectable classes, and a genuinely unbounded one for classes with zero detection power** — and the second set is enumerable by inspection of the check.
2. **Inozemtseva, L. & Holmes, R. (2014), "Coverage Is Not Strongly Correlated with Test Suite Effectiveness," ICSE 2014:435–445 (ACM Distinguished Paper).** [SNIPPET LEVEL — dl.acm.org listing, Semantic Scholar PDF listing, the morning paper and neverworkintheory summaries; **primary NOT fetched**] — The challenge to clause (C) at its strongest point. Five large Java projects, 1000+ test methods each, effectiveness measured by mutant detection: "there is a **low to moderate correlation between coverage and effectiveness when the number of test cases in the suite is controlled for**, and **stronger forms of coverage do not provide greater insight into the effectiveness of the suite**." This is the closest available analogue to the proposed remedy, and it is unkind to it. Coverage is the software world's METHOD field: a recorded, *machine-measured*, non-self-reported description of what the check looked at. It still fails to predict whether faults are found, and refining the description (branch, path) does not help. If a mechanically measured method description is only weakly predictive of detection, a **prose method field written by the reviewer** is not going to carry the weight the item assigns it.
3. **Urbach, D.R. et al. (2014), "Introduction of Surgical Safety Checklists in Ontario, Canada," *New England Journal of Medicine* 370:1029–1038.** [SNIPPET LEVEL — PubMed 24620866, NEJM correspondence 10.1056/NEJMc1404583, secondary summaries; **primary NOT fetched**] — The most direct evidence that a self-declared process field can be near-saturated while the effect it certifies is absent. Over 100 Ontario hospitals; mandatory checklist implementation; **self-reported compliance over 90% at almost all participating hospitals**; complications 3.86%→3.82% and 30-day mortality 0.71%→0.65%, neither statistically significant. The lesson transfers exactly: *a high-compliance process field is compatible with zero improvement in the outcome the field exists to certify.* If C2A2 adds a METHOD field, the predictable steady state is a field that is populated on ~100% of reviews, is stable, is reassuring, and carries no information about whether the review would have caught the defect.
4. **Audit documentation doctrine — ISA 230's "experienced auditor" test, and what PCAOB inspections actually find.** [SNIPPET LEVEL — ISA 230 summary; PCAOB inspection practice summaries via trullion.com, compliance-seminars.com, corpready.in; **CANONICAL — NOT re-verified**: IAASB ISA 230, PCAOB AS 1215] — This source cuts both ways and I record both. **For the item:** the standard is that documentation must let "an experienced auditor, having no previous connection with the audit, understand the nature, timing, and extent of audit procedures performed, the results... and the conclusions reached," and "oral explanations by the auditor, on their own, do not represent adequate support for the work the auditor performed." That is the strongest available warrant for recording method. **Against the item:** the inspection reality is that "documentation deficiencies can result in Part I.A findings **even when the underlying audit work was adequate**, because inspectors can only evaluate what the file contains" — i.e. the field measures the file, not the work, and the two come apart in both directions. The profession's actual answer to "is this verdict trustworthy?" is **re-performance**: the inspector redoes a sample of the work and compares. The method field is the *input* to that check, not a substitute for it. 820 proposes the field and not the re-performance, which is adopting the artefact without the mechanism that gives it meaning.
5. **The four counterexamples are a targeted, not a random, sample.** [internal to the fleet] — "That pair passed on 2026-08-03. Every one of these was found because someone went looking at a specific artifact for a specific reason. That establishes **existence** of pass-marked defects — decisively, and it is worth having — but it supports no rate, and the item's leap to "the 307-pair corpus has no known quality level at all" runs through an unstated rate claim. Under source 1's machinery the honest move is the opposite of the item's: use the *streak* as the denominator, compute the bound for detectable classes, and enumerate the undetectable classes separately.

### Summary

The item's diagnosis is right and is already in the register; its two consequential claims are both weaker than stated. On the corpus's quality level: a clean inspection is not silence. Hanley and Lippman-Hand's rule of three gives a 95% upper bound of 3/n on the rate of any event not observed in n trials, and the whole point of the paper is that a zero numerator should be reported as an interval rather than as a zero or as nothing at all. Nineteen consecutive clean static reports therefore *do* bound the rate of the defect classes the static check can detect, and the bound tightens with the streak; what they say nothing about is the classes with zero detection power — of which "checked whether the ids exist rather than what they say" names one exactly. So the true statement is "a known bound for some classes and none for others, and the partition is enumerable by reading the check," which is both more accurate and immediately actionable, where "no known quality level at all" is neither. On the remedy, the search is unkind. Coverage is the software world's machine-measured method field, and Inozemtseva and Holmes found only a low-to-moderate correlation with fault detection once suite size is controlled, with stronger coverage criteria adding nothing — a *measured* method description barely predicts detection. Urbach et al.'s Ontario study is the human version and is starker: mandatory surgical checklists with self-reported compliance above 90% across a hundred hospitals produced no significant change in complications or mortality. A self-declared process field can sit near saturation while the effect it certifies is absent, and C2A2's proposed field would be exactly that kind of field. The audit profession, which has thought hardest about this, does require a method record — ISA 230's experienced-auditor test, and the rule that oral explanation is not support — but it treats that record as the *input to re-performance*, not as the assurance itself, and its inspectors report the two coming apart in both directions: files deficient where the work was fine, and files describing procedures the inspector cannot confirm were performed. The item adopts the artefact and omits the mechanism. Finally, the evidence base: the four pass-marked defects were found by targeted examination, so they prove existence and not rate, and the register already holds the diagnosis in PREMISE-113 (a clean reading is evidence about the instrument until precision and recall are measured against a labelled corpus), the tuple in PREMISE-101 (scope, method, time), and an explicit prior refusal of a self-descriptive coverage metric in PREMISE-109 — whose stated reason, that such a metric "would read green during exactly the failure it was built to catch," is a precise prediction about what a METHOD field will do.

### Specific risks

**The method-field placebo, and it is the main risk.** A populated METHOD field looks like assurance, is cheap to populate, will reach ~100% coverage, and — on the best available analogues — will not correlate with whether defects are caught. Worse, it is *self-descriptive*: the reviewer who did not know to check what the ids say will write a method line that does not mention it, and the field will read clean. PREMISE-137's formulation is the exact hazard: perceived coverage without detection is worse than the gap it was added to close, because it retires the worry.

**Throwing away the computable bound.** If "no known quality level at all" is adopted, the fleet forfeits a number it can produce in an afternoon (rule-of-three bounds per detectable class) and replaces it with an unquantified anxiety. Unquantified anxieties do not get prioritised against each other; numbers do.

**Rate inflation from a targeted sample.** Four defective pass-marks found by looking hard at four artifacts, if read as a rate over 307 pairs, licenses a corpus-wide invalidation that PREMISE-118's clause (i) explicitly forbids — the obligation is assessment, not automatic invalidation.

**Register fragmentation.** Two items in 48 hours on one mechanism, with (scope, method, time) already minted in PREMISE-101 and the coverage-style remedy already refused in PREMISE-109, is the PREMISE-138 pattern. The next reader gets whichever they grep first.

**Risk of dismissing the item, at full strength.** The four defects were real and predated their pass marks; "the earlier runs checked whether the ids *exist* rather than what they *say*" is a precise, correct and damning mechanism; and the reviewing layer genuinely has no method field. The sentence "nineteen consecutive clean static reports is not evidence of a clean vault" is correct and was, as the item notes, offered as a rhetorical closing line rather than a schema requirement. That is a real gap and it should not be lost in the argument about how to close it.

### Mitigations available

(a) **Replace "no known quality level" with two numbers.** For each check, list the defect classes it can detect and compute the rule-of-three bound from the clean streak; list the classes it cannot detect and mark them unbounded. This is the honest, computable form of the item's claim.
(b) **Adopt re-performance, not documentation, as the assurance mechanism.** Re-review a random sample of already-passed pairs with a deliberately different question set and report the discrepancy rate. That measures the reviewer; a method field only describes them.
(c) **Mutation-validate the review** (PREMISE-137): inject a known defect of each class into a test pair and confirm the review catches it. This converts "which questions did the reviewer ask" from a self-report into a measurement, and it is the only route in this file with evidence behind it.
(d) **If a method field is added anyway, bind it to PREMISE-109's constraint** — record *claims-without-evidence* per verdict (which artifact, which timestamp, which query would have to hold), never a descriptive coverage narrative.
(e) **Fold 820 into PREMISE-101 as the verdict extension of the (scope, method, time) tuple** rather than minting a third item alongside 810.
(f) **Discharge PREMISE-118 on the four known cases** — assessment over results since last known-good review, explicitly bounded against automatic invalidation of the 307.

## STEELMAN

**Item:** PRESUMPTION-820

**Strongest counterargument:** The item is right about what a pass mark is and wrong about both things it concludes from it, and the second error is the expensive one because it is the remedy. Take the corpus claim first. "The 307-pair corpus has no known quality level at all" treats a clean inspection as informationless, but the statistics of zero numerators has been settled since 1983: if an event does not occur in n trials, 0 to 3/n is a 95% confidence interval for its rate, and Hanley and Lippman-Hand's explicit lesson is that a zero should be reported as that interval rather than as a zero *or* as nothing. Nineteen consecutive clean static reports do bound the rate of everything the static check can see, and the bound gets better as the streak grows. What they cannot bound is the classes with zero detection power — and the item has *identified* one of those with unusual precision ("checked whether the ids exist rather than what they say"). So the accurate claim is a partition: bounded here, unbounded there, and the partition is readable off the check in an afternoon. The item's version discards a computable number and replaces it with a mood. Now the remedy, which is where the search bites hardest. Coverage is software's machine-measured method field — an objective, non-self-reported record of what the check looked at — and Inozemtseva and Holmes found only low-to-moderate correlation with fault detection once suite size is controlled, with stronger coverage criteria giving no additional insight. If the *measured* version barely predicts detection, the prose version written by the reviewer will not. Urbach et al. supply the human-process case and it is worse: mandatory surgical safety checklists across a hundred Ontario hospitals, self-reported compliance above 90%, and no significant change in complications or mortality. That is the shape of a self-declared process field at steady state — saturated, stable, and uninformative about the outcome. The auditing profession does demand a method record under ISA 230's experienced-auditor test, and does hold that oral explanation is not support; but it treats the record as the input to **re-performance**, and its own inspectors report documentation and work diverging in both directions — Part I.A findings raised where the work was adequate, because "inspectors can only evaluate what the file contains." 820 proposes the file and not the re-performance. And the evidence: four pass-marked defects, all found by targeted examination of specific artifacts, establish existence and no rate at all. The register meanwhile already holds the diagnosis (PREMISE-113: a clean reading is evidence about the instrument until precision and recall are measured against a labelled corpus), the tuple (PREMISE-101: scope, method, time), and — most tellingly — an explicit prior refusal of a self-descriptive coverage metric on the ground that it "would read green during exactly the failure it was built to catch" (PREMISE-109). That sentence is a prediction about the METHOD field, written before the METHOD field was proposed.

**What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe:** The method field is safe and useful exactly where it is (i) an **input to re-performance** rather than a substitute for it — a random sample of passed items is re-reviewed with a different question set and the discrepancy rate is published; (ii) **evidence-pointer shaped** rather than narrative, per PREMISE-109's instrumentation constraint (which artifact, which timestamp, which query), so it cannot rise with effort while detection stays flat; and (iii) accompanied by **mutation validation** of the review itself (PREMISE-137), so that "which questions were asked" becomes a measured property rather than a declared one. And the corpus claim is safe once restated as the two-part partition — rule-of-three bounds for detectable classes, unbounded for the rest — which is stronger than the item's version, not weaker, because it names which classes are actually at risk. Under those conditions nothing in this file bites. Absent them, the predicted outcome is a well-populated field, a retired worry, and an unchanged detection rate.

**How to test:** (1) **The re-performance sample, and it is decisive.** Draw 30 pairs at random from the 307 that carry a `pass`. Re-review with an explicitly different question set (what the ids *say*, not whether they exist). Report the discrepancy rate. If it is near zero, the item's rate implication is refuted and the four cases were unrepresentative; if it is high, the item is confirmed at rate rather than at existence — and either way the fleet learns a number instead of arguing about a schema. (2) **The rule-of-three computation:** for each check, enumerate detectable defect classes and compute 3/n from the clean streak. One afternoon, and it replaces "no known quality level at all" with a table. (3) **The mutation test on the reviewer:** inject one known defect of each class into a synthetic pair and run the standard review. Classes it misses are the unbounded set, measured rather than inferred. (4) **The placebo forecast:** if a METHOD field is added, pre-register the prediction that within four weeks it will be populated on >90% of reviews and that discrepancy rate under (1) will be unchanged. Urbach is the base rate; if C2A2 beats it, that is a genuine finding worth publishing in-house.
