  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The claim installs the neocortical column as a rung (III.2.B) between "neuronal" and "personal" on the authority of Hawkins' Thousand Brains Theory. The peer-reviewed anatomical and physiological literature is, at best, divided on whether the column is a real unit at all: Horton & Adams (2005) conclude it has no demonstrated function and no consistent definition; da Costa & Martin (2010) find no anatomical correlate; Barbas et al. (2022) show columns are structurally unequal across cortex; Rockland notes the term now covers incompatible definitions. TBT's specific claim — that each column is a complete, near-identical sensorimotor modelling unit — is the strongest form of exactly the assumption these authors reject. The assumption therefore rests on a contested reading of one tradition presented in the corpus as settled fact ("the Brain's interpretation").

  Specific risks: (a) III.2.B may be a rung named for a unit that does not exist as a level of organisation — it would then be an artefact of the source tradition, not of nature, and cells classified to it (27, per PRESUMPTION-905) inherit that fragility. (b) If "column" is not a discrete level, the ladder gains a rung with no clear membership criterion, so B/N and B/P boundaries are undecidable in principle. (c) Building C2A2 agent architecture on "columns as parallel identical modellers" imports TBT's weakest empirical commitment.

  Mitigations available: Re-describe B functionally rather than anatomically (e.g. "local circuit / mesoscale model" — a level Hawkins, Mountcastle and critics all accept exists in some form, even if not as a column). Mark B explicitly as "Hawkins' interpretation" rather than "the Brain's interpretation," which the source cell already half-does. Treat B as a hypothesis rung with a stated exit condition.

STEELMAN:
  Item: ASSUMPTION-1257
  Strongest counterargument: The cortical column is a physiological regularity (receptive-field constancy along a vertical penetration) that has never been matched by an anatomical unit, has no agreed size or boundary, varies systematically across cortical areas, is absent in some species, and is heavily coupled to its surround by horizontal connections. To install it as a distinct level of organisation is to reify a recording artefact; TBT's "thousand brains" doubles down by asserting near-identical function across units that anatomists show are unequal. A ladder rung requires a membership criterion; "column" cannot supply one.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: B is defined as a functional mesoscale (local recurrent circuit / canonical microcircuit) rather than as Mountcastle's or Hawkins' column; the outline labels B as one tradition's reading, not the brain's; classification into B does not depend on cells mentioning "column."
  How to test: Re-read the 27 III.2.B cells and ask whether each would survive if "column" were replaced by "local circuit"; check whether any B cell relies on TBT-specific claims (identical units, per-column reference frames) that the critics above reject.
