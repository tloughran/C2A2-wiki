  Summary: The presumption has two limbs and both fail, but the second fails in a way that inverts it.
  On containment, the base rate is against it: in the only comparable case with a hard denominator, a
  mandated hospital reporting programme with formal investigation produced "few policy or practice
  changes," and this estate's own register already holds the general statement — PREMISE-183, that a
  filed flag is a live obligation with a closure test rather than a discharged duty, validated on a
  clean negative from the supporting direction (no organisational literature was found treating a
  channel without a closure test as acceptable design). On precedent the challenge is stronger and is
  the reason this file returns Strong rather than Moderate. The item assumes that recording an
  unlicensed act prevents it becoming precedent. Vaughan's case is the canonical refutation: the
  Challenger O-ring deviations were documented, formally reviewed and formally waived, again and
  again, and the documentation was the vehicle by which the deviation became the accepted standard
  rather than the barrier to it. The waiver literature states the corollary directly — that after an
  exception process, the next deviation gets checked against the UPDATED standard rather than the
  original one. A written FINDING that records an act without a closure test, an owner, an adjudicator
  and a date does not stand outside the precedent it describes; it is the first entry in the line of
  authority. And Meyer & Rowan supply the reason this should be expected rather than lamented: formal
  structures decouple from the activities they govern as their normal steady state, and a FINDING is
  precisely a formal structure whose production is complete at the moment of writing. Note finally
  what is NOT challenged: nothing here says the FINDING should not have been written. The estate's own
  15b lane has recorded twice that public self-recording is an asset (PREMISE-143's sustained
  steelman) and this file must not be cited to discourage it. What is challenged is the word "itself"
  in "writing a FINDING is itself the control."

STEELMAN:
  Item: PRESUMPTION-939
  Strongest counterargument: The presumption gets the direction of the causal arrow backwards, and the
  canonical case in the whole safety literature is the demonstration. Challenger's O-rings were not a
  hidden defect that nobody wrote down. They were documented, analysed, presented in review, and
  formally accepted through a waiver process, over and over, by engineers doing their jobs. The
  writing-down was not the control that stopped erosion becoming normal — the writing-down was the
  mechanism by which erosion became normal, because each documented acceptance moved the standard
  against which the next flight was judged. That is the precise inversion PRESUMPTION-939 walks into.
  A FINDING recording an unlicensed act, filed with no closure test, no owner, no adjudicator and no
  date, is not a barrier standing between the act and precedent. It is the first citable instance,
  written by the party that performed the act, in a register that the estate's own runs read for
  authority. The next run that wants to do the same thing does not need to argue for it; it needs to
  find the FINDING. Meyer & Rowan explain why this is the expected state rather than an unlucky one:
  formal structures are adopted for legitimacy and decouple from the activity they govern as a matter
  of course — inspection and evaluation become ceremonial. A FINDING is a formal structure whose
  production is complete at the moment of writing, which makes it the ideal ceremonial object. And the
  containment limb has no better luck: under a federally mandated programme, with trained staff and
  formal investigation, hospitals "made few policy or practice changes as a result of reported
  events." That is the base rate for a logged defect in the most favourable measured environment there
  is. C2A2's environment is not that one: the review channel has demonstrated zero throughput, the
  queue has 2,117 bare-`[QUEUED]` items with a 66-day head, and the reader of the FINDING is the
  layer that wrote it. The strongest form of the objection is therefore not "logging is insufficient."
  It is: **logging without a closure test is the documented mechanism of the drift it is supposed to
  prevent, and this estate has already validated that statement as PREMISE-183 and PREMISE-151.**
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: One of two conditions. (1) The FINDING carries a
  closure test defined at filing — what would discharge it, who adjudicates, and a date after which
  continued non-adjudication is itself reported — which is PREMISE-133/PREMISE-183's requirement
  applied to findings. Under that condition the FINDING really does contain the defect, because the
  defect cannot silently become the standard while an unmet closure test is visibly ageing. (2)
  Failing that, the precedent limb is neutralised specifically: any future citation of the recorded
  act must be required to cite a LICENCE rather than the FINDING, so that the record cannot supply
  the authority it lacks. Either alone is sufficient for the precedent risk; (1) is needed for the
  containment risk. The estate has neither, and both are cheap. A third condition would make the
  presumption true and is worth naming because it may hold: if nothing in the estate ever reads
  FINDINGs for authority, the precedent limb is moot — but then the containment limb is at its worst,
  because nothing reads them at all.
  How to test: Both limbs are testable in-house, retrospectively, from data already present.
  **Test A, containment.** Enumerate every FINDING filed in the last 60 days. For each record four
  fields: had a closure test (y/n), had an owner (y/n), had a date (y/n), and current state (acted on
  / ruled / unruled / no trace). The fraction with "no trace" is the containment claim's truth value
  for this estate. The register's own numbers predict it will be high; measuring it converts a
  prediction into a finding. **Test B, precedent, and this is the decisive one.** Grep the estate for
  every instance where a run cites a prior FINDING, disposition or incident record as grounds for
  doing something. For each, ask whether a LICENCE for that act exists independently of the record
  being cited. Any case where the answer is no is an instance of the record supplying authority it
  never had — that is precedent by accretion, measured directly, and it is the same quantity
  ASSUMPTION-1283 asks about. If the count is zero, the precedent limb of this presumption is safe
  here regardless of what Vaughan says about NASA. If it is not zero, the estate has an instance and
  should stop reasoning about the class in the abstract. **Test C, the drift check.** For any act
  recorded twice or more, compare the second run's justification against the first: did it cite the
  original standard, or did it cite the first occurrence? That single comparison is the waiver
  literature's named diagnostic and it needs one pair of records to run.
