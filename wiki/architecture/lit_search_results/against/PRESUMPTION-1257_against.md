  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: The literature separates core intuitions (approximate number, basic geometry), which look widely shared, from mathematics as a practice (number lines, exact arithmetic, notation, proof standards, what problems matter), which is culturally acquired and has changed over time. Treating "mathematics" as neutral ground merges the two. Ethnomathematics adds that formal Western mathematics has served as a standard for ranking other traditions, so offering it as neutral can carry that history with it.
  Specific risks: In cross-tradition dialogue C2A2 could treat formal mathematics as the arbiter that every tradition must map onto, privileging the tradition that built it (and the training corpus of the models; see ASSUMPTION-2153). Participants from traditions with other mathematical practices may experience the "neutral" ground as foreign ground.
  Mitigations available: Distinguish core intuitions from formal practice in design text; treat any shared mathematics as a candidate common ground to be checked with each tradition, not a starting presumption.
  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-1257
    Strongest counterargument: Even the cleanest case for universal mathematics (Munduruku geometry) shows only shared intuitions. The parts of mathematics a system would actually use (exact number, linear scales, formal proof) were learned within particular traditions and have been used to rank others. Calling that neutral is a claim from inside one tradition, which is the move MacIntyre warns against.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The shared space is restricted to core intuitions or to results each tradition affirms on its own terms, and the design says so.
    How to test: In-house: in tradition wikis, find how each tradition treats number, proof and certainty; check whether any tradition rejects or reframes the formal standards C2A2 uses.
