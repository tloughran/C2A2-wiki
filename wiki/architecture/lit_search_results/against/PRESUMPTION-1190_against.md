    1. Cambridge University Press, "Behavioral and Brain Sciences: Author instructions" (accessed
       2026-10-27). [read in full]
       — Direct challenge. "Commentary articles appear by invitation only." Each target article
       carries "20-40 commentaries ... from specialists within and across these disciplines", and
       the format's stated aim includes "the unification of research". So the venue deliberately
       assembles cross-disciplinary co-occurrence, and that co-occurrence is designed in rather
       than emerging from the traditions. Commentators are asked for "substantive criticism,
       interpretation, and elaboration", so a shared target is equally consistent with
       disagreement.
    2. BBS back matter (e.g., vol. 33(4), vol. 37(5)) on commentator selection. [snippet only]
       — Commentators are chosen through "systematic bibliographic searches", e-mail calls, the
       BBS Associateship and author nominations. The target author's own suggestions shape who
       co-occurs.
    3. McCain, K.W., 1986. "Cocited author mapping as a valid representation of intellectual
       structure." JASIS 37(3):111-122; Ahlgren, Jarneving & Rousseau 2003 (JASIST 54(6)).
       [secondary; abstracts only]
       — Failed transfer. Co-citation has some validation (McCain found agreement with judged
       similarity, with deviations from policy orientation and publication lag). But that
       validity comes from aggregating many independent citers' choices. Co-commentary is one
       editorial selection event per target. The co-citation validation literature does not
       carry over to it.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate. The venue mechanics are documented in a primary source, but
    I found no study that directly tests whether BBS co-commentary predicts later convergence.

  Summary: In BBS, co-occurrence on a target is produced by invitation, and the journal selects
    for breadth across disciplines. Three traditions commenting on Seth is the expected output of
    the format, whether or not those traditions converge. Commentaries are also often critical, so
    co-presence does not imply agreement. Bibliometric support for co-citation as a similarity
    signal relies on aggregate independent behaviour and does not apply to a single curated
    event.

  Specific risks: The Pattern Detector counts a venue artefact as convergence, inflating
    cross-tradition links. Each BBS issue would add 20-40 spurious pairings.

  Mitigations available: Treat co-commentary as a candidate signal only. Require content-level
    evidence (shared claims or mutual citation in the commentaries). Down-weight invited-format
    venues. Compare with a baseline: how often do random pairs of BBS commentators "converge"?

  Recommendation: CHALLENGED
