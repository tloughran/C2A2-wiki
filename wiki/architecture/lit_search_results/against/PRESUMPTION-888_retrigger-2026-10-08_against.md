  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The conditional draws its line in the wrong place. Its second clause — use is circular when
    the only consumer is the assessing system's own instrument — is untouched and is, if anything,
    strengthened a fortiori. But its first clause — use is legitimate when consumers are external — is
    contested inside archival theory itself: the provenance/function school (Posner, Duranti) holds that
    value should not be located in use at all, by any user, and macro-appraisal's advocates reject
    user-driven appraisal because external users follow fashion. Independently, a streetlight study
    shows external consumers' use clustering where access is easiest. The variable that matters is
    therefore not whether the consumer is external but whether consumption is shaped by availability,
    and external consumption often is. Use-based valuation remains a defensible position (cycle-0's
    point stands) but not one licensed by externality alone.

  Specific risks: (i) adopting the conditional would certify use-based valuation whenever an outside
    reader exists, re-importing the streetlight bias through the external channel; (ii) material that
    neither the internal instrument nor any external reader touches is valued at zero under both clauses
    — the conditional has no slot for UNASSESSED, which is MONITOR-565's own discriminating test;
    (iii) the conditional's lineage (proposed by 15b, adopted by 15c) means a future "confirmation" from
    the same channel would be self-confirmation.

  Mitigations available: replace the externality criterion with an availability check (is the material
    that is used the material that is easiest to reach?); record unread material as UNASSESSED rather
    than low-value; allocate a small fixed share of assessment to content-based (provenance/function)
    appraisal independent of use, per macro-appraisal; run MONITOR-565's discriminating test.

    consumer is the assessing instrument; use by external consumers is a defensible but availability-
    biased proxy") would survive this search.

STEELMAN:
  Item: PRESUMPTION-888 (the external/internal conditional)
  Strongest counterargument: The conditional treats "external" as the cure for circularity, but the
    circularity was never about ownership of the instrument; it was about value being inferred from
    where light happens to fall. External readers have their own streetlights — access, fashion,
    discoverability — and the archival profession's most theoretically developed school abandoned
    use-based appraisal precisely because external users were "weather vanes." A rule that licenses use
    as a proxy whenever someone outside reads the material will rate everything easy-to-find as valuable
    and everything hard-to-find as worthless, which is the original presumption with an extra step.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: the external consumers' access to the material is
    roughly uniform (no material is systematically harder to reach), or use is one input alongside a
    content/function-based assessment and never the sole determinant.
  How to test: for a sample of material, compare use counts with an independent ease-of-access measure
    (link in-degree, position in navigation, presence in narration tracks); a strong correlation means
    use is measuring access, not value.
