  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: The presumption assumes that misplacement is self-announcing to a sufficiently expert reader. The evaluation-methods literature says the opposite: expert detection without a specified criterion is highly evaluator-dependent (Hertzum & Jacobsen), and reading-technique experiments show that stating *specific* fault targets is what raises detection, while generic or absent guidance leaves reviewers at ad hoc yield (Porter, Votta & Basili and replications). The proofreading literature adds that the error class most likely in a reordered corpus — plausible content in the wrong place — is exactly the class human readers miss most, and that the author's own expectation of meaning can mask it. Finally, the implicit criterion a reader carries is not stable over a long task; vigilance research shows it drifts conservative. "Tom will know it when he sees it" is therefore not a success criterion; it is the absence of one, and the literature predicts that absence lowers yield, makes the result unrepeatable, and makes "the outline holds" indistinguishable from "nothing jumped out."

  Specific risks: (a) The read returns "holds" and the verdict cannot be distinguished from a null result of an under-powered detector. (b) A second reader (or the same reader on a different day) would return a different set of problems, so the ToC is validated against one draw from a 5-65% agreement distribution. (c) Cells misfiled at rung boundaries (III.2.N vs III.2.B, III.3 vs III.4) are plausible in either location and are the least likely to be caught — the exact cells PRESUMPTION-905 and -908 flag as at risk. (d) Without a criterion, there is no stopping rule; the read ends when the reader stops, not when the question is answered.

  Mitigations available: Yes — write the criterion first. Specifically: define misplacement operationally (e.g. "a cell whose content would be classified to a different node by a reader given only outline_v3 and the cell"); name the fault classes to target (boundary confusions, run-splitting, apparatus-as-content); seed known misplacements to measure detection rate; start from the 70 low-confidence cells and boundary nodes rather than line 1; and record per-session findings so evaluator-effect variance is visible rather than hidden.

  Search scope: Preliminary search — broader search recommended. Seven web queries across evaluator effect, reading-technique experiments (checklist / ad hoc / scenario), proofreading of self-generated text, and vigilance. Not covered: the medical-imaging "satisfaction of search" literature (directly analogous but not searched), inter-rater studies on document classification audits, and the archival/editorial literature on manuscript ordering. The Porter et al. effect size and the direction of Burgoyne et al. 2022 were not confirmed from primary text. This is a well-populated field; the challenge is well-grounded even at preliminary depth.

Porter, Votta & Basili's specific effect (scenario ≈ +35% over ad hoc/checklist) is from recall and is deliberately not stated as a number in the Sources block.

STEELMAN:
  Item: PRESUMPTION-910
  Strongest counterargument: Expert recognition is a real capacity, but it is not a measurement instrument until it has a criterion. Every literature that has tested unguided expert review against criterion-guided review — usability evaluation, software inspection, proofreading — finds the same thing: unguided review is low-agreement, low-recall on plausible errors, and drifts over time on task. A reordered 16k-line corpus is a worst case on all three axes: the errors are plausible by construction (the classifier chose the node because the content fit), the reader is the author (whose expected meaning fills gaps), and the task is long enough for criterion drift to dominate. Under these conditions "the outline holds" is what an unguided read will return regardless of whether it holds, because the read is not equipped to return anything else.
  What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: Misplacements in this corpus must be predominantly of the obvious class; the author's familiarity must act as a resource-allocation advantage (Levy & Begin direction) rather than an expectation mask; and the read must be short enough, or session-structured enough, that vigilance drift does not shift the implicit criterion. If any of these fails, an explicit criterion is required before the read is informative.
  How to test: Two-condition seeded read. Insert 20 known misplacements (10 obvious, 10 boundary-plausible) into two matched 400-line slices. Read one slice unguided, the other with a one-paragraph written misplacement criterion and the target fault classes named. Compare recall by class. If the guided condition's plausible-class recall exceeds the unguided by more than ~20 points, the presumption is false for this corpus and the criterion must be written before the full read.
