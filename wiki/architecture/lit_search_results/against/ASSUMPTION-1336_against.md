    Limb split:
      - "Gap-junctional coupling is monotonic with multicellular cooperation": **Strong** challenge,
        and it is a direct contradiction rather than a boundary condition. Polusani et al.'s
        GJIC-defective-mutant controls establish that *functional coupling itself* — not connexin
        protein presence, not membrane localisation — can be the causal driver of **reduced adhesion
        and single-cell migration**, which is the anti-cooperative phenotype. The monotone mapping is
        not merely unproven; the counterexample was engineered on purpose and the negative controls
        were run.
      - "Cancer can be read as loss of coupling": **Strong** challenge. It is true of some primary
        tumours (source 6) and false of metastasis, where coupling is upregulated at exactly the
        moments the gloss would predict it should be lowest — intravasation, endothelial attachment,
        extravasation, and colonisation of host tissue.
      - "Hence a shrunken cognitive light cone": **Moderate, and structural rather than empirical.**
        No retrieved source addresses light cones. The challenge is that the inference is
        underdetermined by its own premise chain: if coupling can rise while cooperation falls, then
        coupling is not a measure of light-cone size, and the light-cone claim needs an independent

  Summary: The literature contradicts the monotone reading directly and from several independent
    directions. A peer-reviewed review read in full this run separates connexin expression, functional
    GJIC, and adhesion into three variables and then documents them dissociating: Cx26-mediated
    coupling reduces cell-cell adhesion and produces single-cell migration, while Cx43-mediated
    coupling increases adhesion to endothelium and promotes extravasation — opposite phenotypes from
    the same underlying mechanism, in the same review. The strongest single experiment is Polusani et
    al. 2016, because its GJIC-defective-but-membrane-localised mutants isolate functional coupling as
    the causal variable and show it driving the anti-cooperative outcome. Meanwhile Cx43 is upregulated
    at micrometastatic sites and, in the brain-metastasis case, assembled with host astrocytes to
    transfer cGAMP into the host network — coupling deployed against the organism rather than for it.
    The tumour-suppressive limb of the literature is real and is recorded above; that both limbs hold
    is the point. Coupling is a channel, and a channel's sign depends on what is sent through it and to
    whom, which is precisely what a scalar "amount of coupling" cannot represent.

    Strongest counterargument: The challenge attacks a claim about *connexin protein levels* that the
      gloss was never really making, and the metastasis literature is measuring a different thing. On
      the most charitable reading the gloss is about **the scope of the goal a collective can hold**,
      and gap junctions are cited as its best-characterised physical substrate, not as its definition.
      Under that reading Polusani et al. is not a counterexample but a scale error: Cx26-coupled HeLa
      cells that shed N-cadherin and migrate singly have a *smaller* cognitive light cone than the
      epithelium they came from, because the unit of agency has collapsed from tissue to cell — that
      the collapse is *mediated by* a junctional protein says nothing about whether the collective's
      goal-scope shrank, and it plainly did. Chen 2016 is the same move at a different level: a
      metastatic cell that couples into the astrocyte network is not enlarging its light cone, it is
      hijacking somebody else's, which is a recognised category in the framework (a subunit defecting
      from the higher-order agent) and not a refutation of it. The review's own three-way decomposition
      actually helps here rather than hurting: if expression, GJIC and adhesion are separable, then the
      framework should be indexed to the one that carries morphogenetic information — the coordinated
      bioelectric state — and the Grodstein–Mathews–Levin result points exactly there, since Vmem
      gating means two cells at different membrane potentials are *functionally decoupled however much
      connexin they express*. On that reading invasive tumours overexpressing connexins are not
      well-coupled at all; they are connexin-rich and voltage-mismatched, which is loss of coupling by
      the only measure that was ever meant, and the estate's gloss is right for a reason it stated
      badly.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the estate must actually hold the Vmem-gated
      functional-coupling reading rather than the expression-level reading — and it does **not**: the
      24 syntheses say what they say, and this steelman is a repair, not a description of the record;
      (b) the Vmem-gating mechanism must be established rather than modelled — on the only evidence
      available to me it is an **in-silico** result in an unretrieved 2026 preprint, which is a
      hypothesis, not a warrant; (c) Polusani's result must survive the reindexing, and it may not:
      the phenotype there tracked GJIC measured *functionally* (dye/electrical coupling, per the mutant
      controls), which is already downstream of Vmem gating, so the dissociation stands at the level
      the steelman retreats to; (d) "hijacking another agent's light cone" must be a pre-specified
      category with an independent test, not a post-hoc rescue — otherwise every observation is
      accommodated and the construct is unfalsifiable, which is the failure mode the informal critique
      names. (a) and (b) are the binding ones and neither currently holds.
    How to test: this is empirically decidable and cheaply. **Ask whether functional coupling and
      cooperative phenotype dissociate when coupling is measured functionally rather than by expression
      level.** The experiment already exists in the literature — Polusani's GJIC-defective,
      membrane-localised Cx26 mutants (R75Y, T135A) versus wild-type — so the test for C2A2 is a
      retrieval task, not a bench task: **retrieve Polusani et al. 2016 as a primary source and read
      the coupling assay.** If wild-type and mutant differ in dye/electrical coupling and the migration
      phenotype tracks the coupling rather than the protein, the dissociation holds at the functional
      level and the steelman's retreat fails. If the assay is expression-proximal, the steelman
      survives and the estate's gloss needs reindexing rather than retraction. Second test, for the
      Vmem limb: check whether Grodstein–Mathews–Levin report any *wet-lab* validation of Vmem
      mismatch in connexin-overexpressing invasive tumours, or only simulation. Both tests are one
      paper-fetch each and together settle the item.
