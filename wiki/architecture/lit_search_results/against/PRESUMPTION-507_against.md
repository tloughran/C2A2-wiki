  Sources:
    1. Office for National Statistics, "The Office for National Statistics' Revisions Policy and Correction of Errors Policy" (ons.gov.uk, retrieved 2026-07-21). Revisions are treated as a normal feature of statistical production, arising from improved methods, new sources, or data unavailable at first publication. The standing posture is publish-then-revise, not withhold-until-resolved. This challenges the item's implicit standard: continuing to publish under an unresolved discrepancy is normal official practice, not a defect.
    2. Same source and department practice (Ofqual, "Corrections and revisions policy for official statistics," gov.uk; DSIT and DBT equivalents, retrieved 2026-07-21). An "r" flag marks figures that change between publications, and where there are definitional or methodological changes they are described in the publications before and after the change where practicable. Marking, not withdrawal, is the standard response to a definitional break.
    3. Office for Statistics Regulation, "Regulatory guidance – Publishing official statistics" and the Government Analysis Function policy on "Publishing official statistics in development" (retrieved 2026-07-21). Withdrawal is reserved for statistics that do not meet user needs or are otherwise not fit for purpose — a high bar, not the default response to a disagreement.
    4. United Nations Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics, implementation guidelines (unstats.un.org, retrieved 2026-07-21). Emphasises documented, published methodology and transparency about sources and methods as the mechanism for handling known limitations, rather than suppression.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate
  Summary: The behaviour the item names as an unexamined presumption is, in the discipline it points to, deliberate and codified policy. Official statistics do not withhold publication pending resolution of a definitional dispute; they publish, flag the affected figures, describe the definitional change in the publications either side of it, and revise. Withdrawal is reserved for statistics not fit for purpose. So "no artifact withdrawn, no decision suspended" is not by itself evidence of a defect — it is what the closest professional analogue would also do. What the analogue would NOT do is the part the item gets right, and it is the third clause rather than the first two: publishing under a known break requires a published revisions and corrections policy, an explicit flag on affected figures, and a documented account of the definitional change. C2A2 has six standing disputes and none of that apparatus. The challenge, then, is to the item's implied remedy — quarantine and invalidation — and the correction is that the right response is marking and a revisions policy, which is cheaper, is standard, and does not stop production.
  Specific risks: If quarantine is adopted as the remedy, six disputes suspend an unknown volume of dependent work, and the pipeline acquires a stop condition that a channel with near-zero decision throughput cannot clear — the disputes would be resolved by abandonment rather than by adjudication. Symmetric risk if nothing changes: figures propagate unflagged, and the discrepancy is rediscovered at a later audit with no record of which artifacts were affected, which is precisely the situation a revisions policy exists to prevent.
  Mitigations available: Adopt the official-statistics pattern rather than the quarantine pattern. Write a one-page revisions and corrections policy stating what triggers a flag, what triggers a correction, and what triggers withdrawal. Mark every affected figure at point of use with a break indicator and a pointer to the dispute record. Log each of the six disputes with a first-flag date and the count of downstream artifacts produced since — which is the item's own in-house test and is worth running regardless of which remedy is chosen. Reserve withdrawal for figures shown to be not fit for purpose.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-507
    Strongest counterargument: The presumption the item surfaces is close to codified professional practice, so surfacing it as an unexamined assumption inverts the burden. National statistical offices face unresolved definitional breaks continuously and their published policy is publish-then-revise: figures that change between publications carry a revision flag, definitional and methodological changes are described in the publications before and after, and withdrawal is reserved for statistics that are not fit for purpose. Under that standard, "no artifact withdrawn, no decision suspended" describes correct behaviour, and the quarantine-and-invalidate remedy the item's search targets gesture toward would be an over-correction that no statistical office applies — one that, in a system whose decision channel already has near-zero throughput, would resolve disputes by abandoning the work rather than by settling the counts. But the item is right about something more specific than it claims. The official-statistics posture is only legitimate because it comes packaged with machinery: a published revisions and corrections policy, per-figure flags, and a documented account of each definitional change. C2A2 has the publishing half and none of the machinery. The defect is not that production continued under six unresolved disputes; it is that it continued silently, with no flag attached to any affected figure and no policy governing when a dispute would ever force a correction.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: That every figure touched by an open dispute carries a visible break marker at point of use, and that a written policy exists specifying what would escalate a dispute to a correction or a withdrawal.
    How to test: Run the item's own enumeration — each of the six disputes with its first-flag date and the count of downstream artifacts produced since. Then sample five of those artifacts and check whether any carries a marker. If none does, the gap is the flagging apparatus, and that is a cheaper and more tractable fix than quarantine.
