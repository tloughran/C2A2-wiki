  Sources:
    1. Tal, E. "Measurement in Science." Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (and Tal, E. 2012, "The Epistemology of Measurement: A Model-Based Account," PhD thesis, University of Toronto). — On model-based accounts, measurement consists of a concrete instrument-object-environment interaction *plus* a theoretical or statistical model of that process, and the measurement outcome is a knowledge claim inferred from an instrument indication, not the indication itself. This is a direct challenge to the item's premise: a `[measured]` figure is already a model-mediated inference, so treating it as a different epistemic kind from a `[cause]` is a difference of degree, not of category.
    2. Bogen, J. & Woodward, J. 1988. "Saving the Phenomena." The Philosophical Review 97(3):303–352. — Distinguishes data from phenomena and argues that theories are tested against phenomena, which are inferred from data by statistical and experimental means rather than observed. Undercuts the clean observation/inference split that a provenance tag of the form `[measured]` vs `[diagnosed]` would encode.
    3. Theory-ladenness literature (Hanson, Kuhn, Feyerabend; reviewed in Schindler, S., "Theory-laden experimentation," Studies in History and Philosophy of Science, 2013). — The standard result that observation statements carry theoretical commitments. Applied here: the choice of *what to count* in a `[measured]` field (what counts as a run, as residue, as a segment) is itself a diagnosis, so measurements in this register inherit the instability the item attributes only to causes.
    4. Hsiao, T-K. & Schneider, J. 2021. "Continued use of retracted papers: Temporal trends in citations and (lack of) awareness of retractions shown in citation contexts in biomedicine." Quantitative Science Studies 2(4):1144–1169. — Of 13,252 post-retraction citation contexts, only 722 (5.4%) acknowledged the retraction. Challenges the implied remedy: formal, machine-readable retraction metadata already exists in biomedicine and does not stop discredited claims being reused, so adding a provenance tag to the C2A2 register is unlikely on its own to change downstream behaviour.
    5. Budd, J.M., Sievert, M., Schultz, T.R. 1998. "Phenomena of retraction: reasons for retraction and citations to the publications." JAMA 280(3):296–297; and Budd et al.'s later replication over 1,112 articles retracted 1997–2009, in which roughly 6% of citations indicated the retraction. — Same challenge, with a two-decade span showing no improvement despite intervening infrastructure. Suggests the persistence problem is behavioural and network-structural, not a defect of record format.
    6. Peerally, M.F., Carr, S., Waring, J., Dixon-Woods, M. 2017. "The problem with root cause analysis." BMJ Quality & Safety 26(5):417–422. — Argues the deeper problem is analysis quality and single-point causation, not the durability of the record. Reframes the item: four causes overturned in one day may be evidence of a healthy correction loop rather than of a defective format — under this reading, high overturn rate is what a functioning register looks like.
    7. Tversky, A. & Kahneman, D. 1974. "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases." Science 185(4157):1124–1131. — The anchoring result the item's search angle invokes. Cited here as a boundary condition: anchoring is a bias on *revision magnitude*, not a claim that anchored values never move. The item's own evidence — four causes overturned in a single day — is a case of causes moving readily, which sits awkwardly with a strong anchoring story.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The item rests on a two-tier picture in which measurements are hard facts and diagnoses are soft, and the register's sin is flattening the tiers. Contemporary philosophy of measurement rejects that picture: on Tal's model-based account a measurement outcome is a knowledge claim inferred from an instrument indication via a model, and Bogen & Woodward's data/phenomena distinction places the epistemically load-bearing content on the inferred side. In this register specifically, the `[measured]` figures — runs, residue percentage, mute count — depend on contested definitional choices, so they are diagnoses wearing numerals. Meanwhile the retraction literature challenges the remedy directly: biomedicine has had formal retraction metadata for decades and only about 5–6% of post-retraction citations acknowledge it, so tagging provenance in the format is weak evidence of changed downstream use. And Peerally et al. suggest an alternative reading of the four same-day overturns: a register whose causes get corrected within a day is exhibiting a working feedback loop, not a durability defect.

  Specific risks: If C2A2 adds a provenance tier that marks causes as soft and measurements as hard, it risks entrenching the *measurements* — the residue percentage, the mute count, the run counts — as unrevisable, when those are exactly the figures that depend on undocumented definitional choices and that no independent register corroborates. That would move the durability problem rather than solve it, and it would do so in the direction of the numbers, which are harder to argue with. If it relies on the tag alone to change downstream behaviour, the retraction evidence predicts near-zero effect: consumers will keep using the superseded cause because they encountered it, not because they mis-read its status. If it treats a high cause-overturn rate as pathology, it may suppress correction to make the register look stable, which is the worst available outcome.

  Mitigations available: Version every register field, measurement and cause alike, with supersession links, rather than creating an asymmetric provenance tier. Require each `[measured]` figure to carry its operational definition and the register it was computed in — the theory-ladenness point made concrete. Push corrections rather than tagging them: when a cause is overturned, actively notify every downstream item that cited it, since the retraction literature shows passive metadata does not propagate. Track and publish the overturn rate for both causes and measurements as a health metric, framing correction as expected. Where a cause drives an irreversible action, require a reproduction rather than a record. Consider W3C PROV-style provenance modelling for the derivation chain, but do not treat the labels as a control by themselves.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-830
    Strongest counterargument: The presumption the item surfaces is real, but the asymmetry it proposes is the wrong repair, because the measurement side of the asymmetry does not hold up. Tal's model-based account and Bogen & Woodward's data/phenomena distinction both say a measurement result is an inference from an instrument reading through a model, and in C2A2's register the models are informal and undocumented — "residue," "run," "mute" are all definitional choices that could be overturned as easily as a cause, and less visibly, because a number invites less argument than a sentence. Grant the asymmetry and you get a register where causes are provisional and numbers are canonical, which is exactly backwards for a system whose numbers are self-reported by the pipelines that produce them. Separately, the retraction literature is a hard empirical result against format-based remedies: biomedicine has machine-readable retraction notices and still only ~5% of post-retraction citations acknowledge them, so a provenance tag is a change to the record, not to the behaviour. Finally, four causes overturned in one day is at least as consistent with a register that corrects well as with one that records badly.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) measurements are versioned and carry their operational definitions, so they are as revisable and as auditable as causes; (b) supersession is pushed to citers rather than only recorded at the source, since passive metadata demonstrably does not propagate; (c) the overturn rate is monitored for both field types and a rising rate is not treated as something to suppress; (d) any irreversible action is gated on a reproduction, not on a register field of any provenance class; (e) no downstream consumer treats a `[measured]` field as exempt from the same challenge process applied to causes.
    How to test: Directly testable within the register. First, take the four overturned causes and check whether any `[measured]` figure in the same entries also changed on re-derivation — if measurements move too, the asymmetry is falsified. Second, re-compute three `[measured]` figures from written definitions produced independently of the originating pipeline; the spread is the measurement's real revisability. Third, run a propagation test: overturn one cause, tag it, and count how many downstream items that cited it are updated within a fixed window without an active notification — the retraction literature predicts a very low number, and if C2A2 reproduces that number, format-based mitigation is refuted for this system.
