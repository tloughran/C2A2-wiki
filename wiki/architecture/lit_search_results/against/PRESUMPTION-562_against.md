  Sources:
    1. Gentner, D. & Markman, A. B. 1997. "Structure Mapping in Analogy and Similarity." American Psychologist 52(1): 45-56. — The canonical statement of the standard the presumption invokes, and it cuts against the presumption's sharp surface/structure dichotomy: similarity and analogy are the SAME process of structural alignment operating on comparisons that share both attributes and relations, and alignment is graded by systematicity rather than gated by a homology test. Gentner & Markman also show that structural alignment is what FACILITATES the noticing of differences (Markman & Gentner, Memory & Cognition) - i.e. entertaining the mapping is the mechanism by which non-alignable differences become visible. Refusing the comparison until homology is proven removes the process that would detect the disanalogy.
    2. Holyoak, K. J. & Thagard, P. 1995. Mental Leaps: Analogy in Creative Thought. MIT Press (multiconstraint theory); see also Bartha, P., "Analogy and Analogical Reasoning," Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. — Multiconstraint theory treats analogy as constraint satisfaction over structural, semantic and PRAGMATIC constraints, with goals admitted as legitimate constraints. Holyoak & Thagard identify discovery, development, evaluation and exposition as distinct uses of analogy, and the literature is explicit that the heuristic/discovery use has a lower entry bar than the evidential use: "an analogy is not a proof," but it is a licit cognitive strategy for creative discovery. The virtual-governor reading is presented as a description, not as an inference from Levin's results, so it sits in the discovery register where three shared descriptors are a normal starting point.
    3. Hesse, M. 1966. Models and Analogies in Science (Cambridge); and "Analogy in the natural sciences: meeting Hesse's challenge," Philosophical Inquiries. — Hesse's account requires only positive, negative and neutral analogy to be distinguished; productive scientific analogies characteristically proceed from partial positive analogy while the negative analogy remains unresolved. Demanding structural homology before any transfer is a standard that would disqualify most historically productive analogies.
    4. Merton, R. K. 1963. "Resistance to the Systematic Study of Multiple Discoveries in Science" (multiple discovery); Whewell's consilience, as summarised in the convergence-of-evidence literature. — Supplies the alternative explanation the presumption does not consider: when an independent researcher working in a different domain converges on a similar description, that is prima facie evidence of a real structure, not merely evidence of the observer's confirmation bias. The presumption treats recognition of one's own framework in external work as presumptively biased; consilience treats independent convergence as evidential. Both readings are available and the presumption asserts one without argument.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The presumption applies a real and correctly-named standard, but applies it at the wrong stage and in a stricter form than its own source supports. Gentner & Markman's structure-mapping theory treats alignment as graded by systematicity rather than gated by a homology test, and Markman & Gentner show that performing the alignment is what makes non-alignable differences visible - so refusing the comparison suppresses the very check the presumption wants. Multiconstraint theory and Hesse both distinguish the heuristic/discovery use of analogy from the evidential use, and only the latter carries a burden of demonstrated structural correspondence; three shared descriptors is an ordinary starting point for the former. The consilience literature further supplies an unconsidered alternative: independent convergence by a researcher in another domain is evidence of shared structure, not automatically confirmation bias. What survives, and survives well, is the narrow procedural point - the standard should be applied symmetrically, and a self-description must not become a CROSS entry without the same transfer check that generated bridges face.

  Specific risks: If over-read, C2A2 applies an evidential standard to a heuristic comparison and loses the discovery function of analogy, including the mechanism (structural alignment) by which the disanalogy would have been found; a productive convergence with an external research programme gets discarded as bias. If under-read, "virtual governor = tradition" enters the corpus at the project's self-description layer, where it is load-bearing for everything downstream, on the strength of three adjectives - and self-description errors are the hardest to detect because every subsequent bridge inherits them.

  Mitigations available: Keep the comparison, grade it. Record it explicitly as heuristic/discovery-stage with the positive analogy, negative analogy and neutral analogy listed separately (Hesse) - which is cheap and directly answers the presumption's complaint. Apply the transfer-condition check (per the in-house adjunct) as a gate on CROSS ENTRY only, not on holding the analogy. Name the causal role claimed to be shared, not just the descriptors: state what the virtual governor DOES in Levin's account and what the corresponding tradition-level mechanism is claimed to do, then check whether the relational structure aligns. If the alignment is only over adjectives, the presumption is confirmed by the very test that would have vindicated a good mapping.

    Strongest counterargument (against the presumption): Structure-mapping theory, the framework the presumption invokes, does not license the surface/structure dichotomy it deploys - similarity and analogy are one graded alignment process, and Markman & Gentner showed that carrying out the alignment is precisely what makes non-alignable differences noticeable. So the demand to prove homology before entertaining the mapping removes the mechanism that would detect the failure. Multiconstraint theory and Hesse both distinguish heuristic from evidential use, and a description offered as an interesting convergence sits in the heuristic register, where partial positive analogy is the normal and historically productive starting point. Finally, an independent researcher in another domain converging on a similar description is what consilience counts as evidence of real structure; reading it instead as confirmation bias is an assumption the presumption does not defend.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: the analogy is held at discovery grade with its negative analogy written down, and the transfer-condition check gates CROSS entry rather than gating the comparison itself; the shared CAUSAL ROLE is stated, not just the shared descriptors.
    How to test: write out the relational structure on both sides - what the virtual governor causally does in Levin's account, what the tradition-level analogue is claimed to do - and count alignable relations versus shared attributes. If the mapping consists of attributes only (abstract, non-physical, causally instructive) with no aligned relations, the presumption is confirmed on structure-mapping's own criteria; if two or more relations align, it is refuted and the comparison is a legitimate candidate.
