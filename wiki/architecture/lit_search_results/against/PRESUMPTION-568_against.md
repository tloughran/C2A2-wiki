  Sources:
    1. Kuorikoski, J., Lehtinen, A. & Marchionni, C. 2010. "Economic Modelling as Robustness Analysis." British Journal for the Philosophy of Science 61(3): 541-567. — Extends Wimsatt's account: a result derivable from multiple partially independent routes is robust precisely because the result "does not depend on the various auxiliary assumptions used in the derivation" (Levins: "our truth is the intersection of independent lies"). This directly challenges the presumption's general form — falsity in stated grounds demonstrably does not always propagate to the conclusion. But the licence is explicitly conditional on the derivations being *independent*, which is what defect (2) denies.
    2. Goddu, G.C. "Walton on Argument Structure." Informal Logic (UR Scholarship Repository) — on Walton's linked/convergent distinction. In a convergent (coordinate) structure, premises supply independent lines of support; the operative test is "if we suspend the one premise, does the other give any reason at all to support the conclusion?" Falsifying one premise costs strength but does not remove support. So "defective ground, intact conclusion" is a recognised and legitimate argument-structural verdict — provided the structure is convergent rather than linked. Goddu also notes the linked/convergent theory is incomplete and hard to apply, so the classification is contestable rather than mechanical.
    3. Anderson, J.M. "Right for Any Reason." Cardozo Law Review 44(3). Also: US Supreme Court practice since 1924 that an appellee may defend a judgment "on any ground supported by the record, even a ground that the district court ignored or expressly rejected"; state variants ("right result for the wrong reason", Virginia; "tipsy coachman", Florida). — An institutionalised doctrine holding that appellate courts review *judgments, not reasons or opinions*. Defective stated grounds with a correct disposition is therefore a well-established, governed category, not a self-serving invention. The doctrine's stated limit is the load-bearing one: the alternative ground must be supported by the record already before the court.
    4. Hedges, L.V., Tipton, E. & Johnson, M.C. 2010. "Robust variance estimation in meta-regression with dependent effect size estimates." Research Synthesis Methods 1(1): 39-65. — The canonical treatment of exactly the defect-(2) failure mode (correlated estimates treated as independent). Its correction is to the *covariance matrix* — i.e. to precision and inferential confidence — while the meta-regression coefficients remain consistent. Non-independence inflates apparent certainty far more reliably than it flips the sign of the estimate. This is the strongest specific challenge: an inflation of apparent convergence propagates to the *confidence* attaching to the disposition, not necessarily to the disposition itself.
    5. PCAOB AS 2201 / AS 1305 (deficiency vs significant deficiency vs material weakness) and ISA 450, "Evaluation of Misstatements Identified during the Audit." — Audit practice does exactly what the presumption treats as suspect: it accumulates defects and classifies them by severity against stated criteria, and a below-threshold classification carries no disclosure obligation. Classification of a defect as presentational is thus normal, governed practice. Two conditions attach, however, and both bear on this item: ISA 450 requires evaluation of misstatements *in aggregate* as well as individually, and it flags as a qualitative indicator of bias "a pattern of individually immaterial misstatements, all of which favour a particular financial outcome."
    6. Wallace, D.B. & Kassin, S.M. 2012. "Harmless Error Analysis: How Do Judges Respond to Confession Errors?" Law and Human Behavior 36(2): 151-157. Context: review of federal appellate criminal decisions 1996-1998 finding errors held harmless in ~87% of cases where error was found; California Supreme Court capital reversal rate falling 94% -> 14% with differential harmless-error use accounting for nearly all the difference. — Reported here because the search target *fails to invert*. 132 judges correctly identified a coerced confession as improperly admitted, then convicted at a significantly higher rate on the strength of it. The empirical harmless-error literature does not support harmless-error classification as reliable; it documents systematic over-application and shows that the same decision-maker who declares a defect harmless is measurably moved by it.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The presumption's general premise — that defects in stated grounds propagate to conclusions — is challenged, and challenged from several independent directions. Robustness analysis (Kuorikoski et al. 2010, after Wimsatt and Levins) is built on the opposite claim: results reachable by multiple partially independent routes survive falsity in the auxiliary assumptions of any one route. Convergent argument structure gives the same verdict at the level of a single argument, appellate "right for any reason" doctrine institutionalises it, and audit severity classification is the routine practice of grading defects below a threshold. But every one of these carries the same condition, and it is the condition defect (2) attacks: independence of the supporting routes. Robustness licences nothing if the derivations are not independent; a convergent structure collapses to a linked one if the premises are not separable; right-for-any-reason requires an alternative ground *already in the record*. The sharpest challenge is the narrowest: Hedges, Tipton & Johnson show that non-independence among estimates corrupts the variance, not the point estimate — so an inflation of apparent convergence propagates most reliably to the *confidence* in the disposition rather than to its direction. Against this, two findings cut back toward the presumption. ISA 450 names a pattern of individually immaterial defects all favouring one outcome as a qualitative indicator of bias — six defects classified uniformly in the direction that preserves the disposition is that pattern. And the empirical harmless-error literature, the presumption's most obvious counterweight, in fact documents the opposite of what the search target predicted: harmless-error findings are made in the large majority of cases where error is found, and judges who correctly identify a defect as improper are nonetheless measurably moved by it.

  Specific risks: If the presumption is over-read, C2A2 acquires a rule that any defect anywhere in a chain voids the conclusion, which the robustness and convergent-argument literatures show to be false and which would make every verification pass maximally destructive of prior work — the system loses the legitimate category of "wrong reasons, right judgment" that appellate practice and audit both maintain. If the presumption is under-read, the specific risk is not that the disposition is wrong but that its *confidence* is unearned: defect (2) is a non-independence defect, and the one thing non-independence reliably corrupts is stated certainty. A disposition retained at unchanged confidence after its convergence ground was shown to be inflated is a calibration failure that will propagate silently into every downstream item that cites it. Second-order risk: six defects graded uniformly in the self-favourable direction is the exact pattern ISA 450 treats as a bias indicator, so the classification decision itself is the thing needing independent review, not the individual gradings.

  Mitigations available: Do the in-house adjunct already specified — re-derive the disposition from the corrected grounds alone and check whether INCORPORATE still follows. That is the robustness test, run properly: if the conclusion survives removal of the defective route, the presentational classification is vindicated on the literature's own terms. Apply the right-for-any-reason constraint: name the surviving ground explicitly and confirm it was in the record *before* the defect was found, not constructed afterwards to save the verdict. Apply the ISA 450 aggregate test: evaluate the six defects in combination as well as individually, and record whether all six were graded in the same favourable direction. And treat defect (2) as a variance defect rather than a point defect — restate the disposition's confidence downward by whatever the inflated convergence was carrying, even if the disposition itself stands. Have the classification (not the defects) reviewed by something other than the agent that produced the disposition; the harmless-error evidence is that the same decision-maker is the wrong one to certify harmlessness.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-568
    Strongest counterargument (against the presumption): The inference from "a stated ground was defective" to "the conclusion is in doubt" is not valid, and the whole point of robustness analysis is that it is not valid: a result derivable by several partially independent routes is robust to falsity in the auxiliary assumptions of any one of them, which is why Levins could call truth the intersection of independent lies. The same structure appears in Walton's convergent arguments, where suspending one premise leaves the others still giving reason for the conclusion; in appellate doctrine, where courts review judgments rather than reasons and affirm on any ground the record supports; and in audit, where grading a defect below a severity threshold is routine, criteria-governed practice rather than evasion. Even the specific charge lands more narrowly than stated: the meta-analytic treatment of exactly this defect shows that treating correlated estimates as independent corrupts the variance while leaving the point estimate consistent — an inflation of apparent convergence inflates confidence, and does not by itself move the verdict. And an is/ought correction to an inference that already had a suppressed normative premise makes explicit what was operative, which is the textbook definition of a presentational fix.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) the disposition's supporting grounds are genuinely convergent rather than linked, so that removing the inflated-convergence ground leaves independent support standing; (b) the surviving ground was in the record before the defect was identified, not constructed to preserve the verdict; (c) the confidence attached to the disposition has been revised downward to reflect the corrected (lower) independence, even though the direction stands; (d) the six defects, evaluated in aggregate, do not all fall in the direction that preserves the prior conclusion — or, if they do, that pattern has been independently reviewed; (e) the is/ought correction in defect (5) restores a premise that was genuinely operative rather than supplying one that was absent.
    How to test: Re-derive the disposition from the corrected grounds only, with the inflated-convergence ground deleted rather than downgraded, and record whether INCORPORATE still follows and at what confidence. Then run the two discriminating checks. First, structure: for each remaining ground, ask Walton's question — suspend it, and does what remains give *any* reason for the disposition? If suspending any single ground destroys the support, the structure was linked, not convergent, robustness does not apply, and the defect does propagate. Second, direction: tabulate all six defects by whether their correction would raise or lower support for the existing disposition. Six of six favourable is the ISA 450 bias signature and warrants external review of the classification decision itself; a mixed distribution substantially defuses the presumption. Additionally, check whether defect (5)'s normative premise appears anywhere in the pre-correction text; if it does not, it was supplied rather than surfaced, and the correction is substantive.
