  Sources:
    1. Baron, S. "Unification and Mathematical Explanation in Science." Synthese (2021) / philarchive BARUAM-4. — Central and new to this file. States the spurious-unification problem directly: "unification under a general pattern is spurious when the explanations unified by the pattern should not be unified," and notes it is "notoriously difficult to give an account of the difference between spurious and non-spurious unification." Since no accepted criterion exists, asserting that a mapping is genuine unification is not a claim the current literature can license — the burden is entirely on C2A2 and it has not been discharged.
    2. "Two Ways of Analogy: Extending the Study of Analogies to Mathematical Domains." Philosophy of Science (Cambridge). — Structure-preserving-mapping accounts of analogy *fail* in object-rich domains. Formal analogy generalises beyond nomic isomorphism, which means demonstrating a shared mathematical form is much weaker evidence of unification than it appears.

The philosophy-of-science literature offers no accepted criterion distinguishing genuine from spurious unification, and explicitly warns that patterns with wide unifying scope are the most likely to be spurious — which is the exact profile of a Markov-blanket-based three-level bridge. Each of the three frameworks independently carries unresolved foundational disputes (FEP realism/instrumentalism still contested in BJPS 2025; Thousand Brains empirically unvalidated per ASSUMPTION-021; conscious-agent theory untestable), so a claimed unification is a conjunction of three unsettled things rather than a consolidation of three settled ones.   Specific risks: If false, FINDING-011/SUPER-BRIDGE is the load-bearing claim of the whole project and it is a formal coincidence. Because it is a *super*-bridge, every finding that references it inherits the defect, and the wiki's link structure means the contamination is maximally connected — the highest-degree node in the graph is the least reliable one.   Mitigations available: (a) Downgrade the claim from "unification" to "formal analogy" — accurate, defensible, and costs nothing; (b) state the mapping explicitly at the level of variables, dynamics and constraints, and identify what is *not* shared (the disanalogies are the test); (c) derive one prediction that follows from the unification but from none of the three frameworks individually — if none exists, the unification has no content; (d) obtain adjudication from a working researcher in one of the three fields; (e) mark FINDING-011 as CONTESTED in the wiki so downstream links inherit the caveat rather than the confidence.

  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: The demand for a criterion distinguishing genuine from spurious unification is one that no successful historical unification could have met in advance either — Maxwell's unification of electricity, magnetism and optics looked like a formal coincidence until it predicted radio waves. The productive response to an apparent cross-level mathematical correspondence is to develop it until it either yields a novel prediction or collapses, not to refuse to name it. Moreover, the three frameworks were independently developed by serious researchers and converge on the same formal structure without having been designed to; convergence from independent origins is exactly the evidential signature that distinguishes real unification from a fitted analogy.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (1) The three frameworks genuinely converged independently rather than through shared intellectual ancestry — but they do not: active inference, Thousand Brains and cellular cognition all draw on Bayesian/predictive-processing lineage, so the convergence is at least partly common-cause, and this must be assessed honestly; (2) the unification generates at least one prediction not derivable from any single framework; (3) the claim is stated as conjecture until (2) is satisfied; (4) the disanalogies are documented as prominently as the analogies.
    How to test: The novel-prediction test is decisive and cheap to attempt. Write down one empirically checkable consequence that follows from the three-level unification and from no single level alone. If, after a bounded effort, none can be produced, record the claim as a formal analogy and remove "unification" from all artefacts. Secondary test: common-cause audit — trace the citation lineage of the three frameworks and determine how much of the shared structure is inherited rather than convergent.
