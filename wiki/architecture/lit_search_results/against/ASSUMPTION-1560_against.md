  Sources:
    1. Stump, E. 2010. *Wandering in Darkness: Narrative and the Problem of Suffering*. Oxford UP. —
       The strongest challenge, and it is from Stump's own corpus. The book's defence is explicitly
       structured on Aquinas's account of *the good for human beings* and on union with God as the end
       toward which human flourishing is ordered: God desires union in love, the fragmented human cannot
       achieve it, integration around the good is what makes it possible. Whatever the label, that is an
       argument whose load-bearing structure is an ultimate end ordering a process. A reader who calls
       this "final causality in Stump" is not fabricating.
    2. Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews, review of *Wandering in Darkness*. — Independent confirmation
       that the book is read as an account of the human good and its ordering, not as a formal-causal
       thesis. Secondary reception matters here because the claim under test is about *attribution*.
    3. General Aquinas-teleology literature (Oliver, "Aquinas and Aristotle's Teleology," Durham
       repository; Purdue diss. "Final Causality in the Thought of Thomas Aquinas"; *New Blackfriars*,
       "Natural Final Causality and Providence in Aquinas"). — Establishes that final causality is
       inseparable from the Thomistic system Stump expounds. An expositor of Aquinas necessarily
       transmits final causality; attributing it to her is a category slide, not a factual error, and
       the two are not the same defect.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The claim is likely right about *emphasis* and overstated as a *contrast*. Stump's own
    metaphysics papers are formal-causal (that much survives this search), but "rather than final
    causality" does not survive contact with *Wandering in Darkness*, whose central argument is ordered
    to an ultimate end. Formal and final causality are complementary in the Thomistic frame Stump works
    in, so a dichotomy between them is the artefact. The more defensible version of 14a's claim is
    narrow: CROSS-008 attributes to Stump a *primary commitment* to final causality that her stated
    metaphysical position does not carry. That narrower claim is not challenged here.

  Specific risks: If CROSS-008 is "corrected" on the basis of the strong reading, the estate replaces
    one misattribution with another and does so in three places. A correction that says "Stump is a
    formal-causation thinker, not a final-causation thinker" would be a new error of the same class as
    the one it fixes. Worse: the correction would be recorded as a *win* for the audit process, which
    makes it harder to reverse.

  Mitigations available: State the correction as the narrow claim, with a citation, in this form:
    "Stump's own statement of causal commitment (2012) is formal-causal and level-indexed; her expository
    work on Aquinas transmits Aquinas's final causality. CROSS-008's attribution conflates the two."
    Read the three CROSS-008 sources before editing — the misattribution may belong to a source rather
    than to the estate's summary of it.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-1560
    Strongest counterargument: The dichotomy is imported. In Aquinas, substantial form and final cause
      are two aspects of one account — a thing's form *is* the principle of its ordering to its end — so
      "formal rather than final" describes a distinction Stump herself would not accept as exclusive.
      Stump's best-known book argues from an ultimate end. A critic could reasonably say the audit has
      found a difference of idiom between Stump's analytic-metaphysics register and her theological
      register, and has upgraded it into a factual error in three sources.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: that CROSS-008's claim is specific enough to be
      checked — i.e. it attributes to Stump a *stated* commitment to final causality as her operative
      category, rather than reporting Aquinas's teleology via her exposition.
    How to test: read the three sources CROSS-008 cites and classify each attribution as (a) Stump's own
      voice, (b) Stump reporting Aquinas, (c) a third party's summary. This is a 30-minute in-house task
      and it decides the item.

  Recommendation: PARTIALLY-CHALLENGED
