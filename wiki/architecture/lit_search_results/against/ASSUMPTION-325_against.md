    1. Discriminant validity (Campbell & Fiske 1959) — if the "269 network" count and the production series measure DIFFERENT constructs (graph footprint vs cumulative creation events), they SHOULD differ and neither supersedes the other; "authoritative single number" is a category error.
    2. Construct-definition-first (measurement theory) — "which is authoritative" is ill-posed until the construct is fixed; recency/auditability cannot adjudicate authority between numbers that answer different questions.
    3. Multiple-measures governance — replacing one number with another that answers a different question creates an apples-to-oranges supersession that loses the information the retired number carried (network connectivity), not just updates it.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate-Strong

  Summary: "Supersedes ... as THE authoritative PRS number" is challenged at its root: if 269 (network footprint) and 264/262 (cumulative production / surviving census) are distinct constructs, supersession is a category error — each is authoritative FOR ITS OWN question, and collapsing to one "authoritative" number destroys information. Recency and auditability make the git series the better PRODUCTION measure, but cannot make it the authoritative count of a different construct. The auditability advantage is real; the supersession framing is not.

  Specific risks: Retiring "269" as superseded silently drops the network-footprint construct; downstream readers think there is one true PRS count when there are several distinct quantities (couples OPEN-081/084); reconciliation effort is spent forcing distinct constructs to one number.

  Mitigations available: Replace "supersedes" with "is the authoritative PRODUCTION measure"; keep 269 as the authoritative NETWORK measure with its construct labeled; reframe OPEN-084 as construct-disambiguation, not reconciliation-to-one-number.
