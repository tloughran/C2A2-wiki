  Sources:
    1. Stout, J., 1988. Ethics After Babel: The Languages of Morals and Their Discontents. Beacon Press. — Stout argues that MacIntyre's framing of modern moral discourse as incoherent "castigation-by-lumping" misrepresents liberal moral plurality, and that moral dialogue across traditions is possible without identifying any single tradition (Thomism or otherwise) as methodologically exemplary. Traditions can be mutually intelligible without one serving as the meta-framework.

    2. Nussbaum, M. C., 1989. "Recoiling from Reason." New York Review of Books 36(19). — Nussbaum argues that MacIntyre's tradition-constituted rationality slides toward relativism and that the privileging of Thomism reflects substantive commitments smuggled in under a methodological guise. The alleged universality of the method is, on her view, a sectarian claim in disguise.

    3. Bernstein, R. J., 1983. Beyond Objectivism and Relativism. University of Pennsylvania Press. — Argues that both foundationalism and tradition-constituted relativism are inadequate, and that dialogical practical reason (drawing on Gadamer, Arendt, Habermas) provides a viable alternative to MacIntyre's framework. This constitutes a direct rival account of how traditions can rationally engage.

    4. Gadamer, H.-G., 1975/2004. Truth and Method. Continuum. — Gadamer's "fusion of horizons" provides an account of cross-tradition understanding through hermeneutical dialogue that does not require one tradition to achieve epistemological victory over rivals, and does not privilege the Thomist dialectical structure. Recent scholarship (e.g., the Academia paper "MacIntyre's Rationalities of Traditions and Gadamer's Hermeneutics") shows the two accounts diverge fundamentally on the role of language and metaphysics.

    5. Habermas, J., 1993. Justification and Application: Remarks on Discourse Ethics. MIT Press. — Habermas's discourse ethics provides a proceduralist account of rational agreement across traditions grounded in communicative competence rather than tradition-constituted rationality. Habermas explicitly rejects MacIntyre's move of grounding rationality in particular traditions, arguing this conflates justification with application.

    6. Lott, M., 2010. "MacIntyre and Tradition." Unpublished ms., Baylor. — Notes the tension between the "methodological MacIntyre" (tradition rationality as a general framework) and the "substantive MacIntyre" (Thomism as the winning tradition), and questions whether the methodological thesis is truly separable from the substantive one. If they are inseparable, the "weaker" methodological Thomism assumption may carry substantive Thomist commitments.

    7. Stout, J., 2004. Democracy and Tradition. Princeton University Press. — Argues that democratic discourse is itself a tradition with its own standards of reason-giving, and that it has resources for adjudicating between traditions without recourse to Thomistic epistemology.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: Even setting aside the stronger sole-source claim, the methodological Thomism assumption faces serious challenges. Rival accounts (Gadamer, Habermas, Stout, Bernstein) provide coherent frameworks for resolving tradition conflicts that do not share MacIntyre's Thomist dialectical structure and that explicitly reject the privilege his method assigns to one tradition's mode of enquiry. The charge that methodological Thomism is not genuinely separable from substantive Thomism (Lott, Nussbaum) is particularly damaging for C2A2: even a "weaker" reading may carry substantive metaphysical commitments that go unacknowledged. Additionally, since MacIntyre himself moved from "methodology" to "Thomism wins" precisely by applying the methodology, any system using the method at scale should expect the same internal pressure.

  Specific risks: C2A2's architecture may embed Thomistic intellectual commitments in its resolution procedures while presenting them as methodology-neutral, meaning that outcomes of tradition-conflict resolution are predetermined by methodological framing rather than genuinely derived. At M8, this could mean the system converges on conclusions that reflect the designer's metaphysical preferences rather than genuine rational adjudication.

  Mitigations available: Explicitly operationalise what "Thomist in MacIntyre's methodological sense" means for C2A2, distinguish it sharply from substantive Thomism, and test whether the resolution procedure yields different results when a non-Thomist method (e.g., Habermas's discourse procedure or Gadamerian hermeneutics) is applied to the same conflict. Treating the Thomist framing as a hypothesis-generating heuristic rather than the resolution procedure itself would also reduce risk.

  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: MacIntyre's methodological claim is comparatively minimal: any tradition that achieves genuine rational progress must be able to account for the crises and failures of rival traditions in terms those rivals can understand. This is arguably a necessary condition for rational adjudication that any successful method (including Gadamer's or Habermas's) must satisfy implicitly. The label "Thomist" may be unfortunate if it invites substantive associations, but the underlying structural claim — that one tradition must achieve dialectical comprehensiveness to be judged rationally superior — is defensible without full commitment to Thomism.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The "methodological" reading would need to be operationalised in a way that (a) is testable independently of any particular tradition's winning, (b) does not beg the question in favour of Aristotelian-Thomist categories, and (c) can accommodate or adjudicate between Gadamerian, Habermasian, and MacIntyrean methods as candidate resolution frameworks.
    How to test: Apply the stated resolution procedure to a tradition-conflict that MacIntyre himself analysed and check whether the procedure, independently applied, reaches the same conclusion. Then apply a Gadamerian procedure to the same conflict and compare results. If results systematically favour Thomistic categories regardless of starting point, the methodology is not neutral.
