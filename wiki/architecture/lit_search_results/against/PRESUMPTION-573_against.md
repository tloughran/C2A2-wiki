  Sources:
    1. Moore, D. A., Swift, S. A., Minster, A., Mellers, B., Ungar, L., Tetlock, P., Yang, H. H. & Tenney, E. R. 2017. "Confidence Calibration in a Multiyear Geopolitical Forecasting Competition." Management Science 63(11): 3552-3565. — Across thousands of forecasters and hundreds of resolved events in a three-year tournament, self-reported confidence roughly matched accuracy; confidence rose at approximately the same rate as accuracy as information arrived, with residual overconfidence around 3% concentrated at the top of the scale and reduced to roughly 1% by training plus team collaboration. This is direct evidence that self-assigned confidence is not epistemically inert: in a domain with resolution and feedback, the label carries real signal about the claim's reliability. The presumption treats "attached by the same process that produced the claim" as sufficient to void the label; this finding says the relevant variable is calibration under feedback, not the identity of the labeller.
    2. Lin, S., Hilton, J. & Evans, O. 2022. "Teaching Models to Express Their Uncertainty in Words." arXiv:2205.14334 (Transactions on Machine Learning Research); test suite CalibratedMath. — The sharpest inversion of the presumption's core move. A model generates both an answer and a natural-language confidence ("90% confidence", "medium confidence") from the same forward process, and those verbalised levels map to well-calibrated probabilities. The authors report the confidence is sensitive to uncertainty in the model's own answers rather than imitating human labelling patterns, and remains moderately calibrated under distribution shift. So a label emitted by the same process that produced the claim can, in principle, be a genuine measurement of that claim rather than a decoration on it.
    3. Kim, S. S. Y., Liao, Q. V., Vorvoreanu, M., Ballard, S. & Wortman Vaughan, J. 2024. "'I'm Not Sure, But...': Examining the Impact of Large Language Models' Uncertainty Expression on User Reliance and Trust." Proceedings of the 2024 ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency (FAccT '24), Rio de Janeiro. — Pre-registered human-subject experiment, 404 participants answering medical questions with or without an LLM-infused search interface expressing uncertainty in varying natural-language forms. Expressions of uncertainty measurably changed reliance behaviour and task performance. This challenges the presumption's implicit claim that a disclosure label does no control work: it establishes that uncertainty labelling has a demonstrable downstream effect on how the labelled claim is used, which is precisely the function a control is supposed to serve.
    4. American Psychological Association. Publication Manual of the APA, 7th edition; APA Style guidance on secondary sources (apastyle.apa.org, "Secondary sources"). — Formalised scholarly practice for the exact situation the presumption treats as disqualifying. Where the original work is genuinely unavailable — out of print, inaccessible, or in a language the citer cannot read — the "as cited in" form is the prescribed handling: name the primary source in text, reference only the secondary source consulted. The convention does not require the citer to have read the original; it requires the dependency to be declared. A third-party description as sole evidence is therefore a recognised and permitted citation state, not an automatic void.
    5. Balshem, H., Helfand, M., Schunemann, H. J., Oxman, A. D., Kunz, R., Brozek, J., et al. 2011. "GRADE guidelines: 3. Rating the quality of evidence." Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 64(4): 401-406; and Cochrane / GRADE handbook practice on low and very low certainty evidence (gradepro.org/handbook; CDC ACIP GRADE Handbook ch. 7). — GRADE's entire design premise is that low-certainty evidence is retained with a grade attached rather than excluded. Evidence rated low or very low still enters the evidence profile and still supports recommendations; GRADE warns against strong recommendations on low-certainty evidence but names five paradigmatic situations in which even that is justified (see also Cochrane-adjacent cross-sectional work on strong recommendations from low certainty evidence, PMC10039768). The dominant global standard for evidence synthesis is therefore "usable-with-grade", not "unverified therefore discard" — which is structurally the same handling the presumption objects to.

  Strength of challenge: Moderate

  Summary: The presumption's analytic core — labelling is not verifying — is not refuted by anything located, and nothing in this search claims a confidence tag substitutes for reading the source. But three of its operative moves are challenged. First, "attached by the same process that produced the claim" is not by itself disqualifying: Lin et al. show self-generated verbalised confidence can be well calibrated and responsive to the generator's own uncertainty, and Moore et al. show self-reported confidence tracks accuracy closely in a large feedback-rich forecasting domain. Second, "the label does not function as a control" is empirically challenged: Kim et al. demonstrate that uncertainty expression changes downstream reliance and task performance, which is what a control does. Third, the implied standard "value cannot survive an unreadable source" is stricter than the two most established evidence regimes: APA's "as cited in" convention exists precisely for unobtainable originals, and GRADE retains low-certainty evidence with a grade rather than excluding it. The common thread in all of this challenging literature is a condition, not an absolution — the label works when the scale is defined in advance, when there is some resolution or feedback loop that could in principle score it, and when the dependency is declared rather than buried. That condition is what should be checked in C2A2, and it is a different and more tractable question than the one the presumption poses.

  Specific risks: If the presumption is over-read, C2A2 adopts an unverified-therefore-discard rule that is stricter than GRADE and stricter than APA, and it loses its own disclosure layer: the marginal effect of writing "Confidence: Speculative" becomes zero, so the incentive to write it disappears, and speculative items either vanish or re-enter unlabelled. That is strictly worse, because Kim et al. show the label was doing measurable work on downstream use. If the presumption is under-read, "Speculative" becomes a laundering token: it is applied without a defined scale, never scored against any outcome, and never revisited, so it discharges the obligation to verify without ever having measured anything — a self-issued grade with no examiner. The second failure is the one the presumption is right to fear, and it is the more likely one in a system with no resolution events.

  Mitigations available: Define the confidence scale before the labels are applied, so "Speculative" has a stated meaning independent of the item it is attached to (the precondition behind both Lin et al. and Moore et al.). Create a resolution channel: log a sample of Speculative items with a check-back date and score whether they later verified, which is the only thing that converts a label into a calibration record — without it, no calibration claim is available in either direction. Adopt the "as cited in" convention explicitly for third-party-described conjunctions: record that the primary is unread, name the secondary that is the actual evidence, and let the citation carry the dependency rather than the confidence tag. Adopt a GRADE-style two-field pattern — separate "certainty of evidence" from "strength of the downstream use permitted" — so a Speculative item can be retained and cited without being permitted to bear weight. Add a reachability field distinct from confidence, since "I judge this uncertain" and "I could not open the source" are different facts and collapsing them into one label is the actual defect.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-573
    Strongest counterargument (against the presumption): Self-issued uncertainty labels are the backbone of every mature evidence system, and they work. GRADE keeps low-certainty findings in the evidence profile with a grade rather than deleting them, APA prescribes "as cited in" for exactly the unreadable-original case, and both are the product of decades of adversarial methodological scrutiny. The presumption's specific objection — that the labeller and the claimant are the same process — is refuted by Lin et al., who show that verbalised confidence emitted alongside an answer can be well calibrated and sensitive to the generator's own uncertainty rather than imitative, and by Moore et al., who find self-reported confidence tracking accuracy across a three-year tournament with only 3% residual overconfidence. And the objection that the label is not a control is refuted by Kim et al., who measure the effect of uncertainty expression on reliance and performance directly. On this reading "Confidence: Speculative" on an item whose source is unreadable is not a failure of handling but the textbook handling: retain, grade, declare the dependency, restrict the weight.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: the confidence scale is defined in writing before use, with stated criteria that do not depend on the item being labelled; at least some labelled items eventually resolve, so calibration is measurable rather than asserted; the "Speculative" tag is accompanied by a separate, explicit record that the primary source is unread and the third-party description is the evidence; and downstream use is constrained by the grade rather than merely annotated by it — nothing rated Speculative is permitted to be a load-bearing premise for a further claim without re-verification.
    How to test: sample 20-30 items carrying "Confidence: Speculative", and (a) check whether a written scale predates them, (b) attempt independent verification of each and compute the hit rate — if Speculative items verify at a rate far above or far below what the label implies, the label is miscalibrated in a measurable direction, which is a stronger and more actionable finding than "labelling is not verifying"; and (c) trace forward from each Speculative item to see whether any downstream claim inherits it without re-grading. If (c) finds inheritance, the label is confirmed to be decorative in this system regardless of what the literature says about labels in general, and the presumption is vindicated on C2A2-specific grounds rather than on the general ones it currently rests on. Note for the specific conjunction at issue: the decisive test is not the label at all but whether any second, independent description of the conjunction exists — a single third-party description is a single point of failure irrespective of how it is graded.
