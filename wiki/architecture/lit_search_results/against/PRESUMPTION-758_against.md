  Original statement: Whether an unbounded exception clause defeats a recall window. Risk: High.

  Reading challenged: The operative (reassuring) reading — that a recall window remains a real, load-bearing constraint even when the governing rule carries an unbounded exception clause ("...unless otherwise relevant"). This search attacks that reading from two sides: (i) evidence that open-ended residual exceptions are invoked far beyond their intended rarity and hollow out the rule they qualify; (ii) evidence that the recall window is *itself* a poorly-justified filter, so that the pairing is a weak rule wrapped in an unbounded escape hatch.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: Two literatures converge to challenge the presumption from opposite directions. Evidence law provides a long, documented natural experiment: a rule qualified by an open-ended residual exception drifts toward routine invocation, was found not to be confined to the rare cases its drafters intended, and required formal amendment in 2019 — and the field's own standing description is that the exceptions swallow the rule. Systematic-review methodology attacks the other component: date-restricted recall is applied in over a quarter of reviews but justified in barely a fifth of those cases, and the methodological guidance is that date limits belong in eligibility criteria, not in the search step, because search-step limits silently discard relevant records. Taken together, the pairing under scrutiny is a filter of doubtful warrant governed by an escape hatch with no stopping condition. The decision-theoretic form of the problem is unfavourable in a way no amount of drafting fixes: an exception invocable at discretion sets no criterion at all, so the operating point on the ROC curve is determined by whoever is invoking it that day, not by the policy.

  Specific risks:
    - Silent nullification: the window can be formally in force and operationally dead, with no telemetry distinguishing the two states. Nothing in the policy text tells you the invocation rate.
    - Budget non-determinism: if the exception is unbounded, the volume of material admitted per run is unbounded, so run cost cannot be estimated from the window. Any budget derived from the window is fictional.
    - Selective admission: discretionary exceptions are exercised non-uniformly. The material admitted through the hatch will be systematically biased toward whatever is salient to the invoking agent — a survivorship artefact injected at intake, which is where it is hardest to detect later.
    - Bidirectional loss: the window silently drops relevant older material (JCE guidance) while the exception silently admits arbitrary older material. Both errors are unlogged, and they do not cancel.
    - Audit impossibility: with no bound and no required justification, there is no post-hoc test of whether the exception was correctly used. Unlike FRE 807, C2A2 has no appellate record.

  Mitigations available:
    - Bound the exception numerically (a per-run quota of out-of-window admissions), which converts an unbounded clause into a costed one — the standard remedy in both alerting and evidence contexts.
    - Require a written justification per invocation, and *measure the invocation rate*. The FRE 807 history shows the diagnostic question is empirical: how often is it actually used?
    - Move the temporal criterion from the search step to the eligibility criteria, per the JCE guidance, so that the window states what is *relevant* rather than what will be *looked at*. This removes the need for an exception clause entirely.
    - Log both arms of the loss: sample material just outside the window that was *not* admitted, to estimate what the window is discarding.
    - Set an explicit operating point (target precision/recall) and monitor drift, rather than expressing policy as a rule plus discretion.

  STEELMAN:
    Item: PRESUMPTION-758
    Strongest counterargument: An unbounded exception clause defeats a recall window only if it is actually invoked. FRE 807's history is a story about a discretionary standard applied by thousands of independent judges with adversarial incentives to push at it; a single pipeline with a single invocation policy and a logged trail has no comparable pressure. If the observed invocation rate is low — say under a few percent of admitted items — the window is doing essentially all the work and the exception is a genuine safety valve preventing the brittle-rule failure that the systematic-review literature warns about. Indeed the JCE guidance cuts against the window, not against the exception: the exception is the thing rescuing recall from an unjustified date limit. On that reading the exception clause is the *correct* component and the window is the defective one.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) The invocation rate of the exception must be measured and must be low and stable, not merely assumed to be; (b) each invocation must carry a recorded reason, so the distribution of reasons can be inspected for drift toward routine use; (c) the window itself must be justified by an eligibility argument (why is material older than N days not relevant?), not by cost convenience; (d) run cost must be bounded by something other than the window, so that unbounded admission cannot silently blow the budget; (e) the material discarded by the window must be sampled periodically so the recall loss is estimated rather than ignored.
    How to test: Instrument the exception. For a fixed number of runs, log every admission with a flag for in-window vs. exception, plus the stated reason. Compute the invocation rate and its trend. Then run a shadow condition with the window removed entirely and compare (i) the set of items admitted, (ii) the set of findings produced, (iii) the cost. Three outcomes discriminate cleanly: if the shadow condition produces materially the same findings, the window is redundant and should be dropped; if the exception's invocation rate is already high and rising, the window is nullified and the presumption is refuted; if invocation is rare and the shadow condition produces large amounts of low-value material, the window plus a rare exception is vindicated. Separately, sample 50 out-of-window items that were *not* admitted and have them adjudicated, to estimate the window's false-negative rate directly.
