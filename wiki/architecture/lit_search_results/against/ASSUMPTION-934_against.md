  Sources:
    1. Marek, S., Tervo-Clemmens, B., Calabro, F. J., Montez, D. F., Kay, B. P., Hatoum, A. S., et al. (2022). "Reproducible brain-wide association studies require thousands of individuals." *Nature* 603(7902), 654–660. doi:10.1038/s41586-022-04492-9. — The decisive methodological challenge. Using ~50,000 participants across three cohorts, shows that brain–phenotype association studies at typical sample sizes are statistically underpowered and produce inflated, irreproducible effects; the authors were prompted by failing to replicate their own N≈1,000 result in a second N≈1,000 sample. nsaf124 is precisely a brain-wide association study of an individual-difference phenotype (loneliness × age) at **N=73**. On Marek et al.'s finding, an N=73 BWAS is roughly an order of magnitude below where such effects begin to stabilise. A metric boundary should not be moved on it in either direction.
    2. Brown, N. J. L., Sokal, A. D., & Friedman, H. L. (2013). Challenges the assumption's implicit premise that the *only* thing wrong with C2A2's positive-affect metric is where its valence boundary sits. The tradition's flagship quantitative claim about positive affect has already been retracted once on formal grounds.

This matters specifically because the "upward spiral / positivity resonance → physical health" pathway is the closest published analogue to what C2A2 uses as a *community-health* metric, and it is the pathway with the worst replication record in the corpus.
    5. "Testing the validity of the broaden-and-build theory of positive emotions: a network analytic approach." *Frontiers in Psychology* (2024), PMC11458511 / doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1405272 [author list not confirmed this session]. — Network modelling of the theory's components found that while positive-emotion→resources→outcomes relations held, "the broadening component consistently deviated from the model's predictions and thus did not contribute to the model as expected," challenging the validity of broaden-and-build in its current conceptualisation. Also relevant: Frontiers in Psychology (2016), PMC5078759, found a broadening effect of low-arousal positive affect in one experiment but no narrowing effect of negative affect, and **no** effect of broadened/narrowed attentional scope on the thought–action repertoire — i.e. the theory's central mediating mechanism does not reliably obtain. The wiki proposes to re-bound a metric derived from a theory whose mediating mechanism is itself unsupported.
    6. Whitehouse, H., & Lanman, J. A. (2014). "The ties that bind us: Ritual, fusion, and identification." *Current Anthropology* 55(6). — Dysphoric, negative-valence rituals ("rites of terror," hazing) are argued to bind groups. **This is not support for the wiki's proposed boundary; it is a rival boundary.** It locates the binding work in shared *intense/dysphoric experience*, not in mutual care. Compounding: the search summary of this literature also records a finding that "negative valence associated with dysphoria was found to inhibit identity fusion" while repetitive rituals grounded in shared meaning foster integration — i.e. the negative-valence literature is itself internally contested, so it cannot be used to settle the boundary either.
    7. "Shared negative experiences lead to identity fusion via personal reflection." *PLOS ONE* (2015) 10(12): e0145611 [Whitehouse and colleagues; full author list not confirmed this session]. — Found that shared negative experience predicted identity fusion among Northern Irish Republicans and Unionists, mediated by reflection, replicated in Bostonians after the 2013 Marathon bombings. And: "The evolution of extreme cooperation via shared dysphoric experiences," *Scientific Reports* (srep44292). — Challenges the *civic-good* inference specifically: what shared dysphoria produces is **extreme, parochial, in-group cooperation**, which is a cohesion outcome, not a community-health outcome, and which in the ethnographic and conflict cases cited is bound up with intergroup violence. Moving C2A2's boundary to "shared affect with mutual care" so that lament counts as a civic good does not by itself protect the metric from scoring high on cohesive groups whose cohesion is achieved at an out-group's expense.
    8. Lawson, K. M., & Robins, R. W. Challenges the operationalisability of the replacement boundary: "mutual care" is a less measurable construct than valence, and swapping to it trades a badly specified metric for a worse specified one.

  Strength of challenge: Strong

  Summary: **I am challenging the proposed boundary move itself — the replacement of "positive shared affect" with "shared affect with mutual care" — and, more fundamentally, the evidentiary licence to move the boundary at all.** The cited N=73 studies are real (verified this session) and Fredrickson is a genuine co-author, so the challenge cannot be dismissed as a citation problem. But an N=73 brain-wide association study of an individual-difference phenotype is, on Marek et al. (2022), roughly an order of magnitude below the sample size at which such effects stop being inflated and irreproducible; the two papers share one cohort and are therefore not independent replications; the wiki's own audit notes no effect sizes are reported anywhere, only directions; and the absence of a positive-versus-neutral contrast is a missing test, not a demonstration that positive valence lacks privilege. A pattern of "effects in pleasant and unpleasant alike, null in neutral" is at least as consistent with a generic social-salience or arousal account as with a mutual-care account, and the wiki has selected the latter without a discriminating test. Separately and more damagingly, the construct whose boundary is being adjusted has a documented integrity record: the positivity-ratio model was formally withdrawn after Brown, Sokal & Friedman (2013); Nickerson (2018) argues the residual empirical claim is unsupported; the closest published analogue to a positivity-resonance health pathway (Kok et al. 2013) did not survive reanalysis; and the theory's central broadening mechanism fails network-analytic validation. Finally, the negative-valence cohesion literature does not support "mutual care" as the operative variable — Whitehouse's dysphoric-ritual work locates the binding in shared intense experience and yields parochial extreme cooperation, i.e. cohesion that is not automatically a civic good. The honest conclusion is that FLAG-019 correctly identified that the *old* boundary is unjustified, and then over-reached by naming a *new* one.

  Specific risks:
    - **Boundary revision on an underpowered single cohort.** N=73, one sample, two non-independent papers, cross-sectional, loneliness measured not manipulated, no effect sizes. Marek et al. (2022) makes this the textbook profile of an effect that will not replicate.
    - **Repairing the wrong layer.** Adjusting the valence boundary presupposes the underlying positivity-resonance construct is sound. Given the positivity-ratio withdrawal and the Kok et al. reanalysis, the live question may be whether C2A2 should have a Fredrickson-derived community-health metric at all, not where its boundary sits. The revision makes the metric *look* freshly audited while leaving the deeper problem untouched — an integrity risk in its own right.
    - **Trading a measurable boundary for an unmeasurable one.** Valence is at least operationalisable. "Mutual care" is a construct with severe jingle-jangle exposure, and its nearest neighbour (behavioural synchrony) is documented as being confounded with empathy components. Expect the metric's inter-rater and construct validity to fall, not rise.
    - **Absence of evidence read as evidence of absence.** "No positive-vs-neutral contrast is reported anywhere" is the wiki's own observation and is a gap in the studies' reporting. It is being used as if it disconfirmed positive-valence privilege.
    - **Cohesion/health conflation.** The negative-valence cohesion literature that would license the move also documents that shared dysphoria produces parochial, sometimes violent, in-group bonding. A metric that scores grief-holding as a civic good on the same basis will also score such groups highly unless it carries a separate out-group clause.
    - **Under-determination between rival accounts.** Salience/arousal, constructionist emotion (the papers' own preferred reading), and mutual care all predict the reported pattern. The wiki picked one.
    - **Overweighting a source's self-revision.** PRESUMPTION-757 already names this: the studies are not an operationalisation of C2A2's metric, and their result binds C2A2's construct only if someone has argued that it does. Nobody has.

  Mitigations available:
    - Convert FLAG-019 from "the boundary may be wrong, here is the replacement" to "**the boundary is unsupported; no replacement is licensed by the current evidence**." That preserves the genuine finding and drops the over-reach.
    - Attach a standing **power annotation** to any wiki claim resting on neuroimaging individual-difference results, citing Marek et al. (2022), with a minimum-N threshold below which results may be recorded but may not move a metric.
    - Mark the two SCAN papers explicitly as **one cohort, not two replications**, in every wiki file that cites them (the wiki's own PRS-38 audit already noticed this; ensure it propagated).
    - Before adopting "mutual care," require an operational definition with (i) a measurement procedure, (ii) an inter-rater reliability target, and (iii) discriminant-validity evidence against behavioural synchrony and against generic arousal — i.e. the Lawson & Robins sibling-construct checklist.
    - Run a **rival-account pre-registration**: state in advance what result would favour mutual-care over salience/arousal and over the constructionist reading the papers' own authors endorse. If no such result can be named, the boundary move is not testable and should not be made.
    - Add an out-group clause to any cohesion-based community-health metric, given the parochial-cooperation findings.
    - Record the positivity-ratio withdrawal, the Kok et al. reanalysis, and the broadening-mechanism null results directly on the Fredrickson tradition page, so any future metric derived from this tradition inherits the caveats rather than rediscovering them.
    - Re-check the nsag008 author string against the paper's masthead (see the discrepancy logged above).

  STEELMAN:
    Item: ASSUMPTION-934
    Strongest counterargument: FLAG-019's core move does not depend on the N=73 result being reliable. Its logic is: C2A2 asserted, on this tradition's authority, that *positive* valence is the operative variable; the tradition's own most recent instruments report effects in pleasant and unpleasant conditions alike and never test positive-versus-neutral at all; therefore the specific warrant C2A2 cited for privileging positive valence does not exist. That is a claim about the absence of support, and absence of support is not weakened by the study being underpowered — an underpowered study that fails to find the privileging contrast is at minimum no evidence for it, and the contrast was never run. On that reading the flag is a straightforward withdrawal of an unwarranted assertion, and "shared affect with mutual care" is a *hypothesis for the next measurement*, not a metric change already made. The practical consequence — that grief-holding and lament stop being scored as the absence of a civic good — is arguably a correction of a known blind spot regardless of which boundary eventually proves right, since the current metric scores communal mourning as a deficit, which is very likely wrong on any account.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: (a) FLAG-019 must be recorded as *withdrawing* the positive-valence warrant, not as *establishing* the mutual-care boundary — and every downstream file must reflect that distinction. (b) No community-health score may be computed under the new boundary until "mutual care" has an operational definition with discriminant validity against arousal and synchrony. (c) The metric must carry the Fredrickson-tradition caveats (positivity-ratio withdrawal, Kok et al. reanalysis, broadening-mechanism nulls) on its face, so users know its provenance. (d) The metric must not treat cohesion as health without an out-group clause. (e) The two SCAN papers must never be counted as independent evidence. (f) PRESUMPTION-757's question — whether a source's self-revision binds a construct C2A2 built — must be answered explicitly rather than assumed.
    How to test: (1) **The discriminating contrast**: the missing positive-vs-neutral test is the direct one. Until someone runs or locates it, neither boundary is supported; record this as the falsification condition. (2) **Dyadic extension**, as FLAG-019 itself proposes — hyperscanning or dyadic physiology with co-present participants, crossed 2×2 on valence (pleasant/unpleasant) × care (mutual care present/absent). Mutual-care-as-boundary predicts a main effect of care and no valence effect; salience/arousal predicts a main effect of intensity; positive-valence privilege predicts a valence effect. Power it to Marek et al.'s standard for whatever the individual-difference component is, or drop the individual-difference component entirely and treat it as a within-subject design, which is far cheaper to power. (3) **Construct audit**: administer candidate mutual-care measures alongside behavioural-synchrony and arousal measures in the same sample and report the discriminant validity matrix; if mutual care does not separate, the boundary is a jingle-jangle relabel. (4) **Downstream sweep**: count how many wiki files currently score grief/lament as a community-health deficit, as the size of the exposure either way. (5) **Author-string recheck** of nsag008 against the publisher masthead.
