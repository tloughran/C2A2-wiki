    1. [Altman, D.G., & Bland, J.M., 1995. "Statistics notes: Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence."     2. [Bramer, W.M., Rethlefsen, M.L., Kleijnen, J., & Franco, O.H., 2017. "Optimal database combinations for literature searches in systematic reviews: a prospective exploratory study." Systematic Reviews 6:245. — Empirically, the best single database achieved only 85.9% recall; methodological standards require at least four sources (Embase, MEDLINE, Web of Science, Google Scholar) for acceptable coverage. A single-venue watch (Indico) is far below any accepted evidence-monitoring standard.]
    3. [Hopewell, S., McDonald, S., Clarke, M.J., & Egger, M., 2007. "Grey literature in meta-analyses of randomized trials of health care interventions." Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, MR000010. — Gray/unpublished material systematically differs from formally published material; monitoring that ignores gray channels (preprints, talks, podcasts, blog posts, workshop recordings) produces a biased picture of what "exists."]
    4. [Mallett, S., Hopewell, S., & Clarke, M., 2002. First 1000 Cochrane reviews analysis. — Nearly half of systematic reviews included no gray/unpublished data, a documented and criticized coverage gap; the same availability bias applies to a tradition-agent watching one conference platform.]
  Strength of challenge: Strong
  Summary: The assumption converts "our one channel saw nothing" into "there is nothing," which is precisely the inference Altman and Bland warned against. The systematic-review literature quantifies the gap: even professional multi-database searches miss 7-14% of relevant items, and single sources miss far more. Thinkers in the C2A2 tradition list (Levin, Friston, Wolfram, etc.) publish heavily through gray channels — preprints (arXiv, bioRxiv, PsyArXiv), podcasts, YouTube lectures, Substack essays — that an Indico-centric watch never touches. The remedy ("a watch item" on the same channel) inherits the same blind spot rather than correcting it: it increases sampling frequency, not sampling coverage. Zero proposals may be the correct output, but the process as described cannot distinguish "nothing happened" from "we weren't looking where it happened."
  Specific risks: Tradition agents systematically under-propose for thinkers whose output is gray-channel-first; the wiki drifts stale precisely for its most active traditions; a 30-day "quiet" verdict becomes a self-reinforcing artifact (no proposals → no new watch channels → no detections); editorial decisions (e.g., deprioritizing a tradition) get made on an availability-biased signal.
  Mitigations available: Multi-channel watch per thinker (arXiv/bioRxiv author feeds, Google Scholar alerts, official sites/blogs, YouTube channel RSS, podcast indexes); explicit per-channel coverage declaration in the daily report ("searched: X, Y; not searched: Z") so absence claims are scoped; periodic retrospective audit — when material is eventually found, check whether it existed during a declared-quiet window.
  STEELMAN:
    Strongest counterargument: For a narrowly scoped question — "did thinker T release primary material tied to this specific expected event (an Indico-listed talk)?" — a single-source watch is the correct instrument, and the agent scoped its claim to "public primary material" with an explicit TBC flag rather than asserting universal silence. A watch item is a proportionate, cheap remedy when the expected-material channel is known, and over-searching every gray channel daily has real cost in noise and false proposals.
    What would need to be true for C2A2 to be safe: The claim is genuinely scoped to the one expected item (not generalized to "the tradition is quiet"); tradition agents have separate broader-sweep cadences (e.g., weekly multi-channel) so the daily single-channel check is one layer of several; and historical audits show quiet verdicts rarely get overturned by late discoveries.
    How to test: Retrospective audit over 90 days — for each declared 30-day-quiet window, later search preprint servers and author channels for material dated inside the window. Overturn rate >10% confirms the challenge; near-zero supports the steelman.
