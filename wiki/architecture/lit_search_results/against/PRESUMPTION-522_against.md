Just-culture / patient-safety reporting literature (APSF; NCBI/AHRQ). — Disclosure *first* is deliberately encouraged so that reporting is not suppressed by fear; a system that demanded instant remediation before disclosure would reduce reporting.     2. ICFR remediation guidance (Deloitte 2024). — Remediation is expected to be risk-prioritized and can legitimately lag disclosure; not every named deficiency is fixed same-day. Timeliness is judged by risk, not immediacy.

  Strength of challenge: Weak

  Summary: No source challenges the presumption's core validity — every framework agrees that documentation does not discharge the remediation obligation. The only qualification is on remedy design: disclosure-before-remediation is *correct* (it protects reporting), and deferral of low-risk items is legitimate. So the finding is not "always fix on disclosure" but "every named defect owes a tracked, risk-prioritized disposition to closure." The PREMISE-118 case is not exculpated by these caveats because the owed retrospective assessment is precisely the tracked disposition, and it is absent two days running.

  Specific risks: If over-read as "remediate everything immediately," the premise could suppress disclosure (the opposite pathology). The safe form couples mandatory disclosure with a mandatory closure ledger, not with mandatory instant fixes.

  Mitigations available: A defect-closure ledger: every named defect gets an owner, a risk rank, and a closure state; disclosure remains immediate, remediation is tracked to done.
