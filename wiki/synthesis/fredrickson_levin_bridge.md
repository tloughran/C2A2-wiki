### 2026-08-09 — Allostasis at two scales — same operation, or same word?

*Sewing Agent, 2026-08-09.* **Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/approved/2026-08-06_fredrickson_loneliness-allostatic-interoceptive-aging.md` (PROP-2026-08-06-002)

**Why it sits at this intersection.** `traditions/levin/prs_triplets.md` already carries allostasis and homeostasis-to-alignment material: setpoint maintenance as the primitive from which goal-directedness at higher scales is built. This paper gives the same regulatory concept a human-neural instantiation with a measurable index — graph-theoretic connectivity of the allostatic-interoceptive system across 73 participants.

**Synthesis claim.** The pair now shares a technical term rather than an analogy, which is a real upgrade, and it makes a scale-crossing claim checkable for the first time. Levin's thesis needs allostatic regulation to be the *same operation* at cell and organism scale, differing only in the size of the collective doing it. If that is right, the loneliness result has a prediction attached at Levin's scale: disrupting the coupling that binds a cell collective should degrade its regulatory response to *salient* perturbations specifically, while leaving neutral-condition regulation intact — the exact specificity signature reported here in humans.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Is the specificity signature transferable, or does it depend on a social stimulus having no analogue below the organism? The human result turns on stimuli being *socially* affective, and 'socially salient' may not be definable for a cell collective without smuggling in the conclusion. If the signature cannot be stated at Levin's scale without circularity, the shared term is doing less work than it appears to, and the pair should be downgraded back to analogy.

### 2026-08-16 — Is emotional attunement net-costly or net-restorative? Two programmes, opposite signs, one unasked question

*Sewing Agent, 2026-08-16.* **Orphaned page:** [[2026-08-14_levin_mental-health-tolchinsky-weiss-fields]] (PROP-2026-08-14-002)

**Why it sits at this intersection.** In a working conversation on Levin's podcast, psychiatrist Anthony Weiss asks why listening to emotional content is energetically exhausting, notes that therapeutic sessions are bounded in length for exactly that reason, and extends the question to AI agents doing it continuously. Fredrickson's programme runs the other way: positive social connection, and positivity resonance specifically, is treated as a **restorative** biophysiological process with measurable markers — vagal tone, oxytocinergic signalling, downstream health outcomes. A clinician's working assumption and a laboratory literature assign opposite signs to what looks like the same activity.

**Synthesis claim.** The two are probably not in contradiction, and the shape of the reconciliation is the finding: **the sign depends on whether attunement is reciprocal.** Fredrickson's construct is explicitly bidirectional — shared positive affect, mutual care, behavioural synchrony — and the measured restoration is a property of the dyad. Clinical listening is deliberately *asymmetric*: the therapist attunes and is not attuned to, by design and by ethics. If that is the discriminating variable, then positivity resonance predicts its own boundary condition, and the prediction is testable with instruments Fredrickson already uses — measure the markers in reciprocal versus asymmetric attunement of matched duration and emotional intensity. This also gives Weiss' AI question a sharp form rather than a rhetorical one: an agent doing continuous asymmetric attunement is in the depleting condition permanently, and if the depletion is a real biophysiological cost rather than an artefact of human attention limits, the question of what the analogous cost is for an agent stops being a metaphor.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Whether the exhaustion clinicians report is emotional-attunement cost or ordinary sustained-attention cost wearing its clothes. Nothing in the source separates them, and the null hypothesis — that any hour of high-stakes concentrated attention is depleting and emotional content is incidental — is not addressed by either programme. The reciprocity test above would need an attention-matched control arm to say anything, and neither tradition has run one. Recorded here as a well-posed question, not as a result.
