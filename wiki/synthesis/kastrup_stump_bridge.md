**Intersecting orphan:** `inbox/proposals/approved/2026-06-24_kastrup_one-free-miracle.md` (PROP-2026-06-24-003)

**Why it sits at the intersection:** Kastrup takes McKenna's "grant one free miracle" — every metaphysics gets exactly one unexplained primitive — and relocates it from physical law to consciousness (mind-at-large "by definition cannot be explained by another"). Stump's Thomism grants that one primitive to God as *ipsum esse subsistens*, defending its non-arbitrariness through divine simplicity and per-se necessity.

**Synthesis claim:** Kastrup and Stump agree on the *shape* of the ultimate explanation — exactly one self-grounding primitive that terminates the regress — and disagree on its *identity and its non-arbitrariness*. Kastrup: the primitive is consciousness, and "it is how it is because it is what it is." Stump: the primitive is subsistent being, whose being-what-it-is is not a brute tautology but per-se necessity secured by simplicity. The dispute is whether "it is what it is" is a *dissolution* (Kastrup) or a *substantive metaphysical claim* (Stump).

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** Is Kastrup's "the question dissolves once you drop the external constrainer" the same move as the Thomistic denial that God's nature needs external explanation — or does relocating the primitive to *consciousness* (rather than *being*) leave the indexical "why this alter, here, now?" unanswered in a way the doctrine of divine simplicity does not? The wiki has no adjudication of whether idealist facticity and Thomistic per-se necessity are rivals or allies.
