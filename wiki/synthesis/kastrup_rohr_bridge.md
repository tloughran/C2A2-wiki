## Why these sit at the intersection

The proposal itself flags this as a paradigm-bridge candidate (CROSS — cosmic-Christ ↔ mind-monism). The structural overlap:

- **Kastrup** holds that there is one underlying consciousness (mind-at-large) whose dissociated alters are the many individual perspectives; the physical world is how mind-at-large's mentation appears across those interfaces.
- **Rohr** holds that there is one eternal Christ/Logos — the pattern of self-giving love — present in and as all creation ("the first incarnation"), of which each creature is a particular expression.

Read side by side, "one consciousness expressed as many interfaces" (Kastrup) and "one Christ-pattern incarnate as all creatures" (Rohr) are the **same one-to-many move in metaphysical versus contemplative-theological registers**. Rohr supplies what Kastrup's program structurally lacks: a *lived practice* (contemplative attention to the "sacred other") that treats the one-in-many not as a thesis to be argued but as a relationship to be inhabited.

## Synthesis claim

**Rohr's Universal-Christ-in-creation gives analytic idealism a recognizably orthodox spiritual practice rather than a freelance pantheism: the contemplative encounter with each creature as a "sacred other" is the practice-side correlate of attributing interiority across mind-at-large's many dissociated interfaces. Where Kastrup argues that the many are appearances of the one mind, Rohr enacts the many as expressions of the one Christ — and the two are complementary articulations of a single unity-in-distinction claim, one metaphysical and one devotional. The C2A2 network can pair them so that its mind-monist members gain a contemplative-Christian companion that does not require translating idealism into pantheism.**

## Open question the wiki does not yet have an answer to

Kastrup's idealism is *dissociative* — the many alters are real boundaries within mind-at-large, and individuation (the daimon, cf. PRS-08) matters. Rohr's cosmic-Christ frame tends toward *participation and ultimate reunion* — separateness as the wound to be healed. **Does the contemplative goal (dissolving the sense of separateness) contradict the metaphysical structure (dissociative boundaries as constitutive of distinct conscious agents)** — or is the contemplative "reunion" compatible with, even dependent on, retaining the boundaries that make distinct perspectives possible? This is the same tension the Rohr×Wright exile bridge raises from the theological side (separation-as-exile vs. separation-as-individuation); the wiki does not yet resolve whether C2A2's metaphysics wants separateness healed or honored.
