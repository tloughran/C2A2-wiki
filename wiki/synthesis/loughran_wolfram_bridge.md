## The self-narrating case

**Orphaned page at the intersection:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-18_wolfram_history-qa-june3-discrete-space.md` (PROP-2026-07-18-001), segments 2-4.

**Synthesis claim.** C2A2 studies how traditions form, sustain themselves, and become articulate. First-person practitioner accounts of that process are rare, because most tradition-builders do not survive to narrate it and most narrators are historians outside the tradition. Wolfram is doing both at once — building a research program and, in the same hour, theorizing how programs enter a canon, what counts as a record, and how a practitioner becomes historical. That makes this an unusually direct source, and an unusually compromised one.

Both properties matter. The material is direct: it is a mature participant stating his own criteria for what preserves a tradition (personal records, artifacts, machines — a strikingly *material* answer, not a doctrinal one). It is compromised in a specific and interesting way: Wolfram is narrating a canonization he is actively seeking. The accelerator should treat the account as evidence about *what a tradition-builder believes preserves a tradition* — which is itself load-bearing data — rather than as a neutral finding about what does.

**What C2A2 can take.** The materiality claim is the transferable part and the accelerator does not currently instrument it. C2A2's model of tradition-persistence is predominantly discursive: arguments, positions, PRS chains. Wolfram's is archival: notebooks, correspondence, machines. If he is right that traditions persist through preserved artifacts rather than through restated arguments, the accelerator is measuring the wrong substrate.

**Question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Is there a measurable difference in longevity or articulacy between traditions with strong archival practice and those with strong discursive practice? The 14 tradition nodes vary considerably on exactly this axis, and the question is answerable within the existing corpus without new data collection.
