## Why these sit at the intersection

List's position, which Carroll endorses as the analytic backbone of his own poetic naturalism, is the **physicalist mirror-image of Kastrup's analytic idealism**. Both deny that the micro-physical level exhausts reality. But they reach that denial from opposite ontological starting points:

- **Carroll/List:** the base is physical; higher levels (agents, choices, values) are real *patterns* that supervene on but do not reduce to physics. Reality is layered, physicalist at the bottom, non-reducible upward.
- **Kastrup:** the base is mental (mind-at-large); the physical is the extrinsic appearance of mental processes on a dissociated alter's "dashboard." Reality is mental at the bottom, with the physical as representation.

They agree on the *structural* claim (no single level exhausts the real; the micro does not fix the explanatory currency) and disagree maximally on the *ground* (physical vs. mental). That makes this one of the cleanest structured-disagreement nodes in the connectome: same anti-reductionism, opposite monism.

## Synthesis claim

**The Carroll/List "non-reductive physicalism" and Kastrup's "analytic idealism" are formally dual: each grants that the micro-physical level is causally complete yet explanatorily insufficient, and each posits an additional commitment to recover the reality of agency, value, and meaning — Carroll by making higher-level patterns ontologically real, Kastrup by making the mental ground ontologically basic. The disagreement is therefore not about *whether* the manifest image is real (both say yes) but about *what makes it real*: emergent pattern-realism versus mind-first idealism. For the C2A2 network this is a high-value pairing because the two positions are nearly empirically indistinguishable on first-person experience and diverge only on the metaphysics of the base — exactly the kind of disagreement the connectome exists to surface and hold open.**

## Open question the wiki does not yet have an answer to

If non-reductive physicalism (Carroll/List) and analytic idealism (Kastrup) make the *same* predictions about the reality and autonomy of higher-level agency, **is there any experiment, observation, or formal criterion that could decide between them** — or is the choice underdetermined and therefore a matter of which base yields the more tractable research program? List's agency-without-consciousness move (PRS-CANDIDATE-03) sharpens this: if agency can be ascribed on functional criteria *independent of consciousness*, then the physicalist can grant real agency to AIs while remaining silent on the hard problem, whereas Kastrup must locate any genuine agency within mind-at-large. The wiki does not yet specify whether that asymmetry is a deciding test or merely a difference in bookkeeping.
