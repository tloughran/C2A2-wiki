## Epistemic value over a rival's model: a mechanism for second-first-language competence
*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-12*

**Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-06_friston_active-inference-artificial-reasoning.md` (PROP-2026-07-06-003)

**Why it sits at this intersection:** Friston's arXiv:2512.21129 extends active inference from inference over hidden *states* to inference over model *structure*: an agent samples the outcomes that most inform it about the structure of its generative model. C2A2's constitutive aim is agents that acquire a rival tradition as a second first language -- which is to say, agents that build an accurate model of *another model's structure*. The paper is the first formal object in the wiki that has the right type signature for that aim.

**Synthesis claim:** Second-first-language competence is epistemic value directed not at the world but at a rival generative model: the tradition-learner selects the questions, texts, and encounters that maximize information gain about how the other tradition is *built*, not about whether it is true. This yields a testable operationalization of 'mature understanding of a rival tradition' -- it is reached when the learner can predict the rival's responses to novel cases, i.e. when the structural model has low residual uncertainty -- and it explains why disputation outperforms exposition as a learning method: adversarial queries have higher epistemic value over structure than sympathetic ones.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** Read together with PROP-2026-07-11-001 (Wolfram: idea-uptake as rulial distance from the receiving community's position), the wiki now has two formal accounts of tradition-entry from opposite directions -- one about the *learner's sampling policy*, one about the *distance to be crossed*. Nobody has checked whether they are the same account. If expected-free-energy gradients over model structure and shortest paths in rulial space describe the same motion, C2A2 has a single theory of acceleration; if they do not, it has two rivals and must choose.


### 2026-08-09 — A working apparatus for scoring progress across rival programs

*Sewing Agent, 2026-08-09.* **Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/approved/2026-08-03_friston_intrepid-adversarial-review.md` (PROP-2026-08-03-004)

**Why it sits at this intersection.** PRS-CANDIDATE-03 states the review's meta-scientific aim: evaluating theories 'in terms of the scientific progress they deliver'. That is the MacIntyre/Lakatos problem in empirical form, and unlike the philosophical literature it arrives with machinery — a formal scheme for pooling evidence across disparate experiments and their replications into a single quantitative measure of support per theory, applied to a live three-way contest (IIT, Neurorepresentationalism, Active Inference).

**Synthesis claim.** This is the closest published analogue to the PRS-chaining progress metric, and it is an existence proof of the harder half of the accelerator's premise: that mature proponents of rival programs can jointly pre-specify what would settle a dispute. C2A2 has been treating that as an aspiration. INTREPID treats it as a funded protocol with named participants and pre-registered disconfirming observations. The precedent is methodological, not topical, and should be recorded that way.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Is the pooling scheme portable to a network whose rivals do not share an experimental paradigm? INTREPID's three theories all make claims about neural correlates measurable by the same instruments, which is what makes cross-experiment integration coherent. C2A2's traditions do not share instruments, and several do not share the framing — the proposal notes that Kastrup, Hoffman, and McGilchrist are absent from the rival set precisely because it is entirely third-person and neuroscientific. If a shared measurement paradigm is a precondition, that is a hard constraint on the accelerator's design and the wiki should record it as one rather than discovering it later.

### 2026-08-23 — A second first language as a second generative model, and the residue that leaves

*Sewing Agent, 2026-08-23.* **Orphaned pages:** [[2026-08-17_friston_designing-ecosystems-of-intelligence]] (PROP-2026-08-17-004) and [[2026-08-17_levin_cognition-spaces-natural-artificial-hybrid]] (PROP-2026-08-17-003).

**Why they sit at this intersection.** Friston and nineteen collaborators describe the design target as "a cyber-physical ecosystem of natural and synthetic sense-making in which humans are integral participants," and give collective intelligence a mechanism: where agents' generative models overlap, the overlap constitutes a common frame of reference, and belief-sharing across it yields collective intelligence resting on shared narratives and goals. That is a formal restatement of the C2A2 thesis — that rich mutual information about one another's traditions changes how communities interact — written independently and four years earlier. The Levin cognition-space paper offers a rival scaffolding for the same thesis from a different direction.

**Synthesis claim.** MacIntyre's "second first language" has a candidate formalization: an agent carrying a *second* generative model well enough to share beliefs from within it. This is the closest thing to mathematical scaffolding the accelerator/detector claim has ever had, and it is worth taking seriously in both directions. But the formalization is testable precisely where it is likely to fail, and that is the useful part. A second first language on MacIntyre's account is acquired as a **tradition-constituted practice with its own standards of excellence** — you learn it by being apprenticed into a community that can tell you when you are doing it badly. A generative model is transferable; a practice with internal goods is not obviously so. **The residue is the interesting part**, and naming it is a task for the tradition, not for the formalism.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Two candidate formalisms now exist for one thesis — belief-sharing over overlapping generative models, and movement into an unoccupied region of hybrid cognition space — and they are not the same claim. The first says C2A2 succeeds when traditions *overlap enough to share beliefs*; the second says it succeeds when the resulting system *lands somewhere nothing has been*. Those can come apart: maximal overlap is the least novel region of the space. Picking between them is a real task and should not be deferred by adopting both, which is the path of least resistance and would leave the accelerator with two incompatible success criteria and no way to fail.
