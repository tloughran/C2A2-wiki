**Why it sits at the intersection:** Kastrup (with Currivan) argues apparent cosmic purpose is the *immanent, instinctive* self-organizing tendency of mind-at-large — directionality without an external planner. Levin locates goal-directedness at every scale as an intrinsic property of living matter, not imposed from outside.

**Synthesis claim:** Both relocate teleology from an external designer to an *intrinsic disposition of the system itself* — Kastrup in universal consciousness, Levin in basal biological agency. "Immanent purpose" is the shared move; the live disagreement is the flagged discrete-vs-continuous divergence: Levin's goal-directedness is nested/discrete (agents within agents with defined competency horizons), while Kastrup's cosmic instinct reads as continuous self-organization of one subject.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** Is cosmic purpose *one* instinctive tendency (Kastrup's single mind-at-large) or a *nested hierarchy* of goal-directed agents (Levin's scaling competency)? The Currivan dialogue is a fresh test of whether analytic idealism's immanent teleology and diverse-intelligence's nested agency are the same claim or genuinely rival on the granularity of purpose.
