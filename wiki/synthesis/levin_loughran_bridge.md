## Atavistic dissociation: a decoherence metric for traditions
*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-12*

**Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-06_levin_aging-goal-directedness-bioelectricity.md` (PROP-2026-07-06-001)

**Why it sits at this intersection:** Levin's aging talk supplies both the C2A2 collective-alignment thesis in cell-biological form -- 'cells align toward common purpose in a space they don't individually understand,' with connection to the large-scale network as the anti-cancer and anti-aging principle -- and, in PRS-CANDIDATE-03, a *measure* of its failure: atavistic dissociation, a phylostratigraphic finding that young tissues agree on the body's evolutionary age while aged tissues drift into disagreement across the tree of life.

**Synthesis claim:** A tradition ages the way a body does: not by damage and not by design, but by its members drifting out of agreement about what they are and where they came from. Atavistic dissociation is the biological instance of a general failure mode -- loss of concordance about one's own history among the parts of a whole -- and it is measurable. C2A2 has been treating tradition-coherence as an assumed background and tradition-*contact* as the object of study; Levin's result suggests coherence is itself a quantity with a decay curve, and that a tradition losing internal agreement about its own genealogy is exhibiting a detectable pathology rather than merely changing.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** Does the accelerator have an analogue of the phylostratigraphic measure -- some way to ask whether the participants in a tradition still agree about the tradition's evolutionary age? The detector currently measures cross-perspective interaction and has no instrument pointed *inward* at a tradition's own concordance. If Levin is right that decoherence precedes decline, C2A2 is missing the upstream signal, and the sewing agent's own connectivity census (orphan/sparse/connected counts) may be the crudest possible version of exactly this instrument, pointed at the wiki instead of at a community.
