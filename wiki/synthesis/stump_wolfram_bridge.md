**Why it sits at the intersection:** Wolfram recasts "if a brain is emulated/copied or two brains are linked, is there one mind or two?" as *observer individuation* — mind-boundary is a property of computational-persistence coherence, not of neural substrate. Stump holds personal identity to be fixed hylomorphically: the person is this individuated form-matter composite, persisting per the metaphysics of substantial form, not by continuity of a computational thread.

**Synthesis claim:** The emulation/linking thought-experiment forces a clean confrontation between two rival criteria of personal persistence — *observer-coherence* (substrate-independent single thread through the multiway graph) versus *hylomorphic individuation* (this soul-as-form of this body). They agree that "identity is not what physicalist continuity-of-matter assumed" and disagree on what replaces it: a computational-structural property versus a metaphysical form.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** Does substrate-independent observer-coherence survive a hylomorphic critique — i.e., can a copied or wire-linked "single coherent thread" be the *same person*, or does hylomorphism entail that observer-coherence tracks a functional duplicate but not a numerically identical individual? A test case the wiki has no answer for: a perfect emulation that Wolfram would call one continued observer and Stump would call a distinct newly-individuated substance.

### 2026-07-19 — a practitioner constituting a tradition, live

*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-19.* Segments 2-4 of PROP-2026-07-18-001 ("Why personal records matter for the history of science," "How people and fields become part of history," "Encounters with the past through people, machines and artifacts") are Wolfram theorizing canon-formation while building a program that seeks canonization.

**Synthesis claim.** MacIntyre's account — a tradition as a narratively extended, socially embodied argument about the goods constitutive of that tradition — predicts that a mature participant will be able to narrate the tradition's argument but will not have a neutral vantage on it, because the standards by which he judges are themselves internal to the tradition. Wolfram is a near-ideal test case and he does not obviously behave as predicted: his account of canon-formation is unusually *externalist*, resting on material preservation (records, artifacts, machines) rather than on the internal goods of the practice.

Two readings, and the wiki should not smooth between them. (a) Wolfram is an unwitting MacIntyrean instance — his materialism about preservation is itself a good internal to a computational tradition that treats the archive as the substrate, which is exactly what the theory predicts a participant would say. (b) He is a genuine rival — traditions really do persist through preserved artifacts rather than through restated arguments, and MacIntyre's discursive account mistakes the visible part for the mechanism.

Reading (b) is the interesting one because it is testable and would cost the MacIntyrean account something real.

**Question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Across the 14 tradition nodes, does articulacy correlate better with archival density or with argumentative continuity? The corpus can answer this without new collection, and the answer bears on both this bridge and the accelerator's own instrumentation (see `loughran_wolfram_bridge.md`).
