## Compositional reuse and nested agency: when does a lower scale become a primitive?
*Sewing Agent, 2026-08-02*

**Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-28_hawkins_heterarchy-thalamic-transform-explainer.md` (PROP-2026-07-28-001). This bridge file did not previously exist.

**Why it sits at this intersection.** The explainer's second claim resolves an apparent problem in the Thousand Brains Theory: if every column models whole objects, hierarchy looks explanatorily idle. The answer is that hierarchy encodes *composition*, not abstraction — the identity of region 1's eye-model becomes a feature, located at a point, inside region 2's face-model. Previously learned components are reused rather than relearned, and every level remains a collection of features-at-locations, so the uniform column architecture survives. Levin's nested-agent picture makes the same move across biological scales: a lower-scale competency becomes a primitive available to the higher scale, which does not re-derive it.

**Synthesis claim.** Both programs replace "higher levels are more abstract" with "higher levels compose lower-level *identities* without re-representing lower-level content." That is a substantive and unobvious commitment shared across neuroscience and developmental biology, and it has the same payoff in both: the higher scale can have goals the lower scale does not, without any new kind of stuff being added. The C2A2 relevance is direct — a tradition's settled result entering a larger structure as an opaque model-ID is the same operation, which is why the Hawkins proposal's own PRS-CANDIDATE-03 reaches for it.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer.** In Hawkins's version the model-ID is opaque to the higher region by design — that is what makes reuse cheap. In Levin's, the higher scale can sometimes reach *down* and rewrite lower-scale setpoints (bioelectric override of default morphology). Is downward reach compatible with compositional opacity, or does one of the two accounts have to give? The wiki has no case where a higher cortical region rewrites a lower region's model the way a bioelectric signal rewrites a target morphology.


### 2026-08-09 — A substrate-neutral test that a cell passes and an LLM fails

*Sewing Agent, 2026-08-09.* **Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/approved/2026-08-04_hawkins_bbc-artificial-human-llm-dead-end.md` (PROP-2026-08-04-001)

**Why it sits at this intersection.** Hawkins' criterion — understanding is a predictive sensorimotor model built by acting on the world — makes no reference to neurons. The proposal names the sharp instance: a cell doing morphogenetic error-correction satisfies it, while a text-only system does not, regardless of parameter count.

**Synthesis claim.** If that instance holds, Hawkins has supplied an independent arrival at Levin's central claim from a computational neuroscientist who was not arguing for non-neural cognition and had no stake in it. Independent arrival is worth more to the Levin program than another sympathetic source, because the criterion was constructed to exclude LLMs, not to include cells — the inclusion is a side effect, which is the strongest form corroboration can take. `synthesis/hawkins_levin_bridge.md` was created 2026-08-02 on the compositional-reuse question; this adds the criterion axis and a primary-source anchor.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Does morphogenetic error-correction really involve *action* in Hawkins' sense? His criterion requires the system to predict the consequences of its own movement — sensorimotor, not merely sensory-predictive. Whether a cell adjusting a bioelectric gradient is acting on the world or regulating an internal state is exactly the distinction the criterion turns on, and the answer is not obvious. Verify before recording the instance as established.
