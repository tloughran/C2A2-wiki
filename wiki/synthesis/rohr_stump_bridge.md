# Rohr x Stump — Change Effected by Being Regarded

*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-19*

## The compassionate gaze as a second-personal mechanism

**Orphaned page at the intersection:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-19_rohr_practicing-just-this-weekly-summary.md` (PROP-2026-07-19-002).

**Synthesis claim.** Rohr's account of how negative motivation is dissolved is grammatically odd and the oddity is the content: the motivation is not fought, resisted, or reframed — it is *exposed under a compassionate gaze* and falls away as counterproductive. The transformative agent is a regard, not a technique. That is Stump's second-personal relation with the metaphysics removed, and it makes a prediction a first-personal attentional account does not: self-administered non-judgmental attention should be *less* effective than being so regarded by another, because on the second-personal reading the gaze must be genuinely other to do its work.

This is the point at which the contemplative literature's habitual first-person framing may be obscuring its own mechanism. Rohr's own texts oscillate — sometimes the practitioner adopts the compassionate gaze toward their own contents, sometimes they are held in a gaze that is not theirs. Stump's apparatus (from *Wandering in Darkness*) is built precisely to distinguish these, and the distinction is not available in the psychological vocabulary the practice literature otherwise uses.

**Question the wiki cannot yet answer.** DesCamp's twenty-three-hours criterion (PRS-CANDIDATE-A, High confidence) gives an externalized, third-party-observable measure of practice efficacy. Does that measure discriminate between solitary practice and practice within a directed relationship — spiritual direction, confession, a formation community? If second-personal regard is the mechanism rather than the setting, the difference should be large and it should show up in behaviour rather than in report. No one appears to have run the comparison.

## Faith as second-personal, at Scripture-scale
*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-26*

**Orphaned page at the intersection:** `2026-07-26_rohr_in-love-with-scripture.md` (PROP-2026-07-26-001), PRS candidate `revelation-as-mutual-self-disclosure`.

**Why it sits here:** Rohr recasts biblical revelation as 'mutual exchange of being,' with the Risen Christ revealing 'the human wounds of God' first and thereby inviting reciprocal self-disclosure. Stump's second-personal knowledge (*Wandering in Darkness*) is the same structure — knowledge of a person given only in personal presence, not propositional assent.

**Synthesis claim:** Rohr supplies a *mechanism of initiation* Stump's account tends to leave abstract: second-personal knowledge of God is bootstrapped by the other's prior self-disclosure of woundedness, which lowers the barrier ('emptiness') across which mutual knowing can occur.

**Open question:** If second-personal knowledge requires the known person's self-disclosure to initiate it, what is the epistemic status of a tradition's members who receive that disclosure only mediately, through the text and the community, rather than directly?
