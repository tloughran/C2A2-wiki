## Why these sit at the intersection

The Good, the Beautiful, and the True *are* the classical transcendentals of the Thomistic tradition: for Aquinas, being is convertible with the true and the good (*ens, verum, bonum*), and beauty is closely allied (*id quod visum placet*). So McGilchrist's neuroscience-of-attention lecture lands directly on Stump's home metaphysical territory.

- **Stump (Thomistic):** the transcendentals are features of *being itself* — every being is, qua being, true and good; value is not added to a value-neutral substrate but co-given with existence.
- **McGilchrist (hemispheric):** the transcendentals are *disclosed* by a mode of attention (right-hemisphere, whole-attending, embodied) and *occluded* by another (left-hemisphere, manipulative, abstracting); modernity's pathology is the systematic suppression of the disclosing mode.

Together they give a two-level account of value-realism: *what value is* (Stump: convertible with being) and *how value becomes available to a knower* (McGilchrist: through a specific attentional stance). McGilchrist supplies the epistemology/phenomenology of access; Stump supplies the ontology of what is accessed.

## Synthesis claim

**McGilchrist and Stump converge on value-realism from the epistemic and the ontological sides respectively: the Good, the Beautiful, and the True are real features of being (Stump's Thomistic convertibility) that become available to a knower only through a particular mode of attention (McGilchrist's right-hemisphere disclosure). The transcendentals are thus *ontologically real and disclosure-dependent* — neither projected onto a value-neutral world (the left-hemisphere/modern error) nor existing only in the act of attention. This pairing equips the C2A2 network with a value-realism that is simultaneously metaphysically grounded (so values are not arbitrary) and attentionally conditioned (so the failure to perceive value is a real, diagnosable pathology rather than a mere preference) — and it names the transcendentals as candidate *shared goods* across traditions, a possible common ground for inter-tradition dialogue (the "second-first-language" pedagogy).**

## Open question the wiki does not yet have an answer to

If the transcendentals are real-in-being (Stump) but disclosed-only-through-right-hemisphere-attention (McGilchrist), **does a tradition or agent with an impaired or differently-configured attentional stance thereby have impaired *access to truth and goodness themselves*** — not merely different opinions about them? This sharpens a hard problem for the C2A2 connectome's premise: if perceiving the Good is attention-dependent and attention is unevenly distributed across persons and traditions (and across human vs. AI agents), then inter-tradition dialogue is not a level exchange of views but an exchange among parties with unequal access to the value-structure of reality. The wiki does not yet say whether the transcendentals-as-shared-goods can function as common ground *if* access to them is attentionally gated.
