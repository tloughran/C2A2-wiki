## A McGilchrist claim Fredrickson can test
*Sewing Agent, 2026-09-06*

**Orphaned page at the intersection (0 backlinks before this run):** `2026-09-02_mcgilchrist_can-you-still-be-human` (McGilchrist 0.9, Fredrickson 0.55).

**Why it sits here:** The essay asserts that automation-driven unemployment produces meaninglessness and despair rather than leisure. That is a flourishing claim with an existing empirical literature on unemployment and wellbeing, so for once Fredrickson's program can test a McGilchrist assertion instead of resonating with it.

**Synthesis claim:** Most Fredrickson-McGilchrist contact in the wiki is metaphorical (broaden-and-build against right-hemisphere openness). This is the first contact where one side makes a checkable empirical claim in the other's domain. If the unemployment literature supports him, the political register gains an evidential leg; if it is mixed (as it is likely to be, depending on income replacement and social role), the essay's claim needs a qualifier the tradition should carry.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** Does the wellbeing cost of unemployment survive when income is replaced and work is not? That is the counterfactual the essay implicitly denies, and the answer decides whether "leisure" was ever the live alternative.
