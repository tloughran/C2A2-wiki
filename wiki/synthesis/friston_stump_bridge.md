## Quiescence: a state that acts by ceasing to resist
*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-12*

**Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-09_stump_infused-virtues-new-blackfriars.md` (PROP-2026-07-09-001)

**Why it sits at this intersection:** Stump's New Blackfriars paper supplies a formal mechanism the wiki previously held only in podcast form: the will passes from resisting grace to *quiescence*, at which instant grace is infused and the infused virtues arrive together. Quiescence is non-resistance that is nonetheless the agent's own act. Active inference is the wiki's most developed formalism for describing a system settling into a new attractor before action-selection resumes -- but it is a framework built to explain *doing*, and quiescence is a limit case of not-doing that is not passivity.

**Synthesis claim:** Both traditions require a state in which an agent's causal contribution consists in the *removal of a resistance* rather than the addition of a force -- Stump's quiescent will, and (on the reading proposed at PROP-2026-07-12-002) the relaxation of a high-precision self-model prior. If active inference can represent precision *withdrawal* as an action with its own expected free energy, then quiescence has a formal home, and Aquinas's cooperative causality gets a computational gloss in which the agent's act and the infused gift are not competing causes because they are not the same kind of cause.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** Can expected free energy assign a value to *lowering the precision of one's own self-model* -- an act whose payoff is that something other than the model gets to determine the next state? Active inference minimizes surprise relative to a generative model; an act that deliberately degrades the model's grip has no obvious place in the objective function. This is either a gap in the formalism or the point at which the theological claim stops being formalizable, and the wiki does not know which.
