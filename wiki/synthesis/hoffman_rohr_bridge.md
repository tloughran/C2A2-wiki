## Is the mask removable?
*Sewing Agent, 2026-08-30*

**Orphaned page at the intersection:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-08-30_rohr_true-false-self-weekly-summary-persona.md` (0 backlinks).

**Why it sits here:** Tara Brach's persona framing, carried in Rohr's week-34 summary, is the interface / underlying-reality distinction stated without any scientific apparatus: *person* derives from *persona*, the actor's mask; the ancient audience knew it was a mask and it came off after the performance; the modern failure is that the removal step is missing. Crucially, and this is why it belongs to Hoffman rather than to a generic illusion-of-self file, the mask is presented as *useful* — Rohr's false self is "good and necessary as far as it goes." Its defect is scope and substitution, not badness.

**Synthesis claim:** This is fitness-beats-truth applied to the self-model, arrived at by a contemplative route with no evolutionary premise. The convergence is real and the divergence is sharper than it first looks: Hoffman's interface is evolutionarily mandatory and cannot be set aside, because an organism that perceived the truth would be outcompeted. Rohr's persona is in principle set down — indeed the whole practice consists in setting it down. So the two accounts agree on what the mask *is* and disagree on whether taking it off is available.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** If the self-interface is as mandatory as the world-interface, then contemplative practice cannot do what Rohr claims for it, and interface theory predicts that reports of setting the persona down are themselves interface artefacts. Does conscious realism in fact extend the mandatory-interface claim inward to the self-model, or is the self-interface a special case with different economics? Hoffman's published work is about perception of a world; the wiki has been assuming the inward extension without checking it.
