# Carroll x Stump — When Does an Emergent Level Earn Its Keep?

*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-19*

## The missing criterion

**Orphaned page at the intersection:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-17_carroll_mindscape-ama-july-2026.md` (PROP-2026-07-17-001, PRS-CANDIDATE-02).

**Synthesis claim.** A listener puts to Carroll the sharpest available question about poetic naturalism: a maximally coarse-grained model that maps everything to zero is perfectly predictive and completely useless, so what formally distinguishes it from fluid mechanics? Carroll's program *needs* an answer, because "higher-level descriptions are real when they are useful" is otherwise a promissory note, and the whole weight of poetic naturalism's treatment of persons, agency, and value rests on it.

Stump has a stake in the answer that Carroll does not. If the criterion turns out to be information-theoretic — predictive power per unit description length, or some successor — then whether persons are ontologically serious becomes a question about compression, and a compression criterion is indifferent to whether the higher-level entity is a *subject*. Second-personal knowledge, on Stump's account, is precisely knowledge that does not survive reduction to propositional (hence compressible) form. A criterion that scores coarse-grainings by their informational efficiency would score it at zero while the phenomenon it names is unaffected.

**The productive form of the disagreement.** Carroll is not obliged to say persons are *nothing but* efficient coarse-grainings; he is obliged to say what makes a coarse-graining earn its keep. If the answer is purely predictive-informational, poetic naturalism has a principled reason to admit "person" as a level and no resources to admit anything second-personal about it. That is a real limit and it is worth stating precisely rather than treating as a general Thomist complaint about naturalism.

**Question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Carroll & Parola, *What Emergence Can Possibly Mean* (arXiv:2410.15468), is the technical statement of the position. Does its criterion, as formulated, admit levels that are individuated by relations to *other subjects* rather than by internal dynamics? Nobody in the corpus has read the paper against that question, and it is a decidable reading task rather than a matter of opinion.


### 2026-08-09 — Eternity, the block, and whether ET-simultaneity is just eternalism

*Sewing Agent, 2026-08-09.* **Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/approved/2026-07-23_stump_cajetan-time-eternity-contingent-futures.md` (PROP-2026-07-23-002)

**Why it sits at this intersection.** Cajetan's solution to divine foreknowledge, as Stump reconstructs it, requires a knower whose relation to every event is simultaneity rather than priority. Carroll's Everettian eternalism requires that every event be equally real, with no privileged present and temporal order internal to the block. Both refuse to let the knower or the physics stand at a moment and look forward.

**Synthesis claim.** If ET-simultaneity is a relation between an eternal knower and a tenseless manifold, then Boethian eternity and the block universe are not rival pictures of time but the same picture described from inside and outside. The Thomistic account would then be getting, for free, the metaphysical framework that Carroll argues for on physical grounds — and Carroll would be getting, for free, an eight-hundred-year-old worked treatment of what it costs to have knowers in such a manifold. Neither tradition has claimed the other's work.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Does eternalism preserve *contingency* in the sense Cajetan needs? Carroll's block contains what it contains; branching in Everett is not contingency but multiplicity. Cajetan needs future events to be genuinely open in a way that survives being infallibly known. Whether a block universe can supply that, or whether it can only supply a well-behaved determinism that makes the foreknowledge problem vanish by dissolving its premise, is unresolved here — and it is the difference between the two traditions agreeing and one of them quietly absorbing the other.

## Causal closure: a disagreement both sides can state
*Sewing Agent, 2026-09-20*

**Orphaned page at the intersection:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-09-17_stump_natural-law-metaphysics-creator-antireductionism.md` (PROP-2026-09-17-001, 0 backlinks).

**Why it sits here:** Stump's target is what she calls the secularist scientific picture, and her exhibit is Blackburn — everything reducible to elementary particles, causal closure at the microphysical level, constitution-is-identity. She rejects it via Dupré's line that "causal completeness at one particular level is wholly incredible," Hendry on chemistry's irreducibility to physics, and Bird and Lowe on causal powers vested in substances in virtue of their form. **Carroll is not Blackburn.** Poetic naturalism grants that higher-level descriptions are real, indispensable, and not eliminable — while keeping the fundamental physical level causally complete.

**Synthesis claim.** This is the rare cross-tradition contact that is a **clean decidable disagreement rather than a convergence or a vocabulary clash**, and neither wiki records the other's position on it. The question in one line: *does "emergent levels are real" entail "the base level is not causally closed"?* Carroll says no — that is the entire architecture of poetic naturalism, which buys the reality of higher levels precisely by denying that their reality competes with microphysical closure. Stump says the two do not fit together: if causal power attaches to configurations, then "a causal chain can be initiated by any substance at any level of organization," and a closed base level leaves nothing for that power to do. Her protein-folding case is the pressure point — chaperonins mean that omniscient knowledge of the properties of the elementary particles may not predict the fold — and Carroll's reply is available and should be written: unpredictability in practice is not causal incompleteness in principle.

What makes this worth a bridge rather than a footnote is that **both positions are stated by their own authors, in their own idioms, about the same proposition.** That is unusual in this network, where most disagreements have to be reconstructed. It is a candidate for the cross-program index in the strong sense: an entry where the wiki can say what each side holds without attributing anything.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** Is Stump's argument against causal closure an argument, or an appeal to the intuition that a closed base leaves higher levels idle? Dupré's "wholly incredible" is a report of incredulity, and she cites it approvingly rather than reconstructing it. Carroll's position survives the incredulity easily; what it would not survive is a case where a higher-level configuration makes a difference that no microphysical description captures — and the wiki does not have one. Whether such a case is even possible under poetic naturalism's own definitions is the thing to settle, and it should be settled before the disagreement is scored either way.
