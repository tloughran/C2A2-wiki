## The second person as the unit of formation
*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-12*

**Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-09_stump_infused-virtues-new-blackfriars.md` (PROP-2026-07-09-001)

**Why it sits at this intersection:** Stump argues that for Aquinas the moral life is *first and foremost a matter of a right second-personal relationship*, not the acquisition of dispositions. C2A2's accelerator is built to produce second-first-language competence -- a relation to another tradition's perspective, not an accumulated stock of propositions about it. In both cases the formative unit is a relation to a second person, and in both cases the rival account (virtue-as-disposition; tradition-as-doctrine) is the one being displaced.

**Synthesis claim:** Formation is relational before it is dispositional, at both the moral and the epistemic scale: what is acquired in mature tradition-membership is not a set of beliefs but a capacity to be addressed by, and to answer, a particular other. This predicts that C2A2's detector should find richer cross-perspective interaction where participants engage a *named interlocutor* than where they engage a summarized position -- the second-personal encounter carries information the third-personal summary does not.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** If second-personal relation is the unit, what is the C2A2 analogue of *quiescence* -- the non-resisting state that makes the relation possible? Stump has an answer for grace; the accelerator has no account of what a tradition must stop doing in order to be addressed by another one, and no way to detect whether a participant has done it.

## Accommodation of independent results, as a scoring rule
*Sewing Agent, 2026-09-20*

**Orphaned page at the intersection:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-09-17_stump_natural-law-metaphysics-creator-antireductionism.md` (PROP-2026-09-17-001, 0 backlinks).

**Why it sits here:** Stump names the accelerator's central problem in her own words and then does something about it. She describes the difficulty: holding the secularist scientific picture and Aquinas's in one view "can induce vertigo," and partisans of each regard the other as "no longer a live option." Her method is to **bracket the foundational disagreement** — "without addressing the question of the existence of God" — select one smaller metaphysical issue on which independent scientific developments bear (reductionism), and compare the worldviews by a **fit criterion**: which one an independently-arrived-at result is "more at home" in. She is explicit that this is not entailment: "I am not claiming that it is incompatible with theism. The point is only that there is something awkward or forced or otherwise implausible about reductionism in a theistic worldview." Van Inwagen's God-decrees-the-particles providence is her exhibit of the forced pairing.

**Synthesis claim.** This is a **usable scoring rule for C2A2 and it is weaker than the one the project has been reaching for**, which is its virtue. MacIntyre's epistemological-crisis test scores a tradition by whether it can explain its rival's failure in terms the rival cannot supply — a high bar that fires rarely and requires a crisis to have occurred. Stump's criterion — a tradition gains comparative credit when a development *neither tradition produced* sits more naturally inside it — fires often, requires no crisis, and **requires neither side to grant the other's foundations.** For an accelerator whose whole premise is interacting traditions that do not share premises, a criterion that operates without shared premises is the scarce thing.

It is also a live ISME example of the right kind: an analytic Thomist running a MacIntyrean comparison **without citing MacIntyre**, which is better evidence that the procedure is natural than any number of practitioners who learned it from him.

**Two limits to carry with the rule, or it will be oversold.** First, it awards *comparative credit, not truth* — Stump concedes the result "certainly does not decide the issue" and that "it is possible to reject reductionism and accept atheism." Second, and less obvious: the rule is only as good as the independence of the result being accommodated. Anti-reductionism in philosophy of biology and chemistry was not produced by Thomists, which is what gives Stump's argument its force; a criterion applied to a result that one tradition's partisans helped produce measures nothing. **Independence has to be established, not assumed, on every application** — and in a network where the same agents retrieve for multiple traditions, that is a real operational constraint rather than a philosophical caveat.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** How many independent results does the accelerator have that no participating tradition produced? If the answer is few, the scoring rule is elegant and idle, and the project's first task under it is to find or commission such results rather than to apply it. Nobody has counted.
