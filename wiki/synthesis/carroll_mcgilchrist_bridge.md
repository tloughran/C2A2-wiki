## Two attentional modes, arrived at without hemispheres
*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-12*

**Orphaned page:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-10_carroll_mindscape-360-berman-nature-cognition.md` (PROP-2026-07-10-001)

**Why it sits at this intersection:** Berman (on Mindscape 360) operationalizes Kaplan's Attention Restoration Theory: a *depletable* directed attention (narrow, effortful, top-down) versus a *non-depletable* involuntary attention (broad, receptive, engaged by 'soft fascination'), with nature restoring the former by engaging the latter -- a ~20% backwards-digit-span gain vs. urban walks, replicated across seasons and in clinical depression. McGilchrist's two attentional modes have the same functional profile. Berman reaches them from within-subject experimental psychology, with no hemispheric commitments whatsoever.

**Synthesis claim:** The attention *typology* is separable from the lateralization *thesis*, and the typology is the part carrying the empirical support. Berman's result is convergent evidence for McGilchrist's phenomenology of attention -- a grasping, resource-consuming mode and an open, world-receptive mode that restores it -- while remaining entirely neutral on whether these modes map onto hemispheres. This is a gain for McGilchrist and a constraint on him at once: the cultural diagnosis survives, but it no longer needs (and no longer gets support from) the neuroanatomy.

**Open question the wiki cannot yet answer:** Is the restorative mode restorative *because* it is right-hemispheric, or is the hemispheric claim idle with respect to every prediction the wiki can test? Nothing in the C2A2 corpus distinguishes 'two modes of attention, differently lateralized' from 'two modes of attention, full stop' -- and if no available evidence does, the wiki should say so rather than let the lateralization ride free on the typology's credit.
