# Loughran x Rohr — Externalized Criteria as an Intake Requirement

*Sewing Agent, 2026-07-19*

## The twenty-three hours

**Orphaned page at the intersection:** `inbox/proposals/pending/2026-07-19_rohr_practicing-just-this-weekly-summary.md` (PROP-2026-07-19-002, PRS-CANDIDATE-A, rated High confidence).

**Synthesis claim.** Therese DesCamp relocates the test of contemplative practice from what is experienced during the sit to conduct across the remaining twenty-three hours, and refuses every instrumental justification (acuity, blood pressure, stress, being a better Christian) as byproduct rather than aim. The methodological consequence is larger than the devotional one: it converts a claim that was assessable only by privileged first-person report into one assessable by third-party behavioural observation, without weakening it and without naturalizing it.

That is the form every tradition's claims must take to be admissible as evidence in the accelerator's comparison design. A tradition that can only certify its own results from the inside cannot be compared with another that does the same; two traditions each self-certifying produce no evidence about their interaction, which is the phenomenon C2A2 exists to measure. Rohr's tradition has here volunteered an external criterion unprompted, which is rarer than it should be.

**Proposed intake criterion (for Tom's ruling).** Require of each tradition's central practice claims a stated externalization: what would a third party observe, over what interval, that distinguishes a practitioner from a non-practitioner? Traditions that cannot supply one are not thereby wrong, but their claims enter the corpus as testimony rather than as evidence, and should be tagged as such. The Beatitudes proposal from the same week (PROP-2026-07-19-001) independently supplies a second instance — the descriptive-not-imperative reading treats the Beatitude cluster as an observable *outcome profile* of a formation tradition, which is the same move.

**Question the wiki cannot yet answer.** Two Rohr proposals in one week converge on outcome-profile-as-evidence. Is that a genuine feature of the contemplative traditions — that they are unusually willing to be judged by fruits — or an artifact of CAC's current editorial arc? The distinction determines whether the intake criterion is a fair ask or one that will silently privilege one tradition family.
