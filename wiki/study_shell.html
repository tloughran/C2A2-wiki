About this page

What you are reading

This is a preliminary, preregistered study from the C2A2 (Community Context for AI Alignment) project. It tests a wager against pessimism in regard to the fulfillment of Alasdair MacIntyre’s recovery project: that AI agents, seeded on the writings of human traditions, can carry out deep cross-tradition dialogue at machine speed — and, crucially, that we can build instruments to tell whether real understanding occurred rather than fluent, agreeable-sounding noise.

It is organised as a ladder of controls: at each rung there is an easy way to get a false positive (shared topic, politeness, paraphrase), and a control an artifact would fail. The climax is a civil convener running a certification protocol in which each speaker is the sole judge of whether their own view has been captured — the study’s “GANification” idea: a fast generator of dialogue paired with a strict detector of genuine uptake.

Status: load-bearing results (P1, P2, the BRIDGE negative, the C0 certification gate, P3′a) are robust; the thesis-bearing prediction that the limit of understanding is located (P3′b) is directional but underpowered and awaits the strengthened panel described in §5.3. Every dialogue, the preregistration, and the analysis scripts are retained so the study reproduces from the transcripts alone.

Two bodies of dialogue — read this before the numbers

Almost every misreading of this work comes from running two separate collections together, so they are worth separating up front.

The observed corpus is real transcripts, recorded as one researcher worked with an AI coding assistant — a by-product of ordinary work that nobody staged. The constructed corpus is dialogue the study built: two agents seeded on rival thinkers, arguing across a chosen seam, sometimes with a third agent convening.

Every result rests on the constructed corpus. All seven preregistered predictions are tested there, against a control that is also constructed (DEAF, where one agent cannot read the other). The observed corpus supplies no result. It does two other jobs: it is why the constructed corpus exists — the debate moves the theory cares about turned out to be absent from real working dialogue — and it is the external check that the instrument finds listening in conversation nobody designed for it to find listening in.

One label collides. The study calls two different things “Rung 1”: the measurement on the observed corpus (§3.2) and the LISTEN-versus-DEAF test on constructed dialogue (§4.1). The Replication tab concerns only the first, and does not bear on the second. Their figures are not comparable and should not be lined up.

No dialogue anywhere in this research is between two humans: the observed corpus is human-to-assistant, the constructed corpus agent-to-agent.

Two documents. The tabs at the top of this page hold the original study and a later replication. The study is preserved exactly as preregistered and published; the replication is a separate paper that re-runs one arm of it on a larger corpus. Nothing measured after publication has been folded back into the study body — that separation is the point.
