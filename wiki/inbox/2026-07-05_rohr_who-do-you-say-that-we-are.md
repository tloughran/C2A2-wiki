## Summary
In this Rohr-curated meditation (July 3), womanist theologian Diana L. Hayes performs a deliberate grammatical shift on Mark 8:29 — from Jesus's "Who do you say that I am?" to the community's "Who do you, God, say that we are?" Identity is answered corporately and by divine address: "children of God" across every racial and religious division, called to "kin-dom" rather than kingdom. Hayes adds an epistemic claim: those who have suffered most and survived hold privileged knowledge of the answer, and the removal of "isms" is described as removing self-imposed blinders that restrict vision of God's light in every face — an anti-dualistic perception argument, not merely a moral exhortation.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Two moves not yet in the rohr wiki: (1) the first-person-plural reframe of the central identity question — individuation by God's address to a community rather than to a lone self — and (2) prejudice modeled as perceptual restriction (blinders) whose removal is a contemplative act. Both extend the non-dual frame from individual awareness to corporate identity, which is exactly the scale the C2A2 network operates at. Citable for Summa syntheses on the image of God (I Q.93), the Mystical Body, and charity toward neighbor.
