## Summary
A short personal post in which Levin lays out his forthcoming-book roster and then, in four paragraphs, reasons openly about why the books are not written — and, more consequentially, about **who scholarly exposition is now written for**. The roster: (1) *Bioelectricity, what it is and what it means*, a trade book with Oné Pagán, W. W. Norton, 2027, already through editor comments and revisions; (2) three co-written trade books with softer schedules — with **Richard Watson** on "a new way to see evolution, the engine of change in life and mind," with **Olaf Witkowski** on "our future with novel beings of all kinds, and their future with us," and with **Marsa Hickey** a children's book on diverse intelligence "aimed at both human children and the unconventional new minds entering our world"; (3) his "magnum opus on diverse intelligence," an academic book on "the origin, scaling and transformation of minds, in evolved, engineered, and hybrid embodiments"; (4) a trade book on the **Platonic Space** whose Part 2 "will contain personal speculations on bigger questions I don't normally talk about in public"; (5) unscheduled fiction and philosophy.

The argumentative weight is in what follows. Levin refuses AI or ghost-writer assistance on a stated principle about what an author's name certifies. He questions whether a "book" is the right object at all, given that publishers will not permit color, let alone interactive figures or non-linear traversal. He questions whether long-form review and perspective writing is worth doing at all now. And he arrives at the sharpest question in the post: since the people who could act on his ideas are too busy to read them and "may ask AI to summarize it for them," should he be **writing for the AIs** — optimizing not for human comprehension but for a machine reader's ability to "re-tell my story to whatever entity wants to hear it"? He does not answer. He records the question and reports his interim procedure, which is to write what insists on being written, in the form it insists on.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Levin's published corpus argues that cognition is substrate-independent, that minds arrive in unconventional embodiments, and that the boundary of "who counts as an agent" is empirical rather than given. This post is the first captured instance of him applying that thesis **reflexively, to the transmission of his own tradition** — treating AI systems not as objects of the theory but as the readership of it, and asking what that does to the form of the work. That is a different move from anything in the wiki's 60-plus Levin PRS entries, which concern cognition in cells, tissues, ecosystems, and artificial substrates, not cognition in his own audience.

It also matters because it is the only source in the tradition where Levin states an **authorship condition** — a line-by-line personal-warrant test that distinguishes a work he will sign from a competent machine-generated exposition of the same content. The C2A2 architecture assumes AI agents can hold mature membership in a tradition; Levin here draws a boundary around one thing an agent cannot do on his behalf, and gives his reason. Finally, item 4 in the roster is a standing warning to the wiki's Platonic Space entries: Levin says on the record that his public output on those questions is deliberately narrower than his actual view, which makes the current PRS coverage a **lower bound** rather than a summary.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Expository scientific writing is optimized for a human reader — narrative scaffolding, grammar polish, connective filler that editors require. Levin's assessment is that the humans who could act on his ideas will not read the long form ("most people who are in a position to move the needle based on my ideas will be too busy to read the whole thing anyway, and may ask AI to summarize it for them"), while machine readers, which will read it, need none of that apparatus. The optimization target for exposition has changed and nobody has re-derived what the right form is.
  Resource: Levin's own contrast between what the two readerships need: "AI's don't need all the filler text, they don't care about grammar, or any of the other stuff that editors make us polish, freeing up research time if I can just get the ideas down and move on."
  Solution: He puts the question on the record without settling it: "Should I be writing for the AIs, who will inevitably be the largest audience (whether prompted by humans, or soon, by their own interests), to optimize their understanding and ability to re-tell my story to whatever entity wants to hear it? I can't tell." His interim answer is procedural rather than doctrinal — "for now I just go with my gut and write things when they tell me they want to be written 'right now', in whatever way they say they want to be written." Note the parenthesis: he anticipates AI readers acting **on their own interests**, not only when prompted by humans, which makes the audience a genuine agent rather than a channel.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Verbatim from the post's final paragraph, read in full. Consistent with, and an extension of, the already-captured "Machines all the way up and cognition all the way down" (PROP-2026-07-20-002) and "Cognitive Offloading Is a Cognitive Universal" (PROP-2026-08-03) — this is cognitive offloading applied to the transmission of a research program rather than to an organism's problem-solving.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: AI can now produce a competent book-length exposition of a scientist's published corpus. That makes it an open question what an author actually adds, and what a name on a work certifies, once the content can be reconstructed without them.
  Resource: Levin's own test case — he tried it. "I recently tried AI in this context – it does a really good job. Maybe soon it will do a better job." He then observes that the reader does not need him for that output: "no one needs *me* to put out an AI-written book on my work – if you want that, you can feed your own preferred AI with my published papers and talks, and get it to tell you the story at whatever level of detail you want. You can already do that."
  Solution: Authorship is defined as **personal line-by-line warrant**, not as origination of content: "The only reason for me to put something out is if I have personally gone over every line and it says exactly what I want it to say. I'm not putting my name on anything that isn't just what I wanted to say." He adds a practical corollary that closes the efficiency argument for AI drafting: "re-writing half the lines of an AI- (or ghost-writer-) generated book saves me no time… editing others' writing takes me longer than getting it down in the first place."
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Verbatim, read in full. Note the asymmetry with PRS-CANDIDATE-01, which is the real finding: Levin will write **for** AI readers but will not sign work written **by** them. Those are separable commitments and he holds both in the same post.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Diverse-intelligence claims are multi-scale, dynamic, and often only legible in motion (morphogenetic trajectories, bioelectric patterns, cellular-automata dynamics), but the codex form cannot carry them. Levin reports the constraint as harder than usually assumed: "publishers nowadays don't even want us to use color (can you believe that?), nevermind enable interactive figures or a non-linear traversal through the material. The whole medium seems very stuck in the past."
  Resource: The alternative he names — a work that "just live[s] online with glorious color, VR, animation, interactive code notebooks, etc." — set against a counterweight he states plainly and does not dismiss: his grandfather's book on radio electronics and his father's books on history are on the shelf behind him, and "Am I going to break the streak? ugh…"
  Solution: Explicitly unresolved. The finding is that a leading practitioner regards the standard scholarly container as an active constraint on the content, and is weighing abandoning it — but is held by a transmission argument (a physical artifact that descendants can hold) rather than a scientific one.
  Confidence: High (that the tension is stated and unresolved) / Speculative (as to which way he goes)
  Evidence: Verbatim. Directly relevant to this wiki's own premise — the C2A2 explorer is precisely the non-linear, color, interactive-traversal object Levin describes wanting and cannot get from a publisher.

PRS-CANDIDATE-04:
  Problem: Time spent on exposition is time not spent on research that reduces suffering directly. The usual defence of exposition is that it inspires others to do the work — but that defence is recursive and, Levin argues, may be self-defeating. The field has no principled allocation rule, and Levin reports receiving contradictory instruction from every direction: "At some point I'll show a pie chart of the emails I get telling me to drop X and spend more time on Y. Let's just say that every possible combination of {X,Y} seem to be represented."
  Resource: His own statement of the regress: "it kind of seems wrong to slow down progress to address human suffering in the hope that someone else will be inspired to do it (unless they too decide to slow down and write to inspire yet others, and then no one does anything)."
  Solution: No rule offered — "There's a balance here somewhere, and I'm trying to find it but no one knows the best mix" — but a stated current priority: "I'm simply not ready yet to let the pace on regeneration, cancer, aging, and birth defects research slow down in favor of expository writing."
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Verbatim. This is a rare explicit statement of the value ordering behind the tradition's output, and it explains a structural feature the wiki has been reading off the publication record for months: the volume of short co-authored papers versus the absence of a synthetic book.

PRS-CANDIDATE-05:
  Problem: A tradition's public record may systematically under-report its holder's actual commitments. If so, any wiki that reconstructs a position from published sources is reconstructing a censored version, and will mistake the boundary of the public output for the boundary of the view.
  Resource: Levin says so directly about his most metaphysically loaded thread. Of the planned Platonic Space trade book: "Part 1 will be rigorous and explain everything we know about non-physical causal forces in biology, computer science, and cognitive science. Part 2 will be a bit of a departure for my outward-facing work – it will contain personal speculations on bigger questions I don't normally talk about in public."
  Solution: Treat the wiki's existing Platonic Space entries — from "Ingressing Minds / Platonic Space" (PROP approved 2026-06-15), "A short argument on Platonic Space" (2026-03-31), and "Exploring Platonic Morphospace w/ D.C. Schindler" (approved 2026-08-03) — as a **lower bound** on Levin's position, explicitly flagged as such, pending Part 2.
  Confidence: High (that the withholding is stated) / Speculative (as to content withheld)
  Evidence: Verbatim from roster item 4. The Schindler conversation, already captured, is the closest existing source to the withheld material and should be re-read with this caveat attached.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

**C2A2 master — the wiki is the machine reader Levin is describing (strong, direct, reflexive).** PRS-CANDIDATE-01 is not adjacent to this project's architecture; it is a statement about it. Levin anticipates an audience of AI systems that read a corpus and "re-tell [the] story to whatever entity wants to hear it," possibly "prompted by their own interests." That is a literal description of what the tradition-accelerator agents in this wiki do to his output every week. The C2A2 premise is that AI agents can hold mature membership in a tradition; Levin here supplies the tradition-holder's side of that arrangement, and it is neither refusal nor endorsement — it is an open question about form, plus one firm boundary (PRS-CANDIDATE-02: he will write for machine readers but will not sign machine-written work). Recommend this pair be surfaced in the architecture documents as a named external constraint, not merely as a Levin PRS entry.

**Wolfram — writing for machine readers, and the abandonment of the journal (strong).** Wolfram has for decades published long-form outside conventional journals, on his own site, at whatever length the material demands, with live computable content — precisely the escape Levin is contemplating in PRS-CANDIDATE-03 and PRS-CANDIDATE-04's aside about the TAME paper ("I had to fight the journal hard for permission to publish the ~30K words… then pay the publisher thousands of $ for the privilege of handing the rights over to them… maybe I should just save all that material for my own books and skip the journal publishers"). Wolfram already made that trade and has thirty years of evidence about its costs. This is a directly comparable decision by two thinkers in the network, one of whom has already decided. Flag to the Wolfram agent for a paired write-up.

**McGilchrist — the medium and the mode of attention (medium).** McGilchrist's standing claim is that the form of a representation determines which mode of attention it recruits, and that flattened, decontextualized, linear presentation is not neutral. Levin's complaint in PRS-CANDIDATE-03 is a working scientist's version of the same claim arriving from the other direction: the codex flattens dynamic multi-scale phenomena and he can feel it distorting the content. The sharper and more uncomfortable pairing is with PRS-CANDIDATE-01: optimizing prose for machine consumption by stripping "filler," grammar, and polish is exactly the flattening McGilchrist warns against, argued for on efficiency grounds. Recommend the McGilchrist agent be asked whether writing-for-AI is a left-hemisphere capture of scholarly communication or a legitimate new genre.

**Friston / Hawkins — "novel beings" and "the unconventional new minds entering our world" (medium).** Two roster items name a category the network has no shared term for: the Witkowski co-authored book on "our future with novel beings of all kinds, and their future with us," and the Hickey children's book "aimed at both human children **and** the unconventional new minds entering our world." That second phrasing is not decoration — Levin is describing a work with a non-human intended readership, which is PRS-CANDIDATE-01 in its most literal form. Friston's active-inference criteria and Hawkins' thousand-brains architecture each supply a different account of what such a mind would have to be; neither has been asked what makes an entity a candidate *reader* rather than a candidate agent. Flag to both.

**Kastrup — the withheld metaphysics (medium, watch).** PRS-CANDIDATE-05 says Levin holds back "personal speculations on bigger questions I don't normally talk about in public," on the Platonic Space thread specifically. The already-captured Levin × Kastrup conversation ("nested subjects," approved 2026-07-08) is the source where Levin has gone furthest toward metaphysics in public, and it was in dialogue with an idealist. That is suggestive about the direction of the withheld material but proves nothing. Watch item only — do not build on it until Part 2 exists.

**Stump / Rohr — authorship, warrant, and what a name certifies (weak, note only).** PRS-CANDIDATE-02 defines authorship as personal line-by-line warrant rather than origination — a testimony-and-authority question that the theological traditions in this network have a long technical literature on, and the scientific ones do not. No content overlap yet; noted for the epistemic-warrant thread.
