## Summary
McGilchrist delivered a two-day lecture event at the Sheldonian Theatre, Oxford (2–3 May 2026) arguing that the classical transcendentals — the Good, the Beautiful, and the True — are not optional ornaments but constitutive of how reality discloses itself, and that "we dismiss the good, the beautiful and the true at our peril." The format combined lectures, a panel, and audience Q&A, with invited guests Dr Martin Shaw, Prof. Angie Hobbs, Dr Jude Currivan, and David Lorimer. A recording is promised to ticket-holders a few weeks after the event.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is McGilchrist taking his hemispheric/attentional framework explicitly into the territory of the transcendentals — the value-laden structure of reality that the right hemisphere discloses and the left hemisphere cannot generate. It is a flagship public articulation of the normative payload of his program (attention as the ontological act by which the Good/Beautiful/True become available), and an unusually direct bridge from neuroscience-of-attention to classical metaphysics.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: A left-hemisphere-dominant modernity treats the Good, the Beautiful, and the True as subjective overlays on a value-neutral mechanism, leaving no account of how value could be real.
  Resource: McGilchrist's transcendentals-as-disclosed thesis: the Good/Beautiful/True are apprehended by right-hemisphere attention to the living whole and are constitutive of reality rather than projected onto it; developed across the two-day Sheldonian lectures with interlocutors from philosophy (Hobbs), myth (Shaw), and cosmology (Currivan).
  Solution: Positions the transcendentals as ontologically real and attention-dependent in disclosure (not in existence), giving the program an explicit value-realism that connects attentional stance to what can be known as good, beautiful, or true.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Event thesis as stated by the organizers — McGilchrist argues "we dismiss the good, the beautiful and the true at our peril"; framing places the transcendentals at the center of his attention-and-reality account. (Full recording forthcoming; confidence to be upgraded on transcript.)

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: The crisis-of-attention diagnosis risks being heard as purely negative (what modernity destroys) without a positive account of what richer attention makes available.
  Resource: The transcendentals as the positive content of right-hemisphere attention — what is gained, not just what is lost, when attention is rebalanced.
  Solution: Supplies the constructive/positive pole of the hemispheric diagnosis: attention rebalanced toward the right hemisphere yields access to value (Good/Beautiful/True), reframing the program from cultural pathology toward cultural remedy.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Event structure pairs the diagnosis of modern fragmentation with a sustained positive case for the transcendentals.
