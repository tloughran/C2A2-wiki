## Summary
Nicolas Rouleau and Michael Levin take the major theories of consciousness and ask what each one actually requires of its substrate, then test whether those requirements are met by unconventional embodiments (synthetic organisms, cell collectives, non-neural tissue, engineered constructs). Their finding: the operations and functional principles that the leading theories of consciousness describe or predict are remarkably similar to one another, and the near-exclusive focus on brains is driven more by convention and the limits of imagination than by anything specific in the theories themselves. Developmental bioelectricity and synthetic bioengineering reveal pre-neural roots for many brain mechanisms — suggesting minds may, in a real sense, have preceded brains.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Levin's most direct assault yet on "mind blindness" (PRS-12) at the level of consciousness *theory* rather than cognition: it shows that the field's brain-centrism is an unforced convention, not a theoretical necessity. It also converts the diverse-intelligence / SUTI agenda (PRS-25) from an empirical detection program into a claim about the consciousness-theory literature itself, giving Levin's substrate-independence thesis purchase on the consciousness debate where it had mostly held purchase on cognition and goal-directedness.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Theories of consciousness are almost universally formulated and tested against brains, leaving it unclear whether their brain-focus reflects a genuine theoretical requirement or merely disciplinary convention — and therefore whether consciousness could in principle be attributed to non-neural or synthetic embodiments
  Resource: A systematic mapping exercise — extracting the substrate requirements implied by each major theory of consciousness and checking them against unconventional embodiments (synthetic organisms, cell collectives, non-neural tissue)
  Solution: Demonstration that the leading theories' functional principles are remarkably convergent and that their brain-restriction is "driven by convention and limitations of imagination rather than by any specific content of existing theories" — opening consciousness, not just cognition, to substrate-independent attribution
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Rouleau & Levin report that "the operations and functional principles described or predicted by most theories of consciousness are remarkably similar, with the focus on brains being more driven by convention and limitations of imagination than by any specific content of existing theories" (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 384:2320, 2026)

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: The evolutionary origin of consciousness is typically tied to the appearance of nervous systems, leaving no account of how cognitive/conscious mechanisms could exist before brains
  Resource: Evidence from evolutionary biology, developmental bioelectricity, and synthetic bioengineering showing that many mechanisms and algorithms found in brains have ancient pre-neural antecedents in cell-cell bioelectric communication
  Solution: The thesis that "minds may have preceded brains" — bioelectric information-processing provides an evolutionary pathway from physiological homeostasis to cognitive agency, dissolving the assumption that consciousness requires a nervous system as a precondition
  Confidence: Medium-High
  Evidence: The paper argues findings in "developmental bioelectricity and synthetic bioengineering are revealing the ancient pre-neural roots of many mechanisms and algorithms occurring in brains — the implication being that minds may have preceded brains"
