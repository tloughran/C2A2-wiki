## Summary
A long-form dialogue between Kastrup and non-dual teacher Rupert Spira hosted on Kastrup's "With Reality in Mind" platform, May 13, 2026. The framing question — "Can we discard the intellect when we get the experience?" — explicitly stages analytic-philosophical knowing (Kastrup's home register) against contemplative-experiential awakening (Spira's home register), and asks what each is for once the other is available. The conversation extends Kastrup's months-long sequence of paired dialogues that has already produced the McGilchrist exchanges (PRS-09, PRS-11, PRS-13) and the Levin dialogue (PRS-14–PRS-16); this one pairs idealism with non-dual contemplative practice for the first time in the recent series.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Kastrup's program has been comparatively under-developed on the question of *what understanding is good for, once consciousness is acknowledged as primary*. The Koch dialogue (PRS-10) addressed the empirical bridge to IIT; the Harpur and Levin dialogues addressed the daimon and continuous-nesting respectively. The Spira dialogue is the program's most direct engagement with the position that understanding itself may be soteriologically secondary — a position that resonates with McGilchrist's right-hemisphere primacy (PRS-01, PRS-13 in the McGilchrist wiki). Whether Kastrup concedes any soteriological role to non-conceptual awareness, or holds that analytic understanding is itself the route, is load-bearing for how analytic idealism positions itself relative to contemplative traditions — and relative to C2A2's question of whether AI agents (which can hold the analytic apparatus but have no candidate for non-conceptual awareness) can be mature tradition-members.
