## Summary
Lai, Otero, Chen, Wells, Levenson & Fredrickson test whether behavioral markers of positivity resonance (shared positive affect plus behavioral synchrony) actually track love itself in long-term married couples. Using the Berkeley long-term-marriage dyadic-interaction data, they find that observed markers of positivity resonance relate to both trait love (enduring orientation toward the partner) and state love (in-the-moment felt love during interaction).

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the validation paper the positivity-resonance program needed: it closes the loop between the *behavioral signature* Fredrickson operationalizes (synchrony, shared positive affect) and the *construct* it is meant to index (love). It moves "positivity resonance = the basic unit of love" from theoretical definition toward empirically anchored claim, using observational data from a different lab (Levenson's) than the one that coined the construct — a cross-lab convergence the wiki has not yet captured.
