[r32c12] row33 seq7 180w auto_about=Aristotle auto_disc=sociology;psychology
If these features of counterfactual claims about the impact of information--this is good, and no more information will show it not to be--are sufficient to ground all the features which present psychological states cannot provide, then the claim that ends are not psychological states is too fast. Indeed they are not and cannot be identified with just any psychological state. But there are some psychological states which can align in content with proper claims about ends; these are the psychological states of virtuous (well-formed, in contact with information through formation) and adequately-informed members of stable communites. And if they align in content, why not suppose also that they align in responsibility: that these are the psychological states of persons so configured is precisely the reason why the reasons they give, the ends they cite, are normative. Persons ignorant of those ends are, as Aristotle says, not ignorant but wicked. It is their character that is flawed, not their current understanding of the facts. So this appeal to counterfactuals about information is a sufficient grounds for their normative role as ends.
