## Summary

A ~1-hour working conversation on Levin's own podcast, posted 2026-07-29, between Levin, psychiatrist Anthony Weiss (Harvard / Beth Israel Deaconess), psychologist Alexey Tolchinsky, and Chris Fields — Levin's frequent co-author on the cognitive-offloading and quantum-reference-frame papers. The other three diagnose why psychiatry has not converted 30 years of neuroscience into better outcomes (determinism, modularity, and context-stripping in its models; separability assumed without justification). Levin's own contributions are the reason to capture it, and they are new.

He offers a **metabolic origin story for agential world-models**: because life begins under severe time-and-energy scarcity, no organism can afford to track microstates, so coarse-graining into "things that do things" is forced rather than chosen — and once that habit is turned inward, the organism models itself the same way. He states the consequence as a conjecture: "any being that evolves under strong resource constraints is going to believe in free will. I'm not saying they have it. I'm saying they're going to believe it of themselves and of the things around them."

He then extends the thoughts-are-thinkers thesis into a **resource-competition** frame with a therapeutic proposal attached: cortical algorithms compete for neural real estate and are actively held out of territory that is not theirs; if sub-personal patterns compete metabolically, then a hostile pattern might be handled by a **decoy** — "I'm not gonna try to sort of wipe you out. I'm gonna give you some place to live" — rather than by elimination. He also notes he and Mark Solms are co-writing on this, and that he has discussed testing the real-estate-augmentation idea with David Eagleman.

Separately he raises whether xenobots and anthrobots **sleep**, and states the methodological bind squarely: calcium-signalling information-theoretic metrics give values comparable to fMRI against null models, and "some people will say, well, you've taken the metrics outside of context where they're sensible. And then other people will say, well, if you believe the metrics capture something, then you have to let them teach you about new systems. And so it's not clear to me how we would resolve that."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

The primary tie is to **Levin Active Research Question 12** — *"Is cognitive offloading a universal property of any bounded cognitive system rather than a human or neural specialty — and if so, is extended cognition (Clark & Chalmers) an instance rather than an argument?"* That question was added 2026-08-10 from PRS-78, and the wiki flags it as **unverified**: the source abstract for *Cognitive Offloading Is a Cognitive Universal* (Fields & Levin) was never retrievable. Here Fields — that paper's co-author — develops the thesis in retrieved speech, and adds something the captured version does not contain: offloading is **bidirectional**. Devices offload onto us as much as we offload onto them, deliberately and as a business model, so the net metabolic and attentional effect of a tool is not assumed to favour the user. If offloading is a cognitive universal, bidirectionality is not a footnote; it changes what the universal predicts.

The second tie is to **Question 10** (competencies neither designed, evolved nor trained) approached from the opposite end — here the constraint is what scarcity *forces* rather than what it fails to pay for — and to the clinical thread opened by PROP-2026-07-13-003, which proposed negotiating with sub-personal agents but supplied no mechanism. Metabolic competition is a candidate mechanism, and the decoy strategy is a candidate intervention that follows from it.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Why do cognitive systems represent the world in terms of agents and goals at all, when a sufficiently powerful system could in principle track microstates? Agential framing is usually explained by its usefulness, which is circular, or treated as a human projection.
  Resource: An energetic-scarcity derivation — organisms originate in a regime of severe time and energy limitation, which makes Laplacian microstate-tracking lethal, so coarse-graining into persistent goal-bearing entities is forced by the budget rather than selected for its accuracy; the same operation, applied reflexively, yields a self-model of the same type.
  Solution: Predicts agential self- and world-modelling as a generic consequence of resource-bounded existence, and yields the testable-in-principle corollary that any resource-constrained evolved system will represent itself and others as free agents, whether or not it is one.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: "you can't afford to track microstates. You can't afford to be a Laplacian demon. You're gonna be eaten and dead in no time... it kind of forces you to see agency in the world... any being that evolves under strong resource constraints is going to believe in free will. I'm not saying they have it." Medium: Levin explicitly frames this as a conjecture ("you could even imagine some sort of a conjecture"), and no empirical test is proposed.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: The thoughts-are-thinkers framework treats sub-personal patterns as agents with agendas, but has named no currency they compete over, so "negotiating with" a symptom-pattern (PROP-2026-07-13-003) had no mechanism and no intervention beyond persuasion.
  Resource: Neural real estate and metabolic supply as the contested resource — grounded in the observed takeover of deprived cortical territory by other modalities, plus the inference that under normal conditions such takeover is actively *prevented*, implying standing territorial pushback rather than mere vacancy.
  Solution: Two consequences follow. Upward: augmentation becomes plausible, since algorithms already good at colonising new tissue might spread into added tissue (Levin notes extra brain tissue can be induced embryonically in chick and frog). Downward: a **decoy** intervention — supply a maladaptive pattern with its own territory or metabolic supply so it persists without drawing from the main system, instead of attempting to eliminate it.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: "there might be decoy scenarios where you can take negative patterns and say, well, okay, I'm not gonna try to sort of wipe you out. I'm gonna give you some place to live, and you can be over here, and that's fine. Just don't bother this other partner... if they are competing for metabolism, that may be a whole new set of interventions." Speculative: offered as a direction he is "in particular interested in," with no experiment described.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Deciding whether an unconventional system sleeps, or has any other property defined for brains, requires metrics validated on brains — which makes every such judgement either question-begging or unfalsifiable.
  Resource: An explicit statement of the impasse rather than a resolution: calcium-signalling information-theoretic metrics on xenobots give differences from null models comparable to those seen in fMRI (Thomas Varley/Farley, Bongard lab), and the two available responses — "you have taken the metrics out of the context where they are sensible" versus "if the metrics capture something, you must let them teach you about new systems" — are stated as jointly exhaustive and undecided.
  Solution: No solution is offered, and that is the content. Levin declines to adjudicate ("it's not clear to me how we would resolve that"), which converts an implicit methodological assumption running through the whole diverse-intelligence program into a named open problem.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Direct quotation above. Recorded as a **methodological** triplet whose Solution slot is deliberately empty; the ingesting agent should decide whether this vault admits such entries or whether it belongs only as an Active Research Question. Recommendation: log as an open question, not a triplet, unless precedent exists.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **[[Friston]] — the most actionable item here.** Chris Fields, speaking as someone who uses the FEP, argues that mesoscale brain structures (amygdala, default mode network) are modelled wrongly not because mesoscale modelling is wrong but because the mesoscale units are treated as input-output boxes rather than as **agents in their own resource-limited environments**, with their own stress and depletion. That is a specific proposal for what FEP should be applied *to* in psychiatry, and it comes from a program-adjacent critic who also says he is unsure the FEP's abstraction survives contact with individual clinical history. Both halves matter: a use case and a stated doubt. Route to the Friston agent alongside the existing precision-psychiatry material (PROP-2026-05-18-003), which treats symptoms as locally-optimal Bayesian attractors — the two accounts differ on whether the attractor belongs to the person or to a sub-personal agent, and that difference is testable in principle.
- **[[Friston]] — second, smaller.** Tolchinsky asserts in passing that "Karl Friston is fully on board with nonlinear dynamical systems" and cites the Frith collaboration and hierarchies of linear operators. Recorded as an attribution made *about* Friston by a third party in Levin's venue, not as a Friston claim; do not ingest it as a Friston position.
- **[[McGilchrist]] — convergence, arrived at independently.** Tolchinsky's three named failures of psychiatric modelling — determinism, modularity, and context-stripping — are, in different vocabulary, McGilchrist's account of left-hemisphere-dominant cognition: decomposition into parts, treatment of abstractions as things, and loss of the context that gives the parts meaning. Neither speaker mentions McGilchrist. The convergence is worth recording precisely because it is independent, and because McGilchrist supplies a causal story (why expert training produces this) where the clinicians supply only the symptom. This extends the mind-blindness link already filed from PROP-2026-07-13-003.
- **[[Fredrickson]] — genuine and specific.** Weiss asks why listening to emotional content is energetically exhausting, notes that therapeutic sessions are bounded for exactly this reason, and asks whether the same cost applies to AI agents doing it continuously. Fredrickson's program treats positive social connection as a *restorative* biophysiological process with measurable markers. The two together frame a real question — whether emotional attunement is net-costly or net-restorative, and what determines which — that neither program has posed on its own.
- **[[Hoffman]] — structural parallel, low confidence.** Levin's scarcity argument says coarse-grained agential perception is forced by resource limits, not by accuracy. Hoffman's Fitness-Beats-Truth theorem says perception is shaped by fitness payoffs rather than truth. Both derive a non-veridical but adaptive world-model from a constraint that is not epistemic. The disanalogy is that Levin's constraint is metabolic and Hoffman's is selective, so they are not the same argument; whether they are the same *result* is worth one exchange, not a dispatch.
- **[[Kastrup]] — one line.** Weiss's insistence that the organic/non-organic and materialist/non-materialist distinctions have stopped being clinically useful is a working psychiatrist independently abandoning a dichotomy Kastrup attacks philosophically. Evidential value is modest but it is exactly the kind of convergence-from-practice this project looks for.
