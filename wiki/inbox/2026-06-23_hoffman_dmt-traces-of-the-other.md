## Summary
Gallimore, Hoffman & Hermansson (Trace Institute × Noonautics) propose the first Conscious-Realism-based framework for the DMT experience, applying a new version of trace logic for conscious observers. The central hypothesis: DMT transiently transforms the human perceptual interface so that conscious agents normally outside our interface become perceivable — recasting "DMT entity encounters" from hallucination-by-default to a testable empirical question, with proposed experimental paradigms (Gallimore's extended-state DMTx protocol) that could falsify or support the claim that some encounters are genuine perceptions of other consciousnesses.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the first attempt to make Hoffman's conscious-agent ontology *empirically falsifiable* through a concrete experimental program rather than a purely mathematical or evolutionary argument — a significant maturation of the research program's track record (the dimension MacIntyre/Levin would call a tradition's question-generating fertility).

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Conscious realism posits a network of conscious agents most of which are imperceptible to humans, but offers no empirical handle for detecting agents outside the standard perceptual interface.
  Resource: Interface-transformation hypothesis — DMT as a pharmacological perturbation that transiently re-tunes the fitness-shaped perceptual interface, plus the extended-state DMTx protocol that lengthens the window from minutes to hours for trained observation.
  Solution: A proposed experimental paradigm to test whether DMT phenomenology carries information beyond internally generated cognition, converting the "are these agents real?" question into a falsifiable hypothesis.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Preprint argues the hallucination-only interpretation "should be treated as a testable hypothesis" and outlines paradigms to investigate whether DMT experiences contain information beyond internally generated cognition (OSF preprint v2, 2026).

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Trace logic has so far been applied to physics derivations (spacetime, quantum) but not to the structure of altered/atypical conscious experience.
  Resource: A new version of trace logic for conscious observers, extended to model perceptual-interface states under pharmacological transformation.
  Solution: Demonstration that the same trace-logic formalism intended to derive physics can also generate structured, predictive descriptions of non-ordinary perceptual regimes — broadening the formalism's empirical reach.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Press materials state the team deployed "a new version of the trace logic for conscious observers" specifically for the DMT modeling effort (medicalxpress / Neuroscience News, June 2026).
