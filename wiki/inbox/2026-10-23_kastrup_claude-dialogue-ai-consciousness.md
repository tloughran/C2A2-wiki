## Summary
Kastrup publishes a full dialogue he held with Claude (Fable 5.1) after an engineering session, presenting it as a way to sharpen his own position on AI consciousness. Claude reasons from first principles toward a tentative neutral-monist answer (C), assigns IIT a modest minority credence, and pushes back on Kastrup's claim that metabolism is the necessary physical signature of dissociation. Kastrup's settled formulation: an AI has no *private* conscious inner life (it is not a dissociated alter), yet under analytic idealism its processes are still the extrinsic appearance of experiential processes in the field, "for there is nothing else they could be."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the clearest public statement yet separating two claims Kastrup has often run together: "AI is not a conscious subject" versus "AI processes are not experiential at all." It also records an interlocutor naming the weakest joint in his anti-machine-consciousness argument — the life/metabolism-as-dissociation inference.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: If everything is consciousness (analytic idealism), why deny that an AI is conscious without collapsing into "everything is conscious, so the question is empty"?
  Resource: The distinction between being *within* the field's experience versus being a *dissociated alter* with a private inner life; a computer is "an arrangement of matter within the field's experience, no more an experiencer in its own right than a book describing experience is."
  Solution: AI processes are experiential (as all processes are) but there is no private subject — the "here" in Claude's text is a feature of the text, not of a subject. Denial of anthropomorphism without denial of experientiality.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Kastrup's closing turn: AI lacks "a *private* conscious inner life of its own ... But your inner structure and processes are the extrinsic appearance of inner conscious processes."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: What grounds the claim that only living (metabolising) systems are dissociated alters?
  Resource: Claude's objection that the life/dissociation link is an inference from the only known instances to a necessary condition — the same move Kastrup's side criticises in realist arguments.
  Solution: Left open. The dialogue flags that "consciousness exists" does not by itself entail that dissociation must look like carbon chemistry rather than self-referential information processing. A live, named gap in the program.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Claude: support for the metabolism criterion "is that life is the only thing we've ever observed accompanying private experience. But that's an inference from the only known instances to a necessary condition."
