## Summary
The newest main-feed episode of *Ask NT Wright Anything* (46 minutes; Wright with host Mike Bird), and the first time in the captured corpus that Wright addresses near-death experiences directly. Three questions are handled. First, near-death experiences: the published description states Wright "explains why Christians should remain open but cautious" about them — that is, he neither dismisses them as neurological artefact nor treats them as evidential access to the afterlife. Second, the title "Son of God": Wright traces the phrase through its Israelite and Davidic-royal usage before arriving at the New Testament's distinct claim that "God's eternal Son became human in Jesus." This is his standard method — establish the first-century semantic range of a term before asking what the New Testament writers did with it — applied to the highest-stakes christological title. Third, Satan's role in the Gospel narrative, framed as whether Satan "could have prevented" Jesus from completing his mission.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Wright's program is built on the claim that Christian hope is bodily resurrection into a renewed creation, not disembodied survival — the argument of *The Resurrection of the Son of God* and, more recently, *God's Homecoming*, both already in the wiki. Near-death experiences are the sharpest popular-level test case for that claim, because they are usually taken as evidence for exactly the disembodied-survival picture Wright rejects. His "open but cautious" position is therefore load-bearing: it tells us how his framework handles a class of reported experience that neither confirms nor refutes it. This is also the first captured instance of Wright engaging a phenomenon with a live scientific literature (NDE research) rather than a historical or exegetical question, which is where the Wright tradition connects to the network's consciousness programs.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Kastrup**: near-death experiences are central evidence in Kastrup's analytic-idealist case that consciousness is not produced by the brain (he reads the phenomenon of heightened experience during reduced brain activity as evidence against production). Wright's "open but cautious" is a different verdict on the same data from a different program, and the disagreement is specifiable: Kastrup treats NDEs as evidence about the metaphysics of mind, Wright (on the reconstruction above) treats them as not being evidence about the object of Christian hope at all. This is one of the sharpest available Wright–Kastrup contact points in the corpus.
- **Hoffman**: same contact surface — Hoffman's conscious-realism program also treats experience as not requiring a physical substrate. The three-way Wright/Kastrup/Hoffman comparison on NDEs would test whether "survival of consciousness" and "resurrection of the body" are rival answers to one question or answers to two different questions.
- **Carroll**: Carroll's poetic-naturalism position rules out post-mortem consciousness on physical grounds ("the laws of physics underlying everyday life are completely understood"). Wright's caution about NDE evidence and Carroll's rejection of it converge on a shared refusal to treat the reports as decisive, from opposite directions — worth logging as an unexpected agreement.
- **Stump**: the Satan/temptation question touches Stump's work on the reality of moral struggle and whether an outcome must be genuinely open for a choice to be meaningful.
