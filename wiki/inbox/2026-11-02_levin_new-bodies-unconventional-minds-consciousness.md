## Summary
A ~39-minute talk Levin gave to a consciousness conference, posted to the Thoughtforms Life podcast on 2026-10-17 with an auto-generated transcript. The first ~33 minutes restate material already in the vault (molecular-network learning, xenobots/anthrobots, "paying for competencies," the space of patterns). What is new is the closing segment (33:28, "Rethinking nature of consciousness"), where Levin, who usually declines to say much about consciousness, makes three explicit claims and states a personal view: consciousness "isn't made," is not a late product of evolution, and he is "skeptical that it's even coherent to say that there's such a thing as an unconscious process." He also proposes a success criterion for any theory of consciousness: it should "output a protocol that puts you in first person in the same state" as another system — at minimum, devices for communication and "some kind of mind sharing" with unconventional systems.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Levin's program has deliberately bracketed consciousness while pushing cognition "all the way down." This talk is the clearest public statement of where he lands when he stops bracketing it, and it turns the question into an engineering target (a protocol for first-person state-sharing), which fits TAME's "experiments over assumptions" stance.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Theories of consciousness are hard to test, and none tells you what success would look like operationally.
  Resource: A proposed adequacy criterion: a good theory of consciousness should output a protocol that places the knower, in first person, in the same cognitive/conscious state as another system.
  Solution: Reframe consciousness research partly as building communication and "mind-sharing" interfaces with unconventional systems — making the theory's payoff a technology of perspective-taking rather than a third-person metric.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Closing segment (from 33:28): "I think a good theory of consciousness would output a protocol that puts you in first person in the same state... so then you really find out what it's like. And that means... devices for communication and some kind of mind sharing with other unconventional systems."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: The default null hypothesis treats most of the universe as unconscious and asks for arguments before attributing experience; the criteria we use for other humans are applied inconsistently to other substrates.
  Resource: Levin's account of why we attribute consciousness to other humans (behaviour that rewards a first-person stance, evolutionary kinship, and further criteria), plus three claims: the focus on neurons is too narrow; anatomical, physiological and gene-expression patterns belong in cognition; the same reasons should push us to take consciousness in other body structures seriously.
  Solution: Reverse the burden: Levin states that consciousness "isn't made," is not a late evolutionary product, and that "unconscious process" may not even be coherent — while explicitly disclaiming a new theory or supporting data.
  Confidence: Speculative (by Levin's own framing; one key sentence is garbled in transcription)
  Evidence: "I don't claim to have data supporting one theory of consciousness, but I will make three claims"; "I think that consciousness isn't made. I don't think it's a late product of evolution"; "I'm skeptical that it's even coherent to say that there's such a thing as an unconscious process. We have no evidence for such a thing."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Kastrup:** "consciousness isn't made" and doubt that unconscious processes exist sit close to analytic idealism, but Levin reaches them from a pattern-space (Platonic) ontology and an interface model, not from idealist metaphysics. Worth a bridge note on where the two differ (Levin: patterns ingress through interfaces; Kastrup: dissociated alters of one mind).
- **Friston:** the attribution criteria (behaviour, kinship) are the same inference-to-other-minds problem Friston describes in his 2026-10-19 Thought Economics interview ("we will never know... it is all a question of the evidence at hand"). Levin wants a protocol to close that gap; Friston treats it as permanently inferential. A clean point of disagreement.
- **Hoffman:** Levin's "front end / thin client" phrasing (companion talk) is close to Hoffman's interface theory of perception.
- **McGilchrist:** a first-person, participatory criterion for a theory of consciousness echoes McGilchrist's case against purely third-person (left-hemisphere) knowing.
- **C2A2 itself:** a "protocol that puts you in first person in the same state" as another system is a strong statement of the "second first language" goal — maturity inside another tradition's perspective — applied to non-human minds.
