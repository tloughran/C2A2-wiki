## Summary
A long-form dialogue (recorded 2026-07-07) between Kastrup and Shamil Chandaria — an advisor to Google DeepMind and a consciousness researcher at Oxford, Cambridge and Imperial College. Chandaria presents perception as a predictive generative model (we "live inside our own simulation," a hallucination updated by a thin trickle of sensory data) and previews five proposed criteria for evidence of consciousness — spanning behaviour, algorithms, IIT's causal structure, living biology, and organism–environment coupling. Kastrup, sympathetic to IIT but firmly skeptical of substrate-independent consciousness, presses the question: if a computer is conscious, what exactly is the *thing* that is conscious — the microchips, the cooling system, the river feeding the coolant?

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Kastrup's most direct 2026 engagement with AI consciousness — a standing C2A2-relevant question — and it stages his dissociation/biology account against a rigorous computationalist-adjacent interlocutor from inside frontier AI research. It sharpens the program's boundary-of-the-alter criterion into an explicit empirical challenge ("what is the thing?") and connects idealism to predictive-processing accounts of perception.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Computationalist arguments for machine consciousness assume consciousness is substrate-independent, but offer no principled account of which physical system is the bearer of the alleged inner life.
  Resource: Kastrup's dissociation model — inner life ("alters") emerges only in biology, which is the sole system exhibiting the clear boundaries a dissociative process requires; the rest of the "dashboard" (chips, cooling, water supply) has no such boundary.
  Solution: A demarcation challenge to computationalism: absent a bounded biological system, there is no identifiable *thing* to be conscious; the burden is on computationalists to name the bearer, which reframes the AI-consciousness debate around individuation rather than information-processing capacity.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Article (2026-07-07) states Kastrup "is sympathetic to IIT, but suspicious of substrate independent consciousness... leans firmly towards biology as the place where alters emerge," and poses "what exactly is the thing that's meant to be conscious? The microchips? The cooling system? The river that supplies the cooling water?"

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: If perception is a generative model built from sparse data (Chandaria: only the thumbnail-sized fovea receives sharp data; the rest is a continuously updated best guess), what is the relationship between that predictive model and the reality it purports to track?
  Resource: The convergence between predictive-processing neuroscience (perception as prior-driven generative modelling, priors discoverable and re-designable in meditation) and Kastrup's idealist claim that the perceived world is a phenomenal representation, not the thing-in-itself.
  Solution: A bridge claim — studying how machines build world-models from language alone may illuminate how human perception constructs its dashboard, without licensing the inference that either system's model is or contains consciousness.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Chandaria's fovea/hallucination account and "we are living inside our own simulation... a generative model"; the piece's closing note that studying machine world-models "we might be learning more about how we do it, too."
