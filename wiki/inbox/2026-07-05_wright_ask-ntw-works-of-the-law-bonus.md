## Summary
In this Ask NT Wright Anything bonus episode (released ~July 3, 2026; date approximate from feed), Wright walks through his intellectual history on the Pauline phrase "works of the law" (ergon nomou) — from Cranfield's "legalistic works" reading, through James Dunn, to his own settled position: the phrase names covenant badge practices (Sabbath, food laws, circumcision) that marked who belonged to God's people. He grounds this in Second Temple sources, especially the Dead Sea Scrolls text 4QMMT, and traces how Augustine, Luther, and Kant shaped the later "earning salvation" misreading. The upshot: Paul's concern in Romans 3 and Galatians is not merit-accounting but who counts as the people of God and who eats at the same table.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the clearest recent restatement of the New Perspective core that underwrites Wright's whole program: justification as covenant-membership declaration, not moral bookkeeping. The wright wiki's active question 3 (Pauline "in Christ" as corporate substance) needs exactly this material — badge practices are the mechanism by which sub-communities individuate themselves, and the one-table imperative is the unity that relativizes the badges. Directly citable for Summa syntheses on law, grace, and membership (I-II law treatise; II-II unity loci).
