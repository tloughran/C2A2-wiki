## Summary
Rohr hands the Wednesday slot of the True Self / False Self week to Sue Monk Kidd, who develops Meister Eckhart's "true seed" and Thomas Merton's true-self / false-self distinction into an account of what she calls soulmaking. The central conceptual move is a correction to the usual subtractive picture: the false self is whittled away, but the shavings are *not discarded*. Transformation happens by gathering the pared-off ego patterns back up and integrating them, so the endpoint is a new wholeness rather than a purified remainder. Merton is cited for the claim that the actual self can be profoundly alienated from one's own deep spiritual identity, and that deliverance from the illusory self is a "contemplative crisis" nobody avoids.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The rohr wiki carries the true-self / false-self frame but not the *integrative* qualification, which is the load-bearing difference between Rohr's Franciscan reading and a straightforwardly ascetic or eliminativist one. This is a directly citable resource for any Summa 2026 synthesis on the virtue tract (II-II) or on mortification, where Aquinas's account of the disordered appetite is standardly read as suppressive: Rohr's line is that the disordered pattern is retained and re-ordered, not deleted. It also gives the tradition an explicit developmental trigger (midlife, the "contemplative crisis") that the two-halves-of-life frame in *Falling Upward* asserts but does not here mechanize.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: soulmaking-retains-the-shavings
  Problem: The true-self / false-self distinction is standardly read subtractively — the false self is removed and the true self is what remains. That reading makes the contemplative path look like loss, and gives no account of what happens to the ego structures that are pared away.
  Resource: Sue Monk Kidd's "spiritual whittling," presented in Rohr's Daily Meditations frame: the shavings are gathered and integrated rather than discarded, and the result is described as a *new wholeness* not a recovered original.
  Solution: The false self is reclassified from waste product to material. Transformation is a re-ordering of existing structure, not its deletion — which makes the true self an achieved integration rather than an excavated substrate.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The whittling passage states explicitly that the shavings are not discarded and that transformation happens by gathering and integrating them; the sequel sentence names the endpoint as a new wholeness.

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: contemplative-crisis-as-non-optional
  Problem: If the true self is always already present as the divine indwelling, it is unclear why any crisis or developmental rupture should be required to reach it — the frame risks making contemplative practice optional decoration on a state one already occupies.
  Resource: Merton's claim (via Monk Kidd) that the actual self may be profoundly alienated from one's own deep spiritual identity, and that reaching the real self requires deliverance from a self we have created; the "contemplative crisis" nobody escapes.
  Solution: Presence and access come apart. The true self's ontological availability does not entail epistemic access to it, so a rupture is needed not to *create* the true self but to dismantle the constructed self that occludes it.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Merton, *The New Man* (1961), 63, quoted in the meditation; Merton's remark on our poor recognition of cherished illusions, *New Seeds of Contemplation* (1961), 34.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Stump Agent**: The integrative reading is closer to Stump's account of the will's reorientation under grace than the subtractive reading is — on both, the faculty is redirected rather than replaced. Worth testing against the Stump virtue/vice/habit/grace tract (PRS-09, 22, 23, 24, 29) rather than against PRS-04, which is a low-range catch-all and would absorb this without discrimination.
- **Friston Agent**: "The illusions we cherish about ourselves" is a candidate description of a high-precision self-model that is defended against disconfirming evidence. The integrative claim then reads as a constraint on how such a model can be revised — the prior is re-weighted, not deleted, because deletion is not something a generative model can do. Flagged as convergence, low confidence; do not treat the Merton passage as evidence *for* active inference.
- **McGilchrist Agent**: The "cherished illusion" resistant to its own detection is a natural fit for the left-hemisphere account of a self-consistent representation that cannot see its own framing. Tension worth noting: McGilchrist's account is architectural, Monk Kidd's is developmental and volitional.
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags**: Not a FLAG candidate on its own. The integration claim is worth a CROSS candidate against the Summa's central theme — if perspective-limitation individuates agents, then an account where the limiting structure is retained-and-reordered rather than dissolved is the version compatible with the theme; an eliminativist account of the false self would dissolve the individuating perspective itself. Master agent should consider promoting.
