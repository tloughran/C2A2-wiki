## Summary

A CAC Daily Meditation from the week of 9–15 August 2026 ("The Mysticism of Francis and Clare"). It pairs Margaret Carney's account of Clare of Assisi's 1212 departure from Assisi with Clare's Third Letter to Agnes of Prague, and then gives Rohr's own reflection. Rohr reads Clare's mirror language — "place your mind before the mirror of eternity… allow your entire being to be transformed into the image of the Godhead itself" — as a Western statement of the Eastern doctrine of *theosis* (divinization), and glosses it mechanically: the divine "image" is objectively given at conception, and "likeness" is what develops subjectively over time. "All we can really do is polish our own mirror to reflect the gift of this Godself more perfectly."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

This bears directly on Rohr **Active Research Question 1**: "Can Rohr's non-dual epistemology be formalized — or does formalization necessarily distort it?" Most of the tradition's material treats contemplative knowing as a *stance*. Here Rohr does something stronger and more formalizable: he states an image/likeness distinction with a definite structure — a fixed given (image, conferred at conception) plus a monotonic transformation toward it (likeness, acquired through contemplation), with the agent's only lever being the fidelity of a reflecting surface. That is a two-term model with a stated invariant and a stated update rule, which is exactly the shape a formalization would need. The tradition can now ask whether the model survives being written down, rather than asking whether Rohr has one.

It also touches **Question 10** (whether Rohr's account of divine "need," PRS-41, breaks with classical divine simplicity). Rohr himself flags that Clare's language "would have seemed presumptuous or even heretical to many (as it may even seem today)" — an explicit acknowledgement that the divinization claim strains Western scholastic boundaries. That is Rohr naming the tension rather than the wiki attributing it to him.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Rohr's contemplative epistemology has been recorded as a posture rather than as a statable structure, leaving open whether it can be formalized at all (Active Question 1).
  Resource: Clare's mirror metaphor read as *theosis*, with an explicit image/likeness split — image objectively given at conception, likeness subjectively acquired over time — and a single operator ("polish our own mirror").
  Solution: Contemplation is characterized as transformation of the self toward a fixed given target, not as acquisition of propositions about a target. This yields a formalizable shape (invariant + fidelity-improving update) without the tradition having to import one from outside.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Rohr, on Clare's third letter to Agnes of Prague: "the objectively given 'image' at our conception becomes subjective 'likeness' over time, building on the foundational metaphors that we were created in both *the image and the likeness* of God." Quoted from the retrieved page.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Whether Rohr's Franciscan-contemplative program is a devotional idiom or makes a metaphysical claim that a scholastic interlocutor would have to accept or reject.
  Resource: Rohr's own identification of Clare's language with Eastern *theosis*, "understood as a true, objective sharing and growing in our divine nature."
  Solution: The claim is put as objective rather than experiential, and Rohr concedes it reads as heterodox from a Western vantage. This converts a soft ecumenical gesture into a locatable disagreement with the Thomist wing of the network.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Rohr: "This was surely not common Catholic language in the Western Church at that time and would have seemed presumptuous or even heretical to many (as it may even seem today)."

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Stump (tension, and a sharp one).** Stump's *The Image of God* (2022) and the mourning material captured on 2026-06-11 use "image of God" as a Thomistic term where union with God is the highest good but the creature's nature is not shared into. Rohr here endorses *theosis* as "a true, objective sharing and growing in our divine nature." Both traditions use the phrase "image of God" and mean measurably different things by it. The wiki should not let the shared phrase imply a shared claim; this is a genuine cross-tradition disagreement that has not previously been isolated.
- **McGilchrist (convergence, with a caveat).** The mirror model — the knower is altered by what is attended to, rather than representing it — is close to McGilchrist's account of right-hemisphere attention as participatory rather than manipulative. The caveat: Rohr's version has a *fixed* target (the Godhead), where McGilchrist's has none. That difference is load-bearing and neither program has drawn it.
- **Levin (structural parallel neither program has drawn).** Image-given-then-likeness-realized is formally the same shape as Levin's morphogenetic target morphology: a stored pattern the system navigates toward, with local repair working to close the gap. Rohr's "polish the mirror" is a fidelity-restoring operation on a substrate whose target is not itself in question. This is a structural parallel, not a claim that Rohr is doing biology — but it is the same abstract object Stump's tradition already links to Levin via final causality (CROSS-008), which means Levin now has two independent theological routes into it.
- **Friston (structural parallel).** "Polishing the mirror" as reduction of distortion between a system and a fixed generative target is expressible as prediction-error minimization against a fixed prior. This is speculative and should not be recorded as a convergence: Rohr's target is a person, not a distribution, and nothing in the meditation is about inference.
