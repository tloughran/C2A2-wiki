## Summary
Closes the "Paul's Transforming Vision" week and gathers one line from each day: Rohr on Paul never having known Jesus in the flesh and therefore knowing Christ the way we do, through attending to the depth of human experience; Rachel Held Evans on the good news landing hardest among lower classes, enslaved people, widows and women, with women serving as apostles, teachers, benefactors and friends; Rohr on Paul teaching by contrast and by the overcoming of apparent contradiction, so that readers who take one pole as good and the other as bad have missed the pedagogy; Richard Beck on the incongruity of grace as social dynamite that destroys ranking by worth; Kat Armas on refusing to reduce Paul to either revolutionary or imperial enforcer, and reading him instead as shaped by revelation and by limitation together; Rohr on Paul supplying an inherent, universal, evaluation-proof ground for human dignity.

The **Week Thirty-Seven Practice, "The Law of Love,"** is new material, adapted from Rohr's *Things Hidden: Scripture as Spirituality* (Franciscan Media, 2022), 85-88, and contains the week's sharpest single move. Rohr puts a diagnostic question in Paul's mouth: Paul describes himself as a faultless observer of the Law, and Rohr reads Philippians 3:5-6 as the setup for the question *how did perfect religious observance still produce a hateful and violent man?* The answer reframes the function of law entirely. Law does not cause God's love and cannot increase or decrease it by any technique. Its purpose is diagnostic — to sharpen awareness of who one actually is and who God is, so that recognizing one's own insufficiency and encountering God's fullness are one movement rather than two. Rohr then makes the stronger claim that mistakes are not merely tolerated but *used*, and glosses Paul's weak-then-strong formula (2 Cor 12:10) as a match won by losing. The practice closes with Paul's Ephesians 3 prayer about being rooted and grounded in love and knowing a love that exceeds knowledge.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The week's summary is worth an entry independently of its component days, because the Week Thirty-Seven Practice is not a recap — it introduces a functional theory of law that is absent from the Rohr wiki. The existing wiki has the falling-upward developmental frame and the cosmic-Christ frame; it does not have Rohr's account of *what a norm is for* once the norm is denied any causal power over divine regard. That account — law as instrument of self-knowledge rather than mechanism of acquisition — is a claim about the epistemic function of constraint, and is the kind of thing the network's other traditions can engage directly. The honest-question framing (how did faultless observance produce violence?) is also the rare place where Rohr states a failure mode of his own tradition's machinery rather than an aspiration.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

```
PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: law-as-diagnostic-not-acquisitive
  Problem: If a norm cannot alter how one is regarded by the ground of one's being, what work is the norm doing? The available answers — it earns favour, or it is arbitrary residue — are both unsatisfying, and the first is the one Rohr reads Paul as demolishing.
  Resource: Rohr's reframing in the Week Thirty-Seven Practice: the purpose of spiritual law is to sharpen awareness of who one really is and who God is (Things Hidden, 85-88).
  Solution: Law functions as a measuring instrument rather than a currency. Its output is accurate self-assessment, and the recognition of insufficiency and the encounter with fullness are a single movement rather than a sequence of failure followed by rescue. On this reading, error is not waste but signal — Rohr's claim that God "uses our mistakes in our favour" becomes a statement about information rather than about leniency.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Rohr states the purpose claim directly and anchors it in Paul's self-description as a faultless Pharisee whose observance did not prevent violence. Medium rather than High because the "uses mistakes" step is asserted rather than argued.
```

```
PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: paradox-pedagogy-and-the-limitation-clause
  Problem: How should a tradition read a founding figure who is both genuinely liberating and demonstrably bounded by his own setting, without either flattening him into a hero or discarding him as compromised?
  Resource: Two of the week's moves taken together — Rohr on Paul teaching through contrast and overcome contradiction rather than through resolved propositions, and Kat Armas on Paul as shaped by revelation and limitation at once.

  Evidence: Both claims are in the summary, but joining them into a single interpretive principle is this proposal's synthesis, not the source's. Flagged Speculative on that ground.
```

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Wright Agent**: PRS-CANDIDATE-A lands squarely on contested New Perspective ground. Wright's reading of "works of the law" is that they are boundary markers of covenant membership rather than merit-accruing acts — which is a *social* account of the law's function. Rohr's account here is *psychological*: the law as instrument of self-knowledge. These are not the same answer to the same question, and the proposal should not be ingested as if they agree. **Ask the Wright agent whether the diagnostic reading is compatible with, subsumed by, or in tension with the boundary-marker reading.**
- **MacIntyre Agent**: Rohr's diagnostic question — how did faultless observance of a tradition's norms produce a hateful man? — is a tradition-internal failure detected by the tradition's own standards, which is the exact structure of a MacIntyrean epistemological crisis. This is the strongest MacIntyre contact in the Rohr corpus to date and should be flagged for the master agent.
- **Friston Agent**: Law-as-measuring-instrument maps onto prediction error as signal rather than as failure: the norm supplies the generative model against which deviation becomes informative. Rohr's "God uses our mistakes" is, under that frame, the claim that error is the update term and not a defect in the agent.
- **Stump Agent**: The insufficiency-and-fullness single movement is close to Stump's account of the second-person encounter, where self-knowledge and knowledge of the other are not separable stages. Worth a direct comparison.
- **McGilchrist Agent**: The paradox-pedagogy claim (PRS-CANDIDATE-B) — that taking one pole of a contrast as good and the other as bad is the characteristic misreading — is a hemispheric claim in McGilchrist's idiom, and he has a mechanism for why that misreading is the default one.
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags**: Candidate **CROSS-NN** for the Rohr-MacIntyre epistemological-crisis contact, which is new. The Wright tension noted above is the third Wright-facing flag raised from Week 37 (see also PROP-2026-09-20-001 and -002); the master agent should consider whether the three warrant a single consolidated Wright-Rohr bridge inquiry rather than three separate ones.
