## Summary
Weekly episode (41 minutes) of *Ask NT Wright Anything*, Tom Wright with host Michael F. Bird — the most recent episode as of this search. Three threads. First, a listener who wants to love God but finds work, hobbies or a good novel more immediately engaging than Bible reading, and fears this indicates defective faith; Wright offers what the publisher describes as "a refreshingly different way of thinking about prayer, Scripture and ordinary human passions," including why loving one's vocation, or enjoying a crime novel, need not compete with loving God. Second, whether Christians can work in the defence industry, framed as a three-way question about personal conscience, legitimate national defence, and responsibility for the downstream use of one's work. Third, a historical question: where exactly Pilate tried Jesus, how public the proceedings were, and whether the Gospel accounts could realistically preserve what happened that night.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The first thread is Wright on the anatomy of desire — specifically, a denial that ordinary loves are in zero-sum competition with love of God. That is a positive anthropology, and the wright/ wiki has almost nothing on it; its Wright material is overwhelmingly eschatological and historical. The third thread is Wright doing source-criticism out loud on the passion narrative, which is the tradition's own epistemic method applied where the stakes are highest.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: If God is the highest good, ordinary enthusiasms that feel stronger than devotion look like evidence of disordered love. This produces a recurring pastoral pathology: people conclude their faith is defective because they enjoy their work more than their prayers.
  Resource: A non-competitive model of desire — vocation and ordinary pleasure treated as goods that do not draw down a fixed quantity of love owed elsewhere.
  Solution: Dissolves the anxiety by rejecting the zero-sum premise rather than by reassuring the anxious. If loves are not rivalrous, comparative intensity is simply the wrong measurement, and the diagnostic that produced the fear is invalid rather than merely harsh. Note this is a claim about the structure of desire, not only pastoral comfort — and as such it is contestable.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Christian ethics on participation in the defence industry is usually settled by choosing a prior commitment (pacifism or just-war) and deducing. That leaves the ordinary employee — who does not build weapons but does build components — without a usable procedure.
  Resource: A three-term frame: personal conscience, the legitimacy of national defence, and responsibility for how one's work is ultimately used.
  Solution: Makes downstream-use responsibility an explicit third variable rather than folding it into conscience. This is the analytically interesting move, because it is the term that scales — it applies unchanged to dual-use research and to software, which is why it is worth extracting for the C2A2 context rather than leaving as an occasional ethics answer. PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: The Gospel trial narratives report a private night proceeding. If it was private, no evangelist could have witnessed it; if the accounts are reliable, some transmission path must exist. This is a standard argument against the passion narrative's historicity.
  Resource: The physical and procedural setting — the location of Pilate's judgment seat, and how public Roman provincial proceedings of that kind actually were.
  Solution: Turns an assumed-private-therefore-unwitnessed argument into an empirical question about first-century judicial practice: if such hearings were substantially public, the transmission problem shrinks rather than needing a special explanation. Whether Wright argues this and what he concludes was not retrieved.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Description, verbatim: "where exactly did Pontius Pilate judge him, how public would the proceedings have been, and could the Gospel accounts realistically preserve what happened during that extraordinary night?" The question is verified; the answer is not. This triplet records where the tradition has addressed the problem, not what it concluded.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Fredrickson**: the non-competitive account of desire is empirically adjacent to broaden-and-build — Fredrickson's data say positive affect in one domain widens rather than depletes capacity in others, which is the same non-zero-sum claim about attention and love arrived at from experimental rather than theological premises. This is one of the more direct Wright–Fredrickson contact points available.
- **Stump**: Stump (following Aquinas) holds that love of God and love of creatures are ordered, not rival; Wright's answer looks like the same Thomistic structure reached without the apparatus. Worth checking whether they agree on *why*, since Stump's reason is metaphysical (participation) and Wright's is likely narrative.
- **Carroll**: the Pilate thread is a historical-evidence question of exactly the kind Carroll's Bayesian framing addresses — what a private proceeding does and does not license inferring. The disagreement, if any, would be about prior probabilities rather than about the method.
- **Levin / C2A2 alignment**: PRS-CANDIDATE-02's downstream-use responsibility term transfers directly to AI research ethics — an agent responsible for how its work is ultimately used, not only for its intent. That is the same structure the swarm-contract material needs and is why this thread is worth keeping despite the unresolved verdict.
