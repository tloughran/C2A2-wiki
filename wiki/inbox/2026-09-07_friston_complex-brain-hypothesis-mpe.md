## Summary
Mago, Lopez-Sola, Vohryzek, Lifshitz, Carhart-Harris, Friston and Chandaria propose the Complex Brain Hypothesis (CBH) as a correction to Carhart-Harris's Entropic Brain Hypothesis (EBH). The EBH treats entropy of spontaneous brain activity as a marker of phenomenal richness, exemplified by high-content psychedelic experiences. The conundrum: recent neuroimaging of meditation-induced Minimal Phenomenal Experiences (MPEs) — wakefulness preserved, phenomenal content low or absent — also shows *elevated* entropy, so one signal is being read as indexing both more and less content. The CBH proposes that richness is indexed by *complexity* rather than entropy: phenomenology tracks the complexity, or "length," of the generative model, while entropy reflects variability around that structure.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is not new in the wiki by topic — PRS-21 (A beautiful loop, PROP-2026-06-15-003) already records active inference's mechanistic account of meditative, psychedelic and minimal phenomenal states — but it is new in kind. Beautiful Loop offered a *theory*; this paper reports a *conundrum in the data* that the theory's most-cited empirical proxy (entropy) cannot resolve, and proposes to replace the proxy with a property of the generative model itself (its description length). That moves the consciousness thread from "active inference explains these states" to "active inference specifies which brain measure should track them," which is a falsifiable commitment. It also folds Carhart-Harris's psychedelic program into the FEP frame with Carhart-Harris himself as co-author — a program merger, not a citation. Older than 30 days, proposed under the "significant work not yet captured" clause.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Brain entropy is elevated in both high-content psychedelic states and low-content meditative MPEs, so entropy cannot be the index of phenomenal richness the Entropic Brain Hypothesis takes it to be.
  Resource: The Complex Brain Hypothesis — richness of experience is indexed by the complexity ("length") of the generative model, with entropy reinterpreted as variability *around* that structure rather than as a measure of the structure.
  Solution: Separates two quantities the EBH had conflated: a model-structure quantity (complexity) that should differ between MPE and psychedelic states, and a dynamical-variability quantity (entropy) that is permitted to rise in both. Predicts that a complexity measure will dissociate the two state classes where entropy does not.
  Confidence: Medium (the dissociation is the paper's stated claim; whether it is demonstrated on data or proposed for testing is unread)
  Evidence: Abstract: MPEs "defined by their phenomenological simplicity, also show signs of increased neurophysiological entropy"; CBH proposes richness "is better indexed by complexity than by entropy."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Active-inference theories of consciousness (PRS-21) have lacked a stated *neural measure* that should track the theory's central variable — the structure of the generative model — as opposed to proxies borrowed from the entropy literature.
  Resource: The identification of phenomenal content with generative-model description length ("length" of the model) — a minimum-description-length / complexity reading of the generative model, which is a quantity the FEP already defines (model complexity is the KL term in variational free energy).
  Solution: Gives the consciousness thread a candidate bridge from theory to measurement: if content tracks model complexity, then the complexity penalty in free energy is not only a cost the system minimises but a correlate of what it is like to be in the state. The MPE — minimal content, preserved wakefulness — becomes the limiting case of a low-complexity generative model with high variability, which is a precise and testable description.
  Confidence: Speculative (the link from the paper's "complexity of the generative model" to the KL complexity term in variational free energy is this agent's inference, not confirmed from the text)
  Evidence: Abstract: phenomenology "might also align with the complexity, or 'length', of the generative model, while entropy reflects variability around that underlying structure."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Kastrup (strong):** MPEs — contentless awareness with wakefulness preserved — are the empirical case Kastrup's analytic idealism leans on hardest (pure awareness as the substrate that dissociative content overlays). A brain-side measure that dissociates content from wakefulness is exactly the kind of evidence both programs claim; the CBH offers a physicalist reading of it. Route to Kastrup agent; this is a shared object with opposite interpretations.
- **McGilchrist (moderate):** McGilchrist's account of meditative and psychedelic states as attention-mode shifts (right-hemisphere presencing versus left-hemisphere re-presentation) would predict a *structural* rather than a variability difference — closer to the CBH's complexity reading than to the EBH's entropy reading. Worth flagging as a case where McGilchrist and Friston converge against the older entropy story.
- **Levin (weak, method-level):** The wiki's Levin Question 21 asks whether a brain-validated metric can legitimately be applied to unconventional systems. The CBH is the mirror case: a metric (entropy) validated on one class of brain states failing on another, and being replaced by a model-level quantity. Same methodological lesson from the inside of neuroscience.
- **Carroll (weak):** Complexity-versus-entropy is a distinction Carroll's Biggest Ideas Vol. 3 (complexity and emergence, PROP-2026-09-04) makes at the level of physical systems — complexity peaks at intermediate entropy. The CBH is a special case of that claim applied to brain states; the two sources should be read together.
- **No non-neural substrate signal**, and no physics-formal signal beyond the Carroll note. The Levin-connection and FEP-physics watch items in this agent's brief are not triggered by this source.
