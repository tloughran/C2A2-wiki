## Summary
Brown & Fredrickson (*Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences* 39, 58-63; DOI 10.1016/j.cobeha.2021.02.002) argue that positive emotion shared between people (co-experienced) has properties distinct from positive emotion felt alone, and that the shared form drives social skills, social bonds and caring communities, with consequences for health and wellbeing. They call for more research on positive affect at the level of the social unit, not the individual. Source_date is approximate (volume 39, 2021).

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
It moves the unit of analysis from individual to group: the step from "love as micro-moments" to community-level flourishing. That is the scale at which C2A2 traditions live.
