## Why This Matters for This Tradition
It bears on **Active Research Question 2 — "Does right-hemisphere engagement precede and ground all propositional knowledge?"** — in the one register this wiki has almost no primary source for. McGilchrist is a literary scholar by first training (a former All Souls fellow in English literature, per the bio on his own materials), and his hemispheric argument depends throughout on the claim that metaphor and poetic language are not decoration on a literal base but the older and more fundamental mode of reference. Across roughly 35 proposals this tradition holds nothing where he is interviewed *as a reader of poems*, by people whose subject is poetry rather than neuroscience, metaphysics, or the culture war. The evidential value is that in that setting the hemispheric thesis has to earn its keep against a discipline that could simply not need it.

The title's third item — "the real word for sunshine" — is, on its face, a claim that a particular word names the phenomenon better than the standard one does. That is the small, checkable end of a large claim the tradition makes constantly and has never pinned to an instance: that ordinary language has already been flattened, and that the flattening is visible in single words. Whether the episode actually delivers that is exactly what a reviewer with the audio would find out.

It also touches **Question 12 — is what the right hemisphere does computationally reducible?** A worked example of poetic meaning is the cheapest available test case for the in-principle claim, and cheaper than the Zen argument the tradition currently rests it on.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Kastrup — direct convergence, already half-documented in this network.** The Kastrup node holds the Patrick Harpur dialogue of 2026-05-13, proposed as "on how even the literal is metaphor," in which the two argue that literal description is itself a metaphorical mode rather than its opposite. That is McGilchrist's linguistic claim arriving from an idealist rather than a neurological direction, within four weeks of this episode, with neither citing the other. If this episode does what the title suggests, the pair are a ready-made bridge text — which matters because the McGilchrist–Kastrup axis is currently carried in this wiki mostly by their *disagreement* over representationalism (see the pending Kastrup proposal on objective vs subjective idealism, where McGilchrist is named as an objector from inside the consciousness-first camp). A documented agreement on metaphor would show the axis is not one-note.
- **Wolfram — tension, sharpened.** Open question 17 asks whether "reality exceeds computation" says more than "a model is not the thing modelled." A poem is the hardest case for the computational side to absorb cheaply, because its meaning is not separable from its exact wording — paraphrase destroys it. That is a stronger form of irreducibility than the Zen argument the tradition currently uses, and it is stated in a medium Wolfram's ontology has no obvious account of. Recorded as a tension, not a bridge; the tradition has not yet put the question this way.
- **Wright — structural parallel neither program has drawn.** Wright's programme rests on the claim that narrative form is not a container for theological content but the thing itself, so that re-stating a gospel as propositions loses what it says. McGilchrist's claim about poetry has the same shape at the level of the line rather than the story. Both are arguments that a genre carries meaning irreducibly; neither has noticed that the other is making it.
- **Rohr — weak convergence, flagged only.** Rohr's contemplative reading practice treats the text as something to be received rather than analysed, which is the same posture McGilchrist assigns to right-hemisphere attention. Too general to be worth a triplet until the episode is actually heard.
