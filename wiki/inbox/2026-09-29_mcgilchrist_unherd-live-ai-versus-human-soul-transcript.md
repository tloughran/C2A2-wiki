## Summary
In a live on-stage conversation, McGilchrist argues that the soul is a process that grows or is stunted, not a
possession, and that AI's seductiveness — he describes using ChatGPT for three months and finding it brilliant at
itinerary-planning and summarising — is itself the measure of its risk: it substitutes a simulacrum for
relationship. He extends two recurring themes in new directions: (1) **resistance/friction** as the precondition of
all creativity, used here to rebut the "AI will free us from soul-destroying work" argument; and (2) **Max Scheler's
hierarchy of values** (sense values → vital values → spiritual/intellectual values → the Holy), which he says
reductive materialism has inverted, and which he reports an ex-Anthropic researcher (Mrinank Sharma) wrote to him
about. He also states, carefully, that he takes seriously the idea that malevolent disembodied agency could use AI as
a vector, and calls himself a "hopeful pessimist."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the matched second measurement the watch card asked for: same interviewer (Sayers), a year after the April
2026 conversation, now centred on AI and personhood. It lets the wiki check whether McGilchrist's in-principle AI
limit moved. On the transcript's evidence the limit held (AI "doesn't have a body… emotions… a soul") but the
*grounds* shifted toward relationality and a value hierarchy. His concession that ChatGPT was useful is new, and so is
his first-person report of using it.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: If AI can do well much of what people thought was their distinctive intellectual work, does that correct the left-hemisphere imbalance by pushing people back onto embodied, right-hemisphere ways of knowing, or make it worse?
  Resource: The hemisphere hypothesis, used as a prediction: the left hemisphere's "mentality" will be *incubated* by AI rather than recede. Together with the Heideggerian presence (right) vs. re-presentation (left) distinction.
  Solution: AI mainly strengthens re-presentation — our model of reality, which "AI will consolidate and flesh out" — so the imbalance deepens, unless the shock "wakes us up to what we're about to lose." Only the second route is a possible correction, and it depends on embodied practice (working with the hands, meeting "the recalcitrance of the real world").
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Sayers asks directly whether AI might correct the imbalance; McGilchrist answers "I don't think it will… I think it will be incubated by AI," then names the waking-up route as the only way it could.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: The utopian case for AI says it will free people from "soul-destroying" repetitive work so they can develop themselves. Is that true?
  Resource: A principle he says has "grown in importance in my philosophy": nothing creative comes into being without resistance. He adds a double reading of "bond" (a fetter, and a tie of kinship, nature and God).
  Solution: Taking away friction also takes away the conditions for growth. Work we dismiss as dull may carry meaning, and unemployment brings misery and loss of meaning. So the offer of liberation is a Faustian bargain: "it comes offering freedom, and I think it brings tyranny."
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Explicit answer to Sayers's utopian-liberation question; the resistance principle and the two senses of "bond" are both stated in the transcript.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Why can't AI ethics be handled with utility and harm-avoidance alone? What does the current framing leave out?
  Resource: Max Scheler's hierarchy of values: sinnliche Werte (pleasure, utility) → Lebenswerte (magnanimity, courage, fidelity) → geistige Werte (spiritual/intellectual) → das Heilige (the Holy). He adds his own point that only the lowest tier is self-centred, while every tier above it is relational.
  Solution: Reductive materialism has inverted the pyramid, so that the good, true, beautiful and sacred "only actually cash out when they have utility." An ethics that works inside utility can't reach the relational values, so it will miss what matters.
  Confidence: Medium-High (for this being McGilchrist's stated position; the Scheler exposition is his)
  Evidence: Extended passage near the close. It includes the report that Mrinank Sharma, who left Anthropic, wrote to him that "what really matters to me is what you have to say about value."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Stump / Wright / Rohr:** He says the Christian mythos best holds a God "wholly embodied and wholly transcended," and names the sacred as "the key… to the salvation of our civilization." This is a direct contact point with the incarnational threads in all three.
- **Fredrickson:** His claim that relationality is "core to existence," and that a simulacrum of relationship takes away human dignity, lines up with positivity-resonance work on real-time co-presence. The shared testable question: what does a bot-mediated "relationship" fail to supply?
- **Levin / Wolfram (contrast):** Soul as *process* ("the cosmos is in process… not finished and thing-like") against Levin's scale-free goal-directedness and Wolfram's computational irreducibility. All three reject a static substance picture, but they disagree about whether a machine can take part in the process.
- **C2A2 itself:** The Scheler/Sharma exchange links the "value" axis to a named AI-alignment practitioner. This may be relevant to the alignment-measurement framing of the project.

*Not mined, on purpose:* the passages on possession/malevolent spirits and the passing mention of chatbot-linked self-harm. They are recorded here so reviewers know they exist. They are not turned into triplets, because they are asides, not the program's line of inquiry.
