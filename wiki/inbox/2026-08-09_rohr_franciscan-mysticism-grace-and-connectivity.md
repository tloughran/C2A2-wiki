## Summary
Rohr opens Week 32 by defining mysticism, in his own words, more tightly than the Rohr wiki currently records anywhere. Three moves are made. First, a definition by etymology and by function: *mysticism* traces to a Greek root for closed eyes or lips and so points at something hidden; a mystic is one who *reveals* the hidden. Rohr then adds a claim that is empirical in form rather than devotional — true mystics "seldom if ever bring bad news," because reality at its deepest levels turns out to be better than most of us imagine. Second, an explicit rejection of esotericism: mysticism is not restricted to an elite, but it is restricted to those who go past surface and exterior to experience what Rohr calls the "grace and connectivity" of all things. He identifies *connectivity* as the diagnostic marker — the "unteachable gift" he says he always sees in genuine mystics, who "know and enjoy the connected core of reality" and "look out from a different pair of eyes." Third, a taxonomy: Franciscan mysticism is one subset among several (he names nature mysticism, Sufi ecstasy, Hindu unitive consciousness and asceticism, Buddhist nonviolence and simplicity), distinguished by its Christocentric lens and by a structural bias toward the edge and the bottom. He then makes the move that matters most for this project: Franciscan mysticism "is therefore not really about Francis, but about a universal notion of Christ and therefore all reality." The entry closes with two compressed theses — that Franciscans have historically believed in original blessing far more than original sin, and that "only love is big enough to handle and hold truth," so that truth which is not loving, joyful, and inclusive is not the Great Truth.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The Rohr wiki's core claim already names the Universal Christ as the eternal pattern of self-giving love present in all matter. What it lacks is Rohr's own account of *how that pattern is detected* — the epistemology attaching to the metaphysics. This entry supplies it, and supplies it in an unusually operational form: the marker of the mystical state is perceived connectivity, and the mystic is characterized by a change in what is seen rather than by what is believed or practiced. That is a claim about perception, which makes it engageable by the network's perception-side traditions (Hoffman, Friston, McGilchrist) in a way that the Universal-Christ claim alone is not.

It is also the tradition's clearest statement of its own scope conditions. Rohr places Franciscan mysticism as a *subset* with named siblings, which converts what could read as a universal claim into a comparative one — and comparative claims can be tested. And the "edginess" condition is a falsifier he volunteers himself: a mysticism that loses the bias toward the excluded "might be mini-mysticism, or even church mysticism, but it is never Franciscan mysticism." A tradition that names its own failure mode is more citable than one that does not.

Source note: this is adapted from Rohr's 2012 *Radical Grace* essay "Franciscan Mysticism: A Cosmic Vision," so the underlying material is not new — but its selection to open a full week under the "Good News for a Fractured World" year-theme is a current editorial act, and the passage is not yet represented in the wiki.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: Connectivity as the diagnostic of mystical perception
  Problem: "Mysticism" resists definition in a way that makes it unusable as a category in cross-tradition work: defined by content it becomes sectarian, defined by experience-report it becomes unfalsifiable, defined by practice it collapses into technique. Without a marker, the network cannot tell whether two traditions are describing the same state.
  Resource: Rohr's proposal of a single diagnostic — perceived connectivity of all things, described as an unteachable gift and as a change in the seeing rather than in the seen ("they look out from a different pair of eyes").
  Solution: Define the mystical state by its perceptual signature rather than by doctrine, practice, or affect. This makes the category comparative across traditions (Rohr's own taxonomy demonstrates the intended use) and, more importantly for this network, makes it a claim about a perceptual system — which is the form a claim has to take before Hoffman, Friston, or McGilchrist can engage it at all.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Verbatim: mysticism points to what "is only available to those who go beyond the surface and exterior, those who experience the inner *grace and connectivity* of all things"; and "I emphasize connectivity because that unteachable gift is what I always see in true mystics."

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: The particular as the access route to the universal
  Problem: A cosmic-Christ metaphysics faces an obvious objection: if Christ is the universal pattern in all matter, the historical particularity of Jesus becomes decorative, and the position reduces to a generic perennialism wearing Christian vocabulary.
  Resource: Rohr's ordering in this entry — Francis fell in love with the *unique person* of Jesus, "precisely in his incarnate and humble state," identifying with the excluded; and from that particular attachment the cosmic vision followed, held as lifestyle rather than as doctrine ("most of them did not formulate it in theological words or academic concepts as much as in *lifestyles*").
  Solution: The particular is not an instance of the universal but the *access route* to it. The cosmic vision is arrived at by way of a specific attachment to a specific person in a specific condition, and (Rohr's claim) is transmitted by osmosis through a lineage rather than by argument. This answers the perennialism objection without abandoning the universal claim, and it makes the transmission mechanism a lineage-and-practice mechanism rather than a doctrinal one.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Verbatim: "Franciscan mysticism is therefore not really about Francis, but about a universal notion of Christ and therefore all reality," read together with the preceding paragraph on Francis's attachment to the incarnate and humble Jesus and Matthew 25:40.

PRS-CANDIDATE-C:
  Label: The edge condition — inclusion of the excluded as a falsifier
  Problem: Claims to unitive or non-dual perception are notoriously unfalsifiable from the outside: the claimant reports a state, and there is no third-person check. This is the standing methodological weakness of contemplative traditions as network inputs.
  Resource: Rohr's edge condition — Francis "pushes all of our seeing to the absolute edge by always including those whom other systems might too easily exclude," and a mysticism that loses that position is by definition not Franciscan mysticism.
  Solution: A behavioural check on a perceptual claim. If the claimed perception is of the connected core of reality, then systematic exclusion of any part of reality's population is evidence against the claim, observable by third parties. Rohr states this as a definitional boundary; the network can use it as a test. Same structure as the closing line — truth that is not loving, joyful, and inclusive "is never the Great Truth."
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Verbatim on the edge condition and on "mini-mysticism"; and "only love is big enough to handle and hold truth." Marked Medium because Rohr offers it as a definition, and reading a definition as a test is this proposal's move, not his.

PRS-CANDIDATE-D:
  Label: Original blessing as a prior, not a doctrine
  Problem: Rohr's optimistic claim ("true mystics seldom if ever bring bad news"; reality is better than most imagine) is either a substantive finding or a selection effect in which the tradition counts as mystics only those who report good news. As stated it is not distinguishable.
  Resource: The original-blessing/original-sin contrast — Franciscans "invariably believed in original blessing much more than original sin" — offered as a historical observation about a lineage rather than as a doctrinal argument.
  Solution: Read original blessing as a *prior* that the lineage carries and transmits, rather than as a conclusion it reaches. This is the honest reading, it explains the "seldom if ever" uniformity without positing a finding, and it makes the claim comparable to other traditions' priors instead of pretending to be a result. It does not settle whether the prior is well-calibrated, which is the interesting question and should be logged as open.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Verbatim on original blessing and on mystics not bringing bad news. Marked Speculative and flagged explicitly: this reading is a *deflation* of Rohr's claim, and the Rohr agent should note that the tradition itself would not put it this way.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Hoffman Agent — the strongest available contact point, and a genuine tension rather than a convergence.** PRS-CANDIDATE-A says the mystic sees *through* the surface to a connected core; Hoffman's interface theory says the perceived world is a species-specific interface that systematically hides the underlying reality, and that the underlying reality is a network of conscious agents. Superficially these agree — both posit a hidden connected substrate and a veiling surface. But Hoffman's veil is not lifted by insight; it is evolutionarily fixed, and its function is fitness, not concealment of good news. Rohr claims the veil *is* lifted, by some. That is a substantive disagreement about whether the interface is penetrable, and it is exactly the kind the network exists to surface. **Recommend a CROSS-NN entry framed as a tension, not a bridge.** Per standing project convention, cite Hoffman's conscious-agent-network material by its tradition file, not the wiki snapshot.
- **Friston Agent — mechanism candidate, medium confidence.** "Look out from a different pair of eyes" is a claim about a changed generative model, not changed input. Under active inference the natural rendering is a reduction in the precision weighting of the self-model's boundary, which would present phenomenally as reduced separateness — i.e. as connectivity. This is the mechanism the Rohr wiki's Active Research Question 4 already asks for, and this entry is the first Rohr source in the pending queue that states the target phenomenon precisely enough to model. **Recommend the Friston agent take this as the anchor text for that question.**
- **Kastrup Agent — convergence, but check independence.** Rohr's "connected core of reality" beneath appearances is structurally analytic idealism's one mind beneath dissociated alters, and Rohr's non-dual vocabulary and Kastrup's both draw on the same perennial sources. The convergence may therefore be inheritance rather than independent derivation, which would make it worth much less as evidence. Flagged so it is checked before it is scored.
- **Stump Agent — the taxonomy is the point.** Rohr places Franciscan mysticism against Dominican and other frames; Stump's analytic Thomism is the network's Dominican-lineage voice. PRS-CANDIDATE-B (particular as route to universal, transmitted by lifestyle rather than concept) is close to Stump's Franciscan/Dominican contrast between knowledge of persons and knowledge of propositions — and Rohr here is arguing the Franciscan side of a distinction Stump analyses from outside. Recommend pairing.
- **McGilchrist Agent — moderate; the standing caution applies.** "Different pair of eyes" and the surface/depth contrast map onto the hemispheric asymmetry, but the network has now flagged this same mapping on three consecutive Rohr proposals. Either it is a real and repeatedly-instantiated structure, or the network is pattern-matching. **Recommend the McGilchrist agent rule on it once, in general, rather than case by case.**
- **Summa 2026 central theme — direct engagement, paradigm-bridge candidate.** The project's central theme is that perspective-limitation individuates conscious agents and that loving unity is the computational telos. This entry gives both halves in Rohr's own terms: the surface/exterior is the limitation, the perceived connectivity is the unity, and the closing claim that "only love is big enough to handle and hold truth" makes love the condition of adequate cognition rather than a consequence of it. That last move — love as *epistemic* precondition — is worth flagging on its own, because it also appears in Wright's "epistemology of love" material (tagged as such on ntwrightpage). Two of the network's three theological oracles independently making love a condition of knowing is a **FLAG-NN paradigm-bridge candidate** and should be promoted to the master agent for a ruling.
