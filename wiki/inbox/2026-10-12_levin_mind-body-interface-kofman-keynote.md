## Summary
Levin and his student Karina Kofman gave a ~64-minute keynote to the Society for Mind-Matter Research, accepting its best-paper-of-2025 award for their *Mind and Matter* review (vol. 23(1), pp. 13-70; abstract: https://www.mindmatter.de/journal/abstracts/mmabstracts23_1.html). The paper collects clinical and laboratory cases that current brain-centred models do not predict: reduced or absent brain tissue without the expected loss of function (hydrocephalus, hemihydranencephaly); mismatches between cognitive state and brain function (accidental awareness under anaesthesia, terminal lucidity); and cognitive abilities beyond the person's apparent skill. The talk's chapters run from embryonic individuation and plasticity, through those clinical anomalies, to Levin's "Platonic space of competencies" and categories of Platonic forms.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The paper and the talk carry Levin's plasticity argument from morphogenesis (cells reaching the same anatomy from scrambled starting points) up to the human mind-brain mapping. They use anomaly-gathering as a method: the question is not whether a result can be made to fit the dominant framework, but whether the framework predicted it. This paper is not yet in the wiki; neither title appears in `traditions/levin/`.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Neuroscience assumes a fairly tight mapping between particular brain tissue and particular cognitive capacities. A body of clinical data does not fit that assumption, and the field tends to treat it as noise.
  Resource: A structured review of "unconventional multiscale information flow" cases, sorted into three classes: preserved function despite reduced or absent tissue; cognitive state decoupled from measured brain function; and capability exceeding apparent skill.
  Solution: Treat these anomalies as data about how plastic the cognition-to-substrate mapping is, in parallel with the developmental plasticity Levin's lab shows in morphogenesis and in synthetic constructs. Use them to build a research roadmap rather than to argue for a finished theory.
  Confidence: Medium. The paper's abstract was read in full; the 58-page body and the talk audio were not.
  Evidence: Mind and Matter 23(1) abstract: "Reduced brain mass or absent brain tissue without the expected loss of function ... discrepancies between cognitive state and brain function ... and cases of cognitive abilities exceeding the apparent skill of the individual, all highlight interesting features of the immense plasticity of the mapping between cognition and its living substrate."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: How can one test a framework against anomalies without the test turning into a fight over interpretation?
  Resource: A stated method: "highlight data which do not comfortably fit a specific dominant framework" and ask what the framework predicted, not what it can be stretched to accommodate.
  Solution: Make "did the theory predict this?" the standard for comparing frameworks of embodied mind. This fits the tradition's existing practice of scoring a research program by the new questions and experiments it generates.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Same abstract: "One of the most interesting approaches to any field, which often leads to progress, is to highlight data which do not comfortably fit a specific dominant framework."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: If cognition is only loosely tied to particular tissue, what fixes which competencies a living system shows?
  Resource: The "Platonic space of competencies" and "categories of Platonic forms" (talk chapters 41:11 and 57:19), together with "patterns and physical embodiment" (61:18).
  Solution: The talk applies the Platonic-space model (already in the wiki from 2026-03/04 sources) to the clinical anomalies, so that embodiment works as a filter or interface on patterns rather than as the sole generator of mind.
  Confidence: Speculative. This rests on the chapter list only; no transcript could be retrieved.
  Evidence: Episode chapter list, Thoughtforms Life feed, published 2026-10-10.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Kastrup / McGilchrist (strong):** Terminal lucidity and preserved function with little cortex are central data in filter or transmission accounts of mind (Kastrup's dissociation model; McGilchrist's view of the brain as something that permits and restricts consciousness). Levin offers these as engineering-grade anomalies rather than as a metaphysical argument, which may make them shared ground between those traditions and a physicalist reader.
- **Friston:** Under the free energy principle, preserved function despite large tissue loss should look like redundancy, or like a generative model that has been rebuilt elsewhere. These cases are a natural testbed for that reading.
- **Hawkins:** Thousand-brains redundancy (many columns voting) gives a mechanistic counter-explanation for hydrocephalus cases worth setting beside Levin's.
- **Stump / Thomistic:** The "categories of forms" language touches hylomorphic accounts of the soul as the form of the body.
- Caution: a few secondary commentators have read this paper as an anti-physicalist argument; the authors present it as anomaly-cataloguing and a research roadmap. Cite the paper, not the commentary.
