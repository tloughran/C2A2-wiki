## Summary
Opening the "Becoming Disciples" theme on Matthew 16:24-25, Rohr draws (from *The Universal Christ*, 216-217 and 212-213) a distinction he does not usually state this sharply: Christ names the resurrected state, while Jesus names the crucified-and-resurrecting *path* to it. He then borrows Ken Wilber's contrast between ascending and descending religions and places himself and Jesus on the descending side — the primary vocabulary is unlearning, letting go, surrender, and service, against the self-development vocabulary he says lurks behind popular notions of salvation. His diagnosis is that Christianity repeatedly converts the descending path back into a climbing one, and that the resulting religion of personal moral perfection and heaven-attainment is what made alignment with empire and colonization possible. He closes by denying that love and suffering are supernatural at all: they are the natural interplay of death and life, and authentic Christianity is a system for learning how to give away one's life, one's love, and finally one's death.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the strongest single statement in months of the *internal structure* of Rohr's cosmic-Christ frame, and it answers a question the rohr wiki's Universal-Christ material leaves open: how the Jesus of history relates to the Christ of the cosmic pattern. The answer given here — state versus path — is a genuine conceptual advance over the wiki's current summary, which treats the Universal Christ as a claim about Christ's extension and says nothing about the mechanism of participation. The descending/ascending axis is also a direct check on the network's own tendencies: it flags that a developmental or maturational reading of contemplative practice (two-halves-of-life, active inference model-refinement) is precisely the "climbing religion" Rohr says the tradition keeps relapsing into. That makes this proposal a constraint on other proposals, not just an addition.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: christ-is-the-state-jesus-is-the-path
  Problem: The Universal Christ frame asserts that Christ is the eternal pattern present in all matter, but leaves unspecified what work the particular first-century human does within that frame — the cosmic claim threatens to make the historical Jesus decorative.
  Resource: Rohr's formulation that Christ represents the resurrected state and Jesus represents the crucified/resurrecting path of getting there, together with his insistence on "a Jesus-kind-of Christ" — a God going to the mat with humanity rather than only presenting a cosmic vision.
  Solution: The two names pick out endpoint and trajectory in one system. The particular is not an instance of the universal but the *route* into it, which is why the cross rather than the resurrection became the central symbol.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Stated directly in the meditation and sourced to *The Universal Christ* (Convergent, 2021), 216-217.

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: descending-religion-as-diagnostic
  Problem: Any account of spiritual development can be restated as a competence one acquires, and the restatement is nearly undetectable from inside. There is no standing test for whether a contemplative framework has quietly become a self-improvement program.
  Resource: Wilber's ascending/descending distinction as Rohr deploys it: the descending vocabulary is unlearning, letting go, surrendering, serving; the ascending vocabulary is self-development, and Rohr's claim is that Christianity has converted the one into the other repeatedly and predictably.
  Solution: The vocabulary itself is the diagnostic. A framework whose primary verbs are acquisitive has relapsed, whatever its stated content — which gives a cheap, applicable test rather than a judgment about sincerity.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The meditation names the distinction, attributes it to Wilber, states Rohr's own alignment with the descending side, and gives the failure mode ("a new form of climbing religion, as we have done so often before") plus its historical consequences.

PRS-CANDIDATE-C:
  Label: love-and-suffering-are-not-supernatural
  Problem: If contemplative transformation requires a supernatural infusion, it is inaccessible to naturalistic description and the network's cross-tradition project stalls at the boundary.
  Resource: Rohr's flat denial that there is anything supernatural about love and suffering — they are natural, and the path runs through the interplay of death and life, surrender and forgiveness, in their basic manifestations.
  Solution: The contemplative path is offered as describable in natural terms without loss, which is what makes it a legitimate target for the Levin / Friston / McGilchrist frames rather than a domain fenced off from them.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The "nothing really supernatural about love and suffering" passage, sourced to *Universal Christ*, 212-213. Note the limit: Rohr is denying that these are *exceptions* to nature, not offering a mechanism.
