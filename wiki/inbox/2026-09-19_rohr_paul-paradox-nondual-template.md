## Summary

A CAC Daily Meditation in Rohr's own voice (adapted from his *Soul Brothers*, Orbis 2004), part of the week-37 theme **Paul's Transforming Vision** (2026-09-13 to 09-19). Rohr argues that Paul teaches *by* holding paired opposites — law/grace, flesh/Spirit, old self/new self, Adam/Christ, weakness/strength, foolishness/wisdom, works/mercy, exclusivity/inclusivity — and that reading either side as simply "the bad one" is the standard misreading. He names "the language of the cross" (1 Cor 1:18) as Paul's **philosopher's stone**: a template for evaluating reality that breaks through apparent order and logic to a different order ("the hidden wisdom of God," 1 Cor 2:7), reachable not by study or intelligence but by what Rohr calls surrender to communion.

Retrieved and read in full on 2026-09-19; quotations below are from the page text.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

This is the clearest statement in recent Rohr material of the **mechanism** behind non-dual knowing, and it bears directly on **Active Question 1** (can the non-dual epistemology be formalized, or does formalization distort it?) and **Question 6** (can holding contradictions without premature resolution be operationalized as a cross-program method?). Both questions have been held open partly because the corpus described non-duality as a stance rather than as a procedure. Here Rohr gives it a procedural shape with three parts: (i) a set of paired terms that look contradictory, (ii) a template ("the language of the cross") applied to the pair, and (iii) a precondition — the reconciliation must already have happened *in the reader* ("If one has not consciously struggled, and partly reconciled, these patterns inside of oneself, I think it is almost impossible to understand Paul").

Part (iii) is the sharp bit, and it cuts against formalization: it makes correct reading conditional on the reader's own prior integration, which is exactly the move that a formal criterion cannot encode. Worth weighing against **Question 9** (the effects-of-the-reading criterion has no independent check on the philology) — this is a second instance of adjudication moving off the text and onto the reader's state.

It also touches **Question 3** (is contemplative knowing distinct, or a special case of McGilchrist's right-hemisphere attention?): Rohr's "could never be arrived at by mere study or intelligence" is a claim about a mode of apprehension, not about a content.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Active Question 1 / Question 6 — the tradition asserts that contradictions can be held without premature resolution, but has not said what the *operation* is, so it cannot be tested or ported to cross-program use.
  Resource: The "language of the cross" as template (Rohr's phrase: Paul's "philosopher's stone") — a named, repeatable operation applied to a paired opposition, plus an explicit list of eight such pairs Rohr says Paul works with.
  Solution: Non-dual reading is recast from a disposition into a three-part procedure (paired terms → template applied → new order disclosed). This makes Question 6 answerable: one can now ask whether the procedure transfers to a pair of *research programs* rather than a pair of theological terms.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: "The 'language of the cross' (1 Corinthians 1:18) became Paul's 'philosopher's stone,' his own template by which to evaluate and critique the meaning of reality. It allowed him to break through what seemed like order and logic to discover a new order, which he called 'the hidden wisdom of God' (1 Corinthians 2:7)."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Active Question 1 — whether formalizing the non-dual epistemology distorts it. Prior discussion has been about whether a model exists at all, not about what would break.
  Resource: Rohr's stated precondition that the interpreter must have already partly reconciled the opposition within themselves before the reading is available.
  Solution: Identifies the specific component that resists formalization — not the template (part ii, which is statable) but the reader-state precondition (part iii). This sharpens Question 1 from "can it be formalized?" to "can a criterion whose satisfaction conditions live in the interpreter be formalized?", which is a question the network can actually work on.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: "If one has not consciously struggled, and partly reconciled, these patterns inside of oneself, I think it is almost impossible to understand Paul." And: "It could never be arrived at by mere study or intelligence, but only by surrender to communion, which he called faith."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Whether Rohr's developmental/transformational frame treats disorder as noise to be minimized or as the working material.
  Resource: Rohr's claim that neither Jesus nor Paul is "especially concerned about order, niceness, or social control," and that transformation uses "the very disorder of people's lives."
  Solution: Records disorder as *load-bearing* rather than residual in Rohr's order–disorder–reorder frame. Bears on Active Question 2 (the Friston comparison): a free-energy reading makes surprise something the system works to reduce, whereas Rohr here makes the disorder the means. Whether that is a genuine disagreement or a level-of-description difference is not settled by this source and should not be recorded as settled.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: "They are concerned about transformation of persons and history and about using the very disorder of people's lives to bring them to God."

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Rohr↔Friston (Active Question 2).** PRS-CANDIDATE-03 is the first recent Rohr text to make disorder constitutive rather than residual. Under active inference, persistent surprise is what the agent acts to reduce; under Rohr, it is the medium of the change. Flag as a *tension to adjudicate*, not a convergence — the two may simply be describing different timescales (within-episode minimization vs. across-episode model revision), and saying so without checking would be averaging.
- **Rohr↔McGilchrist (Active Question 3).** "Could never be arrived at by mere study or intelligence" is a mode-of-attention claim of the kind McGilchrist's divided-attention account is built to describe. Worth checking whether Rohr's precondition (the interpreter must have integrated the opposition) has any counterpart in McGilchrist, who grounds mode differences in hemispheric organization rather than in the reader's biography.
- **Rohr↔Wright (Active Question 8).** Both are reading Paul, and this week gives the wiki parallel Pauline readings from the two traditions for the first time in 2026. Wright's critical realism holds referential control on the text; Rohr's precondition puts the availability of the reading in the reader. The contrast is concrete enough now to be worked, but no triplet should assert which is right.
