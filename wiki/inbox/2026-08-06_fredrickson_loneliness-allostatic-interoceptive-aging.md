## Summary
Ma, West, Wulfekuhle, Escalante, Cascone, Frye, Leshin, Fredrickson, Cohen & Lindquist (2026), *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience* 21, nsaf124. Seventy-three participants rated their loneliness and then underwent fMRI while visualizing socially neutral, pleasant, and unpleasant acquaintances. The team measured functional connectivity in the **allostatic-interoceptive system** (AIS) — the brain regions that predictively regulate the body's internal state in response to socially salient situations. In the pleasant condition, average AIS coherence fell with age, **but only in participants high in loneliness**. In both pleasant and unpleasant conditions, time-varying (dynamic) integration and coherence declined more steeply with age in the lonelier participants. The neutral condition showed no such interaction.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Fredrickson's program has always claimed that the quality of social connection is a physiological variable, not just a felt one — vagal tone, gene expression, longevity. This paper adds a **brain-level** mechanism and, more importantly, a **specificity result**: loneliness does not degrade the allostatic-interoceptive system across the board. It degrades it *only when the stimulus is socially affective*. That is close to a direct neural test of the claim that positivity resonance is the operative unit — the effect lives in the socially-charged condition and vanishes in the neutral one.

It also inverts the usual framing. Most of the lab's output measures what connection *builds*; this measures what its absence *costs*, and shows the cost compounding with age.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Loneliness is robustly associated with worse physical and mental health outcomes in aging, but the mechanism is underspecified — it is unclear whether loneliness degrades general physiological regulation or something specific to social processing.
  Resource: Graph-theoretic static and dynamic functional connectivity of the allostatic-interoceptive system, measured across matched neutral / pleasant / unpleasant social visualization conditions in the same participants (N = 73).
  Solution: The loneliness x age interaction appears in the pleasant and unpleasant conditions and is absent in the neutral one, localizing the effect to affectively salient social cues rather than to general regulatory capacity.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: "Loneliness and age did not interact during the neutral condition to predict AIS functional connectivity, suggesting that the effects are specific to affective social cues." Static AIS coherence decreased with age in the pleasant condition only for high-loneliness participants; dynamic integration and coherence showed steeper negative age associations at higher loneliness in both pleasant and unpleasant conditions.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Broaden-and-build predicts that positive social experience builds durable resources, but the theory has had no account of why the *capacity to be built* should itself decline — i.e. why some people appear less able to convert connection into resource.
  Solution: A candidate reading — if the allostatic-interoceptive system is the machinery that converts a socially salient moment into a regulated bodily response, then loneliness-linked degradation of that machinery is a mechanism by which loneliness becomes self-sustaining: the lonelier one is, the less a pleasant social moment can do.
  Resource: The AIS as a measurable mediator between social experience and physiological regulation, with dynamic (time-varying) connectivity as the sensitive index.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: The paper reports the connectivity interaction; it does **not** test resource-building or an upward/downward spiral. This triplet is an inference the paper licenses but does not demonstrate. Flagged as speculative for that reason.
