## Summary
Da Costa, Seth, Friston, Ramstead and Sandved-Smith propose that beliefs — in the technical sense of probability distributions a system holds over hidden states — can serve as a single hub linking three things that consciousness science normally has to bridge by hand: what an experience is like, what the person does, and what the neurons do. They adopt one explicit technical assumption, that phenomenology is a function of beliefs, and then derive what follows if it holds: quantitative predictions for subjective similarity judgements, cognitive metabolic cost, subjective cognitive effort, and time perception.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Neurophenomenology has been programmatic for thirty years because the mathematics for its "generative passages" was missing. This supplies that machinery from within the predictive-processing formalism, and — importantly for a tradition often accused of unfalsifiability — states its load-bearing assumption openly and makes it testable by whether the derived predictions hold.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Consciousness science cannot connect first-person experience to third-person measurement because the mathematical machinery for such bridges is underdeveloped, leaving neurophenomenology's "generative passages" as a programme rather than a method.
  Resource: A Rosetta Stone hypothesis in which beliefs act as a central hub connecting phenomenology, behaviour and neural dynamics, resting on the explicit assumption that phenomenology is a function of beliefs.
  Solution: A conditional derivation — if the assumption holds, then specific predictions follow for subjective similarity judgements, cognitive metabolic cost, subjective cognitive effort and time perception — completing the passage from beliefs to neural dynamics by drawing on the existing predictive-processing literature.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Peer-reviewed in Neuroscience of Consciousness, published 2026-07-21. The paper states: "This hinges on a central technical assumption that phenomenology is a function of beliefs. We pursue a conditional approach: if this assumption holds, then certain predictions mathematically follow."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: The claim that experience is a function of beliefs is a metaphysical commitment with no obvious empirical handle, so it has not been possible to say what would count as evidence against it.
  Resource: The four derived prediction classes, treated as tests of the assumption rather than merely as results.
  Solution: Converts the metaphysical commitment into a falsifiable one — testing the predictions informs the validity of the belief-phenomenology link itself.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: "Testing our predictions will inform the validity of the central assumption connecting beliefs and phenomenology, and advance the neurophenomenology research programme."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
Connects to Kastrup: this is the physicalist-formalist route to the same bridging problem analytic idealism dissolves by reversing the direction of dependence — the two are direct competitors on one question, which makes the derived predictions a shared test bed. Connects to Hoffman: "phenomenology is a function of beliefs" is a strong claim about what fixes the content of experience, and sits alongside Hoffman's conscious-agent formalism as a rival specification of the same mapping. Connects to McGilchrist on attention as constitutive: cognitive effort and time perception are exactly the phenomenological variables he treats as attention-dependent, and here they are given quantitative derivations.
