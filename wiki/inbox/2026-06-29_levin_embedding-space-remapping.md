## Summary
Hartl, Pio-Lopez, Fields & Levin propose that cognition in both biological and artificial systems reduces to two coupled invariants: the *remapping* of an embedding space and *navigation* within it via distributed error minimization. Biological collectives (cells to organisms) remap transcriptional, morphological, physiological, and anatomical 3D spaces to maintain homeostasis and regenerate structure, while modern AI systems (transformers, diffusion models, neural cellular automata) enact the same remap-and-refine loop over latent embeddings. The paper extends the Fields–Levin framework into a substrate-independent organizational principle of cognition.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is a major theoretical consolidation of Levin's "cognition all the way down" program: it gives a single, formal, substrate-agnostic mechanism (remapping + error-minimizing navigation) that unifies morphogenesis, basal cognition, and machine learning under one description — directly advancing the diverse-intelligence research program and its claim to generality.
