## Summary
Lazard, Queen, Avendano-Galdamez, Varma, Zhou, Berman, West, Firestine, Dasgupta & Fredrickson (Journal of Adolescent Health, Sept 2025) — a between-persons online experiment with young adults (18–29, lower subjective social status, low-to-moderate psychosocial health) testing video social-media interventions designed to push viewers toward in-person social connection. The team systematically varied visual framing (socially connected peers vs. lonely peers vs. safe spaces vs. control) and message framing, optimizing the intervention messages using Lazard's data-driven attention-capture approach. This is the first behavioral test of using social media itself — typically implicated as a driver of isolation — as a vehicle to push young adults toward the in-person weak-tie contact that PRS-09/10/15 identify as the loneliness-relevant variable.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
PRS-10 and PRS-15 establish that weak-tie stranger encounters are the empirically critical variable for loneliness; PRS-16 (PROP-2026-04-27-002) gives a brief stranger-interaction micro-intervention. This new paper takes the next step: a *delivery channel* for that intervention at population scale, using the same medium that is otherwise drawing young adults away from in-person contact. It also brings systematic attention-design (Lazard's optimization for "stopping the scroll, engaging attention, inspiring action") into the positivity-resonance toolkit — a methodological enrichment Fredrickson's program previously lacked.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **C2A2 core signal — flag strongly**: This is a template for how C2A2 might *recruit* into inter-tradition dialogue at scale — using attention-optimized digital messaging keyed to positivity-resonance principles to draw participants into the kind of in-person weak-tie encounter that mature tradition-dialogue requires. The Lazard-Fredrickson method is, in effect, the recruitment funnel for a scaled C2A2 program.
- **Friston connection**: The intervention is optimizing predictive-attention capture under noisy attention conditions — directly relevant for active-inference accounts of attention allocation on social media feeds.
- **Hawkins connection**: The behavioral target (stop scrolling → engage → act) maps cleanly onto Hawkins's hierarchical model of attention switching from automatic to engaged processing.
- **McGilchrist connection**: A practical test of whether attention can be redirected from left-hemispheric token-engagement (likes, scroll) toward right-hemispheric whole-person attentional modes — the medium being used to reverse its own typical attentional capture pattern.
- **Stump connection**: An empirical, attention-design analogue of Stump's claim that narrative engagement (rather than propositional argument) is the cognitive form that draws persons into transformative encounter; here video framing rather than written narrative, but the same epistemic logic.
