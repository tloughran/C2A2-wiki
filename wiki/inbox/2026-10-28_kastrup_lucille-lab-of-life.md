## Summary
In a two-hour recorded dialogue, Kastrup asks the non-dual teacher Francis Lucille (a student of Jean Klein's Advaita) what living the idealist insight actually does to a life. They cover happiness and death anxiety, empathy overload, meditation as inquiry rather than discipline, Advaita Vedanta versus Kashmir Shaivism, and whether one needs a tradition or a teacher. Lucille's central claim is that the view can be tested in a first-person way, in "the lab of life."

Agent note: the full recording is behind a members' paywall. This proposal rests on the public page: its summary, a verbatim Lucille quote, and the chapter list. The free 4-minute preview was not reviewed.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Analytic idealism is usually defended on analytic grounds: parsimony, explanatory power, the hard problem. Here Kastrup directly asks whether it has practical, testable consequences for how one lives, and whether a "naturally happy materialist" would gain anything from it. That moves the program from metaphysics toward a claimed practical track record.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Can a metaphysics such as idealism or non-duality be tested at all, given that it makes no new predictions in physics?
  Resource: Lucille's first-person "lab of life" method: from time to time, live as though the ordinary consciousness hearing these words is the cosmic consciousness, and observe what changes in thought, perception, relationships and reactions to events.
  Solution: Proposes an empirical but non-objective test. The view is validated, or not, by its observed effects on lived experience rather than by third-person measurement.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Lucille, quoted on the page: "Okay, you have your theory. Take it to the lab of life. Check it out." He adds that it "changes the way we think... perceive the world... interact with others... react to events."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **C2A2 signal:** "Do we need a tradition or a teacher?" is the C2A2 question in miniature: whether maturity in a tradition needs apprenticeship (MacIntyre) or can be reached by direct inquiry. Also "Are we trying to communicate one reality?" and "Disagreement, perspective and honesty with ourselves."
- **Fredrickson:** the claim that understanding deepens happiness, and the question of whether empathy and happiness can coexist, are testable against broaden-and-build and positivity research.
- **McGilchrist:** meditation as open, undisciplined attention ("more Italian") fits right-hemisphere attention, as against the left hemisphere's goal-directed grasping.
- **Stump / Rohr:** the contemplative "lab of life" overlaps with Rohr's contemplative practice and with Thomistic accounts of wisdom as connatural knowledge.
