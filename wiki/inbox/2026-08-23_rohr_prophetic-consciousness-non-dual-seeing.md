## Summary
Sunday opener for CAC Week 33 ("The Hebrew Prophets"), adapted from Rohr's *The Tears of Things* (Convergent, 2025), xviii–xx. Rohr's central claim is developmental, not moral: the prophetic books record prophets *changing* — they begin in judgmentalism, anger, and a superiority complex, and that stance collapses over the course of their own writings into something more patient and forgiving. He defines the prophet functionally as one who "sees reality in its fullness and dimension, rather than in dualities like totally right or totally wrong" — i.e., non-dual perception is the prophet's cognitive achievement, not a mystical bonus. The named target sin is idolatry, understood as treating the non-absolute as absolute; and the sharpest application is reflexive: for an untransformed self, religion is the most dangerous temptation, because ego validated by religion is granted advance permission to demean and defraud under a covering of ascribed virtue.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The rohr/ wiki currently carries the Universal-Christ and two-halves-of-life frames but has no entry on Rohr's account of *prophetic* cognition, which is a distinct and more epistemically loaded claim: it says that a specific perceptual reorganization (dropping binary sorting) is what makes truth-telling possible, and that the reorganization happens *over a lifetime* and is legible in the textual record. This is the closest Rohr comes to an empirical claim about developmental cognition, which makes it far more citable in the C2A2 context than devotional material. It also supplies the self-critical mechanism the network needs when contemplative traditions are used as evidence: Rohr himself names religiously-validated ego as the primary failure mode.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: prophetic-development-as-textual-evidence
  Problem: Claims that contemplative development reorganizes perception are usually unfalsifiable — the evidence is first-person and undated.
  Resource: Rohr's reading of the prophetic corpus as a longitudinal record: the same author's stance measurably shifts from judgment toward patience across a single book's arc (*The Tears of Things*, xviii–xx).
  Solution: Treat canonical prophetic books as a naturally-occurring longitudinal corpus for testing whether "moral development" shows up as a *reduction in binary sorting* in the text itself, independent of the author's self-report.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Rohr states the shift explicitly and locates it within the prophets' own writings rather than in later commentary; the claim is in principle checkable against the text.

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: idolatry-as-precision-misallocation
  Problem: "Idolatry" has no operational definition outside a confessional frame, so it cannot enter cross-tradition analysis.
  Resource: Rohr's definition — the habit of making things "God" that are not absolute, infinite, or objectively good.
  Solution: Read idolatry as a precision-weighting error: assigning unrevisable (infinite-precision) status to a model that is in fact contingent. This makes the prophetic corrective a *precision-reduction* operation rather than a moral scolding, and gives the concept a form the Friston register can receive.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Rohr's own wording is about absoluteness and revisability, not about statues or rival deities; the mapping is licensed by his definition but is the proposer's construction, not Rohr's.

PRS-CANDIDATE-C:
  Label: religion-as-adversarial-validation-of-ego
  Problem: The C2A2 accelerator asks what happens when agents are richly informed about one another's perspectives. It has no articulated failure mode for the case where a *tradition itself* supplies the ego with pre-ascribed virtue.
  Resource: Rohr's claim that for the untransformed self, religion is the most dangerous temptation of all, because ego validated by religion has all bases covered.
  Solution: Name a structural failure mode for the accelerator: a tradition can raise, rather than lower, the cost of perspective-taking, by making the agent's current stance non-negotiable in advance. Any protocol that interacts traditions needs a check for this.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Stated directly and at length in the source; also the thesis of *The Tears of Things* as a whole.
