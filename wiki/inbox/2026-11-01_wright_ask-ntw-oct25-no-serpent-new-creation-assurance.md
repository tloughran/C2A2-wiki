## Summary
Wright and Mike Bird take three listener questions. (1) Assurance: reading Romans 10, Wright treats justification by faith as the declaration that a person belongs to God's family, with baptism and the Eucharist as signs of grace that support that assurance. (2) Could creation-fall-redemption repeat forever? Wright answers no, from Revelation 21-22: evil is finally defeated, and the new garden has no serpent in it. (3) Building *for* the kingdom versus building the kingdom: he rejects both naive progressivism and despair, and holds that even small present acts of faithfulness can be taken up into the final new creation. *Evidence caveat: written from the publisher's episode notes; the audio was not transcribed for this run.*

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The "no second fall" answer is a scriptural claim about the *stability* of the eschaton: it is not a reset to Eden's open possibility but a state in which evil is no longer an option. That is the scriptural anchor for any C2A2 claim that loving unity is a *telos* (an attractor the system settles into) rather than one more phase in a cycle. The "build for, not build" distinction constrains how far the network can read human or computational progress as itself producing the kingdom. Citable for Summa 2026 on I-II Q.5 (whether beatitude can be lost) and the Supplementum on the state of the blessed.

## Candidate PRS Triplets
PRS-CANDIDATE-1:
  Label: Eschatological irreversibility (no serpent in the new garden)
  Problem: If created freedom made the first fall possible, why could the new creation not fall again, which would leave creation-fall-redemption as an endless cycle?
  Resource: Revelation 21-22, read as the final defeat of evil instead of a restored but still vulnerable Eden.
  Solution: New creation is not Eden restored to its starting conditions. It is a state in which evil has been removed from the possibility space, so redeemed freedom is stable and does not risk a second fall.
  Confidence: Medium (the episode notes state the conclusion; the argument for *why* freedom stays intact is not visible in the notes)
  Evidence: Episode notes: Tom turns to Revelation 21-22 and the promise that evil will finally be defeated, with no serpent in the new garden.

PRS-CANDIDATE-2:
  Label: Building for the kingdom (present acts taken up into new creation)
  Problem: If God alone brings the kingdom, does present human work matter, and if humans build it, why is the world not improving?
  Resource: Wright's long-standing "build for the kingdom" formula (cf. *Surprised by Hope*), restated against both optimism and despair.
  Solution: Present faithful acts are not wasted and not self-sufficient. They are materials that God takes up into the final new creation.
  Confidence: High (consistent with Wright's published corpus; reaffirmed here)
  Evidence: Episode notes, third segment.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Stump Agent**: Candidate 1 bears directly on Stump/Aquinas on the impeccability of the blessed (beatitude cannot be lost because the will rests in the final good). Wright gives the scriptural-narrative version; Stump supplies the metaphysics of will that explains how freedom survives. Natural pairing.
- **Friston Agent**: "No second fall" can be framed as an absorbing attractor: a free-energy minimum that no longer has an exit route. Flag as **paradigm-bridge candidate**: eschatological stability as attractor-state, scripturally anchored.
- **Levin / Hoffman / Kastrup**: Candidate 2 (present acts carried into the final state) asks for some continuity of identity and information across the transition to new creation. That raises questions for both the mind-monist frames (what persists) and Levin's goal-directed collectives (whether a collective's goals survive a substrate change).
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags**: Possible CROSS candidate tying the Summa 2026 "loving unity as computational telos" theme to the scriptural claim that the telos, once reached, is irreversible.
