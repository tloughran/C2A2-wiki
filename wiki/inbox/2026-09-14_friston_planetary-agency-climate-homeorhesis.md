## Summary
Rubin, Heins, Mitsui and Friston present a proof of concept for a minimal form of *planetary agency*. In a simple coupled biosphere–climate model, the biosphere's internal states infer shifts in net incoming solar radiation through its boundary states — by integrating past and current surface temperatures — and then act, by changing greenhouse forcing, to hold the climate trajectory inside geophysiological bounds. The target being maintained is not a fixed point but a trajectory: *homeorhesis* (a steady flow) rather than homeostasis (a steady state). The authors position this as a formalization of the Gaia hypothesis, extending Rubin et al.'s earlier "Markov blankets in the biosphere" work from description to demonstrated agency.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the free energy principle applied at the largest substrate the program has yet claimed, and it is the sharpest available answer to the standing objection that Markov blankets can be drawn around anything and so explain nothing. The claim here is not that the biosphere *can be described* as inferring, but that a specific model of it acts to keep a trajectory within bounds — which is a falsifiable commitment about greenhouse forcing dynamics rather than a modelling convention.
