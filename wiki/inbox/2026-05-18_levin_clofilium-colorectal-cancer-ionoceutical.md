## Summary
Mathews, Palmer, Hansali, and Levin identify Clofilium — a Kv11.1 (hERG) potassium channel blocker — as a potent inhibitor of colorectal cancer (CRC) growth and metastasis in cell and xenograft models. They report an IC50 of 3.314 μM, Western-blot evidence that Clofilium downregulates key cancer growth pathways and strongly upregulates NDRG1 (a tumor and metastasis suppressor), and an in vivo xenograft study showing significantly reduced tumor volume without overt toxicity. The work frames CRC as a "channelopathy" — a target for ionoceutical therapy that operates on the bioelectric layer rather than DNA.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the first empirical Levin-lab paper to deliver a specific, ready-to-translate small-molecule ionoceutical for a high-mortality cancer with mechanistic readouts (NDRG1 upregulation, growth-pathway downregulation, in vivo efficacy). It moves PRS-02 ("cancer as defection") and PRS-07 ("bioelectric reprogramming as therapeutic paradigm") from conceptual frameworks to a near-clinical drug candidate, sharpening the bioelectric-cancer program's clinical track record.
