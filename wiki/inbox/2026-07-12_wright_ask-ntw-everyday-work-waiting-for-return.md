## Summary
Wright and Mike Bird take three listener questions: does everyday work really matter to God; why must Jesus be fully human and fully divine; and how should Christians live faithfully during the long wait for Christ's return. Wright argues that all kinds of work — he names manual labor and data analysis explicitly — can participate in building for God's kingdom, that the incarnation is essential (not incidental) to salvation because it is where heaven and earth meet, and that eschatological patience is active, hope-shaped faithfulness rather than passive waiting. The episode is the newest in the ANTWA feed (released the week of 2026-07-12, after the July 7 "Kingdom of Heaven vs. Secular Humanism" episode already proposed); exact release date not shown on the Premier page.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Wright's clearest recent statement of the 1 Corinthians 15:58 vocational frame — present labor is "not in vain" because it is taken up into new creation — applied to ordinary secular work rather than church work. It extends the wright/ wiki's Active Research Question 4 (present faithfulness under perspective-limitation) from the Philippians 2:12 anchor to the domain of daily vocation, and gives the Summa 2026 syntheses a citable scriptural anchor for "work as participation in the telos" that the wiki does not yet capture.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Label: work-not-in-vain
  Problem: How can finite agents' ordinary labor have durable significance if the consummation of history is God's act, not theirs — i.e., why work if God will fix everything?
  Resource: Wright's distinction between building *the* kingdom (God's act) and building *for* the kingdom (human vocation taken up into new creation), applied here to all work including manual labor and data analysis.
  Solution: Present faithful work is anticipatory participation: it is preserved and transfigured in new creation rather than discarded, grounding vocation without inflating human agency into the telos itself.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Episode's central segment on everyday work; consistent with Surprised by Hope ch. 13 and the 1 Cor 15:58 reading Wright has defended since 2008.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Label: incarnation-as-heaven-earth-overlap
  Problem: Why is full humanity AND full divinity structurally necessary to salvation rather than a credal formality?
  Resource: Wright's temple-cosmology frame — Jesus as the person in whom heaven and earth actually overlap, so salvation is effected in the joint, not negotiated across a gap.
  Solution: The incarnation instantiates the heaven-earth union that new creation universalizes; salvation is inclusion in that union rather than transfer between realms.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Episode's second question; extends the "heaven and earth meet" motif from The Vision of Ephesians and God's Homecoming.
