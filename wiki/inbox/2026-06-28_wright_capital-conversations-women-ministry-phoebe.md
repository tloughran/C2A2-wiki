## Summary
In this Capital Conversations interview (released mid-June 2026, circulating widely after the Southern Baptist Convention's vote to bar churches with women pastors), Wright makes his historical-exegetical case that Scripture supports women in public ministry. His central move is reconstructive rather than proof-texting: when Paul finished Romans, he entrusted the letter not to a "senior apostle" but to Phoebe, "an independent businesswoman who lives just east of Corinth" (Cenchreae), traveling to Rome — so "the high probability is that the first person to do public exposition of Paul's letter to the Romans was a businesswoman from east of Corinth." His second exhibit is Mary Magdalene, commissioned as the first witness and announcer of the resurrection. Wright reads the restrictive texts (e.g., 1 Timothy 2) as locally contextual, not as a universal ban, and frames ministry roles as conferred by call, gift, and commission within the body.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is a fresh primary statement (past ~30 days) of how interpretive and proclamatory authority is actually distributed in the earliest "people of God" — the load-bearing C2A2 unit. The Wright wiki carries his peoplehood-salvation and corporate-substance material but has no node on the *internal differentiation of the body* — who carries, reads, expounds, and announces the foundational texts and events, and on what basis. Phoebe-as-first-expositor-of-Romans and Mary-Magdalene-as-first-resurrection-herald give a citable, second-temple-disciplined account of role-individuation-by-commission inside one corporate entity. It is directly relevant to Summa 2026 loci on holy orders, the diversity of charisms in one body, and the church as a real composite agent, and it pairs cleanly with the same-day Rohr "chosenness" proposal (PROP-2026-06-28-002) on the move from chosen-few to chosen-all.
