## Summary
Bonus (subscriber-only) episode of *Ask NT Wright Anything*, 21 min. Mike Bird puts to Wright the early-Christian idea, traceable from 1 Corinthians 10:32 through second-century apologists, that Christians are a "third race" alongside Jews and Gentiles. Wright rejects "race" as a dangerous and misleading modern term but affirms the underlying claim: Paul does see a genuinely new people emerging through Jesus, rooted in Israel's story and opened to every nation through the Messiah. His formula for the relation to Israel is "not supersession, but enlargement." He then turns the claim on the present church, arguing that ethnic, cultural and denominational division obscures one of Christianity's most startling claims, that the Church is meant to embody a new transcultural way of being human.

**Evidence quality caveat:** this summary is built from the published episode description; the audio is paywalled and was not heard. Confidence levels below are capped accordingly.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The wright wiki's core claim already defines salvation as inclusion in the new-creation people united with God, but it does not say what *kind* of collective that people is, or how it relates to the collective it grew out of. "Enlargement, not supersession" is a precise structural answer: the new people is the old people with its boundary widened, not a replacement entity. That directly serves Active Research Question 3 (Pauline "in Christ" language and corporate substance), and gives the Summa synthesis a clean scriptural anchor for any day touching Israel, the Church, or the unity of the mystical body (Tertia Pars Q.8 territory).

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: enlargement-not-supersession
  Problem: If the Church is a new people, the standing question is whether it replaces Israel (supersession) or is merely a subset of it; both options make "new" and "continuous" compete.
  Resource: Wright's formula that the Messiah-people is Israel's story with its boundary opened to every nation: continuity of identity with a widened membership condition.
  Solution: Newness is located in the boundary, not in the substance. The same corporate entity persists while its individuation condition changes, which dissolves the replacement-versus-subset dilemma.
  Confidence: Medium (would be High on hearing the audio)
  Evidence: The quoted formula "not supersession, but enlargement" in the episode description, and the description's account of Paul's "radically new" people "rooted in Israel's story."

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: transcultural-humanity-as-ecclesial-claim
  Problem: Church unity is usually argued as an ethical desideratum; it is unclear whether it is also a truth-claim that division would falsify.
  Resource: Wright's move from the "third race" question to present-day division: the Church is *meant to embody* a new, transcultural way of being human, so visible division obscures a claim, not just a virtue.
  Solution: Unity across ethnicity, culture and denomination is treated as evidential: the Church's transcultural shape is part of what it asserts about human nature, so division is a failed demonstration rather than a moral lapse alone.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Episode description's closing argument on church unity.
