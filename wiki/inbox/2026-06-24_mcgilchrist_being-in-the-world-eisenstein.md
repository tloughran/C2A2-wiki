## Summary
A 90-minute Galileo Commission dialogue (recorded 4 June 2026) between McGilchrist and Charles Eisenstein, growing out of McGilchrist's Oxford talk at "The Good, The Beautiful, and The True." McGilchrist argues that the essential role of education is teaching people *how to be in the world* — that fundamental values are intrinsic rather than instrumental, and that our capacity to embody those values constitutes who we essentially are. He links "truth" to the older sense of *treu* (being true to oneself, loyal to others), and the two discuss how to act with agency and centredness rather than passivity in the face of personal and collective challenge.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This shifts McGilchrist's program from cultural *diagnosis* (the left-hemisphere takeover) toward a constructive *pedagogy of being* — answering the open question logged 2026-06-07 about what hemispheric rebalancing looks like at the level of the person, not just the institution. "Being before doing" is the right-hemisphere ground stated as an educational aim.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: McGilchrist's hemispheric program has specified rebalancing institutionally (the Sheldonian/Ralston settings, PRS-29/36) but not what right-hemisphere primacy asks of an individual person navigating daily collective crisis.
  Resource: McGilchrist's claim that the primary task of education is teaching *how to be in the world* — that fundamental values are intrinsic (not instrumental) and that embodying them constitutes the self; truth as *treu*, fidelity to oneself and others.
  Solution: A pedagogy of *being before doing*: orienting from inner state and embodied value rather than instrumental problem-solving, which is the right hemisphere's mode rendered as a teachable disposition rather than a diagnosis.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: "Iain spoke about being in the world – how to be in the world – as the essential role of education... He argued that fundamental values are intrinsic rather than instrumental, and that our capacity to embody these values constitutes who we essentially are. He also reminded us that the word truth is related to 'treu', being true to oneself, and loyal to others."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: How to sustain agency without succumbing to passivity or hopelessness in a culture of "progressive enshittification" — i.e., how the right-hemisphere stance survives contact with a degrading left-hemisphere world.
  Resource: The McGilchrist–Eisenstein joint framing of cultivating inner life *and* corresponding outer engagement ("what is ours to do?") as a single integrated movement rather than a choice between withdrawal and activism.
  Solution: A both/and posture — inner centredness as the precondition for, not the alternative to, effective outer action — modelling how a mature member of the tradition holds attention under cultural pressure.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: "how we can navigate a world of progressive 'enshittification', cultivating both our inner life and a corresponding outer engagement in the world. We all have to ask: what is ours to do?"
