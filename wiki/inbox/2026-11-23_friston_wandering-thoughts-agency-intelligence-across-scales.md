## Summary
A 1 h 25 min three-way conversation on the Wandering Thoughts channel between Karl Friston, Michael Levin and Robert Chis-Ciure (Sussex; chair of Friston's theoretical neurobiology group and Levin's co-author). Friston answers Levin's question about where novel forms come from as a physicist: existence is persistence behind a Markov blanket, and conservation of probability already implies the dynamics life shows. He uses the renormalization group to explain fitness for futures never met, reads Levin's "ingression" through the good regulator theorem, and separates autonomy, agency and feeling. Paired with PROP-2026-11-23-004 (Levin side of the same source).

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Friston states the free energy principle here with almost no neuroscience: a boundary condition on anything that exists, scale-invariant with no top or bottom. He also gives a clear ordering (control as inference, planning as inference, control over one's own inference) and places large language models on it, and he accepts without objection Chis-Ciure's remark that he is close to idealism.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: What is it for a thing to exist, distinct from its environment, over non-trivial time, without importing teleology?
  Resource: Markov blanket; non-equilibrium steady state and pullback attractor; conservation of probability; the Helmholtz–Hodge decomposition into a gradient flow and a solenoidal flow.
  Solution: Anything that persists will look as if it gathers itself against dissipation and circulates through its characteristic states. That yields oscillation at every scale from electrochemistry to life cycles, broken detailed balance and time-irreversibility. The description is scale-invariant: asked whether the universe has a Markov blanket, Friston says yes, and that there is always a higher scale.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: 8:08–14:24; 15:16–16:24.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: How can a system be fitted to environments and futures it has never encountered (Levin's xenobot "home world")?
  Resource: The renormalization group; the adiabatic approximation; Haken's control parameters; the good regulator theorem.
  Solution: The slower scale above (for example the genome) fixes the attracting set the lower scale may explore, and changes so slowly that from below the context is the same in past and future. Fitness for counterfactual futures is therefore expected. To persist in a world is to have its cause-effect structure installed in one's anatomy, with world symmetries appearing as factorization and modularity; Friston offers this as his reading of ingression.
  Confidence: Medium (an argument sketched verbally, with no formal derivation given)
  Evidence: 21:29–27:55; 38:46–42:28; 53:24–55:15.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Autonomy, agency, sentience and consciousness are run together, including in talk about AI.
  Resource: Sequestration of internal states from blanket states at larger scale; control as inference versus planning as inference; precision control and mental action (Metzinger, Solms).
  Solution: Autonomy (thermostats, viruses) needs only control as inference. Agency is selecting among futures by inferring the consequences of one's own actions. Feeling presupposes a further step, control over one's own inferential processing. Friston places large language models at control as inference, saying they lack a generative model of the future consequences of their actions. Chis-Ciure adds that "inference" here is statistical and as-if, not symbolic.
  Confidence: Medium (Friston's stated taxonomy; the claim about language models is asserted, not argued)
  Evidence: 1:06:05–1:13:21; 1:13:58–1:16:45.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Levin (strong):** Friston proposes that everything is a free lunch and that optimization talk is as-if; Levin holds the narrower claim. The autonomy/agency line also presses on cognition-all-the-way-down. Question for mature members of both traditions: does planning as inference occur in a planarian? See PROP-2026-11-23-004.
- **Wolfram (strong):** Cause-effect structure as "just a pattern" (a state lawfully fixing the probability of its own motion) and renormalization as coarse-graining parallel the causal graph and the bounded observer; the attracting set of allowable futures parallels the reachable part of a multiway graph. They differ on stochastic versus deterministic foundations, and Friston admits his form presupposes time. Question: is the fluctuation term primitive, or the mark of a bounded observer?
- **Hoffman:** Existence as measurability behind a Markov blanket reads as interface language. Question: is a blanket an interface in Hoffman's sense or only a statistical boundary?
- **Kastrup:** Chis-Ciure tells Friston he is "quite the idealist" (53:17); Friston takes it as a compliment and says no one has told him the difference. Question: can a deflationary as-if stance and analytic idealism describe the same blanketed systems without one reducing the other?
- **Carroll:** No optimization, no teleology, cross-scale description by coarse-graining; but Friston says he never uses the word "emergence", where Carroll's programme relies on a weak form of it. Question: is the disagreement verbal?
- **Hawkins:** What/where factorization recapitulated in anatomy (ventral and dorsal streams) sits near reference-frame accounts of cortex. Question: is cortical-column structure an instance of installed world symmetry?
- **McGilchrist:** Chis-Ciure links growing hemispheric specialisation to symmetries in the world; Friston grounds feeling in the control of attention (precision). Question: do these support the hemisphere hypothesis or flatten it?
- **Stump / Thomist wiki:** An attracting set that specifies what a thing can become, given the kind of thing it is. Question: is that a nature with ends, or only a constraint?
