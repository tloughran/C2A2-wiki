## Summary
This 2-hour members' session continues the 17 Nov meditation discussion (PROP-2026-11-25-006). Kastrup argues that meditation's original purpose is insight into the nature of reality, not stress reduction, and that such insight can be disturbing: ego dissolution, involuntary movement, existential terror. Drawing on his own psychedelic experience, he discusses several practices. Recognising that "life is not about me" makes thinking the servant rather than the master. He used Jung's shadow concept to prepare for psychedelic trips, including listing his greatest fears beforehand. And a *strong* ego can help integrate mystical experience. Other chapters cover "Logic isn't fundamental (but it is useful)," "Why reductionism holds even under intuitionistic logic," "Is dissociation unnatural?", and "Could suffering protect against nihilism?".

**Reviewer caveat:** the analysis here rests on the public chapter list and summary. The full recording is members-only.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
It applies analytic idealism to practice, specifically to integration. It also takes positions the wiki can test against Kastrup's earlier work: that a strong ego aids integration (a counterpoint to ego-dissolution rhetoric), and that logic is useful but not fundamental while reductionism survives a switch to intuitionistic logic.
