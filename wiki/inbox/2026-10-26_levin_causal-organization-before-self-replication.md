## Summary
Pigozzi & Levin (Allen Discovery Center, Tufts) study the GARD model (Graded Autocatalysis Replication Domain), a chemical-kinetics model of catalytic molecular assemblies that can copy their own composition. They find that causal emergence, meaning predictive information the whole system carries beyond what its parts carry, rises in bursts tied to self-replication. Early causal emergence predicts later self-replication better than standard molecular-dynamics measures. Pushing causal emergence up makes replicators last longer, and pushing it down makes them scarcer. arXiv v2, retitled from the July v1 "Causal Architecture Dynamics Prior to Arrival of Self-replicators...", was posted 2026-10-02.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
It pushes Levin's "cognition all the way down" thesis below biology itself. Organized, integrated causal structure appears before Darwinian selection has anything to select, and it steers what selection later gets. It extends the lab's causal-emergence line (Causally Emergent Alignment Hypothesis; GRN associative conditioning) into the origin of life.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Agents with causal power are usually explained as products of selection among replicators. Can organized causal structure come before replication and shape it?
  Resource: Causal emergence (integrated, irreducible predictive information) measured over time in the GARD catalytic-assembly model.
  Solution: Causal emergence shows punctuated rises tied to self-replication. Its early level predicts later self-replication better than established molecular-dynamics measures.
  Confidence: Medium (single model system, simulation only)
  Evidence: Abstract: "Early causal emergence predicts subsequent self-replication more accurately than established measures of molecular dynamics."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Is causal emergence only a correlate of pre-biotic organization, or does it play a causal role?
  Resource: Interventions on molecular assemblies that raise or lower causal emergence.
  Solution: Raising causal emergence increases how long replicators persist and how abundant they become. Lowering it does the reverse. This makes integrated causality a "functional control knob" before evolution begins.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Abstract: "manipulating molecular assemblies to increase causal emergence raises the persistence and abundance of self-replication, whereas the opposite is true for interventions that decrease causal emergence."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Wolfram:** GARD is a simple rule-based chemical system, and it produces organization before selection. That is close to the ruliology claim that rich structure comes "for free" in minimal computational systems. The causal-emergence measure gives Wolfram's "computational irreducibility" talk a quantitative partner. (Medium)
- **Friston:** Organized structure that persists before replication can be read as a proto-Markov-blanket question: when does a "thing" first exist? It bears on FEP-based origin-of-life work, such as Fields & Levin on the Conway-Kochen theorem and the FEP. (Speculative)
- **Kastrup / McGilchrist:** Integrated, irreducible wholes come before the parts' selection history, which echoes priority-of-the-whole arguments. Mark this as interpretive. (Speculative)
- **Carroll:** Carroll may resist the move from an information-theoretic measure to "causal power." That is a useful rival framing for emergence talk. (Speculative)
