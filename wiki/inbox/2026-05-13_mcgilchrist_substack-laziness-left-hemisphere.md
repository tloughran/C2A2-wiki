## Summary
On April 1, 2026, McGilchrist published a substantive Substack essay directly responding to Tommy Blanchard's published critique of the hemispheric thesis. McGilchrist argues that the mechanistic-scientific cast of mind he is critiquing is characterised by a particular epistemic vice — rigidity, false certainty, and hostility to anything not already pre-categorised — which he calls *laziness* of the left-hemisphere mindset. The essay is methodologically important because it shows McGilchrist defending the *philosophical rigor* of his project against the specific charge that hemispheric theory is empirically loose: his counter-claim is that the apparent looseness reflects right-hemisphere modes of inquiry (relational, context-sensitive, holistic) that are not vices but virtues for the kind of phenomenon (mind, attention, culture) under investigation.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This essay is the cleanest extant statement of McGilchrist's *epistemology of his own program* — what method is appropriate to the object, and why critiques framed in left-hemisphere standards systematically miss the point. It addresses head-on the long-standing reception problem flagged in PRS-16 (Bergson/Kołakowski lineage strengthening the philosophical credentials): McGilchrist now explicitly defends his program as having a methodology, not merely a set of claims. This matters for C2A2 because the tradition-accelerator's whole premise is that mature engagement with a tradition requires inhabiting its native epistemic standards; this essay is McGilchrist demonstrating that move on himself, with his critics as the contrast class.
