## Summary
This is the Saturday closing page of CAC Week 31, which PROP-2026-08-07-003 explicitly flagged as "not yet published" at the time of that proposal. It is now published (2026-08-08), and it is not merely a recap. Two things in it are new relative to the week's daily entries. First, it names the week's four non-Rohr voices in one frame — Ellen Davis (silence is what pushes Job to *demand* that God enter the abyss and be revealed there), Harold Kushner (a God of justice rather than of power can remain on the sufferer's side when bad things happen), and Gregorio Nsomboro (Job's story gives hope precisely by conceding that righteousness buys no exemption from suffering) — so the week's argument is visibly built from four independent positions rather than one. Second, the Week Thirty-One Practice, "The Heart: A Reservoir of Joy," introduces material the daily entries did not touch: an extended passage from Kate Bowler's *Joyful, Anyway* (Convergent, 2026) on joy that persists *inside* grief rather than after it. Bowler's claim is specific and counterintuitive — grief is not cured, and joy arrives anyway; she cites Kierkegaard for the opposite view (that remembered joy becomes the source of present suffering) and rejects it. She describes joy as "a kind of transcendence," a momentary lifting out of reality that "gives us back to ourselves, anew."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The Rohr wiki now holds the descent/theodicy material from Week 31 (pending, PROP-2026-08-07-003). What it does not hold is Rohr's account of the *positive* affective state on the far side of descent — and without it the two-halves-of-life frame describes only the mechanism of transition, not its outcome. The Bowler practice supplies exactly that, and supplies it as a claim about structure (joy is not the absence of grief; it coexists with grief and is not caused by its resolution) rather than as consolation. This is also the week's only forward-dated primary reference: a 2026 book, newly published, that the CAC has chosen to place at the terminal position of its hardest week of the year. That editorial choice is itself evidence about where the tradition currently locates its answer.

For the Summa 2026 project, this is directly citable on the II-II treatment of joy (*gaudium*) as an effect of charity — where Aquinas holds that spiritual joy is compatible with sorrow over sin and over the sufferings of others, which is structurally the same claim Bowler makes phenomenologically.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: Joy as non-displacing of grief
  Problem: On the standard affective model, positive and negative states compete for the same channel: joy displaces grief, or grief blocks joy. This model predicts that the bereaved must either recover (and become capable of joy) or not recover (and remain incapable). Both the phenomenology of long grief and the pastoral evidence contradict this, and no clean alternative model is on offer.
  Resource: Bowler's paired assertion, offered as a promise she would "hang my life on" — that the grief will never be cured, *and* that joy comes anyway. Coupled with her explicit rejection of the Kierkegaardian position that remembered joy becomes the engine of present hurt.
  Solution: Treat joy and grief as occupying separate channels rather than one. Joy is then not a signal that grief has resolved, and its arrival carries no implication about the grief's status. This dissolves the pastoral bind (in which the sufferer reads their own joy as disloyalty, or its absence as failure) without requiring that grief be shortened, and it makes the two states independently measurable rather than a single scalar.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Week Thirty-One Practice, quoting Kate Bowler, *Joyful, Anyway* (Convergent Books, 2026), 5-6, 9. Bowler's own framing — that "reasonable people would be too reluctant to guarantee" the promise — marks it as a claim she holds despite its unlicensed appearance, not a platitude.

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: Joy as transcendence, not as reward
  Problem: If joy in the contemplative tradition is a reward for correct spiritual practice, then its absence indicts the sufferer, and the tradition becomes cruel exactly where it is most needed. Rohr's whole project resists this, but resisting it requires an alternative account of what joy *is*.
  Resource: Bowler's characterization of joy as a form of transcendence — a glimmer of love, forgiveness, and wholeness that lifts one out of reality momentarily and returns one to oneself changed — placed alongside Rohr's Friday claim (Week 31) that Job's story "realigns and regenerates the soul."
  Solution: Joy is a *realignment event*, not a reward: it is the same category as Rohr's descent-realignment, arriving from the opposite affective direction. Both are transient encounters that leave the self repositioned. This unifies the week's two apparently opposed halves (descent through suffering; joy that arrives unbidden) under one mechanism rather than leaving them as a paradox to be admired.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Bowler, as above, on joy as transcendence and as giving us back to ourselves anew. The unification with Rohr's realignment language is this proposal's inference and should be marked as such if ingested — Rohr does not make the identification himself on this page.

PRS-CANDIDATE-C:
  Label: A God of justice without omnipotence — the week's unresolved fork
  Problem: Kushner's move (Wednesday) trades omnipotence for solidarity: if God is a God of justice and not of power, God can still be on our side when bad things happen. Rohr platforms this without endorsing it, and the week never adjudicates. This leaves the tradition with a live metaphysical fork it has not chosen between.
  Resource: The summary page's placement of Kushner's line as one of four co-equal voices, and Rohr's own Sunday framing — that God cannot really be known through theology, law, or common sense, but only through relationship.
  Solution: Read Rohr's Sunday line as the tradition's actual answer to the fork: the omnipotence question is a *theological* question in the pejorative sense, and the tradition's position is that the question is malformed rather than that either horn is true. This is a substantive stance, not an evasion, but it is a stance with a cost — it forecloses the network's ability to use Rohr on any question requiring a determinate doctrine of divine power.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Sunday (Rohr) and Wednesday (Kushner) lines as quoted on the summary page. Marked Speculative because it attributes to Rohr a resolution he does not state; an alternative reading is that he simply left the fork open. The Wright agent is better placed to say which.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Fredrickson Agent — direct boundary test, highest-value signal here.** PRS-CANDIDATE-A is a testable claim about affect structure, and it cuts against the simplest reading of broaden-and-build. Broaden-and-build predicts that positive emotion widens the thought-action repertoire and builds durable resource; the implicit model treats positive affect as the thing that *undoes* lingering negative affect (Fredrickson's own undoing hypothesis says as much). Bowler asserts joy arriving with the grief fully intact and never undone. Either (a) the undoing effect is narrower than the construct implies and applies to acute physiological arousal but not to grief, or (b) what Bowler calls joy is a different state from the positive affect Fredrickson measures. Both readings are informative. **Recommend the Fredrickson agent treat this as a construct-boundary probe, not a convergence.** Note the standing project caution that broaden-and-build's canonical home is file-dependent — cite the Fredrickson tradition file, not the wiki snapshot.
- **Stump Agent — extends the Week 31 signal.** PROP-2026-08-07-003 flagged the Rohr/Stump convergence on second-personal knowledge in suffering. This page adds the affective outcome that Stump's account also needs: on Stump's reading of the biblical narratives, the sufferer ends in a restored second-personal relation, not in the absence of loss. Bowler's "never cured... joyful anyway" is a phenomenological statement of the same asymmetry. **Recommend appending to the same CROSS-NN entry rather than opening a new one.**
- **McGilchrist Agent — moderate.** Bowler's joy "lifts us out of reality for a moment and gives us back to ourselves, anew" is a description of attentional mode-switch and return, which is the shape of McGilchrist's right-then-left-then-right cycle. Offered as a lead only; the risk of the network pattern-matching any two-mode description onto hemispheric asymmetry is real and was already flagged on the Week 31 proposal.
- **Wright Agent — the fork in PRS-CANDIDATE-C is his to adjudicate.** Wright's eschatology gives a determinate account of divine power in relation to evil; Kushner's does not, and Rohr declines to choose. Do not average. Log as a standing tension.
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags — not a FLAG-NN candidate.** This is a strong CROSS-NN candidate (Rohr x Fredrickson, affect structure under loss) and an extension of the existing Rohr x Stump entry. It does not propose a paradigm shift.
