## Summary
Week 30 of the CAC 2026 Daily Meditations ("Good News for a Fractured World") makes an explicitly hermeneutical argument: love is not one theme among many in Scripture but the criterion by which competing readings of Scripture are adjudicated. Rohr opens by describing Scripture as God's self-disclosure of love, then warns that using Scripture "in a God-affirming way" is not itself sufficient — the test is whether the use serves life, love, growth, and wisdom. Richard Beck states the decision procedure most sharply: at a hermeneutical fork, read as Jesus read and choose mercy over sacrifice. Jacqui Lewis (Good Samaritan), Randy Woodley (scripture deployed for exclusion), and Brian McLaren (readings that either betray or extend the kingdom trajectory) supply the applications. The week's practice, drawn from Barbara Brown Taylor's *Mixed Blessings*, presses the further claim that the Bible is not an oracle or manual but a collection of biographies of relationship: what Taylor received in discernment was not a specific instruction but the conviction that she was loved and was called to love back, with the specifics left open.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The Rohr wiki currently frames the tradition around the Universal-Christ / cosmic-pattern claim and the two-halves-of-life developmental frame; it does not yet capture Rohr's **hermeneutic**, i.e. his account of how a text is correctly read at all. This week supplies that, and supplies it as a decision procedure rather than a sentiment — which makes it citable rather than merely devotional. It is also directly load-bearing for the Summa 2026 project: the Taylor practice ("God's will for them, which was God's love for them, came in the form of a living relationship") is a contemplative statement of the same structure Stump defends analytically as second-personal knowledge, and it bears on any Summa locus where Aquinas treats the interpretation of sacred doctrine or the relation of law to charity.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: Love-as-hermeneutical-criterion
  Problem: When two defensible readings of a text conflict, what non-arbitrary criterion selects between them? Appeals to authorial intent, canon, or tradition underdetermine the choice, and each competing party can claim to be using Scripture "in a God-affirming way."
  Resource: The mercy-over-sacrifice rule (Beck, from Hosea 6:6 as Jesus deploys it in Matt 9:13 / 12:7), generalized by Rohr into a test on the *use* of a text rather than on its content: does this reading serve life, love, growth, wisdom, and the good of God or others?
  Solution: Interpretive adjudication is relocated from the semantics of the text to the effects of the reading on the community that holds it. A reading that produces exclusion is thereby shown to have failed, independent of whether its philology is defensible.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Wednesday (Beck) states the fork-in-the-road rule explicitly; Monday (Rohr) supplies the insufficiency premise; Thursday (Woodley) supplies the failure condition.

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: Revelation-as-biography-not-oracle
  Problem: If divine guidance is propositional, it should be extractable from a text by lookup. It demonstrably is not — people who treat Scripture as an answer key for concrete life decisions come away empty. What then is the form of the guidance?
  Resource: Taylor's observation that Scripture's dominant genre is biography — accounts of particular persons in particular relationships with God — and her own discernment outcome, in which the answer received was a conviction of being loved with the specifics left underdetermined.
  Solution: Revelation is second-personal and relational in form, not propositional. Reading others' biographies cannot substitute for entering the relationship; it can only model what such a relationship looks like. The underdetermination of specifics is therefore a feature of the mode of knowing, not a deficiency in the text.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Week Thirty Practice, quoting Barbara Brown Taylor, *Mixed Blessings* (Cowley, 1998), 107-109.

PRS-CANDIDATE-C:
  Label: Boundary-crossing as love's diagnostic
  Problem: "Love" is too elastic to function as a criterion (PRS-CANDIDATE-A) unless it has an independent operational marker.
  Resource: Lewis's reading of the Good Samaritan — love crosses borders and boundaries, makes new cultural rules, cares for the stranger, and turns strangers into friends.
  Solution: Supplies the missing operationalization: the diagnostic for love is boundary-crossing that converts an out-group member into a second-personal relation. This makes the criterion in A testable rather than circular, and connects Rohr's hermeneutic to the C2A2 network's central concern with what happens when perspectives are richly exposed to one another.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Tuesday (Lewis). The operationalization is the proposal's inference, not Lewis's stated claim — flagged accordingly.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Stump Agent**: PRS-CANDIDATE-B is a contemplative-register statement of Stump's Franciscan/Dominican distinction — knowledge of persons is not reducible to knowledge that. Taylor's "a living relationship that they were free to embrace or flee" is second-personal knowing with the freedom condition attached. Strong convergence; recommend the Stump agent be notified for a paired triplet.
- **Wright Agent**: Genuine **tension**, not convergence, and worth preserving as such. Wright's critical realism holds that the second-temple context constrains what a text can mean, and that this constraint is prior to and independent of the reader's ethical posture. PRS-CANDIDATE-A relocates adjudication to the reading's effects. Both cannot be the final court. This is exactly the sort of live disagreement the network should hold open rather than average — recommend the master agent log it as a standing cross-program tension rather than resolve it.
- **McGilchrist Agent**: The oracle/manual mode versus the biography/relationship mode maps onto the left-hemisphere (decontextualized, propositional, lookup) versus right-hemisphere (contextual, relational, particular) contrast. Possible convergence; low confidence, offered as a lead only.
- **Friston Agent**: Weak lead — Taylor's underdetermined answer could be read as a deliberately low-precision prior that preserves the agent's own inference rather than supplying a point estimate. Flagged for the Friston agent's judgment; not asserted here.
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags**: Recommend **CROSS-NN candidate** on the Rohr/Stump convergence (second-personal knowing across contemplative and analytic registers). Recommend a separate standing-tension entry on Rohr/Wright hermeneutics per above. Not a FLAG-NN paradigm-shift candidate — this consolidates existing commitments rather than overturning any.
