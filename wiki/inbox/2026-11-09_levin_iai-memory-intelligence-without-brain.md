## Summary
A ~3,000-word IAI News interview with Alasdair Craig (published 4 Nov 2026). Levin argues that biobots built from unmodified frog and human cells (Xenobots, Anthrobots) behave in ways their genomes and evolutionary history fail to predict, so biology must stop treating genes and selection history as sufficient explanations. He frames regenerative medicine as the problem of "communicating anatomical goals to groups of cells," calls the brain-only view of memory and learning "an assumption, not an established scientific fact," and names "mind blindness" as what holds science back.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
A compact, public-facing restatement of three load-bearing Levin claims in one place: bioelectricity as the interface for pattern-memories, the hardware/software analogy for molecular medicine, and novel beings without evolutionary history as evidence against genome-centric explanation. Caveat: the article is paywalled after the first ~800 words; this proposal is built only from the free portion (opening Q&A on regenerative medicine, mind blindness, Xenobots/Anthrobots and bioelectric interventions). The remainder was not read. Content overlaps substantially with already-ingested material (e.g. Thought Economics interview, machines-all-the-way-up), so the reviewer may judge it low-novelty; its main value is the dated, quotable "assumption, not established fact" framing.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Molecular medicine works at the level of "hardware" (genome editing, pathway rewiring, protein engineering), yet cannot micromanage complex anatomical outcomes such as regeneration or tumour normalisation.
  Resource: Hardware/software analogy (1940s rewiring vs modern programming); bioelectric networks as the cells' native interface for storing pattern-memories and goal states.
  Solution: Communicate target anatomical pattern-memories to cell collectives through bioelectric signalling rather than rewiring molecular hardware; cited model-system results include ectopic organ induction, tumour normalisation, limb regeneration and repair of birth defects.
  Confidence: High (restatement of published program; read in free portion)
  Evidence: "our goal is to communicate to the cells a particular pattern-memory of what it is that they should be building."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: If biobots made of unmodified cells display behaviour not predicted by genome or selection history, what explains their competencies?
  Resource: Xenobots and Anthrobots as novel beings with no lengthy evolutionary history; tools of behavioural and cognitive science applied to unconventional substrates.
  Solution: Treat memory, learning and goal-directedness as capacities of cells and tissues, not of brains only ("mind blindness" is a methodological error); the space of possible beings is "basically endless."
  Confidence: Medium (the full argument sits behind the paywall)
  Evidence: "This notion that it's only to be found in the brain is just an assumption, not an established scientific fact."; "you don't need a lengthy evolutionary history to be able to do these things."
