## Summary
Cervera, Levin & Mafe model the relationship between resting cell membrane potential (Vmem) and gene/protein transcription as a top-down process: rather than transcription being determined purely bottom-up by gene-regulatory networks, the cell's bioelectric state acts as a higher-order control variable that constrains and biases transcriptional outcomes. The paper gives a biophysical account of how membrane voltage feeds back on the molecular machinery that, in turn, sets that voltage.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is a peer-reviewed 2026 formalization of the central Levin thesis that bioelectricity is a genuine control layer "above" the genome. It supplies mechanism for the "bioelectric software" claim (PRS-07) by tying Vmem directly to transcription, and is not yet captured in the wiki.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Mainstream molecular biology models transcription bottom-up from gene-regulatory networks, leaving no principled role for the cell's bioelectric state as a causal control variable over gene expression.
  Resource: A top-down modeling framework linking resting membrane potential (Vmem) to protein transcription, treating Vmem as a higher-order parameter that constrains transcriptional states (Cervera, Levin & Mafe 2026).
  Solution: A biophysical account in which the bioelectric layer exerts top-down causal control over the molecular layer, supporting the claim that Vmem is a real control layer above the genome — and reframing transcription as partly set by whole-cell electrical state.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Cervera, J., Levin, M., Mafe, S. (2026) "Top-down perspectives on cell membrane potential and protein transcription," Scientific Reports 16(1996), doi:10.1038/s41598-025-31696-6.
