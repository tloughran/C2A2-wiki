## Summary
A Thomistic Institute podcast lecture in which Stump treats the seven deadly sins not as a moralizing checklist but as a structured taxonomy of disordered loves — each vice an interior misdirection of the will toward goods that are real but disordered in their object, manner, or measure. This is the natural counterpart to PRS-09 (Seven Cardinal Virtues + Indwelling Holy Spirit), completing Stump's pairing of virtue and vice as the two halves of Thomistic moral psychology.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The Stump wiki captures the virtue side of the Thomistic transformation (PRS-09, PRS-13) but currently lacks an articulation of the corresponding theory of moral failure. Without that, the wiki's account of "what it is to be transformed by grace" floats free of "what is being transformed from." This lecture is also the most accessible long-form Stump treatment of disordered will/intellect dynamics, which are the analytic Thomism counterparts to Friston's active inference (intellect) and motor control (will) — a hinge for the cross-tradition signals below.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Contemporary moral psychology lacks a structured taxonomy of failure modes for the will-intellect system; vice is treated either as character-trait deficit (virtue ethics) or as cognitive error (rationalist ethics), but neither captures the way vices recur as distinctive patterns in human moral life
  Resource: Stump's Thomistic exposition of the seven deadly sins as a structured typology of disordered will — each sin pivoting around a specific real good (pleasure, esteem, possession, etc.) misordered in object, manner, or measure relative to the agent's final end
  Solution: A philosophical psychology of moral failure in which the vices are not arbitrary prohibitions but predictable failure modes of an intellect-will system oriented toward goods that are themselves real — the negative-space complement to her virtue theory, completing a Thomistic dynamics of moral transformation
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Stump's framing of the seven sins as structured around real goods misdirected, paralleling her account in the Seven Cardinal Virtues lecture of how grace perfects the natural powers of intellect and will

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Friston connection**: The "intellect / will" split that Stump uses to structure both virtue and vice maps cleanly onto Friston's separation of inference (intellect ≈ generative model) and action (will ≈ active inference / policy selection). Vices on this reading are failures of policy selection under coherent but misordered priors — a Thomistic precursor to predictive-processing accounts of akrasia.
- **McGilchrist connection**: The vices in Stump's account systematically fix attention on partial, decontextualized goods — a left-hemisphere capture pattern in McGilchrist's framework. This suggests the seven sins are not seven errors but one error (loss of right-hemispheric whole-grasping) refracted through seven domains of human good.
- **Fredrickson connection**: Stump's treatment of sloth (acedia) as the rejection of one's own flourishing has a direct empirical analogue in Fredrickson's findings that some individuals systematically avoid positivity resonance — vagal tone deficits and avoidant attachment patterns as a possible substrate for what Aquinas calls acedia.
- **C2A2 signal**: Vices as structured failure modes of inquiry-bearing communities — pride (refusing to learn from rival traditions), envy (refusing to acknowledge another tradition's goods), sloth (the tradition that has stopped generating new questions). This gives the C2A2 architecture a diagnostic vocabulary for tradition pathology.
- **MacIntyre resonance (Tom's ISM paper)**: MacIntyre's account of tradition-internal corruption and his late-career attention to acedia in *Dependent Rational Animals* makes the Stump-on-vice material a useful bridge for the International Society for MacIntyrean Inquiry paper.
