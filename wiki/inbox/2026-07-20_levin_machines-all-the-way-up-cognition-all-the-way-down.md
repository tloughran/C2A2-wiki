## Summary
Levin and Richard Watson argue that the machine metaphor dominating molecular and cell biology cannot account for self-constructing, goal-driven living agents — but that the dualistic alternative (machine *versus* mind) fails too. They propose a third option, "cognition all the way down," using information theory and Turing-machine arguments to expose where reductionist models break, and inverting the standard causal story to **cognition → code → chemicals**.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the clearest statement to date of the *metaphysical* commitment underneath Levin's empirical program, written jointly with Watson (evolutionary connectionism). Levin flagged the final published version as "quite a bit different than the original preprint" — so the wiki's earlier preprint-era understanding of this argument is stale. The causal inversion (cognition → code → chemicals) is the load-bearing claim and is stated here more sharply than in "Mind Everywhere."

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Molecular mechanism and goal-directed agency appear incommensurable — the machine metaphor cannot generate agency, and dualism cannot be naturalized.
  Resource: A "new kind of cognitivism" — cognition all the way down — unifying bottom-up and top-down causal flows within a single non-dualist frame.
  Solution: Reject the machine/mind dichotomy: living tissue is *both* machine and cognizer, with cognition as the organizing level rather than an emergent afterthought.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: The paper "discuss[es] inadequacies of both mechanist/computationalist frameworks and dualistic conceptions of machine versus mind, proposing instead a new kind of cognitivism."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Standard molecular biology assumes causation runs chemicals → code → phenotype; this cannot explain top-down goal-directed correction of perturbations.
  Resource: Information-theoretic and Turing-machine arguments applied to the reductionist model of gene expression.
  Solution: Invert the sequence — the correct causal ordering is **cognition → code → chemicals**.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Stated directly: "the correct causation sequence is cognition → code → chemicals."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Does the "cognition all the way down" thesis have empirical teeth, or is it a re-description of the same facts?
  Resource: Watson's evolutionary-connectionist machinery brought to bear on developmental plasticity.
  Solution: The claim earns its keep by predicting adaptive plasticity — cells implementing large-scale form and function under novel perturbation — that a machine reading does not anticipate.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The paper offers "examples of collective intelligence and adaptive plasticity as cells implement large-scale form and function."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
**Kastrup (Strong):** "cognition all the way down" is Levin's closest structural approach to Kastrup's analytic idealism — both deny that mind is a late emergent, both locate cognition at the base. They differ on whether the base is *many* cognizers (Levin) or *one* (Kastrup). This is a live disagreement worth staging as an inter-tradition dialogue; note that the wiki already holds a Levin–Kastrup conversation node (PROP-2026-07-08).

**Wolfram (Medium):** the Turing-machine argument against reductionist biology sits directly on Wolfram's computational-irreducibility terrain, and the two reach compatible conclusions from opposite directions.

**Friston (Medium):** "cognition all the way down" and "every Markov-blanketed thing performs inference" are near-identical in scope. Both universalize a mental predicate over all persisting systems; the open question is whether Friston's formalism *derives* what Levin's thesis *asserts*.

**McGilchrist (Speculative):** the machine metaphor's grip on biology is a plausible instance of McGilchrist's left-hemisphere capture of explanation — a possible bridge essay.
