## Summary
A 79-minute recorded Thousand Brains Project research meeting led by Jeff Hawkins with Hojae Lee, reading the hippocampal-formation literature on grid cells and place cells to work out what a cortical reference frame could actually be made of. The chapter structure walks from the older Thousand Brains account (reference frames carried by cortical Layer 4, Layer 6a and grid cells) to a deliberate reset — "Back to the Drawing Board" — and then through the observation that both grid cells and place cells are anchored to distal environmental cues, that more than one grid cell module is needed for a usable reference-frame mapping, two papers on grid-cell remapping, a computational model of grid and place cells, a 2025 paper by Lykken, and a paper on a somatosensory spatial navigation system located outside the hippocampal formation. Hawkins labels the closing stretch speculation.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the companion session to the reference-frame reset two days earlier, and it is where the revision gets its evidence base. It shows the programme's method in the open: when the theory's spatial substrate stops working, Hawkins goes back to the rodent navigation literature rather than to the engineering. The somatosensory navigation paper matters especially — it is a candidate demonstration that grid-like location coding is not proprietary to the hippocampal formation, which is exactly what the cortical claim needs.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Friston**: place-cell remapping is a change of generative model, and the anchoring question — what a reference frame is anchored to — is the same question active inference asks about which hidden states a creature's model posits. A candidate site for a shared formalism rather than an analogy.
- **Hoffman**: if location coding is anchored to distal cues chosen by the organism, then the "space" the cortex represents is a constructed frame, which is Hoffman's interface claim arrived at from neuroscience rather than from decision theory.
- **McGilchrist**: egocentric versus allocentric coding is the neural form of McGilchrist's two modes of attention — the grasping frame centred on the actor versus the frame that situates the actor in a world.
- **Levin**: reference frames in a non-neural modality (somatosensory navigation outside the hippocampal formation) speaks to Levin's claim that the same computational motifs recur wherever a system must model where it is.
