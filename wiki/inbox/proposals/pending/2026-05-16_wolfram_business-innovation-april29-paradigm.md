## Summary
The April 29, 2026 biweekly Business, Innovation and Managing Life Q&A (1h16m) covers three segments: product strategy & company focus (~0:12s), AI's impact on business & society (~11:44s), and productive leadership & paradigm-shifting ideas (~45:44s). The AI segment continues the trajectory of PRS-11 (Computation-Augmented Generation, Feb 2026) and PRS-13 (Mullenweg "what frontier labs are getting wrong," March 2026), now in spontaneous Q&A rather than prepared-essay form: how computation + open infrastructure should sit underneath LLM systems, what the limits of pure scaling are, and what an LLM-using company should *actually* be doing in 2026. The paradigm-shifting-ideas segment is the more interesting half for this tradition: Wolfram describes (a) how to recognize a paradigm-shifting idea before others do and (b) what the institutional / managerial conditions are that let one act on it — material that connects directly to McGilchrist's hemispheric account of attention and to Loughran's tradition-accelerator project. Transcript: https://content.wolfram.com/sites/41/2026/05/Business-Innovation-Managing-Life-QA-April-29-2026-Transcript-.pdf

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the first Wolfram-on-paradigm-shift material the wiki has captured that is *first-person methodological* rather than physics/ruliology content. Wolfram has been quietly building a personal-methodology corpus over years of these livestreams (cf. the standing "Live CEOing" and "Business, Innovation" categories), but most of his published essays do not foreground it. For the C2A2 program — whose entire premise is accelerating mature understanding of rival traditions and detecting paradigm shifts — Wolfram's own working theory of *how he recognizes a paradigm shift in progress* is a directly relevant primary source. The AI segment also extends the open thread on what computation-as-foundation should look like for LLM systems (PRS-11, PRS-13), which is the most C2A2-infrastructure-relevant strand of the Wolfram corpus.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Recognizing a paradigm-shifting idea while it is still pre-paradigmatic is the most valuable and the rarest skill in research and business. Most accounts of this are retrospective (Kuhn, Lakatos), narrative, and hard to operationalize for someone deciding *now* whether to bet on idea X.
  Resource: Wolfram's working methodology, articulated in the April 29 paradigm-shifting-ideas segment (~45:44s): a first-person heuristic ensemble (look for ideas whose implications cascade across previously unconnected domains; look for ideas whose obviousness-in-retrospect is high but whose current advocates are few; look for ideas that change the questions one knows how to ask, not just the answers). Wolfram presents this as practitioner heuristic, not formal theory.
  Solution: A first-person account of paradigm-shift recognition that operationalizes some of Kuhn's retrospective categories into prospective heuristics. For the C2A2 Pattern Detector specifically: Wolfram's heuristic ensemble can be evaluated as a candidate operating definition for what the Pattern Detector should flag as a paradigm-shift candidate.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: April 29 Q&A, paradigm-shifting-ideas segment timestamp 45:44s. Builds on Wolfram's prior writings on "computational thinking" methodology but here applied to *recognizing* shifts rather than *producing* them.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: How should an organization that uses LLMs configure its computational infrastructure in 2026? The PRS-11 / PRS-13 thesis (CAG + open infrastructure as the missing layer beneath frontier-lab LLMs) was published in essay form (Feb 2026) and conversation form (Mullenweg, March 2026); it has not yet been translated into operational guidance for organizations choosing how to deploy LLM-augmented workflows.
  Resource: Wolfram's "AI's impact on business & society" segment (April 29 Q&A, ~11:44s): an in-Q&A application of the CAG thesis to specific business situations — what to compute symbolically, what to delegate to the LLM, where the verifiability boundary should be drawn, and what the institutional cost is of *not* having a computational layer beneath the LLM.
  Solution: An operational gloss on PRS-11 / PRS-13: organizations should treat the LLM as a rulial-space navigator and the computational stack (Wolfram Language / equivalent) as the verifiable substrate. Doing this changes the *kinds* of products and decisions an LLM-using organization can ship — moving from "LLM-only with hallucination risk" to "LLM + symbolic computation with traceable answers."
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: April 29 Q&A AI segment. Consistent with and extends PRS-11 (Feb 2026 CAG essay) and PRS-13 (Mullenweg conversation, March 2026).
