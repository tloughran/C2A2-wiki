## Summary
Week 27 of the 2026 Daily Meditations opens a multi-week series on the Beatitudes (a "Week One" implies at least one more). Rohr contributes two load-bearing formulations: poverty of spirit as "inner emptiness without a need to bolster our own reputation" — living without need for one's own righteousness — and a "weeping mode" in which grief over sin and the world's pain is held "without looking for perpetrators or victims." Guest voices (McLaren, Boyett, Dear, Thomas) frame the Beatitudes as identity-formation ("who we are now and who we want to become") and bind blessing inseparably to justice; the closing practice (Patricia Jordan) reads the Beatitudes as a metanoia program restructuring instinct and will from selfish toward selfless love.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is doing conceptual work, not devotional repetition: the "weeping mode" is a named affective stance that dissolves the perpetrator/victim bifurcation — a non-dual move applied to moral perception rather than metaphysics, which the rohr/ wiki does not yet capture. It also gives the Summa 2026 project a current Rohr source for the beatitudo loci (I-II Q.69 territory — the Beatitudes as the shape of happiness) and for the central theme's claim that unity is reached through, not around, shared grief.
