## Summary
Ma, West (co-first authors), Escalante, Wulfekuhle, Cascone, Frye, Leshin, Carter, Fredrickson, Lindquist & Cohen (2026), *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience* 21, nsag008. Companion study to nsaf124, same N = 73 affect-induction paradigm — participants mentally visualized neutral, positive, and negative self-nominated acquaintances during fMRI. Here the target is the topology of five intrinsic networks (salience, default mode, ventral attention, dorsal attention, frontoparietal), measured by graph-theory metrics for integration with the rest of the brain (participation coefficient) and within-network coherence (within-module degree), both static and time-varying. Findings: static dorsal-attention within-module degree separated the negative from the neutral condition; dynamic ventral-attention participation coefficient separated the negative from the positive condition. The authors read this as support for a **constructionist** account — emotions emerging from flexible network configurations rather than from dedicated affect centers.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Two things. First, the field-level move the abstract opens with: "the examination of the neural mechanisms of affect has shifted from a functional localization approach towards a distributed network neuroscience approach." Fredrickson's program is now being cashed out in the vocabulary of network reconfiguration rather than emotion loci — a substantive change in what a positive-emotion claim even means neurally.

Second, the result is about **attention networks**. Both discriminating findings landed in dorsal and ventral attention networks, not in the salience or default-mode networks. For a program whose founding claim is that positive emotions *broaden the scope of attention*, a finding that affect conditions are distinguished specifically by attention-network topology is close to the theory's home ground — though see the caution below.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Affective neuroscience has looked for emotion in localized regions; that approach has not produced stable region-to-emotion mappings, leaving the neural status of discrete affective states unsettled.
  Resource: Graph-theoretic static and dynamic network metrics (participation coefficient for cross-network integration, within-module degree for within-network coherence) applied to five intrinsic networks during a within-subject affect-induction task.
  Solution: Affective conditions are distinguished by *configuration* — static DAN within-module degree separates negative from neutral, dynamic VAN participation coefficient separates negative from positive — supporting a constructionist account in which emotions emerge from flexible network states rather than dedicated structures.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Direct from the abstract: "Static DAN within-module degree differentiated the negative from the neutral condition, whereas dynamic VAN participation coefficient differentiated the negative from the positive condition. These results are consistent with the constructionist account that emotions emerge from flexible network configurations."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Broaden-and-build asserts that positive affect widens attentional scope, but the claim has rested largely on behavioral measures (global-local processing, thought-action repertoires) rather than on direct measurement of attention-system organization.
  Resource: Dorsal and ventral attention network topology measured under matched positive, negative, and neutral induction.
  Solution: A step toward a neural formulation of the broadening claim — attention-network organization is where the affective conditions separate.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: **This is weaker than it looks and should not be ingested as broadening evidence without the full text.** Both reported discriminations involve the *negative* condition (negative vs neutral; negative vs positive). The abstract reports no positive-vs-neutral contrast. So the study shows attention networks tracking affect, not positive affect broadening attention — the direction broaden-and-build needs is the one not reported here.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **McGilchrist (strong)**: A result in which attention-network *topology* — the balance of within-network coherence against cross-network integration — is what distinguishes affective states is the most quantitative thing yet in the vault for McGilchrist's claim that modes of attention are the fundamental variable rather than a downstream one. The mapping is not literal (dorsal/ventral attention networks are not hemispheres), and should not be presented as if it were.
- **Friston**: Constructionism plus flexible network configuration is close in spirit to predictive-processing accounts of emotion as interoceptive inference; the paper does not make the connection explicitly.
- **Hoffman / Kastrup**: A constructionist result — affect as configuration rather than substance — is the kind of finding both idealist programs tend to read as friendly. Flagged for the pattern detector rather than asserted here.
- **Companion source**: Reads together with PROP-2026-08-06-002 (same cohort, same paradigm, different network target). If both are approved they should be ingested as a pair.
