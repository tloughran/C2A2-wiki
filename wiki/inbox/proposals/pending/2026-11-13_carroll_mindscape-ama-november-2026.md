## Summary
Carroll's monthly solo Ask-Me-Anything episode, answering Patreon supporters' questions. The official transcript was retrieved from preposterousuniverse.com. Three answers carry substance for this tradition. (1) Asked whether he has changed his mind without new evidence, Carroll says yes, often, and names his own move away from moral realism. He then uses Weinberg's 1967 electroweak paper to argue that a better understanding of a theory can count as "new data" for a Bayesian, even when no experiment changes. (2) On free will, he says compatibilists and skeptics like Greg Caruso differ mainly in vocabulary. How we describe agents who make choices is one question; how a justice system assigns desert is a separate one. (3) On cosmic birefringence, he restates the method behind his first paper (with Field and Jackiw): you test a symmetry principle such as Lorentz invariance by writing down a well-defined theory that violates it and showing how to rule it out by experiment.

Retrieval note: the transcript was read in sections around these answers (about lines 341–393, 485–511 of the ~537-line page), not end to end. The episode also covers US politics after the midterms, constitutional design, wormholes, basketball analytics, and other topics not summarized here. A web-search summary said the episode includes Carroll's definition of consciousness. In the transcript, that question appears only in the listener-submission comments; no answer to it was found. Treat that claim as unverified.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The Bayesian "conceptual data" answer gives poetic naturalism a stated account of how theories gain credence without new observations. That is a live gap for any emergence-based program. The moral anti-realism statement is a dated, first-person position change, which belongs in the tradition's track record.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Standard Bayesian updating moves credence only when new data arrive, yet whole scientific communities shift their credence in a theory with no new observations. How should "understanding the theory better" count?
  Resource: Weinberg's 1967 electroweak paper ("A Theory of Leptons") was ignored for about five years because it seemed non-renormalizable. It became dominant around 1972, after 't Hooft and Veltman showed that spontaneously broken gauge theories are renormalizable. No new experimental data appeared in between.
  Solution: Carroll proposes that new ideas and arguments should count as new data for a Bayesian. He openly says he does not know the right formalism for this and is not up on the literature. The answer frames an open problem rather than solving it.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Transcript 0:24:01–0:26:55. Quote: "new ideas or new thoughts should count as new data. But ... how do we count them as new data? This is an excellent question that I don't know the answer to."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Can objective moral facts be derived from the fact that natural selection shaped us to survive and flourish?
  Resource: Carroll's own change of view, reached by reflection and reading rather than new evidence. Evolution supplies preferences, intuitions and inclinations, and these differ in kind from the moral principles we ought to hold.
  Solution: He no longer holds moral realism. He now treats evolved moral instincts as inputs to a constructed ethics, not as objective moral facts.
  Confidence: High (first-person statement)
  Evidence: Transcript 0:24:01–0:24:55. Quote: "So now I'm not a moral realist anymore."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: The free-will debate between compatibilists and skeptics such as Caruso looks substantive. Is it, especially for practical questions such as justice-system reform?
  Resource: A separation of two questions. One is the descriptive vocabulary for physical agents who deliberate and choose. The other is the normative question of desert and punishment.
  Solution: Once desert is set aside, compatibilism and skepticism recommend the same justice policy: rehabilitation and deterrence, not retribution. The remaining disagreement is about terminology. Carroll also rejects "something resembling libertarian free will" as unclear.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Transcript 0:28:39–0:30:56 and 1:11:18–1:12:02. Quote: "They're separate questions. One is ... how we analyze and talk about human behavior. The other is how we organize the justice system."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Theory change without new data (PRS-01) → Arkani-Hamed.** Arkani-Hamed appeals to "inevitability" and mathematical structure as a guide to theory choice (see the arkanihamed tradition's epistemic-guides triplet). Carroll's Weinberg example is the Bayesian form of the same phenomenon: structural understanding moves credence. A bridge essay could ask whether "inevitability" is the kind of conceptual evidence Carroll says he cannot yet formalize.
- **Moral anti-realism (PRS-02) → Stump and MacIntyre-adjacent material.** Carroll's turn is explicitly away from natural-law-style grounding of ethics in human flourishing. This sharpens the existing Carroll–Stump fault line and is a clean test case for C2A2's "second-first-language" exercise.
- **Testing a symmetry by building a theory that breaks it (birefringence answer, 1:20–1:21) → Wolfram and Arkani-Hamed on emergent Lorentz invariance.** This was not drafted as a triplet because it restates 1990 work. It is a methodological signal: emergent-spacetime programs face the question of whether Lorentz invariance is derived or assumed. A listener question on exactly this appears in the comments, but no answer to it was found in the sections read.
