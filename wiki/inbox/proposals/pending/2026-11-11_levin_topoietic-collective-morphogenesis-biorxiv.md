## Summary
Manicka & Levin (bioRxiv preprint, not yet peer-reviewed) introduce "topoiesis" to model Cross-Embryo Morphogenetic Assistance (CEMA) in Xenopus laevis: groups of embryos under teratogenic stress rescue one another with no central organizer. Reported survival rises from 0% for solitary embryos to 92% in collectives of N=306. The proposed mechanism is purely geometric/diffusive: stressed embryos secrete a diffusing stress cue, the collective's outer boundary acts as an absorbing sink so the ratio L/λ (group radius / decay length) sets the steady-state profile, and above a critical L/λ each embryo's bioelectric lattice shifts into a homeostatic state.

Orchestrator note: details above come from a secondary summary (theconsciousness.ai, 2026-11-07); the bioRxiv record itself was not opened this run. Verify figures against the preprint before approving.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Extends Levin's multiscale-competency thesis one level up — from cells forming an embryo to embryos forming a collective problem-solver — with a quantitative, organizer-free threshold (critical L/λ) for when a collective becomes a homeostatic agent.
