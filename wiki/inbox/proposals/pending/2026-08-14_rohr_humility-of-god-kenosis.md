## Summary

A CAC Daily Meditation from the "Mysticism of Francis and Clare" week (9–15 August 2026), curated by Rohr from Ilia Delio's *The Humility of God: A Franciscan Perspective* (2005). It defines Franciscan mysticism as "at heart incarnational mysticism" and reduces God's poverty and humility to two operations of love: "Love gives itself away — this is God's poverty. Love turns toward the other so it can give itself to the other — this is God's humility." The Incarnation is then read as *kenosis*, "the descent of the Word, from the riches of divine glory into our fragile human nature," and Francis's encounter with the leper is presented as the turning point at which that descent became something he could recognize in another person.

**Verification caveat.** The meditation was retrieved in full and read at the URL above (fetched 2026-08-14). But the reader should note what it *is*: this issue carries **no Rohr reflection paragraph** — the body is two long extracts from Delio with Rohr's editorial framing sentences only. Rohr's contribution here is selection and endorsement, not authored argument. Every quotation below is Delio's. That is weaker evidence of Rohr's own commitments than the Clare meditation of 8/11 (PROP-2026-08-14-031), and the confidence ratings reflect it.

Distinct from the pending `2026-08-12_rohr_infinite-in-the-finite-sacramental-universe` (which covers the 8/10 sacramental-universe meditation) and from `2026-08-09_rohr_franciscan-mysticism-grace-and-connectivity`.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

Rohr **Active Research Question 10** asks whether Rohr's account requires divine "need" (PRS-41) to be a genuine structural claim rather than anthropomorphic accommodation, and whether that can be held alongside classical divine simplicity — or whether it is a real break with the Thomist side of the network. This meditation is the closest thing the tradition has yet captured to a positive answer, and it answers in the direction of a break: divine poverty and humility are stated as what God *is* ("It is how God expresses [Godself] in concrete reality"; "The humility of God is not an abstract concept"), not as a mode of appearing to creatures. Delio explicitly forecloses the deflationary reading — "We may be confounded by the mystery of God's humility if we try to understand it through reason alone."

The material is therefore evidence about Question 10, but it is evidence at one remove: it is Rohr endorsing Delio, not Rohr arguing. The tradition should record it as *what Rohr commends*, and flag that the argued version still has to be found in Rohr's own voice before PRS-41 can be strengthened.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Whether Rohr's divine "need" / self-emptying language (PRS-41) is a structural claim about God or a devotional accommodation — Active Question 10, currently open.
  Resource: Delio's two-operation analysis, endorsed by Rohr: poverty = love giving itself away; humility = love turning toward the other in order to give itself. *Kenosis* as the descent of the Word rather than as a temporary posture.
  Solution: Reframes the disputed claim from "God has needs" (which invites the anthropomorphism objection) to "God's mode of being is self-donative" — a formulation that does not obviously require divine lack, and so does not obviously break simplicity. Whether it *actually* survives the simplicity constraint is not settled here; what changes is that the question now has a candidate formulation precise enough to be adjudicated.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: "Love gives itself away—this is God's poverty. Love turns toward the other so it can give itself to the other—this is God's humility." (Delio, *The Humility of God*, 19–20, 25, as reprinted.) Note this is Delio's sentence, curated by Rohr, not Rohr's own.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Whether the Franciscan program supplies an epistemic route to God distinct from inference — the recurring question behind Rohr Active Question 1 and 3.
  Resource: The leper encounter as the reported turning point: God located "not among the popular and the proud… but among the ordinary, the forgotten, the poor and sick and marginalized," with the marginalized person as the site of recognition rather than an occasion for charity.
  Solution: Proposes that the recognition is second-personal and situated — one knows the humble God by encountering a particular person, not by inferring a property. This is the same structural claim Stump makes about second-personal knowledge, arrived at from a different direction.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: "The leper became a source of God's loving embrace and thus someone to whom Francis realized he was intimately related." Speculative because the meditation reports Francis's experience through Delio; nothing here is Rohr's own epistemological claim, and the second-personal reading is the agent's gloss.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Stump (convergence, and it strengthens an open independence check).** Rohr wiki Question 11 asks whether Rohr and Stump arrived independently at the claim that suffering conveys knowledge of a person that propositional theodicy cannot carry. This meditation adds a data point on the Rohr side: the route runs through Franciscan incarnational sources (Delio, 2005; Francis's leper) with no analytic-philosophy contact anywhere in the citation chain. That is evidence *for* independence, symmetric to what the 2026-08-10 log recorded on Stump's side — her route ran through mid-century individualist epistemology and shared-attention research, not devotional literature. Two disjoint citation chains, one conclusion.
- **Stump (tension).** The same passage strains against divine simplicity and impassibility, which the Thomist wing holds. Stump's own open Question 9 — whether the problem of mourning applies to God — is the mirror image of this one approached from the analytic side. Both traditions are now pushing on the same joint from opposite directions, and neither has conceded.
- **Fredrickson (structural parallel neither program has drawn, and a live test).** "Love turns toward the other so it can give itself to the other" is a description of directed other-oriented affect, which is the *mutual care and concern* component of positivity resonance minus the positive-valence requirement. Fredrickson's own Active Question 10 is exactly the boundary test of whether co-experienced grief exhibits the markers of positivity resonance minus valence — and it names Rohr's witnessed grief as the case. The leper encounter is a second instance of that case type: co-presence with a suffering person, no shared positive affect, mutual care present. If the construct has to move to "shared affect with mutual care," Franciscan material supplies the phenomena it would have to cover.
- **McGilchrist (convergence).** "We may be confounded by the mystery of God's humility if we try to understand it through reason alone" is a direct statement that the object resists left-hemisphere-style analytic capture. Consistent with the Rohr↔McGilchrist link already in the wiki; adds nothing structurally new, so it should not be counted as an advance.
