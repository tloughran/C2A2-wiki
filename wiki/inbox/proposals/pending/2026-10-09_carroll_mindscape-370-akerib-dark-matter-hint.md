## Summary
Carroll breaks his usual "we don't chase the news" rule to interview LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) physicist Daniel Akerib about a single nuclear-recoil event in the 2023-24 data (arXiv:2609.02823) that no known background explains. The event sits at higher recoil energy than the classic WIMP search window, carries a global significance of 2.8 sigma, and fits a heavy (best fit ~1 TeV, huge error bars), weakly interacting particle. Carroll frames the episode explicitly as "a good lesson in how science works": blinding, "salting" the data with fake events, neutron-source calibration, and refusing to trust one event or one experiment.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Carroll states his epistemology of science in his own voice here (intro monologue and interjections), not just in a guest's: provisional credence on a weak signal, care over speed, and "don't trust a single event, don't trust a single experiment." It also touches his cosmology home turf (WIMP naturalness under pressure, dark matter vs. modified gravity) at a live moment.

Source note: the transcript was read in full through ~1:04:30 of 1:16; after that, only Carroll's own lines were read. Most substantive content is Akerib's. Carroll's lines are attributed below, and his intro is the main source for the method claims.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: How should a scientific community treat a respectable but statistically weak anomaly (one event, 2.8 sigma) without either hyping it or dismissing it?
  Resource: Carroll's "science in action" framing, plus LZ's procedures as Akerib describes them: blind analysis, salting (a small internal team injects fake signal events, revealed only after the analysis is frozen), neutron-source calibration of the nuclear-recoil band, and a 300-comment internal paper review.
  Solution: Hold the signal provisionally. Publish it either way (a null result would also have been published), let independent experiments (XENONnT, PandaX) check it, and let more exposure decide. Carroll: "Don't trust a single event. Don't trust a single experiment."
  Confidence: High (that this is Carroll's stated view in this episode)
  Evidence: Carroll intro, 0:00-0:04: "It's not at all super statistically significant... They tried very hard to kill it... it is a good lesson in how science works." Exchange at 0:57:24.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Discipline-relative evidence thresholds: the same 2.8-sigma result reads as "take more data" in particle physics and would be a headline in other fields.
  Resource: Akerib's look-elsewhere argument (about 1 in 100 experiments shows a 3-sigma fluctuation, and the field runs hundreds of experiments a year), plus Carroll's aside: "In psychology, this would be huge."
  Solution: An evidence threshold is set by a community's trials factor and its history of fluctuations that went away. It is a property of the tradition, not of the statistic alone.
  Confidence: Medium (Carroll's line is a one-sentence quip; the argument is Akerib's)
  Evidence: Transcript, 0:47:00-0:48:07.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: After 30 years of null WIMP searches, how does a research program respond when its "natural" candidate keeps failing to show up?
  Resource: Akerib's account of the "expand the portfolio" turn (dark sectors, axions, non-relativistic effective-field-theory couplings that motivated searching at higher recoil energies). Carroll's summary: the WIMP non-detection "hasn't led to despair. It's led to, let's think of what else it could be."
  Solution: Widen the search space the theory allows, keep the original search going, and let theory-agnostic searches (here, the high-energy recoil group) find what a narrower prior would have missed. The anomalous event turned up in exactly that broadened window.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Transcript, 0:11:35-0:17:43 and 0:43:13-0:46:32.
