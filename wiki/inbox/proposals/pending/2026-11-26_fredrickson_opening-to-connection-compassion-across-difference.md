## Summary
An online Global Compassion Coalition dialogue (4th event in the 2026 "Insight & Action: The Compassion Science Series") with Barbara Fredrickson, Kamilah Majied (CSU Monterey Bay) and Myriam Mongrain (York, curator). It asks how people keep their hearts open when differences pull them apart, and how everyday micro-moments of joy and love, plus contemplative practice, can soften defensive "us vs. them" habits and build the resources needed to address societal division. Recordings went to registrants; no public recording link was found, so this proposal rests on the published event description only.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Fredrickson applying positivity resonance and broaden-and-build explicitly to *difference and division* — the step from dyadic/stranger connection (already captured) to connection across group boundaries. That is the exact question C2A2 needs answered for respectful inter-tradition dialogue. **FLAG STRONGLY.**

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: When group differences trigger defensive withdrawal ("us vs. them"), how can people stay open to those on the other side?
  Resource: Micro-moments of positivity resonance (shared joy, curiosity, love) plus contemplative practice as builders of psychological resources (broaden-and-build).
  Solution: Proposed that cultivating everyday shared positive moments and contemplative practice loosens defensive habits and supports personal and collective change toward compassion across difference.
  Confidence: Speculative (event description only; no transcript or recording reviewed)
  Evidence: Event description lists four topics — stepping out of "us vs. them" habits; contemplative practice for resilience under social friction; micro-moments of joy and curiosity; personal and collective change supporting everyday compassion.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- C2A2 core: positivity resonance across difference is a candidate mechanism for the "second-first-language" climb between traditions — broadened attention may be the affective precondition for understanding a rival tradition from inside.
- McGilchrist: defensive "us vs. them" narrowing vs. broadened, open attention maps onto left- vs. right-hemisphere modes of attending.
- Stump: second-personal openness and love as union/willing the good of the other — compare Fredrickson's micro-moments of love across difference.
- Caution (not yet in wiki; UNC undergraduate research abstract, White, Fredrickson et al. 2023, https://our.unc.edu/abstract/white-mindful-and-loving-resonance-testing-dose-response-associations-between-duration-and-frequency-of-meditation-practice-and-peoples-experiences-of-positivity-resonance-with-others): that study found meditation frequency did *not* raise positivity resonance (and lowered it among more depressed participants) — the contemplative-practice claim here should be tested against that.
