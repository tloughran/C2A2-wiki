## Summary
A public conversation between Iain McGilchrist and Stephen Wolfram, moderated by Ralston College president Stephen Blackwood, recorded at the May 2026 symposium and released 28 July 2026. Starting from the practical implications of AI, the exchange moves to consciousness, computation, purpose, and whether reality is exhaustively computational. Wolfram argues from computation — simple rules underlying natural complexity; McGilchrist argues that reality exceeds computation, and that consciousness is grounded in embodiment, receptivity, and participation. Ralston's own framing stresses that the two examined each other's *fundamental assumptions* rather than trading conclusions.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is a rare instance of McGilchrist stating his position against a fully articulated rival paradigm rather than against a generic "reductionism." What he has to concede to and what he can resist in Wolfram's computational ontology is a sharper test of hemisphere theory's metaphysical commitments than any friendly interview provides.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Is reality exhaustively computational? If it is, McGilchrist's right-hemisphere "presence" is at best a useful phenomenology and at worst an illusion.
  Resource: The embodiment/receptivity/participation triad, deployed as an ontological claim rather than a psychological one, against Wolfram's simple-rules-underlying-complexity program.
  Solution: McGilchrist's position is that reality has an "irreducible constitution" that computation models but does not exhaust — so computational adequacy of description is separated from ontological completeness.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Ralston's published description: "Wolfram shares his vision through the lens of computation, looking for the simple rules underlying complexity in the natural world... McGilchrist makes the case that reality exceeds computation, suggesting that consciousness is grounded in embodiment, receptivity, and participation."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Cross-paradigm disagreement in philosophy of mind usually stalls because each side argues from its own first principles and neither examines the other's.
  Resource: The symposium format itself — a moderated, unhurried encounter in which each thinker interrogates the other's foundational assumptions, described by the host as "less a debate than an encounter."
  Solution: A demonstrated protocol for productive rival-tradition exchange: mutual examination of assumptions under conditions of intellectual charity, with no requirement that either party concede.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Ralston's description: "The two thinkers do not remain at the level of disagreement, but examine one another's fundamental assumptions with a rare seriousness and intellectual charity. The resulting conversation is less a debate than an encounter between two of the most ambitious attempts to understand reality in our time."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Metaphysical commitments are usually treated as downstream of, and irrelevant to, engineering practice.
  Resource: The observation that AI development now forces once-academic questions (what is understanding, what is a person, what is real) into design decisions.
  Solution: Metaphysics is reframed as an upstream input to technology policy and system design rather than commentary on it.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Ralston's description: "Questions once confined to philosophical inquiry now play an increasingly practical role in the development of artificial intelligence... our technological future depends not only on the capabilities of machines, but on the ideas we hold concerning the human person, knowledge, and what is ultimately real."
