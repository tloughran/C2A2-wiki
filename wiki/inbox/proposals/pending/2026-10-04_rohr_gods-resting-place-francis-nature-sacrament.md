> **Reviewer note.** This is day 1 of a new CAC series, "Francis and the Natural World" (Feast of Francis, 4 Oct 2026). The text is adapted from *Eager to Love* (Franciscan Media, 2024 ed.), pp. 46–48, which is already a canonical anchor work, so this is a restatement rather than new writing. It is proposed because it is the series opener and states the cosmic-sacrament claim compactly. Consider holding it and merging it with the series' weekly summary (expected ~10 Oct).

## Summary
Rohr presents Francis's "nature mysticism" as a real first path to God. Francis treated creatures, including inanimate ones (fire, wind, water, sun, moon, "Sister Mother Earth"), as if they had souls. Rohr follows Bonaventure in reading all things as *vestigia Dei*, traces or footprints of God that reveal a divine pattern running through the Great Chain of Being. Creation, not built ritual space, was Francis's "primary cathedral," and it sent him back to serve the city, much as Jesus moved between the desert and village healing. Rohr calls daily cosmic events "a continuous sacrament." He argues that preoccupation with ritual grows the more we stay untouched by "Reality Itself." He also reports that the biggest breakthroughs in his men's rites of passage came in long silence in nature, where language was not in control.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the creation-wide side of the Universal-Christ frame: if Christ names the divine pattern present in all matter from creation, then matter itself is the first sacrament. The wiki already has the sacramental-universe claim (approved 2026-08-12, "The Infinite in the Finite"). What is new here is the **developmental and epistemic** claim: non-verbal, non-controlled contact with nature produces transformation that verbal and ritual settings tend to domesticate. That is a testable-in-principle claim about which conditions open up a self-model, and it is citable for the Summa 2026 contemplative-life loci and for the Tertia Pars sacraments reframe.

## Candidate PRS Triplets
PRS-CANDIDATE:
- P: Where is divine presence primarily encountered: in constructed ritual, or in creation as such?
- R: Bonaventure's *vestigia Dei* plus Francis's "Canticle" read as nature mysticism; creation as "a continuous sacrament."
- S: Creation is the primary sacrament. Ecclesial ritual is secondary and points toward it, and becomes self-referential when cut off from it.
- Confidence: Medium
- Evidence: Direct statements in the meditation ("primary cathedral"; "continuous sacrament"; the best rituals "can only point" to Reality).

PRS-CANDIDATE:
- P: Under what conditions does deep self-transformation (the false-self to true-self shift) actually occur?
- R: Rohr's 15-year Men's Rites of Passage observation: breakthroughs clustered in extended silence in nature or in "raw and earthy" ritual.
- S: Transformation is made easier when verbal and social control is suspended and the self meets a reality it cannot manage.
- Confidence: Speculative (anecdotal, self-reported by the teacher; no systematic data)
- Evidence: The final paragraph of the meditation.
