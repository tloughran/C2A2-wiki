## Summary
Opening the new "Everyone Is Chosen" series, Rohr reads Romans 11 to redefine chosenness. Election is "definitive and irrevocable" (Rom 11:29), yet "has nothing to do with worthiness… God's choice has to do with God alone, not with us being worthy or ready. No one is ever ready! In fact, the readiness comes from experiencing and surrendering to the chosenness." Chosenness is "for the sake of experiencing mercy" (Rom 11:30–31): not so the elect "can feel superior and saved" — "immature religion always stops" there — but so they "know what it feels like to be God's beloved… to be gazed at with the gaze of God, and to be mirrored by the ultimate mirror," and can then "communicate that chosenness to everybody else." The biblical arc "begins with chosenness for a few, but it always moves toward egalitarian chosenness for everyone," and only those who have "first experienced that boundless abundance in themselves" can convey it. Rohr adds that the creature's inability to love God back as fully as it is loved "keeps us in the realm of desire — always yearning… It keeps us humble and honest."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the most direct recent statement of Rohr's reframing of *election* — the doctrine most prone to elitism — as a relational role conferred for the sake of its own universalization. It supplies the contemplative twin of Wright's Romans/covenant theology and lands squarely on the Summa 2026 central theme: perspective-limited agents individuated precisely so that loving unity can be mediated outward. The Rohr wiki carries the *Universal Christ* as cosmic Christology but lacks a node on **chosenness/election as individuation-for-unity** — the move from "chosen-few" to "chosen-all." The "ultimate mirror / gazed-at" language is also a citable contemplative formulation of consciousness-first metaphysics.
