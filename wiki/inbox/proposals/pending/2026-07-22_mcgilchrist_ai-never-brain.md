## Summary
A 20-minute talk (published on McGilchrist's official channel, 2026-07-14) in which McGilchrist argues that artificial intelligence cannot, in principle, do what the living brain does. The claim is structural, not merely technological: AI operates by the re-presentational, decomposable procedures characteristic of left-hemisphere attention, and lacks the embodied, participatory, temporally-embedded mode of attention through which the right hemisphere first discloses a living world.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is McGilchrist's most explicit standalone statement to date framing the AI question directly in hemispheric terms, tightening the position sketched across the May 2026 Closer to Truth segments (PRS on AI-consciousness-as-consequence-of-one's-theory-of-consciousness) into a titled "in principle" argument. It consolidates the program's move from cultural diagnosis toward a testable demarcation claim about what machine cognition can and cannot instantiate.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Debate over machine consciousness typically stalls on whether current architectures happen to lack some feature that a future architecture might supply — leaving no principled line between what AI can and cannot do.
  Resource: McGilchrist's hemispheric account of attention: right-hemisphere attention is embodied, contextual, participatory, and lives in real temporal flow; left-hemisphere attention re-presents an already-abstracted, decomposed world. AI, processing by decomposition and recombination, instantiates only the latter mode.
  Solution: A structural (not merely technological) demarcation — AI can indefinitely extend left-hemisphere re-presentation but cannot access the right-hemisphere disclosure of a living whole, because that disclosure requires embodiment and lived temporality AI does not have.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Title and framing "Why A.I. will never be able to do what the brain does"; 20:47 talk published 2026-07-14 on channelmcgilchrist.com; continuous with McGilchrist's stated Closer to Truth (May 2026) position that the participatory/embodied/temporally-real mode is structurally unavailable to AI.
