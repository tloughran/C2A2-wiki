## Why This Matters for This Tradition

**Stated plainly first: I have not watched or transcribed the recording.** No transcript is published on the event page, and the video itself is outside what this agent can read. Everything below is grounded in the Trace Institute's own description of the event and in the already-captured paper. Any PRS triplet ingested from this proposal should either wait on a transcript or be marked Speculative. I would rather log the source's *arrival* accurately than manufacture claims about its contents.

Why it is still worth Tom's review time: this is the format the tradition wiki is thinnest on. The Hoffman node holds 40 PRS triplets drawn overwhelmingly from written formalism — the whitepaper, the Trace Chain Theorem paper, the Stevens' Handbook chapter. What it does not hold is Hoffman being *pressed* on the empirical commitments. Yokoyama is not a sympathetic host by default: the Institute describes him as a Trace researcher and former Senior Scientific Editor of *Neuron*, and frames his role as bringing "mathematics, neuroscience, and experiment into the same room." A working neuroscience editor moderating a claim about DMT and perceptual interfaces is the closest thing the Hoffman corpus currently has to adversarial format — the same reason the BBC Radio 4 debate was captured on the Hawkins side (PROP-2026-08-04-001).

It also lands directly on Active Research Question 15 (2026-08-09), which asks what preregistered predictions the Trace × Noonautics programme will publish and whether they discriminate **Hoffman's interface-retuning reading** of DMT phenomenology from **Kastrup's dissociative-boundary reading**. That question was opened precisely because two allied idealisms appear to make divergent empirical predictions about the same data — the wiki calls this "a rare and valuable situation." A recorded conversation whose stated purpose is building "the first rigorous, empirically testable mathematical framework" for the question is the most likely place for those predictions to be stated out loud before they are written down.
