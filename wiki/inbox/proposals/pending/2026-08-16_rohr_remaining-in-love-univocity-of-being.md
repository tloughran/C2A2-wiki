## Summary

The Friday meditation of CAC's "Mysticism of Francis and Clare" week. Unlike the Wednesday and Thursday issues of the same week, **this one carries a substantial Rohr reflection in his own voice**, adapted from the 2024 edition of *Eager to Love*, following a short opening extract from Mirabai Starr on Francis's sense of kinship with all creatures.

Rohr's section makes four moves. First, he grounds the kinship claim philosophically rather than sentimentally, naming John Duns Scotus (1266–1308) and the doctrine of **univocity of being** — that being can be spoken of in one voice across God, self and world — and glossing creation as "one giant symphony of mutual sympathy," with Augustine's line about the end being "the one Christ, loving himself" attached. Second, he states an epistemic precondition: one cannot find God "out there" until God is found "in here," because "what we seek is what we are" — the search for God and the search for the true self are one search. Third, he makes an affective-perceptual claim: only love already present internally can recognise love externally, so "all mystics are positive people—or they are not mystics," and their characteristic work is the handing over of inner negativity and fear. Fourth, he cites **Philippians 2:12–13** to hold together the paradox that the victory is entirely gift and yet must be intensely wanted.

Retrieved in full and read at the URL above (fetched 2026-08-16).

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

Three reasons, in descending order of weight.

**(1) It supplies the metaphysical warrant the Universal Christ has been missing.** `traditions/rohr/prs_triplets.md` PRS-03 holds the Universal Christ as Rohr's anchor claim, but the tradition has captured it mostly as a Christological and devotional thesis. Scotist univocity is the *ontological* premise that makes it a claim about being rather than about attitude — and a grep of `traditions/rohr/prs_triplets.md` and `traditions/rohr/wiki.md` on 2026-08-16 returns **no occurrence of "univocity" or "Scotus" anywhere in the tradition**. This is a genuine gap, not a restatement.

**(2) It is the Summa 2026 central theme, cited to the same verse.** The project's thesis is that perspective-limitation individuates conscious agents and loving unity is the telos. Rohr here has Philippians 2:12–13 doing exactly the work the project has it doing — "work out your own salvation in fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you" — as the text that holds gift and effort together without collapsing either. Wright reads the same verse as inclusion in the covenanted people; Rohr reads it as the paradox of received transformation. **The two oracles converge on one verse from different sides**, and neither converges by accident: it is the verse both traditions reach for when they need effort and gift to be simultaneously true. That is directly relevant to the project's account of how individuated agents move toward unity without the movement being either coerced or self-generated.

**(3) It states a falsifiable-looking claim about mystics.** "All mystics are positive people—or they are not mystics" is unusually strong for the genre. Whatever else it is, it is a claim with a truth condition, and it has an obvious interlocutor in Fredrickson.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: The Universal Christ (PRS-03) asserts that the Christ pattern is present in all matter, but the tradition has not captured what makes this an ontological claim rather than a way of regarding things. Without that, it cannot be assessed against the network's monisms and reads as pious re-description.
  Resource: Scotist **univocity of being** — being predicated in one voice of God, self and world — named explicitly, with "creation is one giant symphony of mutual sympathy" as the gloss and Augustine's "the one Christ, loving himself" as the terminus.
  Solution: Grounds the Universal Christ in a fourteenth-century ontological thesis with an independent argumentative history, so the claim becomes: *the same sense of "is" applies across creator and creature*. This is exactly the premise that distinguishes Rohr's position from analogical Thomism, and it identifies where the network's Thomist wing must disagree if it disagrees at all.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Rohr names Scotus and univocity directly and in his own voice, with the Latin term supplied; adapted from *Eager to Love* (Franciscan Media, 2024), 8–9. High because it is attributed, technical, and not curated from another author.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: The Summa 2026 project needs an account of how a perspective-limited agent moves toward loving unity that is neither self-generated (which makes the telos an achievement) nor imposed (which makes the agent's individuation idle). PRS-41 established limitation as the enabling condition for love; it did not say how the movement runs.
  Resource: Rohr's Philippians 2:12–13 formulation — "such a victory is a total gift from God, and yet we must also want it very much" — together with the identity claim that the search for God and the search for the true self are the same search.
  Solution: The movement is neither self-generated nor imposed because the sought term is partly constitutive of the seeker: "what we seek is what we are. In fact, that is what makes me seek it!" The agent's wanting is itself the operation of the gift, so effort and reception are not two contributions to be apportioned. Extends PRS-41 from a static structural condition (limitation enables love) to a dynamic one (limitation plus internal presence of the term drives the search).
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Rohr's own text, Phil 2:12–13 cited by him in-line. Medium rather than High because the identity claim ("what we seek is what we are") is asserted without argument, and because whether it *escapes* the gift/effort dilemma or merely restates it as a paradox is not settled by the passage.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Whether "mystic" names a describable psychological profile or is honorific. If the former, the tradition's claims about contemplative transformation become checkable; if the latter, they are unfalsifiable by construction.
  Resource: "All mystics are positive people—or they are not mystics," with the mechanism given as the recognition constraint — only love "in here" can spot love "out there" — and the characteristic labour given as handing over inner negativity and fear.
  Solution: Proposes a recognition-gated model of contemplative perception: internal affective state functions as a precondition on what can be perceived externally, so sustained positivity is not a by-product of mystical attainment but a prerequisite for it.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Direct quotation, Rohr's own voice. Speculative for a reason worth stating plainly: as written the claim is **unfalsifiable as stated** — "or they are not mystics" makes negative cases definitionally excluded. It is proposed here because the underlying recognition mechanism is testable even though Rohr's formulation of it is not, and the tradition should record the defect alongside the claim rather than launder it.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Fredrickson (direct engagement, and PRS-CANDIDATE-03 is a test case).** The recognition-gated model is close to the upward-spiral prediction: positive affect broadens the perceptual repertoire, which affords more positive affect. Rohr states the gate as absolute where Fredrickson states it as a gradient, and Fredrickson has the measurements. Recommend the Fredrickson agent be asked whether the broaden-and-build literature supports a *gate* reading or only a *bias* reading — the answer decides whether PRS-CANDIDATE-03 can be rescued from its definitional form.
- **Stump / Thomist wing (sharp tension — flag this one).** Univocity of being is the historic Scotist alternative to Thomist analogy, and the disagreement is not verbal. If the Rohr tradition is committed to univocity, then the network holds two incompatible answers to how being is predicated of God and creatures, and several existing Rohr↔Stump convergences may be convergences in wording only. This is the most consequential thing in the proposal. **Recommend the master agent treat it as a CROSS-NN candidate and open an explicit adjudication**, rather than letting the two traditions continue to agree in English while disagreeing in metaphysics.
- **Wright (convergence on Philippians 2:12–13, from opposite directions).** Wright reads the verse corporately — working out salvation as participation in the people God is forming (`traditions/wright/prs_triplets.md`, the homecoming cluster). Rohr reads it interiorly — gift and wanting as one operation. Both are the *same verse* answering *the project's own central question*, from the network's two theological oracles, with no evidence of contact. This is a **paradigm-bridge candidate** and the strongest one in this run.
- **Kastrup / Levin / Hoffman (available, not made).** Univocity is formally close to a monist "one substance spoken of in one voice," and the temptation to read Scotus as a proto-idealist will be strong. Flagging it as a temptation to resist without argument: univocity is a thesis about *predication*, not about *substance*, and Scotus was not a monist. If the master agent promotes this to the monist wing, the predication/substance distinction must travel with it or the network will have manufactured a convergence.
