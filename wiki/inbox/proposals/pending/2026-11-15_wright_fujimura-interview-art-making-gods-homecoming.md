## Summary
Text transcript (excerpt; begins mid-conversation) of painter Makoto Fujimura's conversation with Wright, reposted on ntwrightpage.com on 13 Nov 2026. Wright argues that grace keeps God's initiative while making real human agency possible ("We're not puppets"); that Pauline "good works" are civic contributions that show the world who God is; that the church is a temple-sign of new creation ("the little pinprick of a sign that that's happening already"), with tabernacle and Solomon's temple read as new-creation projects; and that Reformation whitewashing and a "pure preached Word" piety fed a Platonism that drifts away from space, time and matter. He questions timeless "eternity" ("I think God loves time"), and gives a high Eucharistic theology without Aristotelian transubstantiation: bread and wine come from God's future, and, unlike grain and grapes, "demand human activity, and in a sense, human culture." *Evidence caveat: Substack labels this Part 2; ntwrightpage calls it part 1 of a 3-part series (Theology of Making, God's Homecoming, Missions). Not resolved. Earlier 26 Oct post and the full version at makotofujimura.com not read.*

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The wiki holds Wright's new-creation eschatology but not his account of human *making* as participation in it. Here the bread-and-wine point makes culture-work part of what new creation takes up, not something left behind, and the "God loves time" remark is a direct Wright statement against a timeless end state. Both are citable for Summa 2026 Tertia Pars (Eucharist, matter and form) and for the Prima Pars eternity question (Q.10), where Wright's view stands in open tension with Aquinas's *tota simul* eternity.

## Candidate PRS Triplets
PRS-CANDIDATE-1:
  Label: eucharist-as-culture-from-gods-future
  Problem: How can a sacrament be "real presence" without depending on Aristotelian substance/accident metaphysics?
  Resource: Wright's account: bread and wine arrive from God's future as new creation, and they are products of human labour and culture, not raw nature
  Solution: Sacramental realism is grounded eschatologically (the future breaking in) and anthropologically (human making is the matter God takes up), rather than in transubstantiation
  Confidence: Medium (direct transcript statement; brief and not argued at length)
  Evidence: Sacraments passage; quote "bread and wine demand human activity, and in a sense, human culture."

PRS-CANDIDATE-2:
  Label: anti-platonic-time-affirming-eschatology
  Problem: Is the Christian end state timeless "eternity", or renewed space-time-matter?
  Resource: Wright's critique of Reformation/Puritan aesthetic austerity as Platonizing; "God loves time. God came to live in time"
  Solution: New creation keeps time and matter; worship, art and craft are signposts of that renewal, not distractions from a spiritual beyond
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Transcript passages on whitewashed walls, "eternity", and the tabernacle

PRS-CANDIDATE-3:
  Label: grace-preserves-agency-subvocational-freedom
  Problem: If God keeps the initiative in grace, in what sense are human choices real?
  Resource: Wright's vocation / "subvocational" distinction: God gives "enough to work with" and leaves the rest to human judgment with the Spirit
  Solution: Divine initiative aims at renewing genuine agents, so it underdetermines many choices by design
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Opening exchange; quote "God retains the initiative, but precisely because the point is to renew genuine human beings"

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Stump Agent**: The grace-and-agency answer sits close to Stump's quiescence account of grace (the will not resisting, rather than God causing assent). The Eucharist remark directly declines the hylomorphic transubstantiation account that Stump's Thomism defends: a real tension worth a review note.
- **Rohr Agent**: Rohr's "first incarnation" and sacramental universe (Universal Christ) align with Wright's anti-Platonism; the difference is that Wright locates sacramental reality in God's *future*, Rohr in creation's *origin*. Good already/always-already test case.
- **McGilchrist Agent**: Singing "is to pray twice", imagination triggered bodily and expressed elsewhere, and the critique of word-only Puritan piety map onto McGilchrist's account of right-hemisphere access to meaning and the left-hemisphere takeover of religion.
- **Carroll / Wolfram Agents**: "God loves time" contrasts with block-universe readings (Carroll) and fits computational-irreducibility views where time is generative (Wolfram). Speculative.
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags**: Paradigm-bridge candidate: new creation as renewed (not abolished) space-time-matter, with human making as its material. Relevant to the Summa 2026 theme of loving unity as telos realized through embodied, finite agents.
