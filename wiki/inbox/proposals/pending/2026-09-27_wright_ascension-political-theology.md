## Summary
In a bonus segment attached to this ANTWA episode, Wright argues that the church has drifted toward the wrong commemoration of Christ's political sovereignty: not "Christ the King Sunday" but the Ascension is the proper marker of Christ's present enthronement as "Lord of heaven and earth." He contends that a thin or neglected Ascension theology leaves a vacuum that "authoritarian leaders, Christian nationalism, and the deification of the state" move to fill, and proposes reclaiming the Ascension as, in his words, "the most radical political act of Christian worship."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This extends the political theology of *Jesus and the Powers* (2024, with Bird) — Wright's anchor claim that the kingdom of God is a real-world political event, not a private or future-only reality — into a dated, specific intervention against Christian nationalism. It gives the wiki a citable, contemporary statement of how Wright's "Christ already reigns" Christology cashes out against a live political phenomenon multiple C2A2 traditions are independently tracking this week (see Cross-Tradition Signals).

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: If "the kingdom of God" names a present, already-inaugurated reality (Wright's core claim), what stops that claim from being annexed by state power or nationalist movements claiming to speak for God or the nation — the failure mode critics point to as "Christian nationalism"?
  Resource: Ascension theology — Christ's present, bodily enthronement "at the right hand" — read as a live political claim rather than merely an afterlife or ceremonial doctrine.
  Solution: Wright proposes that a robustly Ascension-shaped Christology functions as a structural check on political idolatry: if Christ, not the state or a movement, already occupies the throne, no earthly authority can claim that seat without committing a theological usurpation — making the observance of the Ascension itself an act of political resistance.
