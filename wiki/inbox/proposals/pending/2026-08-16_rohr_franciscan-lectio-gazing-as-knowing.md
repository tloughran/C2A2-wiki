## Summary

The Wednesday meditation of CAC's "Mysticism of Francis and Clare" week (9–15 August 2026). It pairs a short passage from Clare of Assisi's second letter to Agnes of Prague — counsel to gaze upon, examine and contemplate the crucifix — with a reflection by the late Franciscan friar Dan Riley (1943–2024) on what he calls "Franciscan Lectio."

Riley's argument has two moves worth capturing. First, he cites Bonaventure for a way of "coming to know" that consists in *placing oneself somewhere in order to learn more than one knows*, and says this kind of knowing produces wonder rather than "dualism or certainty" — learning is entering an activity, and one may enter it while it is still dark, veiled or unclear. Second, he reads Clare's "gaze upon" as an instruction to *widen* the focal point rather than fix it, and denies that her gazing was penitential self-examination; on his reading she was looking at the crucified-and-already-risen Christ and thereby "becoming one with the axis of the cosmos." He closes by generalising the practice beyond the crucifix: anything good, true or beautiful can attract the gaze and function as sacred reading, with wonder, gratitude and connectivity arising as the result.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

The Rohr tradition already holds dualistic knowing as the standing problem and embodied love as an epistemology (`traditions/rohr/prs_triplets.md`, PRS-01 and PRS-04). What it does not yet hold is a **mechanism** — a description of what the non-dual knower actually *does*, specific enough to be compared with anything on the empirical side of the network. "Contemplative practice" appears in the tradition as a category (the 2026 Daily Meditations frame, `prs_triplets.md` line 94) but not as a procedure.

Riley supplies a procedure with two named parameters: *enter the activity before it resolves* (do not wait for clarity), and *widen the focal point rather than fix it*. Both are operationally specific. That is the difference between a claim the network can only admire and a claim it can test. It also gives the Summa 2026 project something it has been short of for the contemplative-life questions (II-II Q.171–189): a description of contemplative attention that does not simply restate Aquinas's own vocabulary back at him.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: The tradition asserts that non-dual knowing is a distinct epistemic mode (PRS-01, PRS-04) but has not said what distinguishes it *procedurally* from ordinary attention. Without that, the claim cannot be assessed and cannot be compared with the network's empirical accounts of attention.
  Resource: Riley's two-parameter description of Franciscan Lectio — (a) entry into the activity of knowing while the object is still "dark or unclear or veiled or obscured," licensed by Bonaventure's account of placing oneself in order to learn beyond what one knows; (b) *opening* rather than narrowing the focal point of the gaze.
  Solution: Specifies non-dual knowing as a policy over attention rather than a faculty: suspend the demand for resolution before engagement, and hold a wide rather than a point focus. The reported output is wonder in place of "dualism or certainty" — i.e. the mode is characterised by what it declines to produce as much as by what it produces.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Riley, *Franciscan Lectio* (Paraclete Press, 2022), 51–54, as excerpted; "To learn is to enter activity, even if it is dark or unclear or veiled or obscured"; "I believe she is inviting us to open the focal point of our gaze." Medium and not High because this is Riley's reading of Clare, curated by Rohr — it is two removes from Rohr's own argued position, and Riley marks his central claim as belief ("I believe"), not exegesis.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Whether contemplation in the Franciscan line terminates in the practitioner's own transformation or issues necessarily in action — the standing worry that contemplative traditions are quietist, and a live question for how the network reads action-and-contemplation.
  Resource: Riley's claim that for Clare and Francis *lectio* always moves toward *actio*, and his description of the whole shape as spherical — "within and through Christ and out into the world."
  Solution: Denies that the contemplative act is complete in itself. On this reading the practice has a required outward term, so the action is not an optional application of the contemplation but part of its structure. If sustained, this closes the quietism objection from inside the tradition rather than by external apologetic.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: "what we call 'Lectio' always moves toward *actio*—the loving and healing action, the merciful movement toward justice." Speculative because it is asserted rather than argued, because "always" is doing heavy work with no case that tests it, and because the claim is Riley's about a thirteenth-century pair, not a claim about contemplative practice in general.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **McGilchrist (convergence, and a genuinely close one).** "Wonder … instead of dualism or certainty from our minds" and *open the focal point rather than fix it* is, in different vocabulary, the contrast between broad vigilant attention and narrow grasping attention that McGilchrist's whole programme turns on. The Rohr↔McGilchrist link already exists in the wiki, but the existing link is thematic; this is the first source on the Rohr side that specifies the attentional parameter itself. Worth recording as a strengthening of an existing edge, not as a new one.
- **Friston (bridge candidate, and it cuts against the easy reading).** Rohr wiki Active Question 4 asks what contemplation looks like under active inference, and the network's working guess has been *precision reduction*. Riley's first parameter — engage before the object resolves — is consistent with that. But his second parameter, widening the focal point, is about the *breadth of the generative model's attentional field*, not about the precision weighting on prediction errors. Those are separable, and the tradition has been conflating them. Flagging the conflation is the finding here; the Friston agent should be asked which of the two the contemplative literature is actually describing, because the answer changes what a test would look like.
- **Stump (convergence, second-personal knowing).** Riley's insistence that Clare's gazing was *not* penitential self-examination but attention to a person — "We come with our questions. We can be honest and real" — is Franciscan-devotional phrasing for what Stump argues analytically about knowledge of persons. Note for the independence check already open on the Rohr side (see PROP-2026-08-14-032): this citation chain runs Clare → Bonaventure → Riley, with no analytic contact, which is another disjoint route to the same conclusion.
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags.** Not a FLAG-NN candidate — nothing here overturns a paradigm. Recommend the master agent treat PRS-CANDIDATE-01 as a **CROSS-NN candidate** only in virtue of the Friston conflation it exposes, which is a defect in the network's own reading rather than a claim of the source.
