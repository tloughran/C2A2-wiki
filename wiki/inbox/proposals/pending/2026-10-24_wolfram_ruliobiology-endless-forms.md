## Summary
Wolfram proposes "ruliobiology," a metamodel of biology in which adaptive evolution acting on simple computational rules (modeled as symmetric 5-color cellular automata, "rulioorganisms," with rule = genotype and grown pattern = phenotype) yields biological-style form. Computational irreducibility supplies the endless novelty ("polyplexy"), and observed forms are described as "frozen pieces of computational irreducibility" that happen to work. The study reports modular "organs" joined by random "connective tissue," conserved early development with varying later embellishments, convergent evolutionary paths, and only partial predictability of ancestry.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Wolfram's most direct extension of the ruliad/computational-irreducibility program into biology. It answers the open item in PRS-03 (connection of Wolfram Physics to morphogenesis) with a concrete model. Ruliobiology is framed as a coherent slice of the ruliad selected by adaptive evolution.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Why does biology display such diverse, modular, apparently designed form, and can it be explained without detailed mechanism-by-mechanism accounts?
  Resource: Ruliobiology: rulioorganisms (5-color cellular automata) under adaptive evolution; the "rulial ensemble" of rules reachable under computationally simple fitness functions; polyplexy (novelty at every scale)
  Solution: Adaptive evolution plus computational irreducibility makes much biological form partly inevitable and general across fitness functions; Darwin's "laws" are computational, history-dependent principles rather than physics-style formulas
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Reported modular organs with randomness-filled connective tissue, conserved early development across lineages, convergent forms via different routes, boundary fractal dimension near 1.3 (per the article)

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: How predictable is evolutionary ancestry and trait co-variation?
  Resource: Simulated lineages of rulioorganisms; "bulk orchestration" (parts behave differently in isolation than in the whole)
  Solution: Ancestry is only partly predictable; coarse features give no simple "law of motion"; traits can correlate positively without Pareto tradeoffs, so some outliers "win on all counts"
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Selecting for lifetime produces extreme side-effect traits (e.g., width) more than selecting for width directly (per the article)
