## Summary
The Week-22 Daily Meditations series develops Rohr's standing claim that "the dualistic mind cannot deal with the biggies: love, death, suffering, God, infinity, and the very notion of grace." Across the week (Rohr, plus Jen Austin, Verna Dozier, Cassidy Hall, Jacqui Lewis), the conceptual move is from binary cognition — the mind that sorts reality into in/out, right/wrong, self/other boxes — to a non-dual mode that can hold paradox and provisionality. Dozier's "I do not live by being right, so I am not destroyed by being wrong" reframes epistemic humility as the precondition of love; Hall's "we can hold what we think we know about ourselves with open hands" extends non-dual openness to self-knowledge; Lewis frames "fierce love" as the crossing of boundaries that binary thinking erects.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The Rohr wiki names non-dual awareness as a core commitment but lacks a node treating it as an *epistemology* — a stance toward knowing and being wrong — rather than only a contemplative state. This series supplies exactly that: non-dual mind as the capacity to hold provisional knowledge without being destroyed by error, which is the cognitive shape of contemplative humility. It is citable for the Summa 2026 contemplative-life loci (II-II Q.171–189) and anywhere the synthesis treats the limits of conceptual knowing of God (the via negativa register Aquinas shares).
