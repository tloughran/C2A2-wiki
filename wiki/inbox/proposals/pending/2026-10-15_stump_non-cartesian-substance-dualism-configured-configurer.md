## Summary
Stump's foundational statement of Aquinas's philosophy of mind (Faith and Philosophy 12.4, pp. 505-531; DOI 10.5840/faithphil199512430; open-access PDF read in full, all 27 pages including notes). She reads Aquinas's "form" as an *essentially configurational state* — dynamic, not shape — and the human soul as a **"configured configurer"**: like an angel it is itself configured (so it can subsist), but like the form of any material thing it configures matter (so it is not a complete substance and does not interact causally with the body). Her conclusion is that "the battle lines between dualism and materialism are misdrawn": a restricted materialism (mental states implemented in neural stuff) is compatible with a non-Cartesian dualism.

**Why propose a 1995 paper:** it is not recent, so it passes the quality filter only on the "significant work not yet captured" clause. A grep of `traditions/stump/` and every proposal folder found no prior capture of this paper, the phrase "configured configurer", or the separated-soul argument. The existing hylomorphism triplets (PRS on collective neuroscience, the 2026 *Natural Law, Metaphysics, and the Creator* anti-reductionism argument) all presuppose this paper's account of form without citing it. It is the root text the later triplets hang from. Nothing new from Stump herself turned up in the 30-day window (see run notes).

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is where Stump's hylomorphism is worked out in biochemical terms (the C/EBP gene-regulating protein, prion misfolding, chaperone-assisted protein folding) rather than in theological ones. That makes it the strongest single bridge in the corpus between Thomistic metaphysics and contemporary biology, which is exactly the "hylomorphism as bridge between tradition and science" signal the agent brief flags.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Christianity is widely taken to be committed to Cartesian substance dualism, and Cartesian dualism is widely taken to be false (interaction problem; cognition split between soul-only and body-only functions) — so the religion appears saddled with a refuted philosophy of mind.
  Resource: Aquinas's explicit rejection of the "Platonic" view that a human being is "a soul using a body" (ST I q.89 a.1; SCG II.57; QDA q.9), together with his thesis that the intellective soul is the single substantial form of the body (ST I q.76 a.4).
  Solution: Cartesian dualism is not essential to the major monotheisms; at least one major strand (Aquinas) rejects it "unconditionally" while still affirming survival of death.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: "Aquinas, however, has an account of the soul which is non-Cartesian in character... it suggests not only that Cartesian dualism isn't essential to Christianity but also that the battle lines between dualism and materialism are misdrawn" (abstract, p. 505).

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: If the soul is the form of the body, how can it exist and act after death? A configurational state with nothing configured looks incoherent, and a property-like form "jumping category" into a substance looks impossible.
  Resource: The distinction between forms that *configure* (form of an amethyst), forms that are *configured* (God, angels — "being, even divine being, is being configured"), and the heuristic of the C/EBP protein, which is configured and also configures DNA, and does not change nature when it starts or stops binding.
  Solution: The human soul is a "configured configurer" — "the amphibians of this metaphysical world." At death it stops configuring matter without any category switch, just as unbound C/EBP is still the same molecule. Stump flags four disanalogies herself (note 43), so the protein is heuristic, not exemplary.
  Confidence: High (as a statement of Stump's reading); Medium (as an interpretation of Aquinas — Swinburne, Toner, and Butera dispute it)
  Evidence: "The human soul, then, is a configured configurer. Consequently, in the transition from configuring matter to not configuring matter, the human soul doesn't undergo any radical metaphysical transformation or category switching, any more than the molecule C/EBP does" (pp. 514-515).

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Does reductionism follow from materialism — is a whole "nothing but" its parts?
  Resource: Aquinas's matter-form layering (proton → amino acid → protein subunit → dimer), read with Searle's two senses of emergence; the chaperone-folding case, where a protein's active shape cannot be derived from its atoms' causal potentialities alone; the prion case, where identical composition and different configuration produce health versus spongiform disease.
  Solution: What emerges when form is imposed on matter is "not just properties but substances." The configuration of micro-level parts "isn't itself a microlevel property," so macro-level causal powers (gene regulation) can belong to the whole and not the parts. Reducing a composite to its constituents "is to think that the form of the whole is nothing."
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: pp. 510-511 and p. 522; note 16 separates "causal interactions" in sense (i) (parts' potentialities) from sense (ii) (interactions as configured), warning that sense (ii) "smuggle[s] the configuration... into the properties of the parts."

PRS-CANDIDATE-04:
  Problem: Where does Aquinas sit in the contemporary taxonomy — dualist or physicalist?
  Resource: Comparison with Richard Boyd's "materialism without reductionism," on which mental states are "purely configurational," have no essential compositional properties, and could in some possible world be realized nonphysically; Boyd's reframing of the issue as "composition versus correlation."
  Solution: Boyd and Aquinas converge: both hold that something purely configurational can exist and function apart from matter. Stump declines to say which is "really" the dualist. Because composition is itself a hybrid of matter and configuration, both materialist and anti-materialist intuitions find real support — which explains why the debate persists. She floats "subsistence dualism" as a genus.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: "the real lesson of this detailed examination of Aquinas's account of the soul is to show how misleading the dichotomy between materialism and dualism is" (p. 522); "If we focus on the material stuff... the mental is identical with the physical. If we focus on the configuration... our view will look dualistic" (p. 523).

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Levin (strong).** Stump's "form" is explicitly *dynamic*: it "includes the functioning of and the causal interactions among the parts," which is why a corpse's eye is an "eye" only equivocally. In note 10 she endorses Marjorie Grene's rendering of Aristotelian form as biological **organization or information**. That is close to Levin's bioelectric pattern as a goal-state that configures cells, and it sharpens the agent brief's open question ("is the morphogenetic field the soul-form of the organism?") into a testable distinction. Is a bioelectric prepattern a *configuring* form only, or also *configured* (that is, can it persist and be re-imposed on new matter)? Levin's planarian and frog-face rewriting experiments are the empirical side of exactly that question. Stump's Shoemaker comparison (pp. 516-517 — identity kept by re-imposing a configurational state on new matter, not by reassembling the same atoms) is the philosophical side.
- **Kastrup / Hoffman.** Her claim that "what is fundamentally configured is not matter but being," and that God is form without matter, gives a Thomistic version of "structure is prior to substrate." Kastrup's idealism and Hoffman's conscious realism start from the opposite end (mind first), yet arrive at the same denial that the materialism/dualism split is exhaustive. A useful three-way comparison.
- **Wolfram / computation.** Boyd's "entirely configurational" mental states are explicitly modeled on computational states, and Stump accepts the parallel. This is a pre-AI hook for the C2A2 question of whether an AI agent can be a configured configurer, or only a configuring pattern on borrowed matter.
- **MacIntyre / C2A2 method.** The paper's move is the same one the 2026 *Natural Law* paper repeats: read a medieval position into a live contemporary taxonomy and show that the taxonomy, not the medieval view, is what fails. This is a worked example of second-first-language competence across traditions, and directly usable for Tom's ISMI paper.
- **Caveat for the reviewer:** Stump herself concedes that Aquinas "mistakenly supposes that the intellect is tied to no particular bodily organ" (p. 520), and that Aquinas may lack a principled criterion for when a configuration makes *one* thing (note 19, citing van Inwagen). Both are live weak points for any Levin bridge, since collective and multi-scale agency depends on exactly that unity criterion.
