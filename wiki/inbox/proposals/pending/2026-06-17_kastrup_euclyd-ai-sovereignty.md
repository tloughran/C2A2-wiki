## Summary
Speaking not as a philosopher but as founder/CEO of the chip company Euclyd, Kastrup argues Europe can still reach AI sovereignty only if it stops believing software alone will save it and reclaims control of AI hardware. He is blunt that "GPU is video game hardware... not designed from scratch for the way neural networks work," and frames Euclyd's bet (silicon and systems-in-package for AI workloads, ~$100M raised) as competing on agility over scale and on "a deeper understanding of intelligence and consciousness." Companion source: his IAI TV essay "Europe's last hope in the AI race."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the same Kastrup who calls machine consciousness about as plausible as the Flying Spaghetti Monster — now publicly designing the hardware substrate of AI and explicitly citing his "understanding of intelligence and consciousness" as the design edge. It is a new, untracked dimension of the program: the practical/industrial face of analytic idealism, and a live test of how a consciousness-as-fundamental thinker draws the line between intelligence (which he builds for) and consciousness (which he denies machines have).
