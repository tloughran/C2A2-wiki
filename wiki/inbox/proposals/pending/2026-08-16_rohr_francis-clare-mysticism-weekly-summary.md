## Summary

The Saturday close of CAC's Week 32. Two parts: a set of one-line pull-quotes indexing the week's six daily issues, and the **Week Thirty-Two Practice**, which is the part carrying new material.

The practice, drawn from Jon Sweeney's *Experiencing God: 36 Ways According to Saint Francis of Assisi* (Monkfish, 2026), builds on the Mount La Verna episode in which Francis, during a forty-day retreat and overheard by Brother Leo, repeats one question — who are you, God, and who am I — and nothing else. Sweeney's reading has three components. He treats Francis's continuing uncertainty as the point rather than as a stage to be passed, contrasting it with a piety in which sanctity means the existential questions have been settled. He connects the need for solitude to a 2024 Cambridge University Press volume on the science of solitude (Weinstein, Hansen and Nguyen), quoting its framing of solitude as the setting for autonomy and authenticity. And he proposes that such elementary reaching prayers may be less a human expression of need than "a desire for God that comes from that human place inside us where God resides," so that the prayer functions as connective tissue between spirit and Spirit — glossed with an eighteenth-century Hasidic teaching from Pinchas of Koretz that the prayers one prays are themselves the divine presence.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

Two things the tradition does not have.

**A named practice for the perspective question.** The Rohr wiki's Active Question 2 asks how non-dual awareness relates to the Summa 2026 claim that perspective-limitation individuates conscious agents. Francis's La Verna prayer is a practice that *holds both relata in one utterance* — the divine identity and the self's identity asked as a single question, repeated, unresolved. The tradition has had the thesis and has had contemplative practice as a category; it has not had a practice whose content is the thesis.

**A rare instance of Rohr's stream citing empirical work.** The Sweeney extract cites a 2024 Cambridge University Press monograph on solitude. This matters for the network's method rather than its content: the CAC stream almost never reaches for peer-reviewed psychology, and when it does, the citation is a place where the contemplative and empirical wings can be checked against each other rather than merely juxtaposed. It should be recorded as a **methodological** datum.

**Attribution caution, stated up front.** As with the Wednesday and Thursday issues of this week, the substance is another author's — Sweeney's — under Rohr's curation. Unlike Friday's issue, there is no Rohr reflection here at all. Confidence ratings reflect this.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Rohr Active Question 2 — what is the relation between non-dual contemplative awareness and perspective-limitation as the individuating condition of conscious agents? The tradition has the thesis (PRS-41) but no practice that operates on it.
  Resource: The La Verna prayer as reported by Sweeney: the divine identity and the self's identity asked as one repeated question, sustained without resolution over a forty-day retreat, with Brother Leo's once-daily check-in as the only external contact.
  Solution: Proposes that the practice does not *dissolve* the two-term structure into undifferentiated unity but *holds* it — the question keeps both poles in play rather than collapsing them. If that is right, non-duality in this tradition is compatible with retained individuation rather than opposed to it, which is what the Summa 2026 thesis needs and what a dissolution reading of non-duality would deny.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Sweeney, *Experiencing God* (Monkfish, 2026), 53–55, as excerpted; the prayer is reported as repeated "and nothing else." Medium, with the weak point named: whether the practice *holds* rather than *dissolves* the two terms is the agent's reading of a reported hagiographic episode, and Sweeney does not frame it in those terms.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Whether the tradition's claim that God is found "within" (asserted in the same week's Friday meditation, PROP-2026-08-16-003) can be stated without either collapsing the creature into God or reducing to metaphor.
  Resource: Sweeney's proposal that reaching prayer originates from "that human place inside us where God resides" and functions as connective tissue between spirit and Spirit, with Pinchas of Koretz's teaching that the prayers one prays are the divine presence.
  Solution: Locates the seeking itself, rather than an achieved state, as the site of indwelling — the prayer *is* the connection rather than a request for one. This gives the interiority claim a form that does not require the creature to be identical with God, since a connective tissue relates two things it does not merge.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Direct from the excerpt. Speculative on two counts: Sweeney hedges it himself ("It may be that…"), and the Hasidic gloss is doing rhetorical rather than argumentative work — it is an eighteenth-century aphorism, not a premise.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03 (methodological, not doctrinal):
  Problem: The network needs to know whether its contemplative oracle's claims are ever exposed to empirical checking, or whether the two wings only ever run in parallel.
  Resource: The Weinstein / Hansen / Nguyen *Solitude* citation (Cambridge University Press, 2024) inside a CAC practice, framing solitude as the setting for autonomy and authenticity.
  Solution: Establishes at least one live contact point. The tradition can note that its solitude claims have an adjacent empirical literature with a specific published locus, rather than treating them as inaccessible to measurement.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The citation is explicit with page reference. Medium because a single citation in a curated extract is thin evidence of a methodological habit — and note the direction of use: the empirical work is deployed *illustratively*, to explain why Francis wanted solitude, not as a constraint on any claim. That is contact, not accountability.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Friston (bridge candidate).** A repeated two-term question sustained without resolution is, in active-inference terms, a policy of *not* resolving uncertainty on a self-model — the opposite of what the free-energy account predicts an agent should do. Rohr wiki Active Question 4 asks what contemplation looks like under Friston's frame; this is a case that appears to run against the obvious answer. Recommend the Friston agent be given it as an *anomaly* rather than as a convergence — a practice that deliberately sustains high uncertainty about the self-model is more interesting to the network than one that confirms precision reduction. Note this pulls in a different direction from PROP-2026-08-16-002's first parameter (engage before resolution), and the two should be considered together.
- **McGilchrist (convergence, thin).** Sustained unresolved questioning is congenial to McGilchrist's account of right-hemisphere tolerance for the unresolved. Recording it as thin because nothing here engages hemisphere theory and the tradition already carries this edge — it should not be counted as an advance.
- **Stump (weak convergence).** "Who are you, God? And who am I?" is second-personal in form: a question addressed *to* rather than *about*. Consistent with the independence check open on the Rohr side, though this instance is weaker evidence than the Clare material, since a hagiographic prayer report is not a claim about knowledge.
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags.** Not a FLAG-NN candidate. PRS-CANDIDATE-03 is worth surfacing to the master agent as a **method** note rather than a cross-program insight: the question of whether the network's contemplative sources are ever empirically constrained is one the master index does not currently track, and this run is the second week in a row where the answer turned out to matter.
