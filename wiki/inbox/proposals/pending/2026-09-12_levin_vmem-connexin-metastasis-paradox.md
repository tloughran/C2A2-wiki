## Summary

Grodstein, Mathews and Levin take up two standing paradoxes in cancer bioelectricity.
First, tumour cells are typically depolarised (Vmem approximately -30 mV, close to stem
cells) against roughly -70 mV in fully differentiated cells, which is read as
accelerating the cell cycle — yet when a tumour becomes invasive its cells sometimes
revert to a *hyperpolarised* Vmem, with no accepted reason. Second, non-invasive solid
tumours markedly **under**express connexins (the gap-junction proteins), while invasive
tumours **over**express them; the reversal has had very little explanation. The paper
argues the two paradoxes are one phenomenon: Vmem and connexin expression work together,
and the switch is stage-dependent rather than anomalous.

**Provenance note.** Bibliographic metadata verified against Levin's own preprint index
(drmichaellevin.org, 2026-09-12); DOI 10.64898/2026.09.02.748840. The above is from the
bioRxiv abstract. The mechanism the paper proposes for the joint switch was **not**
retrieved — see the confidence marks below.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

Cancer-as-defection is load-bearing in Levin's philosophical writing and in this wiki:
PRS-02's "cooperative morphogenetic community versus cancerous defection" is cited from
several tradition files, and the recurring gloss is that cancer is a *shrinking* of the
cognitive light cone — a cell reverting to single-cell goals as gap-junctional coupling
is lost. This paper complicates that gloss in the wiki's own terms. If invasive tumours
*over*express connexins, the invasive stage is not simply more disconnected; coupling
returns while the malignancy worsens. The single-axis story ("less coupling = smaller
light cone = cancer") cannot be right as stated, and the wiki currently states it that
way in more than one place.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Two unexplained reversals in tumour progression — depolarised tumours that
    hyperpolarise on becoming invasive, and connexin underexpression in solid tumours
    followed by overexpression in invasive ones.
  Resource: Treating Vmem and connexin expression as a single coupled control variable
    rather than two independent markers, staged across tumour progression.
  Solution: The reversals are the same switch seen twice; proliferation and invasion are
    distinct bioelectric regimes with different optimal settings, so the "anomaly" is a
    stage transition.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Abstract — tumour Vmem approximately -30 mV versus approximately -70 mV in
    differentiated cells; "when the tumor becomes invasive, its cells sometimes revert to
    a hyperpolarized Vmem with no obvious reason why"; non-invasive solid tumours "greatly
    underexpress connexins" while invasive tumours "overexpress connexins." The unifying
    mechanism is asserted by the title; it was not retrieved and is not quoted here.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02 (correction, not extension):
  Problem: The wiki's standing gloss that cancer is loss of gap-junctional coupling and
    hence a shrunken cognitive light cone treats coupling as monotonic with multicellular
    cooperation.
  Resource: The observed non-monotonicity — coupling falls, then rises, as malignancy
    advances.
  Solution: Coupling is necessary but not sufficient for the larger light cone; what
    matters is what the coupled collective's setpoint *is*. An invasive tumour may be a
    well-coupled collective pursuing the wrong target, which is a different failure mode
    from decoupling. Flag PRS-02's downstream citations for review.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: The non-monotonicity is in the abstract; the interpretation is the wiki's and
    must not be attributed to the authors. Ingest only after the full text is read.
