## Summary
Weekly episode (40 minutes) of *Ask NT Wright Anything*, Tom Wright with host Michael F. Bird. Two threads. First, a methodological inversion in trinitarian theology: Wright argues that instead of beginning from an abstract concept of God and then trying to fit Jesus into it, one should look at Jesus in order to discover what God is actually like — with the corollary question of whether "Trinity" is even the best available vocabulary for the relation of Father, Son and Spirit. Second, a pastoral-epistemic question from a listener who has rejected his church's dispensationalist end-times theology and is now isolated: how to remain biblically faithful while disagreeing with one's own community, holding both conviction and humility.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The first thread is Wright's characteristic method — history-first, category-second — applied to the doctrine that is usually treated as the least revisable, so it is the clearest available statement of what his method costs and buys. The second is rarer and arguably more valuable for C2A2: Wright on the *social* problem of holding a well-grounded minority position inside a community that does not share it. That is a governance question, not a theology question, and the wright/ wiki has nothing on it.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Trinitarian theology characteristically starts from a philosophical concept of God (simple, impassible, omnipotent) and then must explain how a crucified first-century Jew fits inside it. The fit is notoriously strained.
  Resource: Method inversion — treat the person of Jesus as the datum and the concept of God as the thing to be revised, rather than the reverse.
  Solution: Makes the strain diagnostic rather than embarrassing: where Jesus does not fit the inherited concept, it is the concept that is under-determined by the evidence. This converts a doctrinal puzzle into an ordinary case of theory revision under data, which is what lets Wright ask whether "Trinity" is the best term without that being a denial of what the term was coined to protect.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: A person who has been persuaded out of their community's theology on textual grounds is left isolated, with no procedure for dissenting that is neither capitulation nor exit. What should they do?
  Resource: A conviction/humility pairing — remaining biblically faithful while disagreeing, with both held at once rather than traded off.
  Solution: Treats the isolation as a real cost to be managed rather than a sign of error, and locates the constraint on dissent in *how* it is held rather than in whether it is voiced. This is the closest thing in the recent Wright corpus to an explicit norm for disagreement inside a formation community, which is why it matters here beyond its pastoral occasion.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **McGilchrist**: "start from the abstract concept and fit the particular into it" is precisely McGilchrist's characterisation of left-hemisphere priority; Wright's inversion is the same corrective argued on historical rather than neuropsychological grounds. Two independent routes to one methodological claim is worth logging as convergence.
- **Kastrup**: Kastrup's objection to classical theism is that the abstract God-concept is incoherent. Wright's move is to revise the concept from the particular rather than defend or discard it — a third option Kastrup's framing does not currently include.
- **Rohr**: Rohr's Trinity-as-relational-template material (already in the wiki) approaches the same doctrine from the relation rather than the substance. Whether Rohr's relational reading and Wright's Jesus-first reading are the same revision in different registers is a checkable question.
- **Loughran / C2A2**: PRS-CANDIDATE-03 is directly on the community-formation strand — a named norm for dissent that is neither exit nor capitulation is exactly what the swarm-contract material needs and currently lacks a tradition-side source for.
