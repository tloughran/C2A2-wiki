## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The Isaiah 53 answer is the centre of gravity here. How prophecy works is the hinge on which Wright's whole method turns: his repeated claim is that the New Testament writers were not collecting fulfilled predictions but claiming that Israel's *story* had reached its intended goal. Those two models make different predictions about what counts as evidence — the prediction model can be defeated by showing a text meant something else in context, whereas the story model requires showing the trajectory itself is mis-drawn. Getting Wright on the record on Isaiah 53, the single most contested case, is therefore high-value for the wiki's account of his method. The parables answer runs on the same logic: Jesus's obscurity is functional, aimed at dismantling an expectation rather than at withholding information.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: On the standard "messianic prediction" model, Isaiah 53 either straightforwardly predicts Jesus (and critics reply that in context it concerns Israel or the prophet) or it does not (and the New Testament's use of it is eisegesis). Both horns are unattractive.
  Resource: The narrative model of prophecy — prophecy as the articulation of a trajectory of God's purposes rather than as a set of isolated forecasts.
  Solution: Dissolves the dilemma by changing what fulfilment claims. The New Testament writers are not claiming Isaiah forecast a specific individual; they are claiming the story Isaiah was telling about Israel's vocation and suffering reached its intended terminus in Jesus. This survives the contextual objection, because the contextual reading (Israel/the servant) is the *premise* of the claim rather than its rival.

  Evidence: The verified Apple Podcasts episode description states that Wright "explains why biblical prophecy is far richer than a series of isolated predictions, revealing instead the larger story of God's purposes culminating in Jesus." Audio NOT retrieved; the reconstruction of how this defuses the contextual objection is inference from Wright's published method (notably *Jesus and the Victory of God*), not quoted from this episode.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Mark 4:11-12 appears to say Jesus taught in parables so that outsiders would *not* understand. Is deliberate obscurity a coherent teaching strategy?
  Resource: The confrontation reading — parables as instruments that confront and destabilize existing expectations about God's kingdom, rather than as encrypted information.
  Solution: Reclassifies the parables from a transmission problem (how do I convey X?) to a demolition problem (how do I dislodge a wrong X the hearer already holds?). Obscurity is then functional rather than defective: a direct statement would be assimilated to the existing expectation, whereas a story that will not resolve forces the expectation itself into view.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The verified episode description states Wright "explains why Jesus' teaching often appeared mysterious, showing how his parables confronted first-century expectations about God's kingdom rather than simply hiding the truth." Audio not retrieved; the Mark 4:11-12 identification and the transmission/demolition framing are this proposal's, not the source's.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Wolfram**: PRS-CANDIDATE-01's distinction between a rule that predicts a specific future state and a trajectory whose terminus is only legible once traversed is close to computational irreducibility — the claim that some systems' outcomes cannot be shortcut and must be run. Wright is making a hermeneutical version of the same point against proof-texting.
- **McGilchrist**: the parables-as-confrontation reading (PRS-CANDIDATE-02) is a mechanism for dislodging a fixed re-presentation by refusing to supply a resolvable proposition — which is close to McGilchrist's argument that metaphor and narrative reach what explicit propositional statement structurally cannot.
- **Friston**: parables understood as expectation-violating stimuli are prediction-error generators, deliberately constructed so the hearer's existing generative model cannot explain them away. That is precisely the condition under which a model gets revised rather than reinforced.
- **Stump**: her use of biblical narrative (notably in *Wandering in Darkness*) to convey knowledge that propositional argument cannot carry is the same claim about narrative's distinctive epistemic role, argued from analytic philosophy rather than from New Testament studies.
