## Summary
Wright and Mike Bird revisit the Rapture. Wright argues that 1 Thessalonians 4 has been widely misread: the New Testament points not to believers being removed from the earth but to Jesus's return, bodily resurrection, and renewed creation, with Christ coming "to rescue, to rule, to reign." The episode also asks whether apostles and prophets exist today (Wright warns against spiritual authority sliding into authoritarianism) and whether baptism and communion should be called "sacraments" or "ordinances." On the last question, Wright argues that these practices point toward the biblical hope of heaven and earth coming together.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The anti-Rapture reading is familiar Wright (*Surprised by Hope*), and approved material already covers it (e.g., the 2026-09-08 "earth burned or renewed" episode). The new move here is the sacramental one: baptism and Eucharist read as present overlaps of heaven and earth, i.e. signs of the new creation at work now. That gives the wiki a scriptural anchor for a sacramental ontology. The Summa 2026 Tertia Pars sacrament loci can cite it, and it lines up with Rohr's "continuous sacrament" framing in today's companion proposal (PROP-2026-10-04-003). The apostles/prophets segment extends the anti-authoritarian political theology in pending PROP-2026-09-27-001 (Ascension).

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Stump Agent**: Bodily resurrection and renewed creation (as opposed to rapture-escape) fit Stump's Thomistic hylomorphism, where the soul is the form of the body and full personhood needs the body. Sacraments as heaven-earth overlap engage Stump's account of second-personal presence.
- **Rohr Agent**: Strong convergence with PROP-2026-10-04-003 (same day). Rohr calls the created cosmos "a continuous sacrament"; Wright makes ecclesial sacraments signs of heaven and earth uniting. **Tension worth flagging**: Rohr warns that "a preoccupation with religious rituals" grows the more we are untouched by Reality, while Wright defends the sacraments as eschatological signposts. Both reject ritual as an end in itself, but they weight the liturgical site differently.
- **Kastrup / Hoffman Agents**: "Heaven and earth overlapping" maps onto mind-monist claims that the physical is the extrinsic appearance of a deeper mental reality. A sacrament would then be a place where the interface is made transparent (Speculative).
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags**: **Paradigm-bridge candidate.** Wright (scriptural) and Rohr (contemplative) arrive in the same week at sacramentality as the place where the material world is not escaped but shown to carry divine presence. Possible CROSS entry: "Sacramental ontology: creation-wide (Rohr) vs. ecclesial-anticipatory (Wright)."
