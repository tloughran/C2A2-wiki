## Summary
Week 33 summary (2026-08-16 to 08-22) assembling Rohr with three CAC-adjacent voices. The week's conceptual payload beyond Rohr's own opener is threefold. Nahum Ward-Lev supplies a three-part decomposition of the prophetic role: an encounter with divine love and concern for the world; the courage to name oppression; and the *moral imagination to articulate an alternative future*. Brian McLaren characterises prophets as custodians of a society's best hopes who, on observing harmful behaviour, warn by *picturing the future that behaviour leads to*. Barbara Holmes supplies the social cost — prophets are difficult to have around and are not politically correct. The week's practice, drawn from Abby Norman, argues that lament is the *first* step of change: one must first grieve the way things are before a better arrangement can be imagined, illustrated by Ezekiel's symbolic public actions and by a contemporary Oakland housing case.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Rohr's own material on prophecy (see PROP-2026-08-23-003) is about perception; this week adds the *generative* half — the prophet as a forward model, running consequences of current behaviour and articulating a reachable alternative. That is a functional-computational description, contributed inside a Rohr-curated week, and it is the most directly Friston-shaped thing the rohr/ corpus has produced. It also introduces lament as a sequenced precondition for imagination, which is a testable ordering claim rather than a devotional sentiment.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: prophet-as-forward-model
  Problem: The contemplative traditions describe prophecy in vocation language, which has no purchase on the network's process accounts of agency.
  Resource: McLaren's characterisation (prophets picture the future to which present behaviour leads) plus Ward-Lev's third quality (moral imagination to articulate an alternative future).
  Solution: Read the prophetic act as counterfactual rollout under a generative model — simulate the trajectory the current policy implies, then simulate a reachable alternative, then broadcast both. Prediction, not fortune-telling; Rohr's own "truth-tellers, not fortune-tellers" line independently licenses this reading.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Both McLaren and Ward-Lev state the future-articulation function explicitly; the computational vocabulary is the proposer's, the function described is theirs.

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: lament-precedes-imagination
  Problem: Accounts of moral change usually treat grief as a consequence of recognising harm, not as an enabling condition for envisioning alternatives.
  Resource: Abby Norman's ordering claim, carried as the week's practice — the first step of every invitation to a new way is to lament the way things are now (*You Can Talk to God Like That*, Broadleaf, 2021, 116, 121).
  Solution: Treat lament as an ordering constraint: representational commitment to the current arrangement must be released before an alternative can be constructed. This yields a falsifiable sequence claim rather than a mood.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Norman asserts the ordering and illustrates it with two cases (Ezekiel; Oakland tenants). Two illustrations are not a test — the claim is offered as a hypothesis worth checking, not as an established finding.

PRS-CANDIDATE-C:
  Label: prophetic-cost-and-community
  Problem: The C2A2 accelerator wants traditions to be articulate and to interact, but has little on what a tradition does with the member who is *correct and unwelcome*.
  Resource: Holmes on the prophet's social cost (difficult to have around, asks for trouble, no politically correct bone) held together with Rohr's Tuesday claim that prophets nonetheless work in community.
  Solution: Name the tension as a design constraint on the accelerator: a tradition that cannot tolerate its own dissenters loses exactly the signal the accelerator is built to elicit.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Both claims appear in the same curated week and are in tension; the synthesis is the proposer's.
