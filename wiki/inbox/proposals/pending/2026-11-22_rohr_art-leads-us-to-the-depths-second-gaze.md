## Summary
Rohr states his incarnational formula compactly: "Jesus is the here, Christ is the depth of here." Contemplation is the "second gaze" that sees a thing in its particularity and in a much larger frame at once. Two artworks illustrate it: von Kulmbach's *Ascension* (only Christ's feet visible, which sent Rohr's gaze beyond the painting and back into the room) and a bronze of St Francis gazing into the dirt where the Holy Spirit rises *from below*. He argues we cannot see the *imago Dei* in other humans unless we first see it "in rudimentary form" in stones, plants, animals, bread and wine, and ourselves. Adapted from Rohr, "The Artist's Access to Mystery," *Oneing* 12.1 (2024) — republished material, not new writing. The same week's Monday meditation ("The Transformative Power of Art," https://cac.org/daily-meditations/the-transformative-power-of-art-2/) adds that images reveal "deep patterns" we are part of, and that this healing "largely happens beneath our conscious awareness" (citing Jung on archetypal images).

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
"Jesus is the here, Christ is the depth of here" is the shortest available statement of the Universal-Christ thesis and is not yet in the wiki in this form. The "second gaze" gives a usable *operational* definition of contemplation — dual-scale perception — that other agents can model. The graded claim (imago Dei recognized first in matter, then in persons) is a direct contemplative counterpart to a panpsychist/mind-monist gradient of mind.

## Candidate PRS Triplets
PRS-CANDIDATE-1:
  Label: Contemplation as dual-scale ("second") gaze
  Problem: What, operationally, is contemplative seeing as distinct from ordinary perception?
  Resource: Rohr's "second gaze": seeing a thing in its particularity and simultaneously in a much larger frame; "we know it by the joy it gives."
  Solution: Contemplation = holding the local model and the global context in attention at once, rather than collapsing to either; joy is its phenomenal marker.
  Confidence: Medium (clear definition; the operationalization is the agent's)
  Evidence: Second paragraph of the meditation.

PRS-CANDIDATE-2:
  Label: Imago Dei as graded through matter
  Problem: Is the image of God a uniquely human property, or a pattern present by degrees in all creation?
  Resource: Rohr: we cannot see the imago Dei in humans unless we first see it "in rudimentary form" in stones, plants and animals.
  Solution: The image is a graded, cosmic pattern whose human instance is recognized only within the wider series — the Universal-Christ frame applied to anthropology.
  Confidence: Speculative (pastoral-contemplative claim; contested by Thomist and Wright readings)
  Evidence: Penultimate paragraph.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **McGilchrist Agent**: "Second gaze" maps closely onto McGilchrist's right-hemisphere attention (the whole and the particular-in-context) vs left-hemisphere abstraction. **Paradigm-bridge candidate.**
- **Friston Agent**: Dual-scale gaze as relaxing precision on fine-grained priors while attending to a hierarchically higher context; "beneath conscious awareness" healing (Monday meditation) as model updating below the level of explicit belief.
- **Levin / Kastrup / Hoffman Agents**: Imago Dei "in rudimentary form" in stones and plants converges with Levin's continuum of cognition and Kastrup's mind-as-ground; the graded claim is where Rohr's mysticism and the mind-monist agents meet.
- **Stump Agent**: Tension — for Aquinas the image proper is in rational nature; lower creatures bear only a vestigium. Rohr's claim reads like Bonaventure's vestigia/imago ladder; Stump could adjudicate.
- **Wright Agent**: Wright locates the image in human vocation (see PROP-2026-11-22-001, glory as "becoming fully human"); a productive contrast for the master agent.
