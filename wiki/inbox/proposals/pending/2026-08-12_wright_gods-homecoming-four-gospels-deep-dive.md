## Summary
A 30-minute subscriber-only bonus episode continuing a multi-part deep dive into Wright's 2026 book *God's Homecoming* (the book itself is already in the wiki as PROP-2026-05-10-001/002; this episode is the working-through of its Gospels chapter and has not been captured). Wright takes each of the four Gospels in turn and argues that all four present Jesus as "the human face of the God who comes," rather than as an agent sent to extract people from earth. The published description names the four moves: Mark as God returning to Zion; Matthew as the fulfilment of Israel's hopes; Luke as the warning that Jerusalem failed to recognize "the moment of God's visitation"; and John as the Word "tabernacling" among us. The conclusion drawn is that "the Gospels tell a far bigger story than escaping earth for heaven," and that the answer to "what does God actually look like?" is given ostensively — "Look at Jesus: healing, weeping, challenging power and ultimately going to the cross."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
*God's Homecoming* is the current centre of gravity of Wright's program, and its core claim — that the biblical story runs toward God coming to dwell on a renewed earth, not toward humans departing for heaven — is already in the wiki from the book and the essay. What this episode adds is the *evidential base*: the claim is argued four times over, once per Gospel, using four different literary strategies. That matters because the single strongest objection to Wright's thesis is that it is a systematic-theological preference read back into the texts. Showing that all four evangelists independently structure their narratives around divine arrival rather than human departure is what converts the thesis from a theological position into a claim about convergent literary evidence. The wiki holds the conclusion; it does not yet hold the four-fold argument.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Popular Christianity reads the Gospels as being about how individuals get to heaven. Is that reading supported by the Gospels' own narrative structure?
  Resource: A four-Gospel convergence argument — Mark (God returns to Zion), Matthew (Israel's hopes fulfilled), Luke (Jerusalem misses the visitation), John (the Word tabernacles among us) — four distinct literary strategies for one directional claim.
  Solution: The convergence across four independent compositions is offered as evidence that the arrival-direction is structural to the material rather than imposed on it. The escape-to-heaven reading would have to explain why none of the four organizes its narrative that way.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The four-fold structure is stated in the publisher-written description on the verified Apple Podcasts episode page. Audio NOT retrieved (subscriber-only). The characterization of this as a *convergence argument* is this proposal's framing; Wright may present it as four illustrations of a thesis rather than as four independent lines of evidence, and the distinction matters for how much the argument is worth. Verify before ingesting.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: "What is God like?" is normally answered by listing attributes (omnipotence, omniscience, impassibility) derived from philosophical theology rather than from narrative.
  Resource: Ostensive definition through the Gospel narrative — the answer is a pointing rather than a predication: "Look at Jesus: healing, weeping, challenging power and ultimately going to the cross."
  Solution: Replaces attribute-listing with a method that makes the character of God read off observable behaviour in a narrative, which allows properties (weeping; being killed by power) that the attribute-list method structurally excludes. This is Wright's standing objection to natural theology done without history.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The "human face of the God who comes" framing and the four-verb list are given in the verified episode description. Audio not retrieved; the contrast with attribute-based theology is inference from Wright's published method, not quoted from this episode.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Friston**: PRS-CANDIDATE-03 is a recognition failure under mis-specified priors — the signal arrives, the generative model has no hypothesis that accommodates it, and it is explained away. Wright's account of Jerusalem missing the visitation and Friston's account of prior-driven perceptual failure are the same functional story in different vocabularies, and the Wright material supplies a worked historical case where the "correct" hypothesis is independently specifiable.
- **Rohr**: the anti-escapist thesis is the shared spine of both traditions in this window. Rohr's "Heaven includes earth… time opens us up to the timeless" (PROP-2026-08-12-040, 2026-08-10) and Wright's "far bigger story than escaping earth for heaven" are the same claim reached from Franciscan incarnational theology and from New Testament exegesis respectively. This is the strongest Rohr–Wright bridge candidate in the current batch.
- **McGilchrist**: ostensive definition of God through a narrative of behaviour, rather than through a list of abstracted attributes, is the right-hemisphere route to knowledge over the left's re-presentation — and Wright treats the attribute-first method as the one that produces the distortion.
- **Loughran / C2A2**: "the moment of visitation" that can be present and unrecognized is directly usable as a frame for the project's own question about whether a community can be measured while it is happening or only afterwards.
