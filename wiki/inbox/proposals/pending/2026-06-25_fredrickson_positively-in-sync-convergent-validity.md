## Summary
Zhou, West, Berman & Fredrickson (Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 2025) test whether three distinct ways of assessing the positive affective quality of a social interaction — self-report, perceived positivity resonance, and behavioral coding — converge on the same underlying construct. Establishing convergent validity across these methods strengthens the measurement foundation of positivity resonance.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is a measurement-infrastructure paper, and the wiki's Fredrickson holdings are mostly substantive findings rather than methodology. For C2A2's own measurement ambitions, the question "do different instruments for the affective quality of an interaction agree?" is load-bearing: it tells us whether resonance can be measured robustly across self-report, perceived, and behavioral channels — which is precisely the multi-channel measurement problem C2A2 faces when instrumenting tradition-dialogue encounters.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: The positive affective quality of an interaction is assessed in incompatible ways across studies (self-report vs. perceived positivity resonance vs. observer behavioral coding), leaving open whether these methods measure one construct or several.
  Resource: A convergent-validity study comparing three distinct assessments of interaction positivity on the same interactions.
  Solution: Demonstrated convergence across self-report, perceived-resonance, and behavioral measures, supporting positivity resonance as a single construct measurable through multiple channels and licensing mixed-method assessment.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The paper's design and aim — establishing convergent validity across three distinct assessments of the positive affective quality of social interaction (J. Social and Personal Relationships, 2025).
