## Summary
Part of CAC's "Nonviolence: A Christian Calling" week (Sept 20-26, 2026), this meditation argues that "the myth of redemptive violence" — the belief that destroying what we judge evil resolves conflict — is the world's default operating system, and that Jesus unmasks it rather than participating in it. Rohr locates the generative mechanism in exclusion: holiness achieved through "separation or exclusion" (instead of radical inclusion) is, in his reading, the "solid foundation" violence gets built on. His alternative is not passive non-resistance but redirected constructive energy — "the best criticism of the bad is the practice of the better" — which he reads as exactly the pattern enacted on the cross.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Rohr doing diagnostic work (a scapegoat/exclusion mechanism as violence's generative root, in the Girardian vein CAC regularly draws on) rather than devotional restatement, and it makes an explicit causal claim — exclusion generates violence; inclusion (Universal-Christ-style) dissolves its foundation — that is directly testable against other traditions' models of group-boundary formation and conflict in the network.

  Solution: Rohr claims exclusion itself (not any particular violent act) is violence's "solid foundation," and that Jesus resolves rather than merely opposes violence by building an alternative, inclusive social pattern instead of destroying the excluding one — "overcoming evil without becoming evil."
