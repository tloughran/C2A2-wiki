## Summary
Subscriber bonus episode of *Ask NT Wright Anything* (Premier Unbelievable; Tom Wright with host Mike Bird), ~16–17 minutes, released Thursday 27 August 2026. Wright takes aim at two accounts of the Holy Spirit that he treats as *both* deficient and, crucially, deficient in the same way: the Spirit as consolation prize (something to tide Christians over while they wait for Jesus to return) and the Spirit as the source of extraordinary experiences and gifts. Working from Romans 8, Acts 2 and Ephesians, he argues that the Spirit's role is set by the larger purpose of new creation, and that the Church filled with the Spirit is meant to function as a *sign in the present* of what God intends for the whole world. The episode covers Pentecost, spiritual gifts, mission and the Kingdom of God, and closes on why being "filled with the Spirit" is about far more than individual experience.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Pneumatology is the least-captured region of Wright's programme in this wiki relative to his eschatology and Paul scholarship, and this episode ties the Spirit directly to the new-creation frame that organises the rest of his work — making it the connective piece rather than a side topic.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Popular Christian pneumatology splits into two options — the Spirit as interim comfort until Jesus returns, or the Spirit as the engine of extraordinary personal experience — and both quietly assume the Church's ultimate destination is elsewhere.
  Resource: Reading the Spirit's function off the *goal* of new creation rather than off the believer's felt experience, grounded in Romans 8 (creation's groaning and the Spirit's role in its renewal), Acts 2 and Ephesians.
  Solution: Both popular options are diagnosed as the same error under two disguises — an escapist eschatology — and are replaced by a single account in which the Spirit is the present operation of the future world.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Episode premise as published: the episode "challenge[s] two common assumptions: that the Spirit is simply a consolation while Christians wait for Jesus to return, or primarily a source of extraordinary spiritual experiences and gifts," and instead "Tom explains how the Spirit fits into God's much bigger purpose of new creation."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: If new creation is genuinely future, it is unclear what the Church is *for* in the meantime, which is what makes the consolation-prize reading attractive in the first place.
  Resource: The Church as sign — a community whose corporate life is meant to be a present, visible instance of the world's intended end state, not merely a body that announces it verbally.
  Solution: Gives the Church a public, inspectable vocation with a corresponding failure condition: a church that signifies nothing about the world's future has not merely underperformed but has misidentified what the Spirit is for. This also relocates "filled with the Spirit" from individual interiority to communal function.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: "The Church, filled with God's Spirit, is called to become a sign in the present of what God ultimately intends for the whole world."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- Rohr: Rohr's meditation of the same week (["Realigned with the True Self"](https://cac.org/daily-meditations/realigned-with-the-true-self/), 28 Aug 2026) reads Romans 8:9 and the divine indwelling as an announcement of "what is happening in all of us." Wright reads much of the same material corporately and eschatologically, Rohr interiorly and mystically. A genuine tension worth staging: whether the Spirit's indwelling primarily individuates or primarily incorporates.
- Fredrickson: "A sign in the present of what God ultimately intends" makes the community's observable quality of life the evidence, which is precisely the register in which positivity-resonance work operates. What Wright asserts theologically about corporate life, that programme measures.
- McGilchrist: The rejected options (Spirit as consolation, Spirit as extraordinary experience) both isolate a part from the whole; the proposed account subordinates both to a purpose. That is the same move from part-attention to whole-attention, in a doctrinal setting.
