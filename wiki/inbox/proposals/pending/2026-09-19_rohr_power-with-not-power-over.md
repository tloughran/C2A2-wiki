## Summary

A CAC Daily Meditation from the week-37 **Paul's Transforming Vision** theme. **Authorship caveat, read first:** the page is bylined Rohr, but the body is almost entirely quoted from theologian **Kat Armas** (*Liturgies for Resisting Empire*, Brazos 2025, 75–77), citing Beth Allison Barr. Rohr's own contribution is the selection, the framing sentences, and the epigraph (Eph 4:32). This passes the tradition filter the way the curated Daily Meditations generally do — Rohr is platforming the reading as the tradition's own — but any triplet minted from it should be attributed as **Rohr-curated / Armas-authored**, not as Rohr's own formulation.

The argument: Paul's household codes sit inside an already-patriarchal Roman framework, and the Christian versions disrupt it by addressing *every* member rather than granting one man authority to speak for the household (Eph 5:21 read as governing 5:22). Armas declines to resolve Paul into either revolutionary or imperial enforcer, holding both 1 Cor 7:4 (the husband's body belongs to the wife — shared bodily authority) and 1 Cor 11:5–10 (head-covering grounded in creation order) as genuinely in tension. Gal 3:28 is the maximal case. The name for the resulting model is **power-with, not power-over**: mutual, requiring listening and the honoring of each person's dignity, as against domination by fear or force.

Retrieved and read in full on 2026-09-19.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

Two reasons, one methodological and one substantive.

**Methodological.** Armas's refusal to "reduce him to either a revolutionary or a mere enforcer of empire, recognizing instead a man shaped by both revelation and limitation" is a worked instance of exactly the operation PROP-2026-09-19-002 describes abstractly — holding a contradiction open rather than resolving it. That the two meditations appear in the same week, one stating the method and one applying it to a hard case, is a rare chance to check the tradition's central epistemic claim against its own practice. Bears on **Active Question 6**.

**Substantive.** "Power-with" is a relational-structure claim, and the Rohr file's **Question 7** asks whether second-personal knowledge scales from a dyad to a community without changing kind (Rohr↔Stump, PRS-39). The household codes are precisely a dyad-to-community case: the claim is that mutual submission at household scale is the *same* relation as between two persons, extended. This is the first recent Rohr material that puts a concrete social structure under that question rather than leaving it at the level of knowing-persons.

It also bears on **Question 9** (the effects-of-the-reading criterion, PRS-42, lacks an independent check on the philology). Here the philological check is *present and doing work*: Armas grounds the reading in the ordering of Eph 5:21 before 5:22 and in the plain sense of 1 Cor 7:4, and she concedes 1 Cor 11:5–10 against her own preferred direction. That concession is evidence against the worry in Question 9 — this reading does not simply produce inclusion and stop. Whether one conceded verse constitutes a *check* or merely good manners is the thing to argue about.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Active Question 7 — whether second-personal relation scales from dyad to community without changing kind.
  Resource: The power-with / power-over distinction applied to the New Testament household codes, with mutual submission (Eph 5:21) read as governing the member-specific instructions that follow.
  Solution: Supplies a test case where the same relation is claimed at two scales, and names what would have to hold for the scaling to work — that each member retains agency and "the right to hear and act for themselves," rather than being represented by a head. Does not settle Question 7; makes it concrete.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: "Mutual submission was a radical shift from the Roman household codes that demanded strict hierarchy, with only one—the father—having ultimate authority. Ephesians 5 disrupts this by speaking to *every* member of the household." (Armas, quoted by Rohr; interior quotation from Barr, *The Making of Biblical Womanhood*, 34.)

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Active Question 9 — whether the effects-of-the-reading criterion can be constrained by anything textual once adjudication has moved off philology.
  Resource: A reading that holds 1 Cor 7:4 and 1 Cor 11:5–10 together without harmonizing them, conceding the verse that runs against its own direction.
  Solution: Weak evidence that the criterion is not unconstrained in practice: the reading declines an available harmonization and records the counter-text. Whether that is a structural check or a stylistic virtue is exactly what Question 9 asks, and this source does not answer it.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: "And yet, in 1 Corinthians 11:5–10, it seems Paul is upholding gendered practices by instructing women to cover their heads while praying, grounding his reasoning in creation order."

- **Rohr↔Wright (Active Question 8), the week's live contrast.** Both traditions are on Paul this week (Wright's own site posted a Romans item 2026-09-13). Wright's critical realism would want the historical-contextual reading to control the outcome; Armas-via-Rohr runs a contextual argument *and* declines a verdict, which is not the same thing as referential control. Flag only — Armas is making a normative-structural claim and Fredrickson an empirical one, and mapping them without an argument would be a category slide. Related in shape to Question 14 (gate or gradient), which already concerns a Rohr absolute against a Fredrickson gradient.
- **Rohr↔MacIntyre (not yet a numbered question).** The passage treats a household as "a microcosm of the state" and asks what world would follow if families lived by Gal 3:28. That is a small-community-as-carrier-of-a-tradition claim, which is the C2A2 architecture's own thesis; flag for the master wiki rather than minting a triplet from it here.
