## Summary
West, Huston, Chandler, Zhou & Fredrickson (Communications Psychology, Nov 2025) — an empirical study in which pairs of strangers were observed in either a 10-minute "deep talk" or a brief "small talk" exchange, with behavioral coding of listening quality (follow-up questions, attentive nonverbal behavior). High-quality listening behaviors predicted both observer-rated behavioral markers and self-reported markers of social connection between strangers, and appeared to mediate the effectiveness of a social connectedness intervention. The authors propose that high-quality listening fosters the conducive conditions of perceived safety and real-time sensory connection — and thereby sets the stage for positivity resonance ("togetherness") to emerge.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the experimental confirmation of the mechanism hypothesized in PRS-08 (high-quality listening as resonance enabler). PRS-08 was based on the 2025 UBC colloquium identifying listening as a candidate mechanism; this paper provides the controlled behavioral-coding evidence that listening behaviors are causally upstream of positivity resonance markers in stranger interactions. It also operationalizes "high-quality listening" precisely enough to support replicable training and intervention — a key step from theory toward population-scale deployment.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **McGilchrist connection (strong)**: Follow-up questions are the operational signature of right-hemispheric attention — sustained, contextual, oriented to the whole person being heard rather than to one's own next contribution. This study is empirically operationalizing what McGilchrist calls right-hemispheric attentional mode and showing it is the precondition for positivity resonance.
- **Friston connection**: High-quality listening = generative-model updating in real time on the speaker's signal. Listening quality may map onto a measurable parameter in active inference: the relative weighting of incoming sensory evidence vs. one's own priors.
- **Hawkins / Hoffman connection**: Real-time sensory connection (one of the two identified mediators) is a measurable interface phenomenon — relevant for Hoffman's interface theory and for Hawkins's hierarchical temporal memory account of attention.
- **C2A2 core signal — flag strongly**: This paper gives C2A2 a *trainable behavioral protocol* (follow-up questions + attentive nonverbal behavior) that produces positivity resonance with strangers. That is exactly the behavioral substrate needed for cross-tradition dialogue at scale: agents and humans entering inter-tradition encounters can be trained on a specific, behaviorally coded listening protocol that the empirical work shows is the active ingredient for resonance with people unlike themselves.
- **Stump connection**: Stump's recovery of "Franciscan knowledge" (second-personal, non-propositional knowing) finds an empirical correlate here — high-quality listening is the behavioral substrate of the kind of knowing-by-encounter that Franciscan knowledge requires.
