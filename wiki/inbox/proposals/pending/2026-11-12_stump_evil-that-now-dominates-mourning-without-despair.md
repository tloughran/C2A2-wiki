## Summary
Stump writes about grieving the evil she sees in every community she belongs to. She cites family separation at the border, cruelty in public life, and harm to the earth. She argues that this grief can be mourning "without despondency": "the baseness of injustice highlights the majesty of justice," and the evil that now dominates "cannot eradicate justice, goodness, and love." She ends with *etsi omnes, ego non* ("even if all others, yet not I") and calls for "fierce witness without despair."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is a lived, first-person use of the framework in *The Image of God: The Problem of Evil and the Problem of Mourning* (already captured). It moves her account of mourning from theodicy to a stance a community member can take toward a corrupted community.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: How can a member of communities pervaded by injustice grieve rightly, without despair and without withdrawing from those communities?
  Resource: Stump's problem-of-mourning framework, plus the Thomistic claim that evil is a privation that cannot destroy the good it corrupts. Contrast reveals the good: "the baseness of injustice highlights the majesty of justice."
  Solution: Mourning "without despondency," where grief is itself a witness to the good, joined to a personal commitment that holds even if the community defects (*etsi omnes, ego non*), that is, "fierce witness without despair."
  Confidence: Medium (short reflective text, not an argued paper)
  Evidence: The quoted lines above, from the full text as reproduced.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **MacIntyre (strong, for the ISMI paper):** this is a Thomist's answer to the question of what a practitioner owes a tradition-bearing community in a time of corruption. It reads like a personal analogue of the "new and very different St. Benedict" ending of *After Virtue*. In Stump's version the response is to stay and witness, not to withdraw.
- **Fredrickson:** grief held "without despondency" alongside commitments to love sits close to broaden-and-build's claim that positive and negative emotion can co-occur and still sustain resilience. This is worth testing against Fredrickson's work on resilience and coping.
- **McGilchrist:** the claim that contrast reveals the good matches his right-hemisphere account of value as something disclosed, not constructed.
- **C2A2 dialogue design:** this models how a mature member of a tradition can name grave wrongs without leaving the conversation. That matters for respectful cross-tradition exchange under real moral disagreement.
