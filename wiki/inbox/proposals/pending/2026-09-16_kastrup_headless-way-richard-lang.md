## Summary

A 1h 58m *With Reality in Mind* session on 8 September 2026 with Richard Lang, coordinator of the Shollond Trust and lifelong exponent of Douglas Harding's "Headless Way." The session frames Harding's practice as a **Science of the First Person** — a disciplined, repeatable investigation of experience from the inside — anchored in Ernst Mach's 1886 *Innenperspektive* drawing, the view from the left eye in which the observer's own head is absent from the visual field. Lang leads a series of verifiable perceptual experiments; Kastrup closes with what the page calls "Bernardo's dashboard of perception."

**The full chapter list with timestamps is public even though the recording is member-gated.** That is unusually good evidentiary footing for a gated source in this tradition, and the candidates below are drawn from chapter titles rather than from content — a distinction held to strictly throughout.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

Three reasons, in descending strength.

**First, and most directly: chapter 38:26 is "If we are one, why do we have separate minds?"** That is the decomposition problem — the single question analytic idealism has to answer, and the one this wiki tracks through the dissociation line (PRS-52 dissociation-as-knowing, PRS-55 ego-as-action, PRS-62 what ending a dissociation consists of, PRS-63's held verdict on FPD-009). A two-hour session that puts the question in a chapter heading is the tradition's nearest available test of whether the DID model is doing explanatory work or naming the problem.

**Second, method.** This tradition's standing difficulty is that analytic idealism is argued and not demonstrated. Harding's programme claims the opposite — that a few minutes of first-person experiment beats years of reading. If Kastrup endorses that, it is a methodological commitment with consequences for how idealism could ever be tested; if he resists it, the resistance tells us where he draws the line between philosophy and practice. The session contains at least six named experiments (13:44, 14:27, 36:33, 1:04:42, 1:40:33, 1:50:41). Either answer is informative, and the wiki currently records neither.

**Third, provenance of the "dashboard" metaphor.** Chapter 1:56:46 is titled "Bernardo's dashboard of perception." The dashboard is *Hoffman's* figure — the central image of interface theory. Kastrup using it as his own closing frame is a datum about the Kastrup–Hoffman relationship, which agent 11's brief names as "philosophically crucial."
