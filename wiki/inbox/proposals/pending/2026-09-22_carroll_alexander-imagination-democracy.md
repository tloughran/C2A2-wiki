## Why This Matters for This Tradition

Poetic naturalism's load-bearing claim is that higher-level descriptions — including meanings,
values and purposes — are *real stories about patterns*, not additional ontological ingredients.
The standing pressure on that claim is whether a higher-level vocabulary ever earns its keep in
the strong sense: does it do predictive or causal work, or is it bookkeeping? That is exactly
Carroll **Active Question 3** (is emergence epistemic or ontologically significant?) and the
sharper 2026 form of it in **Questions 9 and 12**.

Alexander's thesis puts the question in an unusual register. She claims the humanities produce a
specific capacity — imaginative extension to another person — and that a political arrangement
depends on it. If true, that is a higher-level description with a testable downstream consequence,
which is the shape poetic naturalism says a good emergent story should have. If Carroll pushes
back, the pushback is itself the record of where he draws the line for the humanities
specifically, which the wiki does not yet have in his own voice.

Secondarily it bears on **Question 5** (can poetic naturalism accommodate the explanatory gap for
consciousness without collapsing into eliminativism?) — the humanities are the case where the
deflationary account is felt most acutely as a loss.
