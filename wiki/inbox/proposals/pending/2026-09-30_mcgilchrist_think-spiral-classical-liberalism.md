## Summary
A co-authored paper by economist Daniel B. Klein and Iain McGilchrist in the journal *Society*. It is a primer on McGilchrist's hemisphere hypothesis and uses a "spiral" figure to describe the iterative, layered relationship between the two hemispheres (right hemisphere grounds, left hemisphere articulates, and the result must return to the right hemisphere). It then argues that classical liberalism — reliance on inner restraint, conscience and propriety rather than central design — is the political order most likely to keep the left hemisphere from "running amok". Not recent, but a peer-reviewed paper bearing McGilchrist's name that the wiki does not cite anywhere (no match for "Think Spiral", "Klein", or "classical liberalism" in the McGilchrist tradition files).

Note: co-authored, and the political conclusion is framed chiefly by Klein. The reviewer should decide whether it counts as "from McGilchrist himself" for this tradition's standards.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
It is one of very few places where McGilchrist's hemisphere thesis is carried into an explicit political-philosophy claim in a refereed venue, which makes it a citable anchor for "what social order follows from the divided brain" — a question the wiki's cultural-diagnosis material currently leaves open.

  Solution: Classical liberalism is proposed as the arrangement that best keeps the "master" (right hemisphere) in charge, because it relies on emergent order and inner restraint (conscience, propriety) over top-down design
