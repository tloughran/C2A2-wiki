## Summary
Wolfram proposes a general theory of "bulk orchestration" — the characteristic feature of living systems in which even individual molecules are actively orchestrated toward an overall purpose rather than moving randomly. His central claim is that "being adapted for an overall purpose" imposes a *universal* signature on a system's underlying operation: so long as the purpose is "computationally simple," a system that achieves it will display certain common behavioral features more or less regardless of the specifics of the purpose. He grounds this universality in the Principle of Computational Equivalence and the phenomenon of computational irreducibility, and frames the resulting space of purpose-adapted systems as a "rulial ensemble."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the clearest extension yet of Wolfram's computational framework from fundamental physics into biology and goal-directed (adapted) systems — directly answering active research question #4 in the Wolfram wiki ("Can the hypergraph model be extended to biological morphogenesis and cognitive systems?"). It also operationalizes the rulial-ensemble idea as a tool for characterizing purpose/adaptation, not just physics.
