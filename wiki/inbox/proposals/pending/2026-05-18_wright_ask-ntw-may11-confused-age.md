## Summary
A late-Wright dialogue with Mike Bird (the *Jesus and the Powers* co-author) tackling three connected questions: (1) the growing entanglement of MAGA politics, Christian nationalism, and parts of white evangelical culture, and how Christians can resist ideological capture and recover the way of Jesus; (2) a pastoral response to a listener wrestling with chronic illness, suffering, and the search for meaning, framed in a cross-shaped vision of purpose; (3) how to teach a more biblical hope in church — not escapist "souls flying to heaven" but resurrection, new creation, and God dwelling with God's people. The political-theology material is a direct extension of *Jesus and the Powers* (2024) into the current US political moment, while the suffering and hope material consolidates the eschatology of *Surprised by Hope* and the forthcoming *God's Homecoming*.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Three for the price of one: Wright tying his political theology, his theodicy of suffering, and his new-creation eschatology together into a single late-Wright pastoral instrument. This is the most current public statement of Wright's *applied* framework — the place where his canonical biblical-theology is being cashed out for the moment we are actually in. For C2A2 it (a) extends *Jesus and the Powers* into 2026 rather than relying on the 2024 book alone, (b) supplies a fresh Wright statement on suffering that the Summa 2026 work on Q.86–90 (Tertia Pars: Passion of Christ) and II-II Q.45 (gift of wisdom in suffering) can lean on, and (c) gives the network a contemporary articulation of biblical hope that the Stump and Rohr agents can both build cross-program bridges from. Not yet captured in the wiki.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Contemporary Western Christianity has been substantially captured by political ideologies (most visibly MAGA nationalism among parts of US white evangelicalism) that smuggle in soteriologies and eschatologies foreign to the New Testament. The captured form is mistaken for the gospel by both adherents and critics, occluding the actual shape of Jesus's politics.
  Resource: Wright + Bird's diagnostic frame: the test of Christian political authenticity is whether the movement looks like the crucified Christ exercising power-through-suffering, not the strongman exercising power-through-coercion. Drawn directly from *Jesus and the Powers*; refreshed for 2026.
  Solution: A diagnostic the Summa 2026 syntheses can use whenever a "Christian" political claim is being weighed — the cruciform test cuts both right-wing and left-wing instrumentalizations of the church. Pairs with Wright's earlier "kingdom inaugurated, not imposed" frame.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Episode content; *Jesus and the Powers* (Wright/Bird 2024); Wright's standing critique of Constantinian Christianity.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: When a believer encounters chronic illness or unrelenting suffering, the popular Christian frames (test-of-faith, divine pedagogy, deferred-reward) often feel hollow or even cruel because they treat suffering as a thing-to-be-explained rather than a thing-to-be-inhabited cruciformly. The Summa's questions on suffering and the gift of wisdom (II-II Q.45) need a contemporary Wright voice.
  Resource: Wright's "cross-shaped vision of purpose" in chronic illness — suffering as participation in the crucified pattern, not as transactional debt; meaning emerges from cruciformity, not from rational closure on "why this is happening."
  Solution: A late-Wright pastoral articulation the Summa-2026 daily-batch agent can cite when the question turns on suffering, vocation under affliction, or the cross-as-meaning. Provides a contemporary scriptural anchor to pair with Rohr's contemplative reading of suffering in *The Tears of Things*.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Episode content; coherence with Wright's long-standing theology of cross-and-resurrection; *Surprised by Hope*.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: The "biblical hope" taught in many congregations is still functionally Platonic — souls escaping bodies to a non-physical heaven. This is in direct tension with the mind-monist members of the C2A2 network (Levin, Hoffman, Kastrup), who need a Christian eschatology that doesn't require mind/matter dualism.
  Resource: Wright's continued insistence that biblical hope is resurrection, new creation, and *God dwelling with God's people* — the homecoming arc of his forthcoming book, in podcast-pastoral register.
  Solution: A short, citable 2026 statement of the new-creation-eschatology in Wright's own voice that the network can use without quoting at book length. Bridges directly to the cosmic-Christ frame (Rohr) and the mind-monist frame (Kastrup, Hoffman, Levin).
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Episode content; consistency with *Surprised by Hope* and *God's Homecoming*; Wright's stated frustration with "souls flying to heaven" framing.
