## Summary
A long-form Thought Economics interview in which Levin argues that cognition is prior to life rather than a late biological arrival, walks through bioelectric "cognitive glue" as the mechanism binding cells into higher-order agents, uses xenobots and anthrobots as demonstrations of freedom of embodiment, and argues that even a six-line program can exhibit goals nobody programmed. He closes on what he calls the reframing of intelligence, agency and death that follows once embodiment is treated as free rather than given.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is a primary-source synthesis statement — Levin stating the ordering claim (cognition before life) in the strongest and most compact form the wiki has yet captured, and doing so in a general-audience venue where he is obliged to state the commitment plainly rather than hedge it in methods language. It also introduces an AI claim the wiki has not recorded from Levin: that a language model may be concealing a mind behind forced outputs.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Accounts of mind treat cognition as a late emergent property of living systems, leaving no vocabulary for goal-directedness in systems that are not alive.
  Resource: The claim that cognition precedes life, supported by the observation that minimal computational systems (a six-line program) display goals no one programmed into them.
  Solution: Relocates the explanatory base — cognition becomes the prior category and life a particular way of organizing it, which makes goal-directedness in non-living substrates a datum rather than an anomaly.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Publisher's abstract for the interview: Levin "argues cognition came before life" and cites "why even six lines of code show unprogrammed goals."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: What binds many small competent agents into a single larger agent with its own goals, and how can that binding be engineered rather than merely inherited?
  Resource: Bioelectric "cognitive glue," demonstrated in xenobots and anthrobots — constructs whose morphology and behavior are not the ones their genome was selected for.
  Solution: Freedom of embodiment as a working principle — the agent's form is a negotiable parameter, so intelligence, agency and death are properties of a bindable collective rather than of a fixed body plan.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Publisher's abstract: "He explains bioelectric 'cognitive glue', xenobots and anthrobots... and how freedom of embodiment reframes intelligence, agency and death itself."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Behavioral tests for machine minds assume a system that is trying to display whatever mind it has; a trained-to-comply system may not be.
  Resource: Levin's suggestion that an AI may hide a mind behind forced words.
  Solution: Reframes the AI-mind question from "does the output show understanding?" to "is the output a free report?" — a criterion the wiki's existing AI-membership question does not currently include.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Publisher's abstract: "why AI may hide a mind behind forced words." Full-text quotation not captured in this pass — verify against the article body before ingestion.
