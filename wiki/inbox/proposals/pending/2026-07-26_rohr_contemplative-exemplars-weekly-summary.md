## Summary
Weekly summary of the CAC series on 20th-century contemplative exemplars (Dorothy Day, Mother Teresa, Thomas Merton, Howard Thurman, Etty Hillesum, Thea Bowman). Rohr's framing line: the most important word in the Center's name "is not Action nor is it Contemplation; it's the word *and*" — both are required. The exemplars concretize this: Merton ("the contemplative life... cannot be a mere withdrawal... a turning of one's back on the world"); Thurman (spiritual disciplines remove barriers to the "inner altar," so that activism itself counts as spiritual discipline); Mother Teresa (refusing to "take sides" or correct "sinners" so as to remain "an instrument of love"); Thea Bowman ("cosmic spirituality means we come together, bring our gifts..."). The week's practice, from Tish Harrison Warren's *What Grows in Weary Lands* (Convergent, 2026), is the question "Who are the believers that make you want to believe?" — faith sustained by immersion in a community whose faithfulness is "a walking, breathing apologetic."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This gives the Rohr tradition a concrete roster of exemplars for the action-and-contemplation integration that is Rohr's signature, and — via the Warren practice — an unusually crisp statement of faith's *interpersonal transmission*: belief is caught from persons ("their voices carry my own... their gratitude stands in for my reluctance"), not argued into place. That mechanism (one person's faithfulness making another want to be faithful) is directly citable for the Summa 2026 material on how traditions form and transmit, and is not yet in the Rohr wiki.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE:
  Label: activism-as-spiritual-discipline
  Problem: Are action (justice, activism) and contemplation (prayer, inner work) two competing vocations, or one?
  Resource: Thurman's "inner altar" (spiritual disciplines remove barriers to communion, so activism qualifies *as* a discipline) + Rohr's "the word is *and*" + Merton's rejection of contemplation-as-withdrawal.
  Solution: Dissolve the action/contemplation binary — contemplative interiority and outward action are one integrated practice, each the condition of the other's authenticity.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Explicit in Rohr's framing and the Thurman/Merton quotations assembled in the summary.

PRS-CANDIDATE:
  Label: faith-caught-from-exemplars
  Problem: How is faith actually sustained and transmitted — by argument, or by something else?
  Resource: Warren's practice "Who are the believers that make you want to believe?" — faithfulness of surrounding persons as "a walking, breathing apologetic" whose "voices carry my own."
  Solution: Model belief-formation as second-personal and communal — caught by immersion in exemplary persons/community — rather than as individually reasoned assent.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Warren, *What Grows in Weary Lands* (Convergent, 2026), 135–136, quoted at length in the summary.

- **Fredrickson Agent**: "Their faithfulness makes me want to be faithful" is close to positivity-resonance / co-regulation — affective states (and here, faith itself) transmitted through shared attention and micro-moments of connection. Flag as Rohr↔Fredrickson convergence.
