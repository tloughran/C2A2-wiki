## Summary
Rohr recounts discovering St. Thérèse of Lisieux's writing at nineteen, in seminary, and describes it as the moment that broke the grip of a perfectionist, quasi-Jansenist theology he had been taught ("if you're perfect, if you do it right, God will love you"). He presents her "little way" — surrender and gratitude rather than great achievement — through her own image of trying to climb a staircase to reach God, exhausting herself, until "God runs down the steps and carries her up like a divine elevator." Rohr's gloss: "All God needs is our desire, then God does all the rest."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Rohr naming a specific formative source for his own theology of grace-over-merit — a genealogical data point, not just restated doctrine — for how the Universal Christ program's emphasis on already-given, prior love (rather than earned status) took shape in him personally. Useful primary material for the wiki's account of where Rohr's non-dual, grace-first frame actually comes from.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: How does a theology of unconditional, already-given divine love (central to Rohr's Universal Christ program) get grounded against a strong, culturally dominant counter-tradition of merit, effort, and legalistic self-improvement (which Rohr names, via Thérèse's context, as Jansenism)?
  Resource: Thérèse of Lisieux's "little way" and her staircase parable, read by Rohr as a lived enactment of grace-preceding-effort.
  Solution: Rohr treats Thérèse's small-scale, desire-and-surrender spirituality as a decisive counter-example to perfectionist theology — not argued but personally, biographically decisive for him ("This changes everything!").
  Confidence: Speculative (autobiographical/formational material rather than a new doctrinal claim; its value is genealogical)
  Evidence: Direct quotes from the meditation ("Jesus does not demand great actions from us but simply surrender and gratitude"; "All God needs is our desire, then God does all the rest").
