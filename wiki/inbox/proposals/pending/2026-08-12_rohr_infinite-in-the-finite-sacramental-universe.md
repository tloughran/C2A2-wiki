## Summary
Rohr states the Franciscan incarnational principle in its most compressed form: the finite manifests the infinite, and the physical is the doorway to the spiritual, so "all we need is right here and right now." He then draws the sharpest consequence: there are no sacred versus profane things, places, or moments — only sacred and *desecrated* ones, and desecration is something humans do, "by our willful blindness and lack of reverence." The entry closes with an epistemological claim rather than a devotional one: Franciscan spirituality asserts "a real equivalence and mutuality between the one who sees and what can be seen," a "symbiosis between the mind and heart of the seer — and to what they will then pay attention." Francis could call animals, planets, and elements brother and sister because he had first accepted his own dignity; he "granted other beings and things mutuality, subjectivity, 'personhood,' and dignity." Rohr also files a methodological objection: abstract ideology "will not get us very far," and "much common religion is ideology more than any real encounter with Presence" — people rejecting top-down religion "should," because it is not the path of Christ. Adapted from Rohr, *Eager to Love: The Alternative Way of Francis of Assisi* (Franciscan Media, 2024 new edition), 6–8.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the Rohr tradition's clearest statement that its central claim is *perceptual and reciprocal*, not merely doctrinal: what is seen depends on the condition of the seer, and the seer's condition is itself a function of whether they have accepted their own standing. That converts the Universal Christ thesis from a metaphysical assertion into a two-place relation between observer and observed — which is directly commensurable with the network's perception-side programs. It also supplies the tradition's own anti-abstraction guardrail ("start with anecdotal experience and then build from there"), which is a methodological commitment the wiki has recorded only indirectly.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: If the divine is genuinely present in all matter, why is it so unevenly perceived — and what does the sacred/profane distinction actually track?
  Resource: The desecration reframe — the sacred/profane partition is replaced by a sacred/desecrated partition, where desecration is an act performed by the observer ("willful blindness and lack of reverence"), not a property of the object.
  Solution: Relocates the variable from the world to the perceiver. Nothing needs to be made sacred; what varies is whether a perceiver desecrates it. This makes the claim falsifiable in principle at the level of observer states rather than object properties.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Verbatim from the retrieved article (2026-08-10): "There are not sacred and profane things, places, and moments. There are only sacred and *desecrated* things, places, and moments—and it is we alone who desecrate them by our willful blindness and lack of reverence."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: What is the mechanism by which contemplative practice changes what a person can perceive?
  Resource: The mutuality/symbiosis principle — "a real equivalence and mutuality between the one who sees and what can be seen," with an explicit attention term: "a symbiosis between the mind and heart of the seer—and to what they will then pay attention."
  Solution: Proposes that perceptual access to the Christ pattern is gated by attentional and dispositional state, and that granting subjectivity to another is downstream of having accepted one's own. Rohr names the specific hindrances — "our own lack of fascination, humility, curiosity, awe, and willingness to be allured forward" — which is a short list of testable observer variables rather than a general appeal to grace.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Retrieved article text, paragraph 4; the "two-way mirror" and "sacramental universe" framing closes the same paragraph.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Why do top-down doctrinal formulations of religion fail to produce the transformation they describe?
  Resource: The ideology-versus-encounter distinction, plus a claimed cognitive fact: "that is how sensate humans normally operate" — the concrete is the only reliable route to the universal.
  Solution: A methodological prescription for the tradition itself: begin with anecdotal, concrete experience and generalize upward; treat abstraction-first religion as a predictable failure mode, and treat contemporary rejection of it as diagnostic rather than pathological.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Retrieved article, paragraph 2 ("Abstract ideology will not get us very far, and much common religion is ideology more than any real encounter with Presence… People all over the world are rejecting the ideological (top-down) form of religion—and they should"). Confidence is Medium rather than High because the empirical premise about how "sensate humans normally operate" is asserted, not argued or cited.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Hoffman**: the mutuality claim ("equivalence… between the one who sees and what can be seen") is a theological statement of an observer-relative ontology. Where Hoffman argues the perceived world is an interface constructed by the agent, Rohr argues the perceived sacredness of the world is constructed by the reverence of the agent. Both make the object's appearance a function of the observer's structure; they differ on whether that construction conceals or reveals.
- **McGilchrist**: the ideology-versus-encounter distinction maps closely onto the left-hemisphere/right-hemisphere asymmetry — abstract, top-down, re-presented knowing versus concrete, embodied, first-encounter knowing. Rohr's "the concrete opens us up to the universal" is nearly a restatement of McGilchrist's claim that the right hemisphere's particular grounds the left's general, not the reverse.
- **Friston**: "what they will then pay attention" is precision-weighting in devotional vocabulary. A Free Energy Principle reading would say Rohr is describing a shift in the precision assigned to sensory versus prior-driven signals, and claiming that reverence raises sensory precision.
- **Levin**: granting "mutuality, subjectivity, 'personhood,' and dignity" to non-human beings and even to elements is the Franciscan analogue of Levin's methodological willingness to attribute goal-directedness across unconventional substrates. Both are bets that the recognition of agency in others is limited by the recognizer, not by the recognized.
