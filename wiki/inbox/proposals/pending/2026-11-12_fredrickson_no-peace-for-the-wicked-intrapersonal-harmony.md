## Summary
Prinzing & Fredrickson (2023), *Cognitive Science* 47(11), e13371 (DOI 10.1111/cogs.13371). Three experiments (N = 1,185) found that people judge moral agents to be happier than immoral ones, even when the two are described with identical psychological states. Judgments of *inner harmony* fully explain the effect: immoral agents are seen as more internally conflicted. When a character is stipulated to be "at peace with himself," the morality effect disappears. The authors note the folk view matches Plato in tying happiness to inner harmony, but differs from him: people do not treat virtue as *necessary* for that harmony.

**Not a recent item.** It fails the 30-day window and is proposed under the "significant work not yet captured" clause: a search of `traditions/fredrickson/` and `inbox/proposals/` found no reference to it. It is the program's clearest contact point with virtue ethics. The finding is about *folk attributions* of happiness, not measured wellbeing of actual moral and immoral agents. That distinction should carry into any triplet.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Most of the Fredrickson wiki is about positive emotion and resonance *between* people. This paper adds the *within*-person structure that folk psychology thinks positive states need: harmony among one's own desires and judgments. It also links the lab's meaning-in-life work (Prinzing) to classical virtue theory.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Why do ordinary people's judgments of "happiness" depend on whether a person is moral, even when that person's feelings are held fixed?
  Resource: Three vignette experiments manipulating morality while holding described mental states constant; mediation by perceived intrapersonal harmony; a boundary condition where harmony is stipulated
  Solution: The folk concept of happiness includes inner harmony, and immorality is presumed to break it. When harmony is stipulated, the morality effect vanishes, so harmony, not morality as such, does the explanatory work.
  Confidence: High for the attribution finding; Speculative for any inference about actual wellbeing
  Evidence: Full mediation of morality to happiness judgments by inner-harmony judgments; null morality effect in the "at peace with themselves" conditions (Cognitive Science Society summary; preprint excerpt).

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Is virtue necessary for inner harmony (Plato, Aquinas), or only typically associated with it?
  Resource: The paper's own contrast with Plato: contemporary participants accept a harmonious wicked agent as happy
  Solution: An empirical marker separating folk eudaimonism from classical eudaimonism. People share the classical *structure* (happiness requires harmony) but drop the classical *necessity claim* (harmony requires virtue). That turns a long philosophical dispute into a question that can be tested.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Authors' discussion: like Plato, people see happiness as requiring inner harmony, but they do not seem to think moral virtue is necessary for it.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Stump (flag strongly):** Stump's central notion of *internal integration* (Wandering in Darkness; the Dewey Lecture "Dilige et quod vis fac," already in the vault) holds that the will is divided against itself in wrongdoing, and that integration around the good is the condition for union with others. This paper is the closest empirical bridge in the vault to that thesis. It finds the folk *structure* she relies on, and finds that the folk *deny* the Thomistic necessity claim. That gives the Stump Agent a well-posed dispute to carry, not a confirmation. Candidate for a Stump-Fredrickson bridge note.
- **MacIntyre (ISMI paper relevance):** the gap between the folk concept (harmony without virtue) and the Thomistic one (harmony through virtue) fits MacIntyre's "fragments" diagnosis in After Virtue: a moral vocabulary that keeps the shape of a tradition after losing the claims that made it cohere.
- **Fredrickson internal link:** pairs with "Does shared positivity make life more meaningful?" (PROP-2026-10-08-002): harmony *within* and resonance *between* as the two structural conditions of a flourishing life.
- **Friston:** intrapersonal conflict as competing priors within one generative model; "inner harmony" as low internal prediction error across levels.
