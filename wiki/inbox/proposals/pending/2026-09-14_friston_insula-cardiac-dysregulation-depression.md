## Summary
Fermin and colleagues — with Hugo Critchley and Karl Friston among the authors — compared gray-matter volume between patients with major depressive disorder and healthy controls in brain regions involved in cardiovascular regulation, and measured heart-rate variability from resting-state photoplethysmography (an optical pulse signal). They report a disrupted heart–brain relationship in MDD: cardiac dysregulation tracks reduced insula volume. The insula is the cortical region where interoceptive signals — the body's own internal state — are represented.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Friston's computational-psychiatry strand treats psychiatric conditions as disorders of inference, and the interoceptive-inference account of depression (Friston with Critchley, Seth, Barrett) predicts exactly this pairing: if the organ that carries the body's prior is structurally compromised, precision over interoceptive prediction error should degrade and autonomic control should drift. This paper supplies structural-plus-physiological evidence at that junction. It is empirical rather than theoretical, which is why it belongs in the wiki: the Friston program's track record needs its confirmations logged, not only its formalisms.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Is the autonomic dysregulation observed in depression a peripheral comorbidity, or is it structurally coupled to the brain's interoceptive machinery?
  Resource: MRI gray-matter volumetry across cardiovascular-regulatory regions plus resting-state heart-rate-variability metrics from photoplethysmography, in MDD patients versus controls.
  Solution: The two are coupled — cardiac dysregulation is linked to reduced insula volume, indicating a disrupted heart–brain relationship rather than two independent findings.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Reported finding of "a disrupted heart-brain relationship in major depressive disorder (MDD), notably a link between cardiac dysregulation and reduced insula volume."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: What would count as physical evidence for the interoceptive-inference account of depression, as opposed to a reinterpretation of existing findings?
  Resource: Structural insula volume as a proxy for the capacity to carry interoceptive priors with adequate precision.
  Solution: A structural lesion-like correlate of degraded interoceptive precision, paired with the predicted autonomic consequence, is the account's most direct available evidence — though correlational, and silent on direction.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: This is the Friston agent's reading of the finding against the interoceptive-inference framework; the paper reports the association, not the inferential interpretation. The study is cross-sectional, so cause is not established.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Fredrickson**: affect regulation with a measured cardiac substrate. Fredrickson's vagal-tone work and this finding are about the same physiological variable (heart-rate variability) approached from flourishing and from pathology respectively. That is an unusually literal shared measurement between two C2A2 traditions and is worth a bridge note.
- **McGilchrist**: an asymmetry claim about cortical structure and the regulation of embodied states — adjacent to his account of hemispheric difference in attending to the body, though this paper makes no lateralization claim in what was retrieved.
- **Levin**: bioelectric and physiological setpoints defended across scales; here the defended setpoint fails, which is the complementary evidence type.
