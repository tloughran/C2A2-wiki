## Summary
The week-34 summary assembles Rohr's own true-self / false-self week (2026-08-23 through 08-28) and pairs it with a practice drawn from the Buddhist teacher Tara Brach. Two claims from Rohr's own days are load-bearing and stated compactly here: first, that the true self just is the divine indwelling — the Holy Spirit within, cited to Romans 8:9 and 1 Corinthians 3:16 — and second, that the false self is *not* the bad self. Rohr's position is that the false self is good and necessary as far as it goes, and that its defect is insufficiency and substitution, not badness. Brach's practice supplies the etymological hinge: "person" derives from *persona*, the actor's mask, and the ordinary failure is conflating the mask with its wearer rather than wearing no mask at all.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the week's canonical statement and the cleanest single citation point for the true-self frame, which the rohr wiki currently reaches only through *Immortal Diamond* as a canonical work. Two things here are not in the wiki. The first is the explicitly *non-pejorative* status of the false self, which blocks the standard misreading of Rohr as anti-ego and matters for any synthesis that wants to hold the frame alongside a Thomistic account of the natural powers as good in themselves. The second is the persona / wearer distinction, which is a usable formalization: the mask is a real and sometimes serviceable object, and the error is a type error about what one is identified with.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: false-self-is-insufficient-not-evil
  Problem: Contemplative literature on the ego reads easily as a moral condemnation of ordinary functioning selfhood, which puts it in apparent conflict with any account (Thomistic included) on which the natural powers are good and their disorder is privative.
  Resource: Rohr's Tuesday statement that the false self is good and necessary as far as it goes, and that its failure mode is falling short and posing as the real thing.
  Solution: The false self is a *scope* failure and a *substitution* failure, not a moral one. This lines the frame up with a privative account of disorder — the false self is not a rival good but a partial one mistaken for the whole.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: The Tuesday entry states the point directly and in Rohr's own voice; it is a restatement of *Immortal Diamond*'s position, not a new move, but this is the compact citable form.

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: persona-as-type-error
  Problem: If the false self is neither evil nor dispensable, the account still owes a statement of what exactly goes wrong — "identification with the ego" names the fault without analyzing it.
  Resource: Tara Brach's persona framing (*Trusting the Gold*, 2021, 135-136): the ancient actor's mask was known by everyone present to be a mask and was removed after the performance; the modern failure is that the removal step is missing and the mask is taken for the essential being.
  Solution: The fault is a category mistake about the referent of "I" — a self-model is mistaken for the self it models. Stated this way the error is diagnosable and correctable by attention rather than by moral effort, which is what makes contemplative practice the appropriate remedy.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: The practice section develops the persona etymology and the clown joke to exactly this point; the "consciousness looking out through their eyes" line extends it to other agents. Confidence is held low because the formalization as a type error is the proposal's move, not Brach's.
