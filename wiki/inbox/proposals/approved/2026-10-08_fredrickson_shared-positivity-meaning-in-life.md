## Summary
Prinzing, Le Nguyen & Fredrickson (2023) test whether positivity resonance (co-experienced positive affect, mutual care, synchrony) fosters a sense of meaning in life, across four studies (total N = 1,791). In three observational studies, resonance was linked to meaning between and within persons, day-level resonance predicted later meaning (not the reverse), and social resources mediated the link. A 35-day randomized experiment raised resonance but the full mediation path was only marginal (p = .073).

Caveats from the authors: secondary analyses, self-report measures, Study 4 "suggestive but inconclusive", and the unique-of-pleasantness result weakened in Study 3 and the Study 2 mediation after emotion controls.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
It extends positivity resonance from a health and well-being correlate to a candidate source of meaning, via social resources (supportive relationships and communities). That is the Fredrickson program's closest empirical contact with the meaning and teleology questions the other C2A2 traditions treat.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Does shared positive affect, beyond individual pleasant feeling, contribute to a sense that life is meaningful, and through what route?
  Resource: Positivity resonance (perceived, trait and episode level) paired with day-level longitudinal designs, social-resources mediators, and a 35-day randomized induction (mindfulness vs. social connectedness vs. control).
  Solution: Resonance predicts later meaning (not the reverse) in daily data, and the link is mediated by social resources; partial evidence of specificity to resonance over pleasantness.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Studies 1-3 show between- and within-person links and temporal precedence; Study 4 experimental mediation path p = .073 (marginal); mediation in Study 2 lost significance after emotion controls.
