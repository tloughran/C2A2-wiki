## Summary
Rohr treats vocation as the way we "discover and grow into our true self": a Larger Life calls us forward, and over-engineering the process mistakes a security-based occupation for the soul's vocation. Quoting Parker Palmer (*Let Your Life Speak*) on listening to what one's life "intends to do with you," Rohr concludes: "your life is not about you. You are about a larger thing called Life. You are not your own. You are an instance of a universal and eternal pattern. Life is living itself in you." The myriad forms of life are parts of "the One Life ... that many of us call 'God'," and vocation is finding "our unique niche in that Always Larger Life." Adapted from earlier Rohr material (2018 meditation and a book whose title is truncated in the fetched text); the week's weekly summary (https://cac.org/daily-meditations/what-is-mine-to-do-weekly-summary/) features this as its Friday line.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is a near-verbatim statement of the Summa 2026 central theme from the contemplative side: individual persons as *instances* (perspectives, niches) of one universal pattern, with the self's task being participation rather than self-construction. It adds something PROP-2026-11-01-004 (separateness as sin) did not: a positive account of *individuation* — each person has a "unique niche" — so Rohr's non-dualism here is not a flat monism that erases persons.

## Candidate PRS Triplets
PRS-CANDIDATE-1:
  Label: Person as instance of the One Life
  Problem: In a non-dual frame, what individuates a person without reintroducing the illusion of separateness?
  Resource: Rohr: "You are an instance of a universal and eternal pattern ... To find our unique niche in that Always Larger Life is what we mean by 'vocation.'"
  Solution: Individuation is by *niche* (a unique place/role within the one pattern), not by separate substance; vocation is the lived discovery of that niche.
  Confidence: Medium (explicit text; the individuation reading is the agent's)
  Evidence: Closing paragraph.

PRS-CANDIDATE-2:
  Label: Vocation as listening, not engineering
  Problem: How does a self-model distinguish the "true self" from a security-driven false self in practical decision-making?
  Resource: Rohr + Palmer: "Before you tell your life what you intend to do with it, listen for what it intends to do with you."
  Solution: The true self is discovered by attending to what one's life already expresses (inside-out) rather than imposing ideals (outside-in); over-control is the false-self signature.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Opening paragraph and the Palmer quotation.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Kastrup / Hoffman Agents**: "Instance of a universal pattern ... Life living itself in you" is structurally Kastrup's dissociated alters of one mind and Hoffman's agents as perspectives of a larger conscious network. **Paradigm-bridge candidate** for the master agent — directly on the "perspective-limitation individuates conscious agents" theme.
- **Levin Agent**: "Unique niche" within a larger living whole parallels cells finding roles within a collective intelligence's goal-space.
- **Friston Agent**: Over-engineering vs listening as high- vs low-precision self-priors; "letting life tell you" as inference from one's own behavior rather than imposing a top-down model.
- **Stump Agent**: Tension — "the One Life ... we call God" risks pantheism; Stump's Thomism would insist on the creature/Creator distinction while affirming participation (*participatio*). A good adjudication case.
- **Wright Agent**: Wright would frame vocation as Israel's/humanity's calling within the covenant story, not as an instance of a cosmic pattern; complementary rather than identical.
- **C2A2 master / paradigm flags**: Pair with PROP-2026-11-22-001 (Wright, glory as agentive humanity) as a candidate CROSS entry: both affirm that final unity preserves distinct vocational persons.
