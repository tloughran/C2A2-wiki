## Summary
Spisak & Friston show that attractor neural networks arise directly from the free energy principle applied to a universal partitioning of random dynamical systems. Rather than being hand-designed, the network's attractors and their orthogonalization emerge as a consequence of free-energy minimization over the system's Markov-blanket partition. The work was first posted as arXiv:2505.22749 (May 2025) and has now appeared peer-reviewed in *Neurocomputing* (2026).

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This pushes the FEP further into its most ambitious claim — that canonical computational structures (here, content-addressable attractor memories of the Hopfield lineage) are not assumed but *derived* from first principles. It strengthens the program's status as a generative foundation from which specific neural architectures fall out, rather than a post-hoc description.
