## Summary
Stump separates philosophy from theology by their object. The philosopher seeks impersonal wisdom; the theologian seeks a personal God. Knowledge of a person is a different kind of knowledge from "knowledge that" (propositional knowledge), so a theology grounded in second-person knowledge changes how the divine-hiddenness argument (Schellenberg) should be assessed. Originally a chapter in Fiona Ellis (ed.), *New Models of Religious Understanding* (OUP 2017, pp. 172-190); reprinted open-access (CC BY-NC-ND) in *Roczniki Filozoficzne* 69.3 (2021), pp. 9-27, DOI 10.18290/rf21693-1.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the compact, citable statement of the epistemology underneath *Wandering in Darkness*: second-person and narrative knowledge are irreducible to propositions. The wiki already holds the theme indirectly (Dewey Lecture, Biblical Narratives), but not this source, which states it head-on and is free to read. PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: What distinguishes theology from philosophy, if both use the same analytic tools?
  Resource: The difference between knowing a person (second-person knowledge) and knowing that something is true
  Solution: Theology's object is personal, so its knowledge is second-personal at root; philosophy seeks impersonal wisdom
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Abstract: "A philosopher is looking after impersonal wisdom, a theologian searches for a personal God. This differentiation is fundamental because knowledge of persons differs from knowledge that."
