## Summary
In a solo Q&A meeting, Kastrup argues that everything is determined and nothing is random, and that this — not libertarian free will, which he calls incoherent — is what gives life meaning: each choice is the universe (Consciousness) discovering what it is. Choices are determined but unpredictable (predicting them would require a computer the size of the universe), so they feel free; "your actions emerge 'freely' from who you are, but are determined by what you are." Ethical consequences: blame is "metaphysically naive," shame should be cherished, and justice should be more pragmatic and compassionate (Dutch vs US systems), without sliding into apathy. Also covers daimonic/archetypal forces as limits on how far set-in-motion history can be tempered.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Fills a gap in the Kastrup wiki: an explicit statement of the free will / determinism position inside analytic idealism, and its ethical payoff (blame, shame, justice).

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Wolfram (strong):** "determined but unpredictable — you'd need a universe-sized computer" is essentially computational irreducibility; Kastrup's "self-discovery programme of life" is a direct bridge to Wolfram's ruliad and his own account of free will. Flag for master agent.
- **Stump (strong tension):** Thomistic libertarian/compatibilist accounts of free will and moral responsibility vs Kastrup's "blame is metaphysically naive" — a live rival-tradition fault line, good C2A2 dialogue material.
- **Friston/Levin:** agency as determined-but-unpredictable goal-directed behaviour.
- **Fredrickson:** compassion over revenge as a flourishing-relevant ethic.
- AI consciousness, Hoffman, and individual-vs-collective mind: not addressed in the public material.
