## Summary
Sacco, Sakthivadivel and Levin derive necessary conditions on the *topology of a graph* for an ordered phase to exist at all. By studying how free energy scales under domain-wall formation in the Potts model, in autoregressive models, and in hierarchical networks, they show that the combinatorics of who-interacts-with-whom can by itself prevent or permit spontaneous ordering — independent of the physics of the individual units.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Levin's program asserts that collective intelligence and large-scale order arise from local signaling among cells. This paper supplies the missing *negative* result: a formal statement of which interaction topologies make such collective ordering impossible. That converts "cells self-organize" from a descriptive claim into a constrained, falsifiable one, and gives the morphogenetic-field idea a mathematical boundary condition.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Levin's framework says collective order emerges from local interaction, but offers no principled account of when local interaction *fails* to produce order — so the claim resists falsification.
  Resource: Free-energy scaling analysis of domain-wall formation on graphs (Potts model, autoregressive models, hierarchical networks); a topological criterion for the existence of an ordered phase.
  Solution: Necessary conditions on interaction-graph topology for an ordered phase to exist, making "spontaneous ordering" a property predictable from connectivity structure rather than from substrate.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: The paper shows "how the combinatorics of interactions on a graph prevent or allow spontaneous ordering," derived via scaling of free energy under domain wall formation.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Is there a substrate-independent way to compare the collective-intelligence capacity of biological tissue, a neural network, and an artificial agent collective?
  Resource: The result's applicability across Potts lattices, autoregressive models, and hierarchical networks — three substrates under one topological criterion.
  Solution: A candidate substrate-neutral comparator: rank systems by whether their interaction topology admits an ordered phase, not by what the units are made of.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The analysis is carried out uniformly over statistical-physical, machine-learning, and hierarchical-network models.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
**Strong Friston bridge — flag explicitly.** Co-author Dalton Sakthivadivel is a principal author in Friston's Bayesian-mechanics orbit (Markov blankets, free-energy formalism as a physics of beliefs). This is a *direct co-authorship bridge* between the Levin and Friston research programs, not merely a thematic resonance. The paper's use of variational free energy over graph topologies is precisely the formal object Friston's Markov-blanket construction operates on, which suggests a shared formalism in which Levin's morphogenetic collectives and Friston's particular kinds are two readings of one mathematics.

**Wolfram signal (Medium):** the claim that combinatorics of a graph alone constrain what order can arise is close in spirit to Wolfram's hypergraph-rewriting account of emergent structure — both make topology, not substrate, the carrier of possibility.

**Arkani-Hamed signal (Speculative):** a result of the form "the shape of the interaction structure determines what can exist" rhymes with the amplituhedron program's move from dynamics-first to geometry-first explanation. Worth a watch, not yet a claim.
