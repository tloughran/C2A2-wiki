## Summary
Friston (first author, with nineteen collaborators) sets out a decade-scale research agenda for AI built on active inference. The target is a **cyber-physical ecosystem of natural and synthetic sense-making in which humans are integral participants** — "shared intelligence." Intelligence is defined as the capacity to accumulate evidence for a generative model of one's sensed world (self-evidencing). The paper's load-bearing move is from single agents to ensembles: when agents share parts of their generative models, those shared parts constitute a common frame of reference, and belief-sharing across that common ground yields a formal account of **collective intelligence resting on shared narratives and goals**. Bayesian mechanics supplies the explicit dynamics for groups of sparsely coupled systems self-organizing across several scales.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Friston's most explicit statement that the free energy principle is a **design discipline for multi-agent human-AI ecologies**, not only a theory of individual brains. It is the natural home for FEP-applied-to-social-systems questions, and its absence from the wiki is a real gap in the Friston program's coverage.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: AI research lacks a formal definition of intelligence that applies equally to natural and synthetic agents, so "intelligence" in AI is operationalized as benchmark performance rather than as a principled quantity.
  Resource: Self-evidencing — intelligence as the capacity to accumulate evidence for a generative model of one's sensed world, inherited from the physics of self-organization.
  Solution: A substrate-neutral definition of intelligence that reads active inference as a physics of intelligence, applying identically to biological and artificial agents.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: The paper's explicit framing of active inference as a formulation of adaptive behaviour that can be read as a physics of intelligence, with intelligence understood as self-evidencing.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Collective intelligence is typically described rather than derived — there is no principled account of what makes a group of agents a cognitive unit rather than a crowd.
  Resource: Belief sharing in ensembles, where overlapping regions of each agent's generative model constitute common ground or a shared frame of reference.
  Solution: A formal account of collective intelligence grounded in shared narratives and goals, with Bayesian mechanics giving explicit dynamics for the beliefs of agents and of groups of agents.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: The paper's account of belief sharing among agents whose generative models provide common ground, presented as yielding a formal treatment of collective intelligence resting on shared narratives and goals.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Multi-scale systems — agents within groups within institutions — are usually modelled with different formalisms at each level, so cross-scale claims cannot be checked.
  Resource: Bayesian mechanics for sparsely coupled systems that self-organize over several scales.
  Solution: One formalism specifies the dynamics at every scale, making the engineering of multi-scale intelligence ecosystems a well-posed design problem.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The claim that Bayesian mechanics is uniquely suited to engineering ecosystems of intelligence by allowing specification of the dynamics of sparsely coupled systems self-organizing over several scales; presented as a research agenda rather than a completed derivation.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Levin (explicit, flagged)** — Self-evidencing across nested, sparsely coupled scales is the same shape of claim as Levin's multiscale competency architecture and cognitive light cones. Both programs assert that goal-directedness composes across levels; they disagree, or at least differ, on what does the composing (shared generative models vs. bioelectric signalling). This is a live convergence and should generate a dispatch in both directions. See also PROP-2026-08-17-002 (Pezzulo & Levin), which approaches the same junction from Levin's side.
- **C2A2 relevance (very high — this may be the most directly relevant Friston work in the corpus)** — "A cyber-physical ecosystem of natural and synthetic sense-making in which humans are integral participants" is a description of what C2A2 is attempting. More pointedly, the mechanism Friston proposes for collective intelligence — **shared generative models providing a common frame of reference, enabling belief sharing** — is a formal restatement of the C2A2 thesis that rich mutual information about one another's traditions changes how communities interact. MacIntyre's "second first language" competence could plausibly be formalized as an agent carrying a second generative model well enough to share beliefs from within it. Recommend the master agent treat this as candidate mathematical scaffolding for the accelerator/detector claim.
- **Hoffman / Kastrup** — Self-evidencing makes no commitment to the veridicality of the model being evidenced, which is compatible with interface theory and with idealism; a point of non-conflict worth recording, since these traditions are often assumed to be opposed to FEP.
- **Wolfram** — Sparse coupling across scales is a statement about computational architecture; the Markov blanket / computational boundary correspondence flagged in the Friston agent brief applies directly here.
