## Summary
Manrique, Friston & Walker apply active inference to social discrimination, introducing "zones of bounded surprisal" (ZBS) — the band-width of outcomes a mind tolerates without large free-energy spikes. They argue that narrow ZBS band-widths characterise in-group / discriminatory minds, and that the narrowest band-widths are a signature of dehumanising cognition that licenses harm to out-group members.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is FEP applied directly to social systems and inter-group conflict — one of the explicitly flagged cross-tradition / C2A2 signals for this agent. It gives a formal, free-energy account of *why* richly-informed cross-tradition exchange is hard (narrow ZBS resists surprising, perspective-widening evidence) and therefore a measurable target for whether the tradition-accelerator actually widens minds.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Can discriminatory and dehumanising cognition be given a principled, formal (rather than purely descriptive) account?
  Resource: "Zones of bounded surprisal" (ZBS) — the tolerated band-width of outcomes within an active-inference agent's generative model before free energy spikes.
  Solution: Discrimination is modelled as a narrowing of ZBS band-width; dehumanising minds occupy the narrowest band, where out-group-confirming surprise is minimised by exclusion rather than belief update.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: The paper's core claim that narrow ZBS band-widths characterise in-group discriminatory minds and that the narrowest band-widths are characteristic of dehumanising minds that harm others.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: What would it mean, formally, to "widen" a community so it can engage rival traditions without threat?
  Resource: ZBS band-width as a tunable, in-principle measurable parameter of a community's collective generative model.
  Solution: Widening cross-tradition competence = broadening ZBS so out-group-originating evidence is assimilated (belief update) rather than repelled (action/exclusion) — giving C2A2 a candidate metric for "richly-informed" exchange.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: Direct extension of the ZBS construct; the C2A2 application is inferred, not stated in the source.
