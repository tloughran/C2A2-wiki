## Summary
Rohr-curated meditation from the "Faith with Doubt" week, built almost entirely on Barbara Brown Taylor's *Leaving Church* (HarperOne, 2012), 170-172 and 209. The organizing image is a map with a centre and an edge. At the centre stands the institution — sustained practice, inherited objects, named children, continuity across generations — and Brown Taylor's claim is that these people keep the map from blowing away. At the edge is unscripted encounter with an undomesticated God, without the institution's furniture or signage, and those people keep the map from becoming redundant. Both are said to be biblically necessary and to be so unlike each other that inhabitants of one struggle to grant that the other belongs on the same map; what is settled at the centre is open at the edge. Brown Taylor's own reported shift is from faith-as-certainty to faith-as-trust — trusting God to be God without being able to say who God is, and without conclusive evidence — which she describes as turning faith from a noun into a verb. She closes by relocating vocation from office to identity: the calling to love God and neighbour is portable, and priesthood was who she was rather than what she did.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The rohr wiki currently carries the two-halves-of-life frame as a *temporal* developmental sequence. This source supplies the same structure as a *spatial and social* one — centre and edge as simultaneously occupied positions in one community rather than successive stages in one life — and adds the load-bearing claim that both are required. That is a real addition, and it is the piece the tradition needs before any of the network's collective-agency mappings can be made: a tradition that needs both a conservative centre and an exploratory edge is describing a division of epistemic labour, not a maturity ladder. It is also directly citable for Summa loci on the states of life and on the relation of contemplative to active vocation (II-II Q.179-189), where the question of whether a vocation survives the loss of its office is put in almost these terms.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: centre-and-edge-as-a-required-pair
  Problem: Rohr's developmental account reads the move past certainty as growth, which implies the people who stay at the centre are simply behind, and gives no account of what the centre is *for*.
  Resource: Brown Taylor's centre/edge map — the centre keeping the map from blowing away, the edge keeping it from becoming redundant — presented as two biblically necessary positions on one map rather than two stages of one life.
  Solution: Certainty-holding and mystery-seeking are complementary functions distributed across a community: the centre supplies persistence and transmission, the edge supplies contact with what the map does not yet cover, and a tradition with only one of them either drifts or ossifies.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Stated directly in the quoted passage, including the claim that both belong on the same map and that each keeps the other from a named failure mode.

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: vocation-survives-the-loss-of-office
  Problem: If a calling is constituted by an institutional role, leaving the role should terminate the calling — yet the contemplative tradition keeps producing figures whose work continues outside the institution.
  Resource: Brown Taylor's relocation of vocation from function to identity ("not what I did but who I was"), with the vocation to love God and neighbour named as exercisable anywhere, with anyone.
  Solution: Vocation is an identity-level property rather than a role-level one, so departure from the institution is continuity rather than loss; nothing prior is wasted, and the institution becomes one venue for the calling rather than its condition.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The closing paragraph, stated in the first person about her own case; the generalization to a rule is the proposal's inference, not hers.
