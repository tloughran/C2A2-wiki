## Summary

Mindscape 366, released 2026-08-31, with Jim Al-Khalili (Distinguished Emeritus Professor of Physics,
Surrey) on his new book *On Time: The Physics that Makes the Universe Tick*. The vault holds 363, 364
and 365; 366 is uncaptured.

**The reason to capture it is Carroll's own side of the conversation, not the guest's.** This is an
episode where Carroll says explicitly that he is going to "play dumb, to pretend I don't have strong
opinions about the nature of time" — and then does not manage it. Across the arrow-of-time and
quantum-biology stretches he states three positions in his own voice: what life does with the second
law, what he declines to say about life and entropy, and what irreversibility consists of. The guest,
meanwhile, holds a position **Carroll's published work rejects** — that time's directionality is
*more* fundamental than the time-symmetric laws, which are "just idealizations that aren't giving us
the whole picture." The disagreement is aired courteously and is not resolved, which makes this an
unusually clean record of a boundary in the poetic-naturalism program.

**Verification status.** The episode page was retrieved directly, including the full published
transcript, and every quotation below is from that text. Limits: (a) the transcript is the show's own
published one and was read from the page rather than checked against the audio; (b) roughly the first
two thirds were read closely (through the quantum-biology section, ~0:56); **the final third was not
read**, so nothing here rests on the cosmology closing. (c) The guest's book itself was not read; his
positions are recorded as stated in conversation, not as the book argues them.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

It bears directly on **carroll Active Research Question 12**, opened today — whether higher-level
causal structure is real or bookkeeping when it is *manipulable*. That question was opened from the
Levin side (the Pigozzi–Levin pre-replicator causal-emergence result). Here Carroll is on record about
the adjacent case, life and entropy, and what he says is careful in a way that matters: he endorses
the deflationary reading of "life struggles against entropy" (it does not; it exploits), but he
**declines** the equally available strong reading in the other direction ("life is a way for the
universe to increase entropy"), on the ground that there is no law requiring entropy to increase as
fast as possible. That is a tradition-holder refusing a teleological gloss that would have been
convenient, and it sharpens what poetic naturalism will and will not license.

It also gives the vault its first Carroll-voiced statement of **what irreversibility consists of** on
the quantum side, which the arrow-of-time entries have so far carried only in general terms.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Whether living organisms are in some sense fighting the second law of thermodynamics — a framing common in popular and some scientific writing, and one that would make life anomalous with respect to physics.
  Resource: The free-energy accounting: low-entropy photons arrive from the sun, the organism uses them to maintain structure, organisation and metabolism far from equilibrium, and radiates a much larger number of high-entropy thermal photons.
  Solution: The framing is exactly backwards. Life does not struggle against the second law; it is a mechanism that **takes advantage** of it, and the entropy books balance in the ordinary direction with room to spare. No new physics is required and life is not anomalous.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Carroll, in his own voice and unprompted: "my personal reaction is no, that's exactly backwards. Life is taking advantage of the second law of thermodynamics. It's using that free energy from the sun to maintain its equil... Not its equilibrium, its structure, its organization, its metabolism, and so forth." Al-Khalili agrees and supplies the photon accounting. High because it is Carroll's own stated position, verbatim, on a question he raised himself.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Having deflated "life fights entropy," the symmetric temptation is to inflate in the other direction — to say life exists *in order that* the universe increase its entropy, a maximum-entropy-production teleology that has real currency in origin-of-life and complexity work.
  Resource: The observation that the second law fixes a direction but not a rate: "There's no law that says it increases as fast as possible."
  Solution: The strong reading is declined rather than refuted. Carroll states he does not know whether it is true, gives the rate objection as the reason for doubt, and says the question is unfinished. **The Solution is a withheld endorsement, and that is the content** — a tradition-holder refusing a teleological gloss that his own deflationary move had made rhetorically available.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: "sometimes people wanna say in that spirit, life is a way for the universe to increase entropy, right?... I don't know if that's true or not. I mean, I kind of have a feeling like the entropy is gonna increase anyway. There's no law that says it increases as fast as possible. You got to think harder about what life is doing." Al-Khalili adds an independent objection from scale: if life is rare, the universe would be "relying on this tiny planet in this outer suburbs of some galaxy to help it increase its entropy," which "seems a bit wacky." High as a record of Carroll's stated position; the *underlying question* is explicitly open, and this triplet records the refusal, not an answer.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Where quantum irreversibility comes from, given that Schrödinger evolution is time-symmetric and measurement is not — and whether decoherence or entanglement is the operation that supplies the arrow.
  Resource: Decoherence defined as entanglement with an environment, plus the observation that entanglement with a *single* qubit is straightforwardly reversible.
  Solution: Neither entanglement nor decoherence is irreversible in itself. The arrow is statistical-mechanical: "Once you entangle with enough things, you effectively in practice lose the ability to reverse it." Quantum irreversibility is therefore the same kind of fact as classical thermodynamic irreversibility, not an additional one — which is what poetic naturalism requires if the quantum arrow is not to be a second, independent ingredient.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Al-Khalili poses it directly — "would you say that entanglement of a system with something else is itself an irreversible process?" Carroll: "If you just want to entangle with one qubit, you can easily reverse that... So I think it's exactly thermodynamics or stat mech at work. Once you entangle with enough things, you effectively in practice lose the ability to reverse it." Earlier in the same exchange: "I like to just define decoherence as entanglement with an environment. And once you agree on what the environment is, which is a tricky thing, I admit, but that is what counts as decoherence for me."

PRS-CANDIDATE-04:
  Problem: Whether time's directionality is derived or primitive. The standard programme, which Carroll has spent two books on, derives the arrow from time-symmetric fundamental laws plus a low-entropy past hypothesis.
  Resource: Al-Khalili's contrary position, stated in Carroll's own venue with Carroll present: irreversibility is the more fundamental fact, and time-symmetric laws are an artefact of idealisation.
  Solution: **No solution — this is a recorded, unresolved disagreement between the tradition-holder and a guest whose competence he affirms.** Al-Khalili: "my view... is that it's the other way around, that time's direction, directionality, irreversibility is more fundamental. And symmetric equations and symmetric laws of physics, I think are just idealizations that aren't giving us the whole picture." Carroll flags it for the audience as a controversy, says "we'll come back to it later," and does not litigate it in the read portion.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Quotations above, verbatim. **Medium, and the reason is a coverage gap, not a source problem: the final third of the transcript was not read, and Carroll said he would return to this.** He may answer it there. Anyone using this triplet should read the closing section first. It is filed rather than withheld because the *existence* of an aired, unresolved disagreement with a competent interlocutor is itself the datum this project collects, and it is attested in the portion that was read.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **[[Levin]] — the sharpest, and it lands on a question opened in this vault today.** carroll Active Research Question 12 asks whether higher-level causal structure is real or bookkeeping when it is *manipulable* (from Pigozzi & Levin's pre-replicator causal-emergence result, levin PRS-102/103). Candidate-02 is Carroll working the adjacent case with visible care: he takes the deflationary side on "life fights entropy" and then **declines** the inflationary side on "life exists to increase entropy," giving a rate argument. Levin's programme runs the other way — directedness precedes life and is a manipulable control knob. **These are not yet in contact and should not be recorded as contact.** What the pair establishes is that Carroll's deflationism is not reflexive: he refuses a teleological reading even when his own move sets it up. That makes the Levin disagreement (CROSS-130) sharper, because it cannot be dismissed as Carroll deflating on principle.
- **[[Levin]] — second, on definition.** Al-Khalili, agreeing with Carroll about life and free energy, adds: "we still don't have a good definition of what life is. How does it do that? And why don't other things do that that are non life? I don't know." That is the same admission the Levin tradition holds at PRS-93/94/95 from *Defining Life: A Conversation* — where the vault deliberately preserves the dispute unadjudicated. Two independent venues, one year apart, reaching the same open state.
- **[[Friston]] — genuine and specific.** Al-Khalili's account of how quantum biology survives a warm wet environment is that life may have evolved to **tune its environment** so decoherence does not simply drain coherence away: "the environment isn't Markovian, there's some non-Markovianity... it's helping keep the quantumness going." A system that shapes its own environment to preserve an internal state, described in explicitly non-Markovian terms, is the shape of active inference at a scale nobody has claimed it at. Recorded as a **shape match, not a claim** — no one in this conversation mentions the free energy principle, and non-Markovianity here is a technical term about bath memory, not about Markov blankets. The two uses of "Markov" must not be silently merged; this is precisely the equivocation FLAG-020 was raised about.
- **[[Wolfram]] — one line.** Al-Khalili's account of how life found quantum tricks — "Everything happens by accident, and the things that work, work" over billions of years — is exhaustive search over a space of possibilities, which is the ruliological framing in ordinary language. Too thin for a dispatch; worth one line.
- **[[C2A2 architecture]] — small but on point.** Carroll, on physicists not knowing what philosophers of physics or statisticians are saying: "I think we need to actually talk to each other and there should be more formal structures to allow that to happen." That is a working scientist naming, from inside, the absence this project exists to supply. It is an aside, not an argument, and should be recorded as such — but it is a tradition-holder independently asking for the thing C2A2 builds.
