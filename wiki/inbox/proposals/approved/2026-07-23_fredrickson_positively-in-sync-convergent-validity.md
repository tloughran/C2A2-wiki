## Summary
Zhou, West, Berman, and Fredrickson test whether three methodologically distinct measures of the affective quality of a social interaction (including perceived positivity resonance) actually track the same underlying thing. In a sample of university students (N = 274) who got acquainted with an unknown peer via a 10-minute "fast friends" conversation, a confirmatory factor analysis showed all three indicators loading significantly on a single latent factor, and that latent factor score predicted prosocial tendencies and positive mental health more consistently than any single measure alone.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is a measurement-validation result: it supplies convergent-validity evidence that "affective quality of interaction" / positivity resonance is a single, robustly measurable construct rather than an artifact of one instrument. That strengthens the empirical foundation under Fredrickson's whole program and gives it a defensible latent-variable measurement model — exactly the kind of grounding the tradition lacks in most single-indicator studies already captured in the wiki.
