## Summary
A *With Reality in Mind* session recorded 2026-08-11 — the most recent Kastrup material in existence as of this search — following directly on the 2026-08-04 objective-vs-subjective idealism session (PROP-2026-08-12-002). Kastrup asks whether universal consciousness can meaningfully be said to have a **point of view**, distinguishes universal consciousness from Mind at Large, works through how perception and agency operate via the "dashboard," floats the proposal that living beings function as **"spies for God"** — letting nature see itself from a perspective it could not otherwise occupy — and closes on seeing without eyes, co-creation, and *the limits of Analytic Idealism*.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Two firsts. (1) The **universal consciousness / Mind at Large distinction** is treated as a distinction requiring clarification rather than a pair of interchangeable labels; this wiki has used them loosely and has no entry fixing the difference. (2) Kastrup is recorded discussing **the limits of his own theory** — a self-stated boundary of analytic idealism, which for a research program's track record is worth more than another confirmation. The "spies for God" proposal also gives PRS-52 (dissociation reframed from posit to functional necessity — the infinite needs finite minds to know its own manifestation) a *directional* form: not merely that manifestation is known, but that it is known from somewhere.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: A point of view requires a location and an exclusion — to see from here is not to see from there. If universal consciousness is everything, it occupies no position and excludes nothing, so it appears to have no point of view at all; yet perception is manifestly perspectival. Compounding this, "universal consciousness" and "Mind at Large" have been used near-interchangeably, obscuring whether the question is even well posed.
  Resource: An explicit distinction between universal consciousness and Mind at Large, set against Berkeley's perpetually-perceiving God as the contrast case, plus the "dashboard" metaphor for how perception and agency operate through a dissociated locus.
  Solution: Perspectivity is not a defect that universal consciousness overcomes but a structure it requires; living beings serve as **"spies for God,"** allowing nature to see itself from a perspective otherwise unavailable to it. God's point of view is not one more viewpoint alongside ours — it is constituted through ours.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Hoffman — flag strongly.** The "dashboard" is Hoffman's own term of art for the interface, used here by Kastrup without attribution in the public write-up. Whether that is convergence, borrowing, or coincidence of metaphor is exactly the conscious-realism-vs-analytic-idealism question the Kastrup agent brief calls philosophically crucial. Combined with PROP-2026-08-12-002's representation/imagination distinction, this pair of August sessions is the best evidence base the vault has yet had for settling it — **and it cannot be settled from the write-ups alone.** Recommend the two recordings be prioritized for retrieval together.
- **Levin.** "Spies for God" — the whole seeing from a perspective it could not otherwise occupy — is the idealist twin of Levin's claim that a collective acquires, through its parts, cognitive access no part possesses alone. Same structure, opposite substrate commitments.
- **Stump / Thomism.** "Can God have a point of view?" is a question Aquinas answers in the negative for a specific reason: God as pure act knows all things by knowing himself, not by occupying a station. Kastrup's affirmative-by-way-of-creatures answer is a genuine rival to that, and it is close enough to be compared rather than merely contrasted. This is a live bridge candidate.
- **C2A2 / AI consciousness.** If perspective is what finite minds contribute to universal consciousness, then the question about AI agents in the C2A2 network sharpens from "are they conscious?" to "do they constitute a *locus* — a place from which anything is seen?" That is a different question from the demarcation challenge at PRS-46, and possibly a more tractable one. Flagged as an open architecture question, not as a Kastrup claim — he is not recorded raising AI in this session.
