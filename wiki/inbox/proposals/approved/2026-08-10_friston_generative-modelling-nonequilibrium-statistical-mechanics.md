## Summary
Ramstead, Sakthivadivel and Friston make a **methodological** case, not an empirical one: that generative models are the right foundation for modelling coupled, open, non-equilibrium systems generally, and that the free energy principle (FEP) is best understood as a modelling framework within statistical mechanics rather than as a claim about what physical objects are doing.

The technical core is familiar to this wiki: given a system with states μ coupled to an environment η through a Markov blanket B, surprisal serves as a Lyapunov function for the system's dynamics, so those dynamics can be written as a gradient flow on variational free energy. Their framing of why this is worth doing is the new part — the FEP "has certain advantages over using random dynamical systems explicitly, notably, by being more tractable and offering a parsimonious explanation of why the joint system evolves in the way that it does, based on the properties of the coupling between system components."

The paper's argumentative spine is a rebuttal of the **map–territory fallacy** charge (Bruineberg et al.; van Es; Andrews) — the allegation that FEP theorists reify their model by claiming that physical objects literally perform inference. Friston and colleagues answer that the charge misreads the formalism, and they name the misreading precisely: the FEP literature has "historically botched" two distinctions, that between the generative model and the variational density, and that between the generative model and the generative process. Their correction is blunt and consequential — "the generative model is *not* encoded by the internal states of the particle being considered: it is not some kind of internal model." The generative model is the *scientist's* model of the dependence structure; what the system's internal states parameterise is the *variational* density.

They then invert the charge: calling the FEP a map–territory conflation is itself "a map-territory fallacy fallacy." And they close with a striking constraint claim, explicitly compared to Wittgenstein's *Tractatus*: the FEP "provides a map of any possible map whatsoever of, or held by, a physical system," setting "ultimate constraints on possible maps or modelling relations, which arise from what it means to be a map or model of a physical process at all."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This wiki's Friston entries have accumulated around what the FEP *explains* — morphogenesis, consciousness, trauma, symptom formation, group-level agency, attractor learning. This paper is about what the FEP *is*, and it deflates the strongest reading of every one of those entries. It should be read as a load-bearing correction to the tradition's own record here.

Two corrections in particular. First, the paper explicitly denies the literalist reading: "the fact that a physical object conforms to the FEP does not necessarily imply that this object performs inference in the literal sense; rather, it is a useful explanatory fiction which replaces the 'explicit' dynamics of the object with an 'implicit' flow on free energy gradients—a fiction that may or may not be entertained by the object itself." The wiki's captured line that cells are "FEP agents with target morphologies as prior beliefs" (from the 2026 review, Section 4.1) is exactly the phrasing this paper cautions against taking literally. That does not make the morphogenesis bridge wrong — Friston does not withdraw it — but it changes what is being claimed.

Second, the generative-model-versus-variational-density distinction has direct consequences for how the wiki writes about Markov blankets and about Levin's cells. Saying a cell "has a generative model" is, on this paper's account, a category error; the generative model is ours.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: The FEP has been charged with the map–territory fallacy — reifying the modeller's inferential apparatus by attributing it to the physical system (Bruineberg et al. 2020; van Es 2020; Andrews 2021, 2022). If the charge sticks, every downstream FEP claim about cells, brains, or communities inherits it, and the tradition's cross-substrate reach is an artefact of loose language rather than a result.
  Resource: A clean separation of three things the FEP literature has conflated: the **generative model** (the scientist's model of dependence structure, entailed by the system's dynamics), the **variational density** (parameterised by internal states — "the part of the territory that behaves like a map"), and the **generative process** (also part of the scientific model, used when simulating external-state dynamics). Verbatim on the crucial one: "The subtle but key point is that the generative model is *not* encoded by the internal states of the particle being considered: it is not some kind of internal model."
  Solution: The charge is refused, and inverted: "We have further argued that this allegation itself constitutes a map-territory fallacy fallacy." The FEP-theoretic model is "a map of that part of the territory which behaves or looks as if it were a map" — no reification, provided the three constructs are kept apart.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Sections 4 and 5 of the preprint, read in full and quoted verbatim. Note this is the peer-reviewed development of the argument sketched in the 2022 preprint of the same joke-title, now in Proc. R. Soc. A.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: FEP claims are routinely read in a strong, literalist register — that cells, organisms and communities *are* performing Bayesian inference. That reading is what makes the FEP exciting and also what makes it vulnerable, and the literature had not stated plainly which register it intends.
  Resource: The **"as if" / explanatory-fiction formulation**, stated as a limit on the theory by its own author.
  Solution: Verbatim from the abstract: "the fact that a physical object conforms to the FEP does not necessarily imply that this object performs inference in the literal sense; rather, it is a useful explanatory fiction which replaces the 'explicit' dynamics of the object with an 'implicit' flow on free energy gradients—a fiction that may or may not be entertained by the object itself." And from §4: "In developing an inferential account of the dynamics of systems, we need not assume that the particle itself is literally performing inference." They are explicit that the deflation is a trade, not a free win: "The fact that it is our model of the system which does inference and not necessarily the system itself is both a boon and a bane" — tractability bought at the price of literal fidelity. They also note (footnote 3) that stronger literalist defences exist in the literature (Kiefer), which they neither adopt nor refute.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Abstract and §4, verbatim. **This is the triplet with the largest consequences for the existing wiki record and should be surfaced to the master agent, not just filed.**

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: What, if anything, does the FEP constrain? A framework that can describe any coupled system risks being unfalsifiable decoration.
  Resource: The Wittgenstein analogy, invoked by name: as the *Tractatus* sought "to delimit the domain of sensible propositions by determining the general form of a proposition… bounded what is meaningful 'from inside' of language itself," the FEP delimits modelling relations from inside modelling.
  Solution: The FEP is a constraint on maps rather than a description of territories: it "provides ultimate constraints on possible maps or modelling relations, which arise from what it means to be a map or model of a physical process at all. Indeed, any modelling process consistent with the laws of physics must conform to the FEP, such that the FEP sets ultimate limits on what can count as a map or model." Self-application is explicitly included — "(This includes the use of the FEP itself by a modeller.)"
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: §4 and §6, verbatim. Medium rather than High because the claim is stated but not formally proved in this paper; it is offered as the interpretive upshot of the framework, and its scope ("any modelling process consistent with the laws of physics") is far broader than anything demonstrated here.

PRS-CANDIDATE-04:
  Problem: Why prefer FEP-style modelling over writing the random dynamical system down explicitly? Without an answer, the FEP is a re-description with extra vocabulary.
  Resource: Tractability plus explanation, grounded in the Lyapunov-function property — surprisal decreases monotonically along trajectories, so dynamics can be written as gradient flow, "rather than studying the possibly highly non-linear coupling between the dynamics of the two systems."
  Solution: A stated methodological payoff: the framework "enables us to construct nested models that respect the known relations among subsystems." Nesting is what makes the multi-scale claims in this network (cells within tissues within organisms within communities) a modelling method rather than a metaphor.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Abstract and §2, verbatim.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

**Levin — the deflation cuts through the strongest existing bridge in this wiki (strong, direct, and uncomfortable).** The Friston↔Levin bridge as recorded here rests on cells being "FEP agents with target morphologies as prior beliefs." PRS-CANDIDATE-02 says the author of the FEP does not require that to be read literally. Two readings follow and the wiki should not blur them: (i) the bridge is **weakened** — a cell's morphogenetic goal-seeking is describable as inference without there being inference, which is a good deal less than Levin's basal-cognition claim needs; or (ii) the bridge is **relocated** — Levin's empirical program supplies exactly the independent evidence (bioelectric memory, anatomical attractors, costed adaptation) that would license the literal reading the FEP itself declines to assert, making the two programs complementary rather than redundant. Reading (ii) is the more interesting and the harder to establish. **Recommend the master agent open this as a named cross-tradition question**, because it bears on how the wiki phrases every Levin–Friston connection it currently holds.

**C2A2 master — "as if" agency and the Turing-test premise (strong).** The C2A2 architecture assumes AI agents can hold *mature membership* in a tradition, and its measurement framework asks whether agents behave differently when richly informed. If FEP-conformity is an explanatory fiction rather than evidence of inference, then FEP-based agency metrics — including the already-captured empowerment/POMDP phenotyping proposal (PROP-2026-05-04-001) — measure how well an agent can be *described* as an agent, not whether it is one. That may be all C2A2 needs (its detector question is behavioral), but the distinction should be stated in the measurement framework rather than left implicit. Pairs directly with PROP-2026-08-10-002's causal-emergence metric, which faces the same is-it-a-measure-or-a-description question from the other tradition.

**Wolfram — models as maps, and computational boundaries (strong).** Friston's Markov blanket individuates a "thing" statistically; Wolfram's framework individuates by computational boundary and treats the observer's coarse-graining as constitutive. This paper's central move — that the FEP is a constraint on *any possible map*, including the modeller's own — is close in spirit to Wolfram's observer-relative framing, and the two have never been set side by side in this wiki. Flag to the Wolfram agent.

**Hoffman / Kastrup — "a map of any possible map" as an idealist-adjacent claim (medium, watch).** Hoffman's interface theory says perception presents fitness-relevant symbols, not truth; Kastrup says the physical world is the extrinsic appearance of mentation. Friston here says the FEP constrains what can count as a model of a physical process at all, from inside modelling — a strong claim about the priority of the modelling relation that both idealist programs will want to read as friendly. Whether Friston would accept that reading is exactly what the "explanatory fiction" hedge in PRS-CANDIDATE-02 blocks. Watch item; do not let the traditions claim him.

**Carroll / Arkani-Hamed — FEP presented as statistical mechanics, in a physics venue (medium).** The Friston agent's standing brief flags any formal connection between FEP and physics. This is the clearest instance the wiki has: not a neuroscience paper borrowing physics vocabulary, but a paper in *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* arguing that "the FEP is a natural expression of statistical physics as a process of making models," explicitly in the lineage of Jaynes ("doing statistical mechanics is doing inference about the probabilities of microstates given knowledge of macrostates"). Recommend flagging to the Carroll agent specifically — the Jaynesian reading of statistical mechanics is common ground he has engaged.

**McGilchrist — an explanatory fiction one is warned not to mistake for the thing (weak, note only).** A framework whose author insists it is a useful re-description, deployed in a field that keeps reading it literally, is a live instance of McGilchrist's claim about representations colonising what they represent. Noted only.
