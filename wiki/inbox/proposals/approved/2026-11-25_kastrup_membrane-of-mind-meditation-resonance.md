## Summary
In a 2-hour members' session, Kastrup recasts the mind not as an ocean with ripples but as a multidimensional vibrating membrane. Experiences are its modes of vibration, and the ego is one structural configuration of it. Through resonance (the pushed swing, the Tacoma Narrows Bridge), focused and repeated thought or feeling can build up until it reconfigures the structure that hosts it. On this view meditation (and, negatively, OCD and anxiety) is self-directed neuroplasticity. The companion post (https://www.withrealityinmind.com/how-meditation-can-transform-the-mind/) reproduces, with his permission, the relevant section of *Why Materialism Is Baloney*, ch. 6 ("The Oscillating Membrane Metaphor"). The session extends it with "no things, only doings": field theory, from Faraday to QED, read as excitations of one field.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The wiki has no capture of the membrane/resonance model. A grep of `traditions/kastrup/` and all Kastrup proposals finds no "membrane," "neuroplasticity," or "Tacoma." This model is Kastrup's mechanism for how a dissociated alter can change itself. It also answers the question of why the ego cannot simply will its own transformation: "Precisely because it has freewill, it freely won't."

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: If free will is evenly distributed through mind, why can't the ego simply restructure itself at will (and achieve "enlightenment" on demand)?
  Resource: The ego as a closed-loop structure inside larger boundary constraints (the body as the image of the personal unconscious, and the universe/laws of nature as a broader region of mind). Structural change too large maps onto physical death.
  Solution: The ego *is* its structure, so radical restructuring means its death and it freely declines. It can, however, choose which of its existing modes to vibrate in. That is the opening that resonance exploits.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: "the ego is the structure... changing the structure too much means dying... Precisely because it has freewill, it freely won't."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: How can thoughts and feelings change brain anatomy (self-directed neuroplasticity, e.g. Schwartz's OCD studies) without a contrived physicalist story?
  Resource: The resonance metaphor: a vibration matched to a natural mode builds cumulatively until it reconfigures its host structure. Schwartz & Begley (2004); Schwartz, Stapp & Beauregard (2005).
  Solution: Focusing mental energy on a single resonant mode (meditation) rather than dispersing it across interfering modes builds vibration until the psychic structure, and hence its image the brain, reconfigures and supports new modes. Under idealism this is natural; under materialism it is "promissory."
  Confidence: Medium (the empirical claims are drawn from contested literature and the mechanism is a metaphor)
  Evidence: "When one focuses one's thoughts and feelings this way, there is no cancelation, but a cumulative build-up of vibration... the ego will have transformed into something else."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: What is the ontology implied by field theory that physics practice often misses?
  Resource: The history of the field concept from Faraday (1830s) through quantum electrodynamics, with every particle treated as an excitation of an underlying field; Kashmir Shaivism's spanda (vibration).
  Solution: "There are no things, only doings": the dancer in the activity of dancing, and ultimately one field vibrating. Kastrup calls the field idea "the most powerful idea ever had in science and philosophy."
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Session chapter 0:53:21 "No things, only doings"; recording page summary.
