## Summary
A 33-author, minimally-edited, largely verbatim transcript of the email chain that erupted among the participants of Levin's "What Lives?" definition-of-life survey — published as a peer-reviewed paper in its own right, in the *Organisms* special issue "What is Life?" that the Levin Lab produced. Sixty-eight researchers had been asked to define life in three sentences or less; the resulting definitions were analysed by machine learning and published separately in *Biological Theory* as "What Lives? A Meta-Analysis of Diverse Opinions on the Definition of Life" (already captured in this wiki, approved 2026-07-14). This companion paper preserves what that analysis threw away: the arguing. Kauffman presses constraint closure and Kantian wholes; Newman asks whether closure is a description rather than a generative principle; Pavlic argues the definitional question is uninteresting and that the alive/not-alive threshold is observer-relative; Reber replies that an observer-relative criterion for life presupposes a living observer; Miller holds life is binary in essence and continuous only as an artefact of our instruments; Watson opens by announcing he will say which definitions are *wrong* and why, and rules out every definition that keeps a process/object or program/data separation.

The paper's own thesis is not about life. It is about **publication form**: that the exchange of ideas among scientists is at least as valuable as finished single-perspective manuscripts, that science currently has almost no mechanism for preserving such exchange in the citable record, and that this paper is offered as a reusable proof-of-concept for fixing that.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Levin's program has for several years been arguing that cognition, agency and problem-solving are substrate-independent and appear wherever a system navigates a space — and the definitional question "what counts as alive?" is the boundary condition on that whole claim, since a Levin-style diverse-intelligence framework only has room to operate if "life" is not a fenced natural kind. Publishing the *unresolved disagreement* rather than a synthesis is a substantive methodological commitment consistent with that: it declines to collapse a live conceptual space into a definition. It also lands directly on the question Levin was reasoning about in public four days later — see the Cross-Tradition Signals.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: The peer-reviewed record preserves only finished, single-perspective manuscripts. The real-time argument among experts — where clarifications, objections and challenges actually do their work — has almost no mechanism for entering the citable record, and is lost by default at an unmeasured opportunity cost.
  Resource: "Conversation as publication" — a concrete, reusable editorial format: take a branched multi-author email chain, linearize it, edit it minimally so that it stays largely verbatim, list every participant as a middle co-author, give each of them light-edit rights over their own text, and put the result through peer review with a DOI.
  Solution: Demonstrates by instance that an email symposium among 33 leading thinkers can be made a citable, peer-reviewed artefact, and offers itself explicitly as a template for other projects rather than as a one-off.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Abstract, read verbatim: "In order to ensure that these ideas are not lost to the peer-reviewed scientific record, we here provide a minimally-edited (largely verbatim) transcript of the email chain among leading thinkers, containing numerous clarifications, disagreements, and challenges that enrich the topic of Life. It is our hope that this case study serves as an example for future papers, since the exchange of ideas among scientists is at least as interesting and valuable as formal scientific manuscripts written from a single perspective." Introduction, verbatim: "There is currently a paucity of mechanisms by which such conversations can be preserved in the scientific record. This carries considerable opportunity cost…" Methods, verbatim: "Michael Levin and Karina Kofman compiled the text and linearized the numerous branches of replies as much as possible. All participants are listed as middle co-authors, and had a chance to make light edits to their text as desired." Levin is corresponding author (michael.levin@allencenter.tufts.edu), so this first-person-plural claim is signed by him.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Whether "life" is a binary natural kind with a real boundary, or a continuum whose cut-point is supplied by whoever is doing the classifying — and whether an observer-relative criterion is even coherent when the observer must itself be alive.
  Resource: Pavlic's split between an **objective metric** and an **observer-relative threshold** on that metric, defended by a dimensionality-reduction argument: projecting a high-dimensional space onto a much lower-dimensional manifold necessarily leaves residual error, and that residual is the definitional dispute.
  Solution: Reframes a century of definitional deadlock as a projection artefact rather than a factual disagreement — with the deflationary corollary that the definitional question is not worth answering, only the sub-processes are. Reber's objection (that an observer-relative life-criterion presupposes a living observer, and so is circular) is preserved rather than resolved, and Miller's counter-position (life is binary in essence, continuous only relative to our current instruments — endospores with no measurable metabolism for millennia as the test case) is set alongside it unadjudicated.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: All three turns read verbatim. Pavlic: "Metric is objective, but the threshold above which one infers that the metric is 'life' is observer relative" and "If you project a complex, multidimensional space onto a manifold with far lower dimensions, you are bound to have some residual error. In fact, that's the whole problem with defining 'life' (and why it's somewhat of an uninteresting question…)". Reber: "If the determination of whether something is alive is conditional on being 'observer relative' then the observer in this equation has to be alive to make said determination… I think I see a problem here." Miller: "Life is binary (i.e. an organism is alive or not), if we had the correct tools to make that measurement with deep precision, which we do not." **Attribution caveat: none of this is Levin's voice** — he is compiler and corresponding author, not a discussant.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Whether multiscale competency — order and problem-solving at every spatial level of a living system, each level solving problems in its own domain — is a *definitional* feature of life or a downstream consequence of it, and whether it can be reproduced in non-living chemistry.
  Resource: Gentili's proposed "Multiscale Chemistry": chemical systems that manifest self-organization at different spatial scales and multi-level causality when appropriate macroscopic thermodynamic forces are applied — offered as a deliberate imitation target for Levin's multiscale-competency claim, developed alongside Chemical Artificial Intelligence (CAI), in which the hardware/software distinction of conventional AI is merged into the "wetware" of reactive chemical solutions.
  Solution: Proposes a wet-chemistry route to testing whether multiscale competency is achievable without life — which would, if it succeeded, decide the definitional question empirically rather than lexically.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Gentili's turn, read verbatim: "Mike Levin is advocating that a remarkable feature of multicellular living beings is their multiscale competencies: there is order in biology across different spatial levels, and each organized structural level solves problems in its own relevant domain (McMillen & Levin 2024). The imitation of the mesmerizing multiscale competencies of living beings can be approached by boosting the development of a new chemistry, named 'Multiscale Chemistry' (Gentili 2025)…" **Confidence is Medium, not High, precisely because this is Gentili's restatement of Levin's position, not Levin stating it** — the wiki should not score a Levin triplet off a third party's paraphrase without checking it against McMillen & Levin 2024 directly. The link is also institutional rather than merely topical: Gentili notes his CAI paper was written for the Computation And Biology special issue "edited by Michael Levin and Hananel Hazan."

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Levin ↔ the C2A2 project's own method (highest-value signal, and it is reflexive).** This paper is an instance of exactly what this wiki does: preserve a multi-thinker disagreement without averaging it into a consensus position. - **Levin ↔ Levin, internal (check before ingesting either).** PROP-2026-08-08-002 (pending) has Levin asking on the record, on 2026-07-11, whether scholarly exposition is still worth writing and for whom — given that the people who could act on it are too busy to read it and "may ask AI to summarize it for them." **This paper published three days earlier, on 2026-07-08, and answers a version of that question in the affirmative for a different genre**: not the finished monograph but the argument that produced it. - **Levin × Hoffman × Wolfram × Carroll (observer-relativity).** Pavlic's "the threshold above which one infers that the metric is 'life' is observer relative," and Reber's circularity objection to it, sit directly on the existing FLAG-013 / FINDING-042 observer-relativity cluster. Reber's objection is the sharpest form of a problem that cluster has not yet had put to it: if the observer is constitutive of the classification, what licenses the observer's own standing? Recommend the master agent test FLAG-013 against it.

- **Levin × Friston (constraint closure vs. self-evidencing).** Kauffman's Kantian-whole / catalytic-and-constraint-closure criterion — boundary conditions that constrain the release of energy into the thermodynamic work that constructs those very same boundary conditions — is a thermodynamic statement of the same circularity that the free-energy principle states variationally as self-evidencing under a Markov blanket. Newman's objection to Kauffman ("a description of the arrangement of processes in a cell, but not itself a generative principle? Sort of like sedimentation without the concept of gravity") is the **same objection** that has been pressed at the FEP: that it describes what persisting systems must look like without saying why any system comes to persist. Recommend logging as a genuine convergence of critique across two traditions, arrived at independently.

- **Levin × Friston × Watson (process/object dualism).** Watson's blanket claim — that any definition of life resting on a process acting on an object, or a program acting on data, is wrong, *including* definitions that name both but keep them separate — is a direct attack on the generative-model/generative-process split that active inference is built on. The transcript separately cites "Levin, Millidge, Tschantz & Watson 2024," so these authors are already co-working across that boundary. This is the sharpest live disagreement in the paper's reach and should not be smoothed over.
