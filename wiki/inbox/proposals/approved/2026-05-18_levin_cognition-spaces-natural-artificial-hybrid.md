## Summary
Solé, Seoane, Pla-Mauri, Bennett, Hochberg, and Levin propose a "cognition space" framework that replaces substrate-bound definitions of cognition with a comparative representation across organizational and informational dimensions. They identify three currently realized cognition spaces — basal aneural (single cells, simple multicellular organisms, xenobots, organoids), neural (animal brains), and human–AI hybrid — and show that their occupation is highly uneven, with clusters of realized systems separated by large unoccupied "voids." They argue these voids reflect evolutionary contingencies, physical constraints, and design limitations rather than impossibility, and that the hybrid space is the most promising frontier for novel forms of cognition.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Levin's most explicit attempt yet to provide a unified geometric framework for comparing cognitive systems across substrates. It generalizes the "Mind Everywhere" / TAME / diverse-intelligence apparatus into a structured space with measurable axes, gives an empirical question (what occupies the voids?) that the xenobot/anthrobot program is uniquely positioned to answer, and explicitly elevates hybrid human–AI cognition as a research target — which is the precise terrain on which the C2A2 tradition-accelerator operates.
