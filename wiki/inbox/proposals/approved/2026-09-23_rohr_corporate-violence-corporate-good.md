## Summary

CAC Daily Meditation for Wednesday 23 September 2026, in the week-theme *Nonviolence: A Christian Calling*. Authored by Rohr throughout (unlike the 09-21 Katongole and 09-22 Zaru meditations, which were skipped as guest-authored). Rohr argues that systemic ("corporate") evil can only be met by corporate good, that the "corporate ego" is harder to face than the individual ego because it is better disguised and defended, and that retributive atonement theory has legitimated "good and necessary violence" in Christian culture.

**Provenance caveat.** Both passages are *adapted* from earlier books: *Healing Our Violence through the Journey of Centering Prayer* (with Thomas Keating, 2002) and *The Universal Christ* (2021 ed., pp. 140–142). The content is Rohr's own but not new thinking; the novelty for the wiki is the pairing of the two claims under the nonviolence theme.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition

It extends the "power-with, not power-over" line already pending (PROP-2026-09-19, *Power With, Not Power Over*) from the individual to the institutional scale. It also names a specific mechanism — psychic numbing as paralysis from not knowing what to do, rather than bad will — and ties Rohr's non-retributive theology of atonement directly to social ethics.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Individuals who oppose injustice still feel helpless against large systemic evils; the result looks like apathy.
  Resource: The distinction between the individual ego and the "corporate ego" (collective self-protection that is more disguised, protected, and denied), plus a diagnosis of psychic numbing as paralysis, not bad will.
  Solution: Systemic evil is met only by collective ("corporate") good — the gospel lived institutionally and socially, not just individually. Contemplative practice that does not issue in collective servanthood has failed its own test.
  Confidence: Medium (stated directly; adapted from a 2002 text)
  Evidence: "corporate evil can only be opposed by corporate good"; the German bishops' appeasement of Hitler used as the case of an institution protecting itself instead of living the gospel.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Why does Christian culture tolerate, and sometimes sanctify, violence?
  Resource: Critique of retributive (penal) atonement theory; Walter Wink's "myth of redemptive violence" (named as what "some call" it); Pauline "powers and principalities" (Eph 3:9–10, 6:12).
  Solution: A retributive notion of God's justice legitimates "good and necessary violence all the way down." Dropping it removes the theological cover for violence and restores a redemptive alternative to history.
  Confidence: Medium (restatement of an established Rohr position from *The Universal Christ*)
  Evidence: Second adapted passage, *The Universal Christ* pp. 140–142.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

- **Fredrickson:** collective-scale positivity resonance vs. Rohr's "corporate good" — both locate the remedy above the individual. Fredrickson's multilevel loneliness work (ingested 2026-04-08) supplies a measurable version of the collective level Rohr asserts.
- **Levin / Friston:** "corporate ego" as a higher-scale self with its own self-protective boundary maps loosely onto multiscale agency and Markov-blanket nesting. Speculative; flag only.
- **Wright:** direct overlap on atonement and "powers and principalities" — Wright's *The Day the Revolution Began* also rejects a narrowly retributive reading, but keeps more of the substitutionary frame. A likely point of productive disagreement.
- **Stump:** Stump's Thomistic account of atonement is non-retributive in a different way (union, not payment); a three-way comparison with Rohr and Wright is available.
