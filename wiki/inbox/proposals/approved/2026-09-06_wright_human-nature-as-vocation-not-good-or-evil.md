## Summary
Weekly *Ask NT Wright Anything* episode, 36 min. Three listener questions. On whether humans are basically good or sinful, Wright refuses the binary: the primary biblical identity of humans is neither "good" nor "bad" but *created for God's vocation*, with goodness and sin both read against that prior calling. On the "beloved disciple" of John's Gospel he surveys the evidence and asks whether the identity can be known at all. On the Sermon on the Mount he denies it is legalism or salvation-by-works: it is a description of what human life looks like when God's kingdom breaks into the world.

**Evidence quality caveat:** summary built from the published episode description; audio not heard.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The vocation-first answer to the good/evil question is the anthropological premise behind Wright's whole new-creation program, and the wright wiki does not yet state it. It also matters for the Summa synthesis: Aquinas's treatment of original justice, sin, and the natural desire for God (Prima Secundae Q.81-85, Q.109) is usually paired with a nature-is-corrupted-versus-nature-is-good framing; Wright supplies a scriptural third option, nature-as-calling, that the syntheses can cite by tradition path instead of falling back on canonical works. The Sermon-on-the-Mount reading gives the same move for the New Law (I-II Q.106-108): description of kingdom-life rather than a code.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-A:
  Label: human-nature-as-vocation
  Problem: The good/evil question about human nature presupposes that the primary predicate of a human is a moral valence; every answer inherits that presupposition.
  Resource: Wright's claim that the primary biblical identity of humans is vocational: people are created for God's calling, and goodness and sin are secondary descriptions of how that calling is met or refused.
  Solution: Replace the valence question with a teleological one. The relevant fact about an agent is what it is *for*; moral valence is derivative on the orientation toward or away from that end.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Episode description: "our primary identity is not simply 'good' or 'bad' but people created for God's vocation."

PRS-CANDIDATE-B:
  Label: sermon-on-the-mount-as-kingdom-description
  Problem: Read as commands, the Sermon on the Mount looks like a stricter law and invites the legalism charge; read as ideals, it looks unlivable.
  Resource: Wright's reading of the Sermon as a description of life under an inbreaking kingdom, not a set of conditions for entering it.
  Solution: The Sermon is indicative before it is imperative: it describes the shape of a life already caught up in new creation, which removes both the legalism reading and the impossible-ideal reading.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Episode description's closing segment on the Sermon.
