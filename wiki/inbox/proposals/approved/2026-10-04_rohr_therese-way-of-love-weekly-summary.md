## Summary
The weekly summary (James Finley, recapping Rohr's Sept 27–Oct 2 series) presents Thérèse's "little way" as an antidote to perfectionist theology: spiritual maturity is small acts done in conscious love, childlikeness is maturity rather than immaturity, and "we come to God not by doing it right, but by doing it wrong." Dorothy Day is invoked to contrast Thérèse's love with "preparedness for war and the show of force." The practice, "Our Holy Faces," invites seeing the "unborn divinity" in each person's face.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This closes the Thérèse series already represented by pending PROP 2026-09-27 (Thérèse of Lisieux) and links it to the approved true-self/false-self and descending-religion material. It supplies a developmental claim not yet explicit in the wiki: transformation proceeds through accepted limitation (finite perspective) rather than overcoming it. That is directly citable for the Summa 2026 contemplative-life loci (II-II Q.171–189).
