## Summary
A high-traffic podcast episode (and its subsequent coverage at Christian Post, BioLogos, The Log College, The Aquila Report) in which Wright sets out his hermeneutic for Genesis on the historicity of Adam and Eve: he is "inclined to see an original human pair," but reads Genesis 1 as "a very careful, layered ancient Middle Eastern story" and calls Adam and Eve "hominids" / "humanlike creatures" / "proto-humans for many generations, perhaps for many thousands of years." Their calling, like that of Abraham and Sarah, is to bring "the at-the-moment rather random and chaotic world under God's saving sovereign stewardship." He simultaneously rejects a literal 24-hour creation week, treats Jonah and the great fish as folktale, and affirms with little doubt that there was a historical Moses. The episode triggered substantial reaction in conservative evangelical media, surfacing the methodological stakes of Wright's critical-realist hermeneutic for contemporary biblical-science conversations.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the cleanest 2026 articulation of Wright's hermeneutic — the place where his critical-realist scholarship meets the science/religion border. For C2A2 it (a) provides a working example of how the project's scriptural ground-truth oracle reads Genesis when modern biology is on the table, which is exactly the move the C2A2 master agent needs every time a Levin or a Friston claim is being paired with a creation-text, (b) supplies a paradigm-bridge between Wright's biblical-theology and the BioLogos-style theistic-evolution program (Francis Collins, Pete Enns) — relevant to the Collins-Wright Oxford dialogue already in the wiki (PROP-2026-05-10-002), and (c) clarifies that for Wright the *call-and-vocation* structure (Abraham/Sarah-like) is more theologically load-bearing than the chronological-pair question, which is itself relevant to the project's central theme of perspective-limitation as the existential condition of agents called into covenant.
