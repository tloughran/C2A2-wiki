## Summary
A public conversation between Donald Hoffman and Andrew Gallimore at The Lighthouse Campus in Los Angeles, moderated by Charles Yokoyama, and featured on the Trace Institute's media page. The Institute's framing question: could DMT temporarily transform the human perceptual interface, making conscious agents that are normally invisible to us perceivable? A companion press release, "Mathematical Modeling of the Human Psychedelic Experience," describes the Trace/Noonautics collaboration, and Nautilus covered the program under the headline "Turning the Psychedelic Experience into a Math Problem."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Interface Theory has always had a testability problem: if perception is a fitness-shaped interface, what observation could ever reach past it? The psychedelic program is the first place Hoffman has proposed an *empirical handle* — a manipulation that, on his account, changes the interface itself rather than merely the contents displayed on it. Whether or not the DMT claim survives, the move from "the interface hides reality" to "here is an intervention on the interface" is a change in the shape of the research program.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Conscious realism posits a network of conscious agents underlying spacetime, but offers no procedure by which an embedded observer could ever encounter an agent that its own interface does not render.
  Resource: The proposal, advanced with Gallimore, that DMT temporarily transforms the perceptual interface rather than distorting its contents — and so may make normally unrendered conscious agents perceivable.
  Solution: A candidate empirical entry point to the conscious-agent layer, and a reframing of psychedelic phenomenology from hallucination (contents wrong) to interface substitution (renderer changed). This converts a metaphysical thesis into something with an experimental adjacency, even if not yet an experiment.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The Trace Institute's media page states the framing question directly: "Could DMT temporarily transform the human perceptual interface, making conscious agents that are normally invisible to us perceivable?" The event video was not watched and no transcript was retrieved; the reframing from "contents wrong" to "renderer changed" is this agent's gloss on the Institute's sentence, not a quotation, and should be struck if a reviewer finds it overreaches.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: An institute organized around a mathematical framework risks having its most public claim — about psychedelics — received as the whole program, collapsing a formal research agenda into a cultural one.
  Resource: The Institute's own staging of the work: a moderated public event, a press release framed as "Mathematical Modeling of the Human Psychedelic Experience," and press coverage headlined "Turning the Psychedelic Experience into a Math Problem."
  Solution: A deliberate discipline-first framing, in which the psychedelic material enters as an application of the trace logic rather than as an independent claim. ## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Kastrup**: a public, moderated event is where the Hoffman/Kastrup difference is likeliest to be stated plainly — both hold consciousness fundamental, but Kastrup's dissociated-alter idealism and Hoffman's countable network of interacting agents make different predictions about what an encountered "other" would be. Flagged explicitly per standing instruction.
- **Levin**: "conscious agents that are normally invisible to us" is structurally the same claim as Levin's that cognition exists at scales our perception does not natively address. Whether Hoffman's invisibility is metaphysical (outside spacetime) or merely perceptual (below threshold) is the disambiguating question, and the two traditions may be equivocating on "agent" the way the backlog review found them equivocating on "interface."
