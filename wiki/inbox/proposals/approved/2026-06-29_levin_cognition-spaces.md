## Summary
Solé, Seoane, Pla-Mauri, Bennett, Hochberg & Levin propose a "cognition space" framework that replaces substrate-dependent definitions of cognition with a comparative representation built on organizational and informational dimensions. Cognition is treated as a graded capacity to sense, process, and act on information, letting cells, brains, artificial agents, and human–AI collectives be located in one common landscape. They examine three cognition spaces — basal aneural, neural, and human–AI hybrid — and show their occupation is highly uneven, with realized systems clustered amid large unoccupied "void" regions attributed to evolutionary contingency, physical constraint, and design limitation.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This complements Levin's morphospace work by extending the same logic to a *cognition* morphospace: a map of possible minds. It operationalizes the diverse-intelligence thesis as an explicit, comparative, partly empty design space — turning "mind everywhere" into a coordinate system where unoccupied regions become predictions about constructible novel cognitive systems.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: There is no unified framework for comparing the forms, limits, and unrealized possibilities of cognition across natural, artificial, and hybrid systems.
  Resource: A "cognition space" — a comparative coordinate system defined by organizational and informational dimensions, with cognition as a graded sense-process-act capacity.
  Solution: Three mapped cognition spaces (basal aneural / neural / human–AI hybrid) whose uneven occupation locates both realized systems and the unoccupied regions that physical and design constraints leave open.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: The paper introduces the three named cognition spaces and argues that the voids between occupied clusters "are not accidental but reflect evolutionary contingencies, physical constraints, and design limitations."
