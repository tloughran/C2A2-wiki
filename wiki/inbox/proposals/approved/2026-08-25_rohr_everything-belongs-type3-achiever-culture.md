## Summary
Episode of the CAC podcast *Everything Belongs: Living the Teachings of Richard Rohr Forward* (95 minutes) in the current Enneagram season, the same series as the already-ingested Type 1 episode. Fr. Richard Rohr unpacks Enneagram Type Three, "the achiever," and makes a diagnostic claim about the surrounding society rather than about individuals: we live, in his phrase, in "a Type Three culture" — one organised around image, achievement and approval. The developmental mechanism he gives is that Threes learn as children that love is earned by performance rather than given, which produces over-identification with the presentable self at the expense of authenticity. Actor and director Josh Radnor joins as guest with a first-person account of professional success and of reaching the top of a ladder "leaning against the wrong wall."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Rohr applying the Enneagram as a diagnostic of a *collective*, not a personality inventory — the claim that a whole culture can be typed is a much stronger and more contestable claim than the individual typology, and it is the version that bears on C2A2's interest in social formation. It also supplies the mechanism (love conditioned on performance → identification with the presentable self) that connects his Enneagram teaching to his true self / false self material, which the rohr/ wiki currently treats as separate strands.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: The Enneagram is normally used to describe individuals. Can it describe a society, and if so what does the diagnosis assert?
  Resource: Rohr's "Type Three culture" — a society whose default organising principle is image, achievement and approval, so that the compensations of one type become the ambient norm rather than a deviation from it.
  Solution: Reframes what is usually treated as personal pathology as environmental fit: in a Type Three culture the Three's strategy is rewarded and therefore invisible as a strategy. This predicts that the characteristic cost (loss of contact with the non-presentable self) will be widely distributed and under-reported, which is a claim about prevalence that could be checked.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Fredrickson**: "love is earned through performance rather than given freely" is a claim about the conditions under which co-experienced positive affect can occur at all. Fredrickson's positivity-resonance construct requires mutual care and cross-person synchrony; a performance-conditioned relationship should systematically fail the mutual-care condition. That is a specifiable, measurable disagreement with the folk view that achievement environments are high-connection environments.
- **McGilchrist**: a culture "obsessed with image, achievement, and approval" is close to McGilchrist's characterisation of left-hemisphere dominance — the manipulable, re-presented world taking priority over the presented one. Rohr's "presentable self" and McGilchrist's "re-presentation" are doing similar work and the overlap is worth testing rather than assuming.
- **Loughran / C2A2**: the collective-typing move is directly relevant to the community-formation strand — if a culture can carry a characteristic compensation, then community design has to account for which strategy the environment rewards, not only which individuals are present.
