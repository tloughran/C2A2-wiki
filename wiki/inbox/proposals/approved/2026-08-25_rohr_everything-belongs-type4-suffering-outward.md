## Summary
Episode of the CAC podcast *Everything Belongs* (95 minutes), continuing the Enneagram season. Fr. Richard Rohr's contribution concerns Enneagram Type Four: he names the Four's gift as the capacity to see beyond conformity, and issues a directional instruction — that Fours should turn their suffering *outward*, in solidarity with others. Guest Dorcas Cheng-Tozun, drawing on her book *Social Justice for the Sensitive Soul*, discusses belonging, burnout and emotional wounds among Fours and highly sensitive people, and argues for sustainable rather than self-consuming forms of justice work.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Rohr's "turn the suffering outward" is a compact statement of the action-and-contemplation thesis that names the CAC itself, applied to a specific failure mode: sensitivity that terminates in the self. It is also his most explicit recent statement of a *use* for suffering that is neither redemptive-by-itself nor merely to be endured — which is the exact question the stump/ tradition treats analytically, and gives the wiki a Rohr-side term for that comparison. The burnout material adds the sustainability constraint that the rohr/ wiki's justice strand currently lacks.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Heightened sensitivity to what is wrong is the precondition of prophetic perception, but it is also the precondition of burnout and self-absorption. What distinguishes the two outcomes?
  Resource: Rohr's directional instruction to Type Fours: turn suffering outward in solidarity, rather than inward into identity.
  Solution: Makes the *direction of attention*, not the intensity of feeling, the discriminating variable. On this account the same sensitivity yields solidarity or paralysis depending on whether the suffering is treated as material for identity or as a point of contact with others' suffering. This is a mechanism claim and it predicts that interventions targeting direction should outperform interventions targeting affect regulation.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Fredrickson**: "turn suffering outward in solidarity" is a prediction about affect dynamics — that other-directed attention under distress produces a different trajectory than self-directed attention. Fredrickson's broaden-and-build and her work on shared affect under adversity give the measurement apparatus; the burnout material is a boundary condition her intervention studies do not currently model well.
- **Stump**: the question of whether suffering can have a use without being justified is the central problem of *Wandering in Darkness*. Rohr's answer here is functional (suffering becomes a route to solidarity) rather than theodicy-shaped, which makes it a genuinely different kind of answer to the same question rather than a restatement.
- **McGilchrist**: "seeing beyond conformity" as the Four's gift is a claim about perceptual independence from consensus — adjacent to McGilchrist's account of attention that is not pre-shaped by the already-known.
