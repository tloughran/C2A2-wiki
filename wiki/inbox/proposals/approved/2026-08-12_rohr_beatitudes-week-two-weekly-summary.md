## Summary
This is the closing page of CAC Week 28, the second of two Beatitudes weeks. The wiki already holds Week One (PROP-2026-07-12-002); Week Two has not been captured. Its central Rohr claim is a genre reclassification: the Beatitudes are "paradoxical 'commandments'" that "don't tell Christians what to do; they tell them what they will be like if they are living in the kingdom." That is, the Beatitudes are read as *descriptive diagnostics of a state* rather than as prescriptive imperatives — a claim that changes what kind of evidence could confirm or disconfirm them. Rohr's second contribution reframes persecution: "To live joyfully in the midst of misunderstanding and persecution points beyond our smaller 'kingdoms' to the larger kingdom of God" — joy under adversarial conditions functions as a signal that one's frame of reference has enlarged. The week's other voices sharpen this: Rich Villodas argues peacemaking is not conflict-avoidance and "sometimes peacemaking *creates*" conflict; Nadia Bolz-Weber reads the Beatitudes as Jesus blessing precisely those who had concluded "blessings would never be in the cards" for them; Carl McColman reframes purity of heart as willingness to do repair work rather than an achieved state; Clarence Jordan glosses mercy as the disposition to "share gladly all that they have." The Week Twenty-Eight Practice supplies an operational instrument: Jim Forest's eleven-question examination-of-conscience derived from the Orthodox use of the Beatitudes in preparing for confession, plus his objection that "spirituality" has come to suggest being "lifted above the world" — whereas "Christian spirituality has nothing to do with losing contact with those who suffer."

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The Rohr wiki records the descent/second-half-of-life mechanism and the nondual frame, but it is thin on how the tradition proposes to *measure* whether a person is in fact living in the kingdom. This week supplies both halves of that: a theoretical claim that the Beatitudes are state descriptions rather than commands, and a concrete instrument (Forest's eleven questions) that operationalizes the state descriptions as self-report items. That combination — a diagnostic theory plus a self-administered instrument — is directly usable by the C2A2 measurement work, which needs exactly this shape of artifact from the contemplative traditions.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: The Beatitudes read as commands are either trivially unachievable or morally perverse (command someone to mourn?). How should they be classified?
  Resource: Genre reclassification — the Beatitudes as paradoxical "commandments" that are in fact descriptions of the condition of someone already living in the kingdom.
  Solution: Converts an unfulfillable imperative set into a diagnostic set. If the Beatitudes describe rather than prescribe, then possessing the traits is evidence of the state, and failure to possess them is diagnostic information rather than moral failure. This is what makes an instrument like Forest's possible at all.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Verbatim from the retrieved weekly summary (Sunday entry, attributed to Richard Rohr): "The Beatitudes are paradoxical 'commandments.' They don't tell Christians what to do; they tell them what they will be like if they are living in the kingdom."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: How can an inner state that is not directly observable be given an external behavioral marker?
  Resource: Joy-under-persecution as the marker — sustained joy in conditions that would normally extinguish it.
  Solution: Proposes a falsifiable-in-principle behavioral signature: the enlargement of one's frame beyond "our smaller 'kingdoms'" shows up as affect that does not track local adversity. Adversity becomes the discriminating condition, because under favorable conditions joy is uninformative.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Retrieved summary, Thursday entry (Rohr): "To live joyfully in the midst of misunderstanding and persecution points beyond our smaller 'kingdoms' to the larger kingdom of God." Medium rather than High because the summary gives the claim without the supporting argument from the underlying daily entry, which was not separately retrieved.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Contemplative practice is chronically vulnerable to being co-opted as escape from the world rather than deeper engagement with it. How is that failure mode detected?
  Resource: Jim Forest's eleven-item examination of conscience (*The Ladder of the Beatitudes*, Orbis, 1999, 142), plus his diagnosis that the bookshop category "spirituality" now "often suggests that by adopting the right method of prayer or meditation you will be lifted above the world."
  Solution: An operational instrument that forces each Beatitude into a concrete relational question ("What enemies do I love? For which enemies am I praying?", "Whose threatened life am I trying to safeguard?"). Every item names a specific other person, which structurally blocks the escapist reading Forest is objecting to.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Full instrument and both Forest quotations retrieved verbatim from the article's Week Twenty-Eight Practice section, with page citations to Forest, *Ladder*, 69 and 142.

PRS-CANDIDATE-04:
  Problem: Does peacemaking mean reducing observable conflict?
  Resource: Villodas's distinction between peacemaking and peacekeeping — "Peacemakers don't avoid conflict; in fact, sometimes peacemaking *creates* it. We see this with Jesus."
  Solution: Decouples the target variable (a just settlement) from the proxy that is usually measured (absence of visible friction), and predicts that a genuine peacemaking intervention will often *raise* short-run conflict. This is a directional prediction, and therefore checkable.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Retrieved summary, Wednesday entry, attributed to Rich Villodas — a guest teacher, not Rohr, so it is tradition-adjacent rather than Rohr's own voice; flagged for the ingesting reviewer.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Fredrickson**: PRS-CANDIDATE-02 is a claim about positive affect persisting under aversive conditions, which is squarely in Fredrickson's territory. Her broaden-and-build account predicts that positive emotion widens attentional scope; Rohr's claim is that a widened frame ("the larger kingdom") is what *produces* the joy. The causal arrow runs opposite ways, which makes this a genuine and testable disagreement rather than a restatement.
- **Stump**: Forest's insistence that Christian spirituality "has nothing to do with losing contact with those who suffer" is close to Stump's second-person account of the presence required in the face of another's suffering, and both resist the move to treat suffering as a problem to be theorized from a distance.
- **Rohr internal / Job week**: this sits adjacent to the Week 31 Job material (PROP-2026-08-07-003) and the Bowler "joy anyway" practice (PROP-2026-08-09-001). All three make versions of the claim that joy and affliction co-occur rather than succeed one another; the wiki should check whether they are one claim or three.
- **Loughran / C2A2 measurement**: Forest's eleven items are a ready-made self-report scaffold with the same structure the GPRS community-measurement work needs — each item names a concrete relation rather than an abstract disposition.
