## Summary
A With Reality in Mind session, written up by host Amir Giles on 2026-10-26 with direct Kastrup quotations. It was recorded on the day someone close to Kastrup died. Kastrup gives the analytic-idealist account of death (consciousness "cannot go anywhere because it's that within which things come and go"). He then goes further: under analytic idealism, reality and imagination are not different ontological categories, and a personal "fantasy" of death (a welcoming committee of loved ones) should be judged pragmatically, by how well it disposes you toward the transition. The members' recording is at https://www.withrealityinmind.com/death-imagination-reality/ (not fetched, so the claims here come from the host's write-up only).

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Two new moves for the program. First, it states outright that collapsing the reality/imagination distinction is a *logical consequence* of analytic idealism, not an optional mystical add-on. Second, it gives a criterion for judging a belief that is not a truth criterion: a death-fantasy is measured "by how warm it makes you feel," on analogy with science's convenient fictions measured by prediction. That is a change of register from his usual technical argument, and it puts the program's existential claims in testable (or at least assessable) form.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: If everything is mind, what grounds the ordinary distinction between what is real and what is imagined?
  Resource: The dissociation model. Inside the dissociative boundary we identify with the alter (the "avatar") and the world seems objective and independent. Beyond it, "it's mind manifesting itself to itself."
  Solution: Reality vs. imagination is a distinction of point of view (degree of identification), not of ontological category. Kastrup claims this follows necessarily from analytic idealism.
  Confidence: High (direct quotation)
  Evidence: "If you really take analytic idealism to heart you must conclude (because it's an inevitable logical consequence,) that reality and imagination are not different ontological categories."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: How should someone hold beliefs about what follows death, when no cognitive structure we now have is guaranteed to carry over?
  Resource: An analogy with science's "convenient fictions", which are judged by how well they predict, not by being literally true.
  Solution: Hold a consciously chosen "fantasy" (the welcoming committee) and judge it pragmatically: does it put you in a mental state that welcomes the coming state of mind? He says he means it "less metaphorically than you might think," because on the previous triplet imagination is not unreal.
  Confidence: Medium (paraphrased by the host, with partial quotation)
  Evidence: "Bernardo expects the transition to be something like being greeted by a welcoming committee… He is careful to emphasise that this is a conscious fantasy." Plus: "Today, my welcoming committee just grew by one face."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: What actually ends at death on an idealist account, and is anything lost?
  Resource: The claim that memory is not stored in the brain but belongs to mind, together with the Ram Dass image of birth as putting on a tight shoe and death as taking it off.
  Solution: What dies is the *fantasy* that the outer world is literally real rather than symbolic. Memories persist because they "were never in the brain."
  Confidence: Medium (host's paraphrase. The memory claim is a strong empirical-adjacent claim, and the write-up gives no argument for it.)
  Evidence: "In death, nothing is lost. All the memories remain - they were never in the brain. They are part of mind."

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **McGilchrist (close collaborator):** "Meaning is the oxygen of life… for idealism, meaning is so vital that it requires multiple points of view," and "the worst insult to mind is to say 'it was all just a dream'." Both line up with McGilchrist's right-hemisphere attention to the living, meaningful whole, as against left-hemisphere literalism (taking the world "out there" as literally real). Flag as a convergence.
- **Hoffman:** the avatar/alter language and "the world out there seems real, objective and independent" while identified echo Hoffman's interface theory (perception as a user interface over a deeper conscious reality). Worth checking whether Kastrup's point-of-view account of reality maps onto Hoffman's interface or departs from it.
- **Stump / Wright / Rohr:** a "welcoming committee of loved ones" and death as homecoming invite comparison with Christian communion-of-saints and resurrection accounts. The contrast matters: Wright insists on bodily resurrection, and Kastrup holds that present cognitive structure will not carry over.
- **Fredrickson:** judging a belief by the affective state it produces (warmth, welcome) is close to broaden-and-build's account of positive emotion as functional.
- **C2A2 / AI:** no direct AI-consciousness content in this session.
