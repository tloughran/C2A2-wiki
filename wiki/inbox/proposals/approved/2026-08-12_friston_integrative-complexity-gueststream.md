## Summary
A recorded Active Inference Institute livestream in which Karl Friston joins Alexander Hemming, Dylan Grove and Jacob Hohwy to discuss integrative complexity in the framework of active inference. *Integrative complexity* is an established construct outside the free-energy literature — originating in political and social psychology, it scores a piece of reasoning on two dimensions: **differentiation** (how many distinct dimensions or perspectives on an issue the reasoner recognizes) and **integration** (whether those dimensions are then connected into a higher-order structure rather than left as a list). Jacob Hohwy is a philosopher of mind long associated with predictive-processing theory and already present in this wiki via the approved 2026-04-08 "minimal theory of consciousness under active inference" proposal, which suggests this conversation continues a standing collaboration rather than beginning one.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
If it delivers what the title promises, this would extend the free-energy principle into a construct it has not previously formalized: not perception, action or planning, but the *quality of a reasoner's model of a contested question* — how many dimensions it carries and whether they cohere. Formalizing differentiation and integration as properties of a generative model would give active inference a normative handle on good and bad reasoning, which is a different target from its usual explananda.

## Cross-Tradition Signals

**Flagged as prospective only — no signal below can be scored until the recording is watched.**

- **Friston × McGilchrist (the strongest prospective link, and it is a real disagreement).** Differentiation-then-integration is very close to McGilchrist's account of what the two hemispheres respectively contribute to understanding — the right attending to the whole and to what resists articulation, the left dividing and re-presenting. If active inference formalizes integrative complexity as a scalar property of a generative model, that is precisely the move McGilchrist argues is the characteristic error: treating an integrative capacity as a quantity the re-presenting faculty can measure. Whether the two frameworks agree or collide here is a genuinely open question and worth deciding on evidence rather than assuming convergence.

- **Friston × Levin.** Multiscale competency in the Levin program is differentiation and integration realized *architecturally* — distinct problem-solving levels that nonetheless cohere into one agent. An active-inference formalization of integrative complexity might supply a common measure for what the two programs are separately calling scale-integration.

- **Friston × Loughran / the C2A2 measurement program.** If this yields an operational measure of differentiation-and-integration in reasoning, it is directly relevant to the wiki's own Level-2 and dialogue-quality measurement work, which needs exactly such a construct and currently has none. Worth checking against the Rung-1 listening-uptake instrument.
