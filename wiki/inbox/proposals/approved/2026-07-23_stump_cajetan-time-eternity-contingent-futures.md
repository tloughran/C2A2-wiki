## Summary
Stump examines Tommaso de Vio Cajetan's commentary on Article 13 of Question 14 of Aquinas's Summa Theologiae, treating the problem of God's knowledge of contingent futures. She reconstructs Cajetan's solution as an ordered set of relations between the absolute, eternal nature of God and the relative, contingent nature of created existents, linking the gnoseological problem (how God knows) to the metaphysics of temporality, eternity, necessity, and contingency.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This continues Stump's long-running Thomistic program on eternity and free will (with Kretzmann's ET-simultaneity account), applying a later scholastic commentator (Cajetan) to sharpen how divine timeless knowledge can be reconciled with genuine future contingency — a core node in her metaphysics of God's relation to time.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: How can God have certain knowledge of future contingents without collapsing their contingency into necessity?
  Resource: Cajetan's commentary on ST I q.14 a.13, structured as an ordered relation between God's absolute eternal being and the relative contingent being of creatures.
  Solution: A relational ordering in which eternal knowledge is simultaneous-with rather than prior-to contingent events, preserving contingency while grounding infallible knowledge.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Paper's stated thesis that Cajetan "establishes a precise order of relations between the absolute, eternal nature of God and the relative, contingent nature of generated existences."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: The gnoseological question (how God knows) is often treated apart from the metaphysical question (what time and eternity are).
  Resource: Cajetan's linkage of God's knowledge to the metaphysics of temporality, eternity, necessity, and contingency.
  Solution: An integrated account in which the theory of divine knowledge is derived from a prior metaphysics of the eternity-time relation rather than treated as an isolated epistemic puzzle.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Abstract framing that the knowledge problem is "linked to metaphysical concepts of temporality, eternity, necessity, and contingency."
