## Summary
This weekly summary curates a convergent, multi-voice account of hope as something other than optimism about outcomes. Rohr (Wednesday): "Hope is a participation in the very life of God. It has nothing to do with circumstances or events going well. It can even thrive in the midst of adversity." Bourgeault: hope "is a quality of aliveness… it lies at the beginning, as a pulse of truth that sends us forth," not a feeling that results from a happy outcome. Batiste: hope is "the language of the invisible… just as real, if not more real, than the things we can see." Kaba: hope is "a discipline… practiced every day." McLaren sharpens the logic: "If we can see a likely path to our desired outcome, we have hope; if we can see no possible path… we have despair. When our prime motive is love, a different logic comes into play. We find courage and confidence, not in the likelihood of a good outcome, but in our commitment to love." The week's practice (Luther E. Smith Jr., via Howard Thurman's "I Will Sing a New Song") frames singing as embodied hope: "Do you have a tune that carries you?"

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The week states, in contemplative register, a precise computational claim the C2A2 network cares about: hope and despair are usually functions of a perceived *path* to preferred states — and love reorders that function. This is the cleanest recent bridge between Rohr's contemplative tradition and the active-inference / affective-science programs in the network, and it is high-value for Summa 2026 loci on hope as a theological virtue (II-II Q.17–22) and on the contemplative life under adversity ("salvation in fear and trembling"). The Rohr wiki has no node on **hope-as-participation-not-prediction** or on the love-reframing of action under apparently unreachable goals.
