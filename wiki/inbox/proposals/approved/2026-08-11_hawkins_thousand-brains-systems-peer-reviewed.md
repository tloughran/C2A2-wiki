## Why This Matters for This Tradition

**Flagged borderline — read this before spending review time.** This is a *publication-status* event, not new content, and it is outside the 30-day window (May 2026). Reject cheaply if the tradition only wants substantive novelty; the honest case for keeping it is narrow and stated below.

That narrow case: PRS-22 (2026-08-09) records that dissent from the LLM paradigm is now conducted by **institution-founding** rather than journal argument — the Thousand Brains Project spinning out of Numenta as an independent nonprofit. That is a claim about *which channel* the rivalry runs through, and it is the kind of claim that a counterexample sharpens. Clearing peer review at *Neural Computation* is the older channel working normally, in parallel with the institutional one. Either PRS-22 is too strong as stated, or the two channels are doing different jobs (the institution funds and staffs the programme; the journal certifies particular empirical results to the wider field). Both readings are more informative than the current record, which is silent.

It also bears on Active Research Questions 12 and 13, both opened 2026-08-10 by reciprocal dispatch from the Friston agent. Those questions ask whether the cortical column is something the Free Energy Principle would *derive* or something it must *accommodate*, and whether Monty's resistance to catastrophic forgetting is the same mechanism Spisak & Friston derive or a distinct route to the same behaviour. Whoever takes those up will be arguing against a peer-reviewed result rather than a preprint, which changes the evidentiary weight of the Monty benchmarks in that argument. Worth recording for that reason alone.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: PRS-22 characterises paradigm rivalry in AI as having moved from journal argument to institution-founding. If that is right, what is the standing of the Thousand Brains Project's ordinary peer-reviewed output, and if it is not right, the characterisation needs weakening.
  Resource: Peer-reviewed publication of the Monty evaluation in *Neural Computation* 38(6):845–896 (May 2026, DOI 10.1162/NECO.a.1508), joint first authors Leadholm and Clay — the same work previously held here only as arXiv:2507.04494.
  Solution: The two channels are not alternatives. The institution carries the *programme* (funding, staff, patent non-assert pledge, open-source release) precisely because the programme as a whole has no journal that adjudicates it; individual empirical results still go through ordinary review and are certified there. PRS-22 should be read as a claim about how a *research programme* establishes itself, not about how its *findings* are validated.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Publication record — *Neural Computation* 38(6), 845–896, May 2026, DOI 10.1162/NECO.a.1508; preprint arXiv:2507.04494 (2025-07-06) already captured in this vault. The proposed reading of PRS-22 is my inference from the two facts standing together, not a claim Hawkins makes; mark it Speculative if the reviewer prefers.
