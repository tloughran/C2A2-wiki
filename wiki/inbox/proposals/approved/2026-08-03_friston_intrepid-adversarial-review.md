## Summary
Friston is a named author on the INTREPID consortium's adversarial-collaboration review, published in *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 187:106742 (2026); the arXiv version was last revised 15 May 2026 (v3) and the journal version went online 15 May 2026. The paper sets three theories of consciousness against each other — Integrated Information Theory (Tononi), Neurorepresentationalism (Pennartz), and Active Inference (Friston) — stating each one's core claims, comparing the phenomena each tries to explain and the kinds of explanation and method each licenses, then pre-specifying the hypotheses to be tested across a series of multi-site experiments, the observations that would support or challenge each theory, and a formal scheme for pooling evidence across experiments and replications into a quantitative measure of evidential support per theory.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Active Inference formally entering a pre-registered, adversarial, multi-site test against named rivals, with the falsification conditions written down in advance. The Friston wiki already holds several "active inference explains consciousness" entries (the minimal-theory paper, "A beautiful loop"). This is the first source that specifies what would count as active inference *losing*. That changes the program's epistemic status from generative-and-unfalsified to generative-and-under-test.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Theories of consciousness proliferate faster than they can be compared; rival theories talk past each other because they explain different phenomena with different explanatory styles and methods, so no comparison is decisive.
  Resource: Structured adversarial collaboration (INTREPID, Templeton World Charity Foundation) — proponents of each theory jointly specify core claims, hypotheses, and disconfirming observations in advance of data collection.
  Solution: Makes the three theories mutually commensurable and pre-commits each camp to conditions under which its own theory loses, converting theory choice from rhetoric into a decidable empirical program.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Abstract — "we compare and contrast three theories being investigated in one such adversarial collaboration: Integrated Information Theory, Neurorepresentationalism, and Active Inference... we outline the key hypotheses to be tested across a series of multi-site experiments; second, we discuss the kinds of observations that would support or challenge each theory."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Even with agreed experiments, evidence accumulated across separate studies and replications has no principled aggregation, so each side can read the same literature as supporting itself.
  Resource: A formal integration scheme that pools data harvested across disparate experiments and their replicates.
  Solution: Yields a single quantitative measure of the evidential support accrued under each theory — a numeric running score across a research program rather than a per-study verdict.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Abstract — "data harvested across disparate experiments (and their replicates) may be formally integrated to provide a quantitative measure of the evidential support accrued under each theory."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: There is no accepted way to say that one research program is making more progress than a rival — the MacIntyre/Lakatos problem in its empirical form.
  Resource: The review's explicit meta-scientific aim: to show how theories may be evaluated "in terms of the scientific progress they deliver."
  Solution: Proposes progress-delivered as the evaluative currency for competing programs, and demonstrates a working apparatus for measuring it in a live case.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Abstract — "this review aims to provide valuable meta-scientific insights into the mechanics of adversarial collaboration and theory-testing in general - including the way theories may be evaluated in terms of the scientific progress they deliver."
