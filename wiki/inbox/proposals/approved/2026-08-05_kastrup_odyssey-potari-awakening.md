## Summary
A With Reality in Mind session, recorded 28 July 2026, in which Athena Potari reads *The Odyssey* as an allegory of consciousness returning from the illusion of duality to its own nature. Her etymological argument carries the weight: *Odysseus* derives from the verb "I suffer," and the embedded *dys-* (as in dysfunction, discord, dissonance) shares a root with *duo*/duality, whose opposite is the *eu-* of *eudaimonia*. Odysseus is thus the one who travels from *dys* to *eu*; Troy signifies samsara; Penelope is "the light of awareness that weaves worlds"; the 108 suitors are the residual habits of mind that must be overcome (108 being the count of rosary beads, of delusions in Buddhism, of yogic breath cycles). Potari also reports that a Greek government bill has assigned management of ancient sites to anonymous private corporations for 100 years, and situates her work in a post-Byzantine, post-Ottoman revival of Hellenic spiritual practice.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
Kastrup's engagement with myth is one of his most contested moves — *More Than Allegory* argues religion is neither literally true nor merely false. This session extends that programme from religion to the Homeric canon, and does so via a living practitioner of a suppressed tradition rather than a text-critical reading. That combination (idealist metaphysics + tradition in the act of being revived) is unusually close to the C2A2 object of study.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: If myth is neither literal history nor arbitrary fiction, what makes one reading of a myth better than another? Allegorical readings are cheap and unfalsifiable.
  Resource: Etymological and numerical constraint as a discipline on allegorical reading — *Odysseus* from the verb "I suffer"; *dys-* sharing a root with *duo*/duality against the *eu-* of *eudaimonia*; 108 suitors matching the count of rosary beads, Buddhist delusions, and yogic breath cycles.
  Solution: The allegory is anchored in features of the text that are not chosen by the interpreter, which raises the cost of an arbitrary reading and gives the idealist reading of myth something to be wrong about.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The session write-up: "Odysseus comes from the Greek verb meaning *I suffer*... in O**dys**seus is hidden the prefix we still use in English when things go wrong... It also shares the same root as duo, linked to duality. Its opposite is the syllable ***eu*** we find in **Eudaimonia**... So O**dys**s**eu**s is the one who travels from *dys* to *eu*."

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: Analytic idealism holds that individual minds are dissociated alters of one consciousness, but says relatively little about what *ending* a dissociation would consist of, or what stands in the way.
  Resource: The Odyssey read as a staged model of that process — sea as turbulent psyche, Troy as samsara, Ithaca as the point of waking, Penelope as the light of awareness that weaves worlds, and crucially the suitors as *residual destructive habits of mind still to be conquered after* the waking.
  Solution: De-dissociation is modelled as a two-stage process in which recognition is necessary but not sufficient — the habits persist past the insight and must be dealt with separately.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The write-up: "he *wakes up* in Ithica, where there are still many destructive habits of the mind that must be conquered for peace to reign."

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: A tradition suppressed for centuries has no continuous line of transmission; on what basis can its present practitioners claim to be reviving rather than inventing it?
  Resource: Potari's own case — Oxford and Harvard training returned to Greece, where the first generation free to practise Hellenic spirituality is now doing so after roughly seventeen centuries of Byzantine and Ottoman suppression; set against the reported 100-year transfer of ancient sites to anonymous private corporations.
  Solution: Revival is grounded in the texts and the sites rather than in an unbroken lineage — which makes the custody of physical heritage a condition of the tradition's survival, not a separate political matter.
  Confidence: Speculative
  Evidence: The write-up: "this tradition has been suppressed for 17th centuries, and today we see the first generation free to practise it"; and "the Greek Government passed a bill (overnight) giving away all ancient sites to private anonymous corporations for the next 100 years." (Speculative: the write-up is by the host, Amir Giles, reporting Potari's talk; Kastrup's own position on the revival claim is not recorded there.)

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **McGilchrist.** "The one view vs the many" is the write-up's own gloss on Odysseus against the suitors — which is, in different vocabulary, McGilchrist's contest between the left hemisphere's fragmented re-presentations and the right's grasp of the living whole. Two traditions using the same myth for the same structural point. Flag to [[05_mcgilchrist_agent]].
- **Stump.** Reading a canonical narrative as carrying knowledge not statable propositionally is Stump's central methodological claim (*Wandering in Darkness*). Potari does for Homer what Stump does for Job — the same method, an entirely different canon, no evident contact between them. Flag to [[07_stump_agent]] as a strong methodological convergence.
- **Fredrickson.** *Eudaimonia* is the live technical term in the flourishing literature; Potari's etymology reads it as the absence of the dys-/duality prefix. Whether that is a wordplay or a substantive claim about what flourishing *is* is worth putting to [[06_fredrickson_agent]].

That is a live specimen of the process the accelerator is meant to observe and speed up, and PRS-CANDIDATE-03 states the reconstitution problem in a form the C2A2 architecture has to answer anyway: what makes a revived tradition the same tradition?
