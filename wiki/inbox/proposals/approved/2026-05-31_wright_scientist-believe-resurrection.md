## Summary
Wright argues that the resurrection is not a violation of natural law to be adjudicated by science, but the inauguration of God's new creation breaking into the middle of the old one — "the world cannot cope with a Jesus who comes out of the tomb." The objection that a scientist cannot believe in resurrection rests on a category error: it treats new creation as an anomaly within the closed system of the present creation, rather than as the firstfruits of a transformed cosmos. The historical case for the empty tomb and appearances stands on its own evidential terms; the metaphysical resistance comes not from data but from a prior commitment to a closed naturalism.
