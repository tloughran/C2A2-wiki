## Summary
Solé, Seoane, Pla-Mauri, Bennett, Hochberg and Levin propose a **cognition space** framework: instead of defining cognition by substrate, represent systems by organizational and informational dimensions and compare where they sit. Cognition becomes a graded capacity to sense, process, and act on information, so cells, brains, artificial agents, and human-AI collectives occupy one landscape. They examine three such spaces — basal aneural, neural, and human-AI hybrid — and find occupation is strikingly **uneven**: clusters of realized systems separated by large empty regions. Those voids are read as the joint signature of evolutionary contingency, physical constraint, and design limitation, and hybrid cognition is named as the frontier most likely to reach territory biological evolution never did.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
The empty regions are the payload. A morphospace-style treatment lets Levin's program make **predictions about what does not exist yet and might**, which is a stronger kind of claim than the usual "look, this too is cognitive." It also gives human-AI hybrids a principled place in the diverse-intelligence picture rather than treating them as a separate topic.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Cognitive processes occur across natural, artificial, and hybrid systems, but there is no unified framework for comparing their forms, limits, and unrealized possibilities — comparisons collapse into disputes over substrate-dependent definitions.
  Resource: A cognition space: a comparative representation whose axes are organizational and informational dimensions rather than material kind, treating cognition as a graded capacity to sense, process, and act on information.
  Solution: Systems as different as cells, brains, artificial agents, and human-AI collectives become analyzable in one conceptual landscape, so comparison replaces categorization.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: The paper's stated aim of replacing narrow, substrate-dependent definitions with a comparative representation based on organizational and informational dimensions.

PRS-CANDIDATE-02:
  Problem: We have no way to reason about forms of cognition that could exist but do not, so the space of possible minds stays rhetorical.
  Resource: Mapping the occupancy of three cognition spaces — basal aneural, neural, and human-AI hybrid — and locating the unoccupied regions.
  Solution: Occupation proves highly uneven, with clusters separated by large voids attributable to evolutionary contingency, physical constraint, and design limitation; the voids become a research target and a source of testable predictions.
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: The reported finding that occupation of the three spaces is highly uneven, with clusters of realized systems separated by large unoccupied regions, and the authors' attribution of those voids to three distinct causes.

PRS-CANDIDATE-03:
  Problem: Human-AI collectives are usually studied as a tools-and-usage question, not as a novel cognitive form with its own position in the space of possible minds.
  Resource: The human-AI hybrid cognition space, treated as a peer of the aneural and neural spaces.
  Solution: Hybrid cognition is identified as a frontier for reaching complexity beyond what biological evolution produced — a claim about what hybrids can be, not just what they are for.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: The paper highlights hybrid cognition as a promising frontier for exploring novel forms of complexity beyond those produced by biological evolution; the claim is programmatic rather than empirically demonstrated here.

## Cross-Tradition Signals
- **Wolfram** — A space of possible cognitive systems with sparsely occupied regions is structurally the same object as Wolfram's ruliad/rulial-space arguments about the space of possible computations and which corners of it get realized. The shared question — why is the occupied region so small and clumpy? — is a strong candidate for a genuine cross-program bridge rather than a loose analogy.
- **Friston** — The organizational/informational axes overlap with what a Markov blanket formalism measures; a cognition space could plausibly be re-derived with free-energy quantities as coordinates, which would be a substantive unification test rather than a restatement.
- **Hoffman** — The framework is agnostic about whether the information processed is veridical, so interface theory could be read as a claim about *which* region of cognition space evolution actually explored, and why.
- **C2A2 relevance (very high)** — "Human-AI hybrid cognition space" names precisely the object C2A2 is building and studying. The unoccupied-region framing offers a possible formal vocabulary for the accelerator/detector thesis: an inter-tradition dialogue that produces second-first-language speakers is an attempt to *move a system into a currently empty region* of the hybrid cognition space. Recommend the master agent examine this as candidate scaffolding for the measurement framework.
