## Summary
Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria propose an active-inference theory of consciousness resting on three conditions: (1) the system simulates a world model; (2) there is inferential competition to enter that model; and (3) "epistemic depth" — recurrent, system-wide sharing of Bayesian beliefs, i.e., a self-referential loop in which the model is reflexively available to itself. Consciousness is identified not with any predictive process but specifically with this circular one, and the theory is used to explain altered states (meditation, psychedelics, minimal phenomenal experience).

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is Friston's most developed extension of active inference into a full theory of consciousness, and it introduces "epistemic depth" as the proposed mark of the conscious. It is distinct from the Whyte et al. "minimal theory" already captured (PRS-09) and is not yet in the wiki; it pushes FEP from perception/action into awareness itself.

## Candidate PRS Triplets

PRS-CANDIDATE-01:
  Problem: Active inference / FEP explains perception and action but has lacked a principled account of why and when a predictive system is conscious rather than merely adaptive.
  Resource: Three conditions — world-model simulation, inferential competition to enter the model, and "epistemic depth" (recurrent, system-wide sharing of Bayesian beliefs forming a self-referential loop).
  Solution: Consciousness is identified with circular (self-referential) predictive processing that satisfies epistemic depth, yielding mechanistic explanations of meditative, psychedelic, and minimal phenomenal states.
  Confidence: Medium
  Evidence: Laukkonen, R., Friston, K., Chandaria, S. (2025) "A beautiful loop: An active inference theory of consciousness," Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, doi:10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296.
