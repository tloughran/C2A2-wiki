## Summary
Opening meditation of the new CAC series "Returning Home," adapted from the revised *Falling Upward* (2024, pp. 55–56). Rohr argues that the archetype of "home" points two ways at once: back to an original taste of union and forward to its realization, like Ithaca in the *Odyssey*. He reframes homesickness as a "desirous dissatisfaction" arising from original union with God, and identifies the force that both sends and draws us with the Cosmic Christ as alpha and omega. He adds that God plants in us a desire for what God already wants to give.

## Why This Matters for This Tradition
This is the clearest recent statement linking Rohr's Universal-Christ Christology (alpha/omega) to his developmental two-halves-of-life frame. It gives the wiki an explicit teleology: the end is present in the beginning as an operative pull, not only a goal. That is directly citable for Summa 2026 loci on beatitude as the final end (I-II Q.1–5) and on natural desire for God.

## Candidate PRS Triplets
PRS-CANDIDATE:
- P: How can a final end act on an agent before it is reached, without positing backward causation?
- R: Rohr's "homing device": one origin-union that both sends and draws (Cosmic Christ as alpha and omega).
- S: The telos is present from the start as an implanted desire, so being "pulled" by the end and "pushed" by the origin are one structure seen from two times.
- Confidence: Medium
- Evidence: Meditation text, paragraphs 2–3 (alpha/omega; God plants desire for what God wants to give).

PRS-CANDIDATE:
- P: Why does longing for an ideal "home" persist even in people whose actual origins were bad?
- R: Original union as a foundational seed prior to biographical experience.
- S: The longing indexes a pre-biographical union, not a memory of a good childhood; this explains its persistence across histories.
- Confidence: Speculative
- Evidence: Paragraph 2 (children with sad or abusive childhoods still long for "home").
