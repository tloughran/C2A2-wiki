### Institute for Social Ecology (ISE)  
**Type:** Civic & Political | **Subtype:** Social ecology education community — anarchist direct democracy and ecological thought  
**URL:** https://social-ecology.org/wp/  
**Country:** USA  

**Description:** Founded in 1974 in Plainfield, Vermont by Daniel Chodorkoff and Murray Bookchin, ISE has provided experiential radical education in social ecology for over fifty years, through intensive summer seminars, online programs, and partnerships with movements. It is the primary institutional home for Bookchin's political philosophy—libertarian municipalism, democratic confederalism, the critique of hierarchy—which has influenced movements from Occupy to the Rojava cantons in Syrian Kurdistan.  

**Problem:** Radical political theory exists primarily in academic texts while movements for systemic change lack the philosophical frameworks needed to articulate and sustain genuine democratic alternatives to both capitalism and authoritarian state socialism.  

**Resource:** Bookchin's social ecology as a systematic political philosophy linking ecological crisis to hierarchy, combined with ISE's fifty years of applied education that trains grassroots organizers in direct democratic theory and practice.  

**Solution:** A lineage of organizers and thinkers who carry social ecology into environmental, feminist, Indigenous rights, and democratic movements—with its most dramatic partial realization in Rojava's democratic confederalism, which explicitly draws on Bookchin's framework.

### Mind & Life Institute  
**Type:** Contemplative & Spiritual | **Subtype:** Contemplative science research community — Buddhist-Western scientific dialogue  
**URL:** https://www.mindandlife.org/  
**Country:** USA (Global)  

**Description:** Founded in 1991, Mind & Life brings together Tibetan Buddhist contemplatives—anchored by ongoing collaboration with the Dalai Lama—and Western scientists to create and advance contemplative science as a rigorous field. It funds research, convenes annual institutes, and runs a Summer Research Institute that gathers scientists, monastics, and policymakers. It has shaped major research programs in neuroscience, psychology, and clinical medicine, and in 2026 turned its community forum toward depolarization.  

**Problem:** Contemplative traditions possess millennia of first-person methodologies for investigating the nature of mind, but these have been systematically excluded from Western scientific accounts of consciousness, emotion, and wellbeing.  

**Resource:** A unique institutional bridge—direct working relationships between senior Tibetan Buddhist teachers and leading neuroscientists and psychologists—combined with grant-making and a global research network that produces empirically testable programs from contemplative insight.  

**Solution:** The integration of rigorous first-person contemplative inquiry with third-person empirical science, transforming both—producing new fields (mindfulness-based interventions, contemplative neuroscience) and beginning to rehabilitate subjective experience as scientifically legitimate data.

### International Neuroethics Society (INS)  
**Type:** Interdisciplinary Synthesis | **Subtype:** Neuroethics and neurotechnology governance  
**URL:** https://neuroethicssociety.org/  
**Country:** Global  

**Description:** INS is the primary professional society at the intersection of neuroscience, ethics, law, and policy. Its 2025 annual meeting in Munich focused on neuroethics and AI; the 2026 meeting (Stanford/Lucca/Stellenbosch) has the theme 'Building Bridges and Cultivating Community.' INS addresses BCIs, neural data privacy, AI-neuroscience interaction, and cognitive enhancement. An inaugural Neuroethics Asia conference is planned for Singapore in November 2026, organized with the National University of Singapore.  

**Problem:** Neurotechnology — brain-computer interfaces, neural data collection, AI-augmented neuroscience — is advancing faster than governance frameworks can track. A nationwide survey found most Americans consider brain data more sensitive than financial or genetic data, yet there are no agreed frameworks for neural data privacy, consent, or corporate use.  

**Resource:** An interdisciplinary community spanning neuroethics, law, AI ethics, and clinical neuroscience; annual conferences connecting researchers to policymakers; partnerships with the Bavarian Academy of Sciences, Stanford, and NUS; working groups producing governance guidance documents; growing global reach through INS Asia.  

**Solution:** Developing workable governance frameworks — including consent, privacy, and equity standards — for neural data, BCIs, and AI-augmented brain science, ensuring neurotechnology development is guided by ethical analysis that integrates patient, clinician, ethicist, and community perspectives.

### Global Ecovillage Network (GEN)  
**Type:** Local & Embodied | **Subtype:** Ecovillage federation  
**URL:** https://ecovillage.org/  
**Country:** Global (HQ: Germany)  

**Description:** Founded in 1995 following a conference at Findhorn, GEN connects more than 6,000 intentional communities across 114 countries, with 1,251 ecovillages mapped on its interactive platform. Celebrating its 30th anniversary in 2025, the network bridges Global South and Global North communities through shared education, an ecovillage marketplace, and a newly established Global Ecovillage Research Institute. Member communities practice ecological design, renewable energy, food sovereignty, and regenerative economics.  

**Problem:** Industrial civilization has severed the relationship between human settlement and ecological function. Most people live in places that consume resources at multiple times planetary capacity, with no community architecture for sustainable collective life. The dominant housing and food model offers no template for post-carbon living at scale.  

**Resource:** Sixty years of accumulated practice across cultures and climates showing how communities can reduce ecological footprints to half or less of national averages while supporting human flourishing. GEN holds this knowledge as a living comparative archive, including approaches to governance, renewable energy, water, food, and social cohesion.  

**Solution:** Demonstrating ecovillages as replicable policy-ready responses to the climate crisis, with active engagement at COP30 in Belém in 2025. The goal is mainstream policy integration of community-led ecological settlement models rather than marginal lifestyle experiments.

### Columbia University Division of Narrative Medicine  
**Type:** Practice Communities | **Subtype:** Narrative Medicine  
**URL:** https://www.mhe.cuimc.columbia.edu/narrative-medicine  
**Country:** USA (Global influence)  

**Description:** Columbia inaugurated the first and only Master of Science in Narrative Medicine in 2009. The Division of Narrative Medicine offers the MS degree, a Certification of Professional Achievement, VP&S medical school electives, a Post-Graduate Fellowship, and community seminars including the Narrative, Health, and Social Justice University Seminar and the Graphic Medicine Club. The program trains clinicians and humanists in close reading, reflective writing, and narrative competence as tools for clinical practice, medical education, and health advocacy. It has defined and institutionalized a new discipline.  

**Problem:** Modern medical training produces technically skilled clinicians who lack the narrative competence to hear patients' illness stories fully, attend to their own emotional experience, or understand suffering in its social and biographical dimensions—producing burnout, diagnostic error, and fractured therapeutic relationships.  

**Resource:** A fully institutionalized graduate program and international fellowship model; a body of methods (close reading, reflective writing, parallel charts) developed specifically for clinical contexts; interdisciplinary faculty from medicine, literature, and public health; a global network of narrative medicine training programs inspired by the Columbia model.  

**Solution:** Making narrative competence a recognized and teachable clinical skill, transforming medical education and clinical practice. Partial progress: MS program established and growing; international programs in Italy, France, and Brazil inspired by Columbia; narrative medicine methods now included in palliative care and oncology training.

### Professional Guilds (14 records — below 20 target)

Professional Guilds came up thinnest (14 records). The challenge: many guild-like bodies are large professional associations (AMA, ABA) where the guild character — apprenticeship, tradition-transmission, internal standards debate — has been bureaucratized away. Active standards reform debates exist but are often sub-committee activities rather than community-level phenomena. Several craft guilds (glass, stone, textile) exist but their web presence is sparse and PRS hard to articulate without richer sourcing.

### Practice Communities (17 records)

Practice Communities also came up a few short of 25. The issue: many practice communities (nursing, social work, occupational therapy) have professional associations but their community character is diffuse. The richest PRS entries cluster around reformist or emergent movements within professions — narrative medicine, evidence-based practice reformers, trauma-informed care — rather than the professions as wholes. More targeted searches on sub-specialty reform movements would help.

### Interdisciplinary Synthesis (18 records)

Interdisciplinary communities are plentiful but often have generic mission statements ('bridging disciplines'). Many think-tanks and institutes describe interdisciplinary ambitions without specifying the convergence problem. Quality over quantity meant dropping several that lacked a sharp problem.
