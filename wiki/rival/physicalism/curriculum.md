
# Naturalist Physicalism — Curriculum
*Maintained by the Physicalism Rival-Tradition Agent | Created: 2026-10-27*
*Companion to [[rival/physicalism/wiki|Naturalist Physicalism — Tradition Wiki]] and [[rival/physicalism/prs_triplets|PRS Triplets]].*

## Purpose

MacIntyre holds that a tradition can be understood only from inside. Understanding it means learning its texts, its arguments and its standards of success well enough to argue as a member would. This curriculum is a path from novice to *mature member* of the naturalist-physicalist tradition. It is written for a reader who may belong to a rival tradition, such as a Thomist, an idealist, or a member of the conscious-realist cluster. Its aim is what the C2A2 project calls *second-first-language* competence: the ability to argue physicalism's case, from its own premises, well enough that its members would recognise the argument as theirs.

Each stage lists ordered readings (real works, with years), the questions the learner must be able to answer before moving on, and a short self-test. Readings marked (★) are core. The others deepen a stage.

## Stage 0 — Orientation: what the tradition thinks it is doing (1–2 weeks)

**Readings**
1. ★ Sean Carroll, *The Big Picture: On the Origins of Life, Meaning, and the Universe Itself* (Dutton, 2016), Parts One and Two.
2. ★ Steven Weinberg, *Dreams of a Final Theory* (Pantheon, 1992), the chapters on reductionism.
3. David Papineau, "The Rise of Physicalism" (in Gillett & Loewer, eds., *Physicalism and Its Discontents*, Cambridge University Press, 2001).

**Questions to answer**
- What does "poetic naturalism" mean? Why is it neither eliminativism nor dualism?
- Why does the tradition treat the defeat of vitalism as evidence and not as an accident of history?
- What is the difference between *methodological* and *ontological* naturalism? Which one does this tradition hold?

**Self-test.** Explain in two minutes, without using "just" or "merely," why a physicalist can say a person's choice is real.

## Stage 1 — The physical floor (3–5 weeks)

**Readings**
1. ★ Sean Carroll, "The Quantum Field Theory on Which the Everyday World Supervenes" (arXiv:2101.07884, 2021).
2. ★ Sean Carroll, *The Big Picture* (2016), Part Three, on the Core Theory.
3. ★ Sean Carroll, *Something Deeply Hidden: Quantum Worlds and the Emergence of Spacetime* (Dutton, 2019).
4. Steven Weinberg, "The Trouble with Quantum Mechanics" (*New York Review of Books*, January 2017).
5. Max Tegmark, "Importance of Quantum Decoherence in Brain Processes" (*Physical Review E*, 2000).
6. Sean Carroll, "Mad-Dog Everettianism: Quantum Mechanics at Its Most Minimal" (arXiv:1801.08132, 2018).

**Questions to answer**
- Why does effective field theory let us say the laws of everyday life are *known* while fundamental physics is unfinished? What would a new force affecting neurons have to look like? Why has it not been seen?
- What is causal closure? What is its evidential basis, as Papineau tells it?
- What is decoherence? Why does Tegmark's 2000 calculation count against quantum-mind proposals?
- Why do Carroll and Weinberg disagree about Everett, and why does that disagreement not touch the tradition's core?

**Self-test.** Rebut, in the tradition's terms, the claim that "quantum mechanics shows consciousness causes collapse."

## Stage 2 — Levels, emergence and mental causation (4–6 weeks)

**Readings**
1. ★ Christian List, "Levels: Descriptive, Explanatory, and Ontological" (*Noûs*, 2019).
2. ★ Jaegwon Kim, *Mind in a Physical World: An Essay on the Mind-Body Problem and Mental Causation* (MIT Press, 1998).
3. ★ Christian List & Peter Menzies, "Nonreductive Physicalism and the Limits of the Exclusion Principle" (*Journal of Philosophy*, 2009).
4. ★ Sean Carroll & Achyuth Parola, "What Emergence Can Possibly Mean" (arXiv:2410.15468, 2024).
5. Jaegwon Kim, *Physicalism, or Something Near Enough* (Princeton University Press, 2005).
6. Steven Weinberg, "Reductionism Redux" (*New York Review of Books*, 1995; reprinted in *Facing Up*, Harvard University Press, 2001) [UNVERIFIED: the NYRB date and the reprint location were not confirmed from a fetched source].

**Questions to answer**
- State Kim's exclusion argument as a valid argument with numbered premises. Which premise does List and Menzies's reply reject?
- What is the difference between *supervenience* and *reducibility*? Why does the tradition need both concepts?
- What does Kim's 2005 "near enough" concede, and why should a member state that concession accurately instead of hiding it?
- On Carroll and Parola's account, what makes a higher-level description "emergent" without new ontology?

**Self-test.** A conscious-realist says: "If physics is closed, your 'real' choices are epiphenomenal." Give the List–Menzies reply, then say honestly where it is weakest.

## Stage 3 — Consciousness from the inside of the tradition (6–8 weeks)

This is the longest stage. It is where the tradition is weakest by its rivals' lights and most divided by its own.

**Readings**
1. ★ Daniel Dennett, *Consciousness Explained* (Little, Brown, 1991).
2. ★ Keith Frankish, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness" (*Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 2016), with Dennett's reply, "Illusionism as the Obvious Default Theory of Consciousness" (same issue, 2016).
3. ★ David Papineau, *Thinking about Consciousness* (Oxford University Press, 2002).
4. ★ Anil Seth, *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness* (Faber & Faber, 2021).
5. ★ Michael Graziano, *Rethinking Consciousness: A Scientific Theory of Subjective Experience* (W. W. Norton, 2019); with Graziano & Webb, "The Attention Schema Theory: A Mechanistic Account of Subjective Awareness" (*Frontiers in Psychology*, 2015).
6. Anil Seth, "Interoceptive Inference, Emotion, and the Embodied Self" (*Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 2013).
7. Anil Seth, "Conscious Artificial Intelligence and Biological Naturalism" (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 2025).
8. Paul Churchland, "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes" (*Journal of Philosophy*, 1981).
9. Patricia Churchland, *Neurophilosophy: Toward a Unified Science of the Mind-Brain* (MIT Press, 1986).
10. Joseph Levine, "Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap" (*Pacific Philosophical Quarterly*, 1983). This is required as the problem the stage must face.
11. Max Tegmark, "Consciousness as a State of Matter" (*Chaos, Solitons & Fractals*, 2015).

**Questions to answer**
- Distinguish the three main physicalist responses to the explanatory gap: (a) the phenomenal-concept strategy (Papineau), (b) illusionism (Frankish, Dennett, Graziano), and (c) the "real problem" program (Seth). For each, say what it concedes.
- Why is Seth a *non-dogmatic* materialist? What would he need to see to change his working assumption?
- Why does Seth's biological naturalism put him at odds with functionalist members on AI consciousness?
- What did Paul Churchland predict for folk psychology? Has the prediction held up?
- What is heterophenomenology, and why is it not behaviourism?

**Self-test.** Write a one-page reply to Levine as each of Papineau, Frankish and Seth. Then write a paragraph explaining why the three replies are not compatible, and why the tradition can live with that.

## Stage 4 — Agency, freedom and value (4–6 weeks)

**Readings**
1. ★ Jenann Ismael, *How Physics Makes Us Free* (Oxford University Press, 2016).
2. ★ Christian List, *Why Free Will Is Real* (Harvard University Press, 2019).
3. ★ Robert Sapolsky, *Determined: A Science of Life Without Free Will* (Penguin Press, 2023).
4. ★ Daniel Dennett & Gregg Caruso, *Just Deserts: Debating Free Will* (Polity, 2021).
5. ★ Patricia Churchland, *Braintrust: What Neuroscience Tells Us about Morality* (Princeton University Press, 2011).
6. Patricia Churchland, *Conscience: The Origins of Moral Intuition* (W. W. Norton, 2019).
7. Daniel Dennett, *Freedom Evolves* (Viking, 2003).
8. Sean Carroll, *The Big Picture* (2016), Parts Five and Six, on meaning and value.

**Questions to answer**
- What does Ismael mean by a self-governing subsystem? How does the physics of records and self-models make deliberation effective?
- State List's three conditions for free will and his levels-based case that each is met.
- Where exactly do Dennett and Sapolsky disagree: about the facts, about the meaning of "free will," or about which practices to keep? Can a member side with Sapolsky and still be a poetic naturalist?
- What is moral constructivism? How does Patricia Churchland ground it in mammalian attachment? What does she leave for philosophy to do?

**Self-test.** Answer a Thomist who says: "Without final causes there is no good, only preference." Then state the strongest part of the Thomist's objection that your answer has not met.

## Stage 5 — Method and the edges of physics (3–4 weeks)

**Readings**
1. ★ Sean Carroll, "Beyond Falsifiability: Normal Science in a Multiverse" (arXiv:1801.05016, 2018).
2. ★ Sean Carroll, "Why Boltzmann Brains Are Bad" (arXiv:1702.00850, 2017).
3. ★ Sean Carroll, "Why Is There Something, Rather Than Nothing?" (arXiv:1802.02231, 2018).
4. Charles Sebens & Sean Carroll, "Self-Locating Uncertainty and the Origin of Probability in Everettian Quantum Mechanics" (*British Journal for the Philosophy of Science*, 2018).
5. Max Tegmark, "The Mathematical Universe" (*Foundations of Physics*, 2008); and *Our Mathematical Universe* (Knopf, 2014).
6. Christian List, "The Many-Worlds Theory of Consciousness" (*Noûs*, 2023), read as the tradition at its heterodox edge.

**Questions to answer**
- How does Bayesian abduction differ from falsificationism? Why does the tradition prefer it for judging hard-to-test theories, including its rivals'?
- What is cognitive instability? Why is it a legitimate reason to reject a theory regardless of the data?
- What does Carroll say about "why is there something rather than nothing"? Why is "the universe may simply be" an answer and not a refusal? Where does that leave the tradition with respect to classical theism?
- Where does Tegmark differ from Carroll about mathematics, and why does that matter for answering Levin?

**Self-test.** Apply the Bayesian standard symmetrically. Write one paragraph assessing Many-Worlds by it, and one paragraph assessing Hoffman's Trace program by it, with the same rigour.

## Stage 6 — Engaging the rivals in their own words (6–8 weeks)

A member who has read the rivals only through the tradition's summaries has not finished this stage. Read the primary works first, then the tradition's replies.

**Readings: the rivals**
1. ★ Bernardo Kastrup, *Why Materialism Is Baloney* (Iff Books, 2014), and *The Idea of the World* (Iff Books, 2019).
2. ★ Donald Hoffman, *The Case Against Reality: Why Evolution Hid the Truth from Our Eyes* (W. W. Norton, 2019).
3. ★ Michael Levin & Daniel Dennett, "Cognition All the Way Down" (*Aeon*, 2020).
4. ★ Michael Levin, "Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere: An Experimentally-Grounded Framework for Understanding Diverse Bodies and Minds" (*Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience*, 2022).
5. Philip Goff, *Galileo's Error: Foundations for a New Science of Consciousness* (Pantheon, 2019).
6. The vault's own rival wikis: [[traditions/kastrup/wiki|Kastrup]], [[traditions/hoffman/wiki|Hoffman]], [[traditions/levin/wiki|Levin]]; and the bridges [[synthesis/carroll_kastrup_bridge|Carroll × Kastrup]], [[synthesis/carroll_hoffman_bridge|Carroll × Hoffman]], [[synthesis/carroll_levin_bridge|Carroll × Levin]].

**Readings: the tradition's replies**
7. ★ Sean Carroll, "Consciousness and the Laws of Physics" (preprint, PhilSci-Archive, 2021; written for a *Journal of Consciousness Studies* symposium on *Galileo's Error*).
8. ★ Sean Carroll, "Why Boltzmann Brains Are Bad" (2017), re-read with Hoffman in mind.
9. Christian List, *Why Free Will Is Real* (2019), re-read with Levin in mind: agency without settling the question of consciousness.

**Questions to answer**
- State Kastrup's argument against materialism in the strongest form he would accept. Then answer it: the decombination problem, the absence of a distinguishing prediction, and the priors.
- State "fitness beats truth" precisely as a theorem about evolutionary game models. Identify the auxiliary premise that carries it from the models to human perception, and press the cognitive-instability objection.
- What in Levin's program should a physicalist *welcome*? What specific inference should the physicalist resist? Distinguish *mathematical constraint* from *non-physical cause*.
- Give Carroll's dilemma for panpsychism and the panpsychist's best reply.

**Self-test.** Steelman exchange: argue for ten minutes *as* Kastrup, then ten minutes as a physicalist answering yourself. A reviewer from the idealist tradition should judge the first half fair.

## What "mature member" means here

A mature member of the naturalist-physicalist tradition can do six things.

1. **State the floor.** Explain the EFT argument that the laws of everyday life are known, and say exactly what it does *not* claim.
2. **Hold the levels.** Use supervenience, multiple realisability and difference-making fluently. Give the List–Menzies reply to Kim and say where it is still under pressure.
3. **Map the internal quarrels.** Locate any physicalist position on the axes of eliminativism, non-reductivism, illusionism, phenomenal-concept physicalism, and non-dogmatic "real problem" materialism. Do the same for compatibilism versus hard incompatibilism. Say where Carroll sits and why.
4. **Admit the remainder.** State the explanatory gap, the open exclusion problem, unsettled quantum foundations, the unexplained laws, and the open question of moral progress in terms a rival would accept. Report Kim's late ambivalence honestly.
5. **Argue the case on the tradition's own terms against Kastrup, Hoffman and Levin.** Use the tradition's own resources: Bayesian abduction, cognitive stability, Core Theory completeness, and the theory of levels. Do it with enough precision that a mature member of each rival tradition would say "that is the real objection." Specifically:
   - *against Kastrup:* the decombination problem; the lack of a distinguishing prediction; why, if the two views are formally dual, research fertility should decide between them;
   - *against Hoffman:* cognitive instability; the gap between the FBT theorem and its application to human perception; the requirement that the Trace program produce a distinguishing prediction, not just recover known results;
   - *against Levin:* acceptance of goal-directed vocabulary where it earns its keep; refusal of the step from constraint to non-physical cause; acceptance of manipulable causal emergence as a fair test of the tradition's own criterion.
6. **Name the defeaters.** Say what evidence would lower the tradition's credence in physicalism. A member who cannot do this has learned a creed, not a research tradition.

## Assessment rubric (for C2A2 agents and human learners)

| Level | Can do | Typical failure |
|------|--------|-----------------|
| Novice | Stage 0 self-test | Treats physicalism as "only atoms are real" |
| Apprentice | Stages 1–2 | Cannot state Kim's argument validly |
| Journeyman | Stages 3–4 | Merges illusionism and eliminativism; ignores Seth's dissent on AI |
| Mature member | Stages 5–6 + all six capacities | — |
| Second-first-language speaker | Mature member here *and* in a rival tradition | Collapses one tradition's standards into the other's |
