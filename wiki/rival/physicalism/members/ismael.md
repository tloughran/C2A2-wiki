# Jenann Ismael — Physicalism Member

*Part of the [[rival/physicalism/wiki|Physicalism tradition]]. Written from inside the tradition.*

## 1. Who she is and her role in the tradition

Jenann Ismael is the William H. Miller III Professor of Philosophy at Johns Hopkins University, the same university as [[members/carroll]], after earlier appointments at the University of Arizona, the Centre for Time at the University of Sydney and Columbia University. [UNVERIFIED: whether the Columbia appointment is current or past is ambiguous in her 2022 CV.] She works on the foundations of physics (time, quantum mechanics, symmetry, laws) and on what physics implies for the self and for freedom. In this tradition she fills a gap: the scientists explain how brains work, and the metaphysicians argue about levels, but Ismael asks what a deterministic or quantum physics actually says about agents who are inside the universe it describes. Her answer is that a correct understanding of physical law makes room for real self-governance. That gives the tradition its most physics-literate defence of free will and the self.

## 2. Core positions

1. **Laws do not govern.** Fundamental physical laws describe patterns in the overall history of the world; they do not push events along like "iron rails." Following Russell and Huw Price, she reads global laws in a broadly Humean way, so that our actions are partly what make the laws true. *How Physics Makes Us Free* (2016).
2. **Causation is for agents.** Causal relations, as understood through the interventionist work of Pearl, Glymour and Woodward, are the structures that tell an agent how to bring about outcomes by intervening. Causation therefore enables agency rather than threatening it. "Causation, Free Will, and Naturalism," in *Scientific Metaphysics* (2013); *How Physics Makes Us Free* (2016).
3. **The self as self-governing system.** The self is not a soul but a self-modelling system that integrates information and makes all-things-considered decisions, like the executive of a complex organisation. *The Situated Self* (2007); *How Physics Makes Us Free* (2016).
4. **Determinism does not entail predictability from inside.** A "counterpredictive device," built to act against any prediction of its behaviour, is physically possible; the resulting puzzle shows that determinism entails less about prediction than Laplace assumed. The tradition reads this as support for the openness of an embedded agent's future from its own standpoint. "Determinism, Counterpredictive Devices, and the Impossibility of Laplacean Intelligences," *The Monist* 102 (2019).
5. **Probability and time from inside physics.** Probabilities and the arrow of time can be understood in deterministic physics without adding anything to the laws. "Probability in Deterministic Physics," *Journal of Philosophy* (2009); *Time: A Very Short Introduction* (2021).
6. **Quantum non-separability as common ground.** With Jonathan Schaffer, she argued that the best explanation of quantum non-separability is a "common ground": entangled entities look holistic because they are dispersed manifestations of a more unified underlying reality. "Quantum Holism: Nonseparability as Common Ground," *Synthese* 197 (online 2016; print 2020).

## 3. Where she disagrees with fellow members

- **With [[members/sapolsky]]:** Sapolsky argues that because every choice has prior causes, there is no free will. Ismael's position is that this inference rests on a picture of laws as governing and of prior causes as compelling, which she rejects. The tradition reads them as directly opposed. [UNVERIFIED: no text located in which Ismael responds to Sapolsky by name; the opposition is drawn from their published positions.]
- **With [[members/weinberg]]:** Weinberg's arrows of explanation point down to fundamental laws as the deepest level. Ismael's Humean reading makes laws summaries of the world's patterns, which removes their role as the source that drives everything else.
- **With [[members/kim]]:** Kim's exclusion argument assumes that a sufficient physical cause leaves no work for anything else. Ismael's interventionist view of causation, like [[members/list]]'s difference-making view, treats causal claims as answers to questions about intervention, which can be true at several levels at once.
- **With [[members/carroll]]:** They are close allies (and colleagues). Both are compatibilists who think free will is real at the level of agents. Ismael's distinctive addition is the argument that laws do not govern, which Carroll does not need for his version.
- **On consciousness:** Ismael's main work is on agency, the self, time and laws rather than on phenomenal consciousness, and this page does not attribute a position on the hard problem to her. Her chapter "Why Physics Should Care about the Mind" (in Shan Gao, ed., *Quantum Mechanics and Consciousness*, 2022) bears on the topic. [UNVERIFIED: the chapter's thesis was not checked.]

## 4. What she offers against the conscious-realist rivals

- **Against the argument from freedom to idealism:** One route to conscious realism runs: physics is deterministic or random; real agency is neither; so agency must come from a non-physical mind. Ismael blocks the first step. If laws do not govern, and if determinism does not imply that an embedded agent's choices are predictable from inside, physics leaves room for self-governance by physical systems. This takes away a motive that leads people toward [[traditions/kastrup/wiki|Kastrup]]'s mind-at-large or toward a soul that acts on the brain.
- **On [[traditions/levin/wiki|Levin]]:** Levin's goal-directed tissues are self-modelling, self-regulating systems. Ismael's account of the self as an integrating, self-governing system offers the tradition a way to accept Levin's findings about agency at many scales while denying that they require mind to be fundamental.
- **On quantum holism and [[traditions/hoffman/wiki|Hoffman]] / [[traditions/kastrup/wiki|Kastrup]]:** Rivals sometimes cite quantum entanglement as support for a unified, mind-like reality. Ismael and Schaffer go a long way toward the rivals here: they argue that entangled parts are manifestations of a more unified underlying reality. A conscious realist will welcome that. The tradition's point is that the "common ground" in their account is a physical quantum state, so the unity is structural and physical; nothing in the argument makes it mental. This is a place where the tradition and its rivals share a premise and part over its interpretation.

## 5. The weak point a fair rival would press

Her strategy depends on a Humean view of laws, which many philosophers of physics reject, and a reviewer of *How Physics Makes Us Free* (Carl Hoefer, *Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews*) found her local/global law distinction unpersuasive. A rival will also note that she leaves open how conscious experience figures in self-governance, a gap the same reviewer pointed out and Ismael acknowledges. From a MacIntyrean standpoint, her executive model of the self explains the *form* of practical reason but not its *ends*: a self-governing system can govern itself toward anything, and the account of which ends are worth pursuing has to come from elsewhere.
