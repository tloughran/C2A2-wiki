# Paul Churchland — Physicalism Member

*Part of the [[rival/physicalism/wiki|Physicalism tradition]]. Written from inside the tradition.*

## 1. Who he is and his role in the tradition

Paul M. Churchland is Professor Emeritus of Philosophy at the University of California, San Diego. Together with [[members/patricia-churchland]], he developed a programme in which the philosophy of mind answers to neuroscience. His role in this tradition is to mark its most revisionary edge. Most members say that ordinary talk of beliefs and desires is true of physical systems at some level. Churchland argued that this talk is a theory, that theories can be false, and that this one may turn out to be false. He also gave the tradition a positive account of how brains represent the world, using the vector-coding and state-space ideas of connectionist neural networks.

## 2. Core positions

1. **Eliminative materialism.** Folk psychology is an empirical theory of behaviour. Like phlogiston chemistry, it may be replaced rather than reduced by a mature neuroscience. "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes," *Journal of Philosophy* 78 (1981); the idea appears earlier in *Scientific Realism and the Plasticity of Mind* (1979).
2. **Introspection is theory-laden and revisable.** We could learn to perceive our own states in neuroscientific terms, just as people learn to see the night sky in astronomical terms. "Reduction, Qualia, and the Direct Introspection of Brain States," *Journal of Philosophy* 82 (1985).
3. **Neurocomputational representation.** Brains represent the world as points and trajectories in high-dimensional activation spaces across neuron populations, not as sentence-like propositions. *A Neurocomputational Perspective* (1989); *The Engine of Reason, the Seat of the Soul* (1995).
4. **Conceptual frameworks as learned maps.** Concepts and even abstract universals are structures learned by neural networks, which gives an epistemology for the physicalist. *Plato's Camera: How the Physical Brain Captures a Landscape of Abstract Universals* (2012).
5. **Brain-like AI over classical AI.** With Patricia Churchland, he argued that symbol-manipulating AI was unlikely to yield minds, while systems modelled on brain architecture might. "Could a Machine Think?" *Scientific American* (January 1990). The tradition reads this as an early forecast of the connectionist approach behind today's neural-network systems.

## 3. Where he disagrees with fellow members

- **With [[members/carroll]] and [[members/dennett]]:** Both keep folk psychology as a true higher-level vocabulary (poetic naturalism; the intentional stance). Churchland's position is that usefulness does not guarantee truth. Folk astronomy was useful too. This is the tradition's sharpest internal disagreement about the status of the manifest image.
- **With [[members/list]]:** List argues that intentional explanation has irreducible explanatory autonomy and that free will is real at the agential level. For an eliminativist, that autonomy is a fact about the theory we currently use, not about the world.
- **With [[members/papineau]]:** Papineau is a realist about phenomenal consciousness and an identity theorist; Churchland's 1985 paper treats qualia as states we currently describe badly and could learn to describe neuroscientifically. The two agree on identity at the end of inquiry but disagree about how much of current mental vocabulary survives.
- **With [[members/patricia-churchland]]:** The two have worked as a pair for decades. The tradition's reading is that Paul's emphasis falls on epistemology and representation theory, Patricia's on neurobiology and its implications for ethics, but this page does not claim a substantive doctrinal split between them. ## 4. What he offers against the conscious-realist rivals

- **Against [[traditions/kastrup/wiki|Kastrup]]:** Kastrup treats immediate acquaintance with experience as the most certain datum. Churchland's argument that introspection is theory-laden undercuts that claim: what seems given in experience is already conceptualised, and the concepts can be wrong. If the self-evident starting point is a theory, it can be revised like any other.
- **Against [[traditions/hoffman/wiki|Hoffman]]:** Hoffman's evolutionary argument holds that fitness-tuned perception does not track truth. Churchland's neurocomputational work offers a different picture: learned activation-space maps can be accurate in their structure (as in *Plato's Camera*), so evolution and learning do not by themselves cut perception off from the world. [UNVERIFIED: Churchland has not been located responding to Hoffman by name; this is the tradition's application of his framework.]
- **On [[traditions/friston/wiki|Friston]] and [[traditions/levin/wiki|Levin]]:** Predictive and neural-network models of cognition are welcome to an eliminativist, because they describe cognition without propositional attitudes. The tradition's point is that these models are physical computational accounts and give no support to the claim that consciousness is fundamental.

## 5. The weak point a fair rival would press

The standard objection is self-refutation: if there are no beliefs, then eliminativism cannot be believed or asserted as true. Churchland answers that the objection assumes the folk theory it is evaluating. But the debt is real, because after more than forty years no replacement vocabulary has displaced belief and desire in science, law or ordinary life. A MacIntyrean will also note that eliminativism threatens the vocabulary of practices, virtues and reasons on which any community, including a scientific one, depends.
