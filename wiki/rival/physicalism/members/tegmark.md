# Max Tegmark — Physicalism Member

*Part of the [[rival/physicalism/wiki|Physicalism tradition]]. Written from inside the tradition. This page states plainly where Tegmark fits the tradition and where he does not.*

## 1. Who he is and his role in the tradition

Max Tegmark is a professor of physics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, a cosmologist by training, and a co-founder of the Future of Life Institute, which works on risks from advanced AI. He belongs in this tradition on three counts. He is an Everettian, like [[members/carroll]]. He has argued that quantum effects are irrelevant to how brains work, which closes off a favourite route for mind-first theories. And he treats consciousness as a physical phenomenon: a property of matter arranged to process information in certain ways. But he is a boundary member. His mathematical universe hypothesis holds that physical reality *is* a mathematical structure, which is a form of Platonism. Ordinary physicalism takes physical things and laws as basic; Tegmark takes mathematics as basic and physics as one structure among many. The tradition includes him and records the tension rather than hiding it.

## 2. Core positions

1. **Quantum effects do not matter for neural computation.** Decoherence in the warm, wet brain happens far faster than neurons fire, so the brain can be treated as a classical system for the purposes of explaining thought. "Importance of Quantum Decoherence in Brain Processes," *Physical Review E* 61 (2000).
2. **The mathematical universe hypothesis (MUH).** External physical reality is a mathematical structure; mathematical existence is physical existence. Observers are self-aware substructures within such a structure. "Is 'the Theory of Everything' Merely the Ultimate Ensemble Theory?" *Annals of Physics* (1998; arXiv 1997); "The Mathematical Universe," *Foundations of Physics* (2008); *Our Mathematical Universe* (2014).
3. **The four-level multiverse.** Level I (regions beyond our horizon), Level II (other inflationary bubbles with different effective constants), Level III (Everettian branches), Level IV (other mathematical structures). "Parallel Universes," *Scientific American* (2003); *Our Mathematical Universe* (2014).
4. **Consciousness as a state of matter.** Consciousness may be understood as a phase of matter with particular information-processing properties (he coined "perceptronium" for it), studied with the tools of physics. "Consciousness as a State of Matter," *Chaos, Solitons & Fractals* 76 (2015).
5. **Substrate independence and AI.** What matters for mind is the pattern of information processing, not the material, so artificial systems could in principle be conscious; this makes AI safety a pressing practical question. *Life 3.0: Being Human in the Age of Artificial Intelligence* (2017).

## 3. Where he disagrees with fellow members, and the tension with physicalism

- **The central tension.** Physicalism, as [[members/carroll]] and [[members/papineau]] state it, says everything is fixed by physical facts. The MUH says physical facts are mathematical facts and that every consistent mathematical structure exists. If that is right, the base of reality is not physical in any ordinary sense, and our universe is one structure among all mathematical structures. A physicalist can read the MUH in one of two ways: as a strong form of structural realism about physics (which the tradition can live with), or as mathematical Platonism (which it cannot fully absorb). Tegmark himself describes it as a form of Platonism. The tradition records this as unresolved.
- **With [[members/carroll]]:** Both are Everettians. Carroll takes a physical quantum state and its law as fundamental and does not adopt the Level IV multiverse.
- **With [[members/weinberg]]:** Weinberg doubted that every interpretation of quantum mechanics, Everett included, was adequate, and he treated speculation beyond testable physics with caution. Level IV goes far beyond what Weinberg thought physics could check.
- **With [[members/seth]]:** Tegmark's substrate independence (*Life 3.0*) and his use of integrated-information ideas (2015) contrast with Seth's view that consciousness may depend on being a living, self-maintaining system, which makes conscious AI less likely than many suppose (Seth, "Conscious Artificial Intelligence and Biological Naturalism," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 2025).
- **With [[members/frankish]] and [[members/dennett]]:** Tegmark treats consciousness as a real state of matter to be characterised physically; the illusionists treat phenomenal consciousness as a misrepresentation. These are different research programmes inside the same tradition.

## 4. What he offers against the conscious-realist rivals

- **Against quantum-mind arguments generally, and [[traditions/kastrup/wiki|Kastrup]]'s use of measurement:** The 2000 decoherence paper is the tradition's main quantitative argument that quantum coherence plays no role in neural computation. Its target was Penrose and Hameroff, but it bears on any view that links consciousness to quantum effects in the brain.
- **Against [[traditions/hoffman/wiki|Hoffman]]:** Hoffman and Tegmark agree that spacetime may not be fundamental, and both look to mathematics for what lies beneath. They disagree on what the mathematics is about. For Hoffman it describes networks of conscious agents; for Tegmark observers are substructures *inside* a mathematical structure, and consciousness is something that particular substructures do. The tradition uses Tegmark to show that "spacetime is not fundamental" does not imply "mind is fundamental." The point bites harder now that the Hoffman-Prakash program's core is a theorem about Markov kernels and their traces (wiki §6.2). On Tegmark's view such a structure is real as mathematics and needs no conscious interpretation to be so.
- **On [[traditions/wolfram/wiki|Wolfram]]:** Both treat the universe as a computational or mathematical object; Tegmark's Computable Universe Hypothesis and Wolfram's ruliad are close relatives. The tradition treats this as an ally on method and a shared exposure on testability.

## 5. The weak point a fair rival would press

Testability. Critics have pressed several objections against the MUH: there may be no coherent way to assign probabilities across all mathematical structures; Gödelian incompleteness creates problems that Tegmark answers by restricting to computable structures; and an ensemble of causally disconnected universes is hard to test. A conscious realist can press a sharper point: if what exists is a mathematical structure, the physicalist has given up matter as the base. Then the dispute with idealism is no longer physical versus mental but mathematical versus mental. A Thomist will ask what makes a mathematical structure *exist* rather than merely be consistent, which is the question of esse.
