# Anil Seth — Physicalism Member (pragmatic, not dogmatic)

*Part of the [[rival/physicalism/wiki|Physicalism tradition]]. Written from inside the tradition. **Status note:** Seth does not present physicalism as settled. In a 2021 interview with* Interalia Magazine *he called physicalism his "preferred stance," which he cannot prove or disprove, and described his approach as pragmatic. In a 2021 Quanta interview he said he does not go all the way to idealism and keeps a broadly realist view, while admitting he cannot settle whether consciousness is substrate-dependent. This page does not claim more for him than that.*

## 1. Who he is and his role in the tradition

Anil Seth is Professor of Cognitive and Computational Neuroscience at the University of Sussex, where he directs the Sussex Centre for Consciousness Science, and co-directs the CIFAR Program on Brain, Mind, and Consciousness. He is the tradition's working consciousness scientist. Rather than asking how matter could possibly produce experience (the hard problem), he asks how to explain, predict and control particular properties of experience in terms of brain mechanisms, which he calls the "real problem." His central idea is that perception is the brain's best guess about the causes of its sensory signals, and that the most basic of these guesses concern the body's own internal state. That ties consciousness to life and self-regulation, which has led him to be sceptical that current AI systems are conscious.

## 2. Core positions

1. **The real problem.** Instead of tackling the hard problem head on, consciousness science should explain, predict and control the properties of experience; done well, this may dissolve the sense of a hard problem. "The Real Problem," *Aeon* (2016); *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness* (2021).
2. **Perception as controlled hallucination.** Perceptual experience is the content of the brain's top-down predictions about the causes of sensory input, kept in check by sensory prediction errors. *Being You* (2021).
3. **Interoceptive inference and the embodied self.** Emotions and the sense of self arise from predictive modelling of the body's internal state. "Interoceptive Inference, Emotion, and the Embodied Self," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 17 (2013); with Karl Friston, "Active Interoceptive Inference and the Emotional Brain," *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 371 (2016).
4. **The beast machine.** Selfhood is grounded in the predictive regulation that keeps a living body alive, so consciousness is closely tied to being a living organism. With Manos Tsakiris, "Being a Beast Machine: The Somatic Basis of Selfhood," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* (2018); *Being You* (2021).
5. **Scepticism about conscious AI.** Computation alone may not be sufficient for consciousness; if consciousness depends on properties of living systems, current AI is unlikely to be conscious. "Conscious Artificial Intelligence and Biological Naturalism," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* (2025); "The Mythology of Conscious AI," *Noema* (2025).
6. **Comparing theories fairly.** With Tim Bayne, he surveyed the main theories of consciousness (global workspace, higher-order, integrated information, predictive processing) and how they might be tested against each other. "Theories of Consciousness," *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* (2022).

## 3. Where he disagrees with fellow members

- **With [[members/tegmark]] and [[members/graziano]]:** Both treat consciousness as substrate-independent and so possible in suitably organised machines; Graziano has argued that the attention schema could be engineered. Seth's "biological naturalism" paper argues that consciousness may depend on properties of living systems. This is the tradition's main internal disagreement about AI.
- **With [[members/frankish]] and [[members/dennett]]:** Seth treats the properties of experience as real targets for explanation and does not adopt illusionism. The real-problem strategy is meant to make progress without having to decide whether phenomenal consciousness is an illusion.
- **With [[members/carroll]]:** Carroll argues physicalism from the completeness of physics; Seth holds it as a working stance justified by its scientific productivity. Seth's position is weaker but harder to refute, since it makes no claim the evidence has not earned.
- **With [[members/patricia-churchland]]:** They share the view that the hard problem should be approached through neuroscience, but Seth's emphasis on the body and on life gives more weight to the organism than to the brain alone.

## 4. What he offers against the conscious-realist rivals

- **Against [[traditions/kastrup/wiki|Kastrup]] and [[traditions/hoffman/wiki|Hoffman]]:** Seth's controlled-hallucination view agrees with the rivals on one point: perception is a construction, not a window. Hoffman uses that point to argue that the world behind perception is made of conscious agents; Kastrup uses it to argue that the world is mental. Seth uses it the other way: the construction is built by a living body to keep itself alive, and its contents are predictions about real physical causes. The tradition presents this as the physicalist reading of the shared insight that perception is constructed. Seth also explicitly declines to go "all the way" to idealism (Quanta interview, 2021). Against the current Hoffman-Prakash claim that trace blankets subsume Markov blankets, the tradition deploys the Friston-adjacent framework Seth works in. There the boundary between organism and world is a physical boundary that a living body maintains, not an observer's window by definition. Seth has not written on trace logic; this is the tradition's use of his work (wiki §6.2; PHYS-PRS-36).
- **On [[traditions/friston/wiki|Friston]]:** Seth co-wrote with Friston on active interoceptive inference (2016). The tradition reads the predictive-processing framework as a physical theory of how brains and bodies work. Using it as support for conscious realism, as some readings of Friston in this vault do, is a further step that Seth does not take.
- **On [[traditions/levin/wiki|Levin]]:** Seth's beast-machine view shares Levin's emphasis on self-maintaining living systems as the ground of selfhood. Seth draws a physicalist conclusion from it; Levin increasingly draws a conclusion about mind throughout nature. The tradition treats this as one of the closest points of contact between the two camps.

## 5. The weak point a fair rival would press

The real-problem strategy can look like postponement. Explaining, predicting and controlling the properties of experience is valuable, but a rival will say it explains the *correlates* and *contents* of experience, not why there is experience at all. Seth's answer is that the hard problem may dissolve as the real problem is solved, which is a bet, not a result. His own admission that he cannot prove physicalism means he cannot rule out the rivals, only argue that his programme is more productive. And his biological naturalism raises a question the tradition has not answered: if consciousness depends on being alive, what exactly about life matters, and is that "something" still captured by physics in the ordinary sense?
