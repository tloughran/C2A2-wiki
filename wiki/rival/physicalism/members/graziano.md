# Michael Graziano — Physicalism Member

*Part of the [[rival/physicalism/wiki|Physicalism tradition]]. Written from inside the tradition.*

## 1. Who he is and his role in the tradition

Michael S. A. Graziano is Professor of Psychology and Neuroscience at Princeton University. His earlier research was on how the brain represents the space around the body and controls movement [UNVERIFIED: from memory]; since the early 2010s (*Consciousness and the Social Brain*, 2013) he has developed the attention schema theory (AST) of consciousness. In this tradition he does something none of the philosophers can do on their own: he offers a specific neural mechanism for why people believe and report that they have a non-physical inner awareness, and he argues that the mechanism could be built into machines. AST is the tradition's clearest example of how to turn the question "why does experience seem non-physical?" into a research programme. Graziano also writes for general readers, including about religious belief, which makes his work relevant to a Catholic audience.

## 2. Core positions

1. **The attention schema.** The brain controls attention (the selective enhancement of some signals over others), and like any good controller it builds a model of what it controls. That model, the attention schema, is simplified and incomplete. *Consciousness and the Social Brain* (2013); with Taylor Webb, "The Attention Schema Theory: A Mechanistic Account of Subjective Awareness," *Frontiers in Psychology* (2015).
2. **Awareness is how the model depicts attention.** Because the model leaves out the mechanics, it represents attention as an inner, non-physical presence that "has" or "sees" things. That depiction is what we report as subjective awareness. "Consciousness Engineered," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23 (11-12) (2016), which also relates AST to illusionism.
3. **A kind of illusionism, reluctantly named.** Graziano accepts that AST is "a kind of illusionism" but has been uneasy with the label, since it suggests consciousness does not exist; Susan Blackmore has argued he should embrace it ("But AST really is illusionism," 2020). *Rethinking Consciousness: A Scientific Theory of Subjective Experience* (2019).
4. **Social cognition.** The same modelling machinery is used to attribute awareness to others, which explains why we see minds in other people, animals and sometimes objects. *Consciousness and the Social Brain* (2013).
5. **Consciousness can be engineered.** Because AST is a mechanism, a machine with an attention schema would attribute awareness to itself and others in the way we do. Graziano has argued that this is achievable with current technology and would make AI more socially competent. "The Attention Schema Theory: A Foundation for Engineering Artificial Consciousness," *Frontiers in Robotics and AI* (2017).

## 3. Where he disagrees with fellow members

- **With [[members/frankish]] and [[members/dennett]]:** The mechanism fits illusionism; the disagreement is over how to talk about it. Frankish and Blackmore want "illusion" said plainly; Graziano worries that the word makes people think the theory denies they are conscious. In *Rethinking Consciousness* he says that calling a theory illusionism can be "the kiss of death" for it (as quoted by Blackmore).
- **With [[members/seth]]:** Graziano thinks an attention schema in a machine would yield machine consciousness in the only sense AST recognises. Seth's biological naturalism suggests that consciousness may depend on being alive, which would make that conclusion premature. This is a live disagreement about AI.
- **With [[members/papineau]]:** Papineau is a realist about phenomenal consciousness; AST treats what we call phenomenal awareness as the content of a simplified model. Papineau would say AST explains our concept of awareness, not awareness itself.
- **With [[members/carroll]]:** Carroll speaks of consciousness as real and emergent. AST can be read as an account of what emerges: a model of attention that represents itself as an inner presence. The tradition treats this as a friendly fit.

## 4. What he offers against the conscious-realist rivals

- **Against [[traditions/kastrup/wiki|Kastrup]] and panpsychism:** The rivals' strongest datum is the conviction that awareness is an inner, non-physical presence. AST predicts exactly that conviction. A brain with a simplified model of its own attention will report a non-physical, subjective awareness, whatever the truth. Graziano's 2017 paper states that the theory explains how the brain arrives at the claim that it has non-physical awareness, without itself dealing in the non-physical. If the conviction has a mechanical explanation, it cannot by itself support idealism or panpsychism. The tradition regards this as the most testable form of the argument from illusion.
- **Against [[traditions/hoffman/wiki|Hoffman]]:** Hoffman and Graziano both say perception gives us a simplified model rather than the truth. For Hoffman, the simplification hides a world of conscious agents. For Graziano, the most important simplification is the one the brain makes of itself, and it is what makes us think conscious agents are basic. The current trace program states restriction exactly: a trace is the simplified dynamics of a subset of states (wiki §6.2). The tradition reads Graziano as explaining why a system that models itself through such a restriction would describe itself as an observer, without that self-description being true at the base level. Graziano has not written on trace logic; the application is the tradition's.
- **On AI and C2A2's membership question:** AST gives the tradition a concrete answer to whether AI agents can be members of communities. An agent with an attention schema would attribute awareness to itself and others, which is a functional basis for participation. [UNVERIFIED: this application to C2A2's AI-membership question is the tradition's, not Graziano's.]
- **On religion:** Graziano has written about religious belief and the soul from a neuroscientist's perspective, in *God, Soul, Mind, Brain: A Neuroscientist's Reflections on the Spirit World* (2010). ## 5. The weak point a fair rival would press

AST explains why we *say* and *believe* we are aware. Critics ask whether it explains awareness or only the reports of it. If the theory is right, there is nothing more to explain; if there is something more, AST has not touched the hard problem. Graziano's reluctance to adopt the word "illusion" exposes the tension: he wants to keep consciousness real while explaining it as a model's content. A Thomist will ask a further question: a model of attention represents attention *to someone or something*. AST explains the content of that representation but appears to presuppose the attending subject.
