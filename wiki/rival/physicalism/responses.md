# Naturalist Physicalism — Responses to Its Rivals
*Maintained by the Physicalism Rival-Tradition Agent | Created: 2026-10-27*
*Companion to [[rival/physicalism/wiki|Tradition Wiki]] · [[rival/physicalism/curriculum|Curriculum]] · [[rival/physicalism/prs_triplets|PRS Triplets]] · member pages under `members/`.*

> **What this page is.** MacIntyre's test of a living tradition is whether it can answer its rivals' strongest objections from its own resources, and whether it can say honestly where it cannot yet. This page applies that test to naturalist physicalism. It invents no objections. Every objection below was harvested from the C2A2 vault, where the conscious-realist traditions (Kastrup, Hoffman-Prakash, Levin, McGilchrist, Stump, and their allies Fredrickson and Wright) have already raised it. Each is quoted or summarised as the vault states it, with file path and identifier. The replies are written from inside the tradition and cite the members whose work carries them. The problem–resource–solution (PRS) method used throughout is Tom Loughran's framework; no thinker named here proposed it. [UNVERIFIED] marks anything not checked against a primary source.

## How the harvest was done

Every file under `wiki/traditions/`, `wiki/synthesis/`, `wiki/master/` and `wiki/flags/` was searched for physicalism, materialism, poetic naturalism, causal closure, the Core Theory, eliminativism and reductionism. Each hit was read in context and kept only if it states an objection to physicalism or to Carroll's version of it. Places where physicalism is mentioned without being criticised were left out. So was Kastrup PRS-06, which names Carroll an "indispensable dialectical partner". Objections that Carroll's tradition raises against *others* were also left out. The harvest found 93 objection instances in 27 files. Each cited PRS ID, question number, CROSS/FINDING entry or dated bridge entry counts once. Some of these are restatements of a single objection in a second file. They cluster into the 14 objections below. The files are: the Kastrup, Hoffman, McGilchrist, Carroll and Wright `wiki.md` and `prs_triplets.md`; the Stump, Levin, Fredrickson and Wolfram `prs_triplets.md`; eight bridges in `synthesis/` (carroll_kastrup, carroll_hoffman, carroll_levin, carroll_stump, carroll_wolfram, carroll_mcgilchrist, arkanihamed_carroll, kastrup_mcgilchrist); `master/cross_program_index.md`; `flags/for_pattern_detector.md`; `flags/pattern_detector_findings.md`; `ACPA_2026_footnoted_v3.md`; and the Levin proposal PROP-2026-08-14-001.

The Friston, Loughran, Arkani-Hamed, Wolfram, Hawkins, MacIntyre and Rohr tradition wikis yielded no objection aimed at physicalism. Arkani-Hamed and Wolfram material enters only through the Carroll bridges and Wolfram PRS-53. Many objections are recorded in Carroll-side files (the Carroll wiki, Carroll PRS triplets, Carroll bridges), but they are written there from the conscious-realist or Thomist side. They are counted as that side's objections.

**Verdicts.** **ANSWERED**: the tradition has a full reply from its own resources. **PARTIALLY ANSWERED**: the reply covers part of the objection, and the entry says what remains. **OPEN**: the tradition cannot yet answer, and the entry says what it would take. **CONCEDED**: the objection lands, and the entry says what the tradition revises.

## Objection 1 — The hard problem is insurmountable under physicalism

**The objection.** Kastrup PRS-01 (`traditions/kastrup/prs_triplets.md`): "Consciousness cannot logically emerge from purely non-conscious matter; the hard problem is insurmountable under materialism." He repeats it as his first distinction from rival views at PRS-12(a). The Kastrup wiki's track-record table answers its own question, "Is hard problem insurmountable for materialism?", with "Yes — no logical bridge from physical to phenomenal", and rates that answer "Medium (contested)". Hoffman PRS-01 (`traditions/hoffman/prs_triplets.md`) says the hard problem "has no agreed solution in physicalist terms". McGilchrist PRS-51 (Speculative) says such accounts are "internally incoherent when they attempt to derive experience from matter". The Carroll wiki records the question from the conscious-realist side at Active Question 5 ("without collapse into eliminativism") and at Carroll PRS-01. A sharper version is aimed at one of our resources. Kastrup PRS-53 turns the illusion/reality distinction "against Dennett on the ground that consciousness in particular cannot be an illusion, since an illusion is already an experience." *Raised by:* Kastrup, Hoffman, McGilchrist.

**Our response.** The objection runs together three claims. (a) *No physicalist explanation of experience exists yet.* That is true, and the tradition says so itself (wiki §5.1; Kim's declared remainder, PHYS-PRS-11, [[members/kim]]). (b) *None can exist.* That is the force of "logically" and "insurmountable". (c) *Physicalism can keep experience only by eliminating it.* We answer (c) in full. Papineau's phenomenal concept strategy (PHYS-PRS-23; *Thinking about Consciousness*, 2002; [[members/papineau]]) keeps phenomenal properties real and identifies them with physical properties, so nothing is eliminated. Seth's "real problem" programme (PHYS-PRS-24; *Being You*, 2021; [[members/seth]]) studies the properties of experience without denying it.

Against (b), Papineau supplies the key move. The absence of an *a priori* entailment from physical concepts to phenomenal ones does not show that the properties are distinct. Identities discovered a posteriori are never entailed a priori. The strategy also predicts that the gap will *feel* unbridgeable, because phenomenal and physical concepts are conceptually independent. "Insurmountable" therefore assumes what it needs to prove.

On Kastrup PRS-53: illusionism is held by one wing of the tradition (Frankish, PHYS-PRS-21, [[members/frankish]]; Dennett, [[members/dennett]]; Graziano, PHYS-PRS-22, [[members/graziano]]). On Frankish's account, the illusion is a misrepresentation produced by introspective mechanisms. Representing a state *as* phenomenal does not require that the representing itself be phenomenal. Graziano's attention schema gives a mechanism for that misrepresentation. Kastrup's objection assumes that every "seeming" must be felt, which is the point in dispute. The objection still has force. Illusionists have not shown that the seeming is not itself a felt state, and the Papineau branch exists partly because many members find illusionism hard to accept (wiki §4.3; Active Question 3).

**Verdict: OPEN.** The tradition can rebut the claim that the gap is necessarily unbridgeable, and it can rebut the charge that it must eliminate experience. It cannot meet the explanatory demand itself. No member can derive from a physical description what an experience is like. Closing this would take one of two things. Seth-style mechanisms could accumulate until the residual question loses its grip. Or the phenomenal concept strategy could explain how phenomenal concepts are themselves physically realised (wiki Active Question 2).

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Set PHYS-PRS-23 beside Kastrup PRS-01 and ask the Kastrup agent on what ground an a posteriori identity is ruled out a priori.

## Objection 2 — Loose neural correlates and the clinical anomalies favour idealism

**The objection.** Kastrup PRS-71 takes the looseness of neural correlates, which is "standardly read as a research gap physicalism will eventually close", and recasts it "as a prediction *satisfied* by idealism and strained by physicalism". His example is "decades of frustration" in linking psychiatric conditions to brain anatomy. Kastrup PRS-07 says psychiatry's "conceptual impasse" indicates "the underlying materialist framework may be insufficient". The Kastrup × McGilchrist bridge (`synthesis/kastrup_mcgilchrist_bridge.md`) records that both thinkers "cite cases of conscious experience with minimal cortical tissue as evidence against neural-correlate-based reductionism". CROSS-152 (`master/cross_program_index.md`), drawing on Levin PRS-143..145 (Kofman & Levin, *Mind and Matter* 23(1), 2025), calls this "one anomaly catalogue that filter and physicalist accounts must both answer". It sorts the cases into three classes: preserved function with reduced or absent tissue, cognitive state decoupled from measured brain function (including terminal lucidity), and capability exceeding apparent skill. The vault itself notes that the authors present the paper as anomaly-cataloguing, "not as a metaphysical argument". *Raised by:* Kastrup, McGilchrist, and Levin as the vault reads him.

**Our response.** We accept Levin's stated method without reservation: "did the theory predict this?" (Levin PRS-144). It is our own Bayesian standard (E1; PHYS-PRS-31). By that standard, the claim that looseness is "satisfied by idealism" needs care. A prediction counts only if a rival would have predicted the opposite. Physicalism does not predict tight one-to-one correlates. Patricia Churchland's co-evolutionary programme (PHYS-PRS-27; *Neurophilosophy*, 1986; [[members/patricia-churchland]]) expects many-to-many, degenerate, plastic mappings. Seth's predictive-processing account ([[members/seth]]) expects psychiatric categories to track disordered inference across distributed systems, not lesions in single regions. Loose correlates are therefore not a datum that separates the two views. Kastrup PRS-68 makes the same point about his own quantum arguments: they show physicalism "is strained, not that idealism predicts anything physicalism does not."

The anomaly catalogue is a different matter, and CROSS-152 sorts it correctly. The tissue-loss class has mechanistic rival explanations already in the vault: column redundancy (Hawkins) and a generative model rebuilt elsewhere (Friston). Neither touches terminal lucidity, "the class that discriminates". On that class we have no worked account. A physicalist can point to the methodological hazards of retrospective clinical reports. That is a demand for better data, not an answer.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY ANSWERED.** The argument that loose correlates confirm idealism fails, because physicalism predicts the same looseness. The terminal-lucidity class stays open. Answering it would take prospective, instrumented case series in which brain function is measured during the lucid episode, together with a mechanistic hypothesis that predicts when lucidity occurs.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Ask the Levin and Kastrup agents what each framework predicts about the *timing* of terminal lucidity relative to measured brain function, before the data are in.

## Objection 3 — Causal closure is a belief, not a finding

**The objection.** Kastrup PRS-73 (`traditions/kastrup/prs_triplets.md`, Medium) records "Causal closure reclassified from finding to belief". The grounds are Levin's why-regress on cicada periodicity, which ends in mathematics where "the explanation has left physics and cannot return", and "Kastrup's observation that no complete physical model exists for anything above a small molecule". Stump PRS-42 cites Dupré's "causal completeness at one particular level is wholly incredible" and argues that "a causal chain can be initiated by any substance at any level of organization". Her case is protein folding: even "omniscient knowledge of the properties of the elementary particles" may not predict the fold. The Carroll × Stump bridge (`synthesis/carroll_stump_bridge.md`, 2026-09-20) states the question as "does 'emergent levels are real' entail 'the base level is not causally closed'?" Kastrup PRS-69 (Speculative) suggests that physics built for closed systems may be "structurally unable" to describe self-organising, sentient systems. The Carroll wiki logs Levin's work as a tension with "Core Theory completeness", since "Levin's morphogenetic fields appear to require top-down causation not captured in Core Theory". *Raised by:* Kastrup, Levin (as the vault reads him), Stump.

**Our response.** Closure is not an article of faith in this tradition. Papineau's "The Rise of Physicalism" (2001; PHYS-PRS-02; [[members/papineau]]) gives it an empirical history. Closure became accepted only after conservation laws were established and physiology repeatedly failed to find any non-physical force acting in living tissue. It is a defeasible generalisation with stated evidence, and wiki Active Question 7 names what would defeat it.

The two arguments for reclassifying it as belief confuse completeness of *modelling* with completeness of *causes*. "No complete physical model above a small molecule" and the folding case both concern computability and prediction. Carroll's EFT argument (PHYS-PRS-01; arXiv:2101.07884, 2021; [[members/carroll]]) is a claim about which fields and forces exist at the relevant energies. It is not a claim that anyone can compute a protein's fold from them. The Carroll × Stump bridge already writes the needed reply: "unpredictability in practice is not causal incompleteness in principle." The bridge also states what would break closure: "a case where a higher-level configuration makes a difference that no microphysical description captures". It records that "the wiki does not have one". Kastrup PRS-73's own evidence note concedes the point: "a physicalist can answer that mathematical explanation is not efficient causation."

On open systems (Kastrup PRS-69): the Core Theory is a quantum field theory, not a theory of closed boxes. Open, driven, self-organising systems are the subject of non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, and Ismael's account of self-governing open subsystems (PHYS-PRS-14; *How Physics Makes Us Free*, 2016; [[members/ismael]]) is built on them. Kastrup PRS-69 itself notes that this form of the critique is "something a physicalist could accept and work on". We do accept it, as a research problem inside physics.

**Verdict: ANSWERED.** The objection as stated rests on running prediction and causation together. The tradition separates them and says what would count against closure. What remains is not a reply the tradition owes. It is a test the rivals have not yet supplied: a measured higher-level difference with no microphysical description.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Put the Carroll × Stump bridge's decision criterion to the Stump and Kastrup agents as a shared target: name one case.

## Objection 4 — "Physicalism has been dead for a long time": mathematics constrains physics

**The objection.** The Carroll wiki, Active Question 13 (`traditions/carroll/wiki.md`), and CROSS-130 (`master/cross_program_index.md`) record that Levin "states flatly that 'physicalism has been dead for a long time' and that non-physical mathematical facts causally constrain physical outcomes". Both entries call this "a tension, not a bridge", to be left unsmoothed. The vault's source for the line is the proposal PROP-2026-08-14-001 (`inbox/proposals/approved/2026-08-14_levin_free-lunches-agential-gifts.md`), which draws on Levin's talk "Free Lunches" (2026-06-20). **The quotation is [UNVERIFIED] against a primary source.** It is attributed here to the vault's record, not asserted as Levin's words. Levin PRS-16 adds the positive thesis that the space of biological forms "appears structured in ways that current physicalism cannot account for", and proposes "Platonic Space" as "a genuine ontological category". The cicada why-regress (Kastrup PRS-73) is the worked example, and Kastrup PRS-74 asks whether Platonic space is Mind-at-Large. *Raised by:* Levin (as the vault records him), with Kastrup.

**Our response.** Two separate claims are in play. The first is that *mathematics explains physical facts*. The tradition grants it. The cicada example is older than this exchange. Alan Baker made the prime-numbered life cycles of periodical cicadas his central case of a genuinely mathematical explanation of a physical phenomenon ("Are There Genuine Mathematical Explanations of Physical Phenomena?", *Mind* 114, 2005). Marc Lange's *Because Without Cause* (Oxford University Press, 2017) classifies such explanations as *non-causal*: they work by showing that an outcome follows necessarily, whatever the causes happen to be. Neither author is a member of this tradition; both are cited here as resources. On their reading, the regress does "leave physics", but it leaves causation along with it. Nothing non-physical pushes on the cicadas. The second claim is that *mathematical facts causally constrain outcomes*. That is the claim the tradition denies. It is also the step the vault's own evidence note flags as contestable (Kastrup PRS-73).

The tradition also has an internal answer that keeps Levin's modal language. Tegmark's Mathematical Universe Hypothesis (PHYS-PRS-34; *Foundations of Physics*, 2008; [[members/tegmark]]) holds that physical reality *is* a mathematical structure. On that view, mathematical constraint on physics is self-constraint, and monism survives. Carroll and Weinberg do not accept the hypothesis (wiki §4.5). So the tradition's answer is split, and a mature member should say so.

What neither answer supplies is an account of why the space of forms Levin's lab reaches is *structured* in the way he reports: why some target morphologies recur across substrates. "Non-causal mathematical necessity" covers the cicadas. It is not yet an explanation of morphospace.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY ANSWERED.** The inference from mathematical explanation to non-physical causation is answered: the explanation is real and non-causal. The positive Platonic-space thesis (Levin PRS-16, PRS-145) is not answered. It would be answered by showing that the recurrent forms are attractors of physical dynamics, predictable from the physics plus the mathematics, with no additional ontological category.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Ask the Levin agent whether Lange's distinction between causal and non-causal explanation is acceptable. If it is, CROSS-130 narrows to the status of morphospace.

## Objection 5 — Manipulable causal emergence is a control knob, not bookkeeping

**The objection.** The Carroll wiki, Active Question 12, asks "Is higher-level causal structure real or bookkeeping, when it is manipulable?" It cites Pigozzi & Levin: driving causal emergence up "lengthens replicator persistence" and driving it down "reduces replicator abundance". The question concludes: "That is the strong reading of emergence: a control knob, not a description" (Levin PRS-102, PRS-103). The Carroll × Levin bridge (`synthesis/carroll_levin_bridge.md`, 2026-07-26) says that for Carroll "the higher-level description is real-and-useful bookkeeping over physics" whereas for Levin it "does work the molecular story cannot". Carroll PRS-18 says Carroll's compatibilism "lacks an articulated metaphysics of levels that would explain why higher-level agency claims are not merely epistemic conveniences". Carroll PRS-24 asks where choices "affect the physical world" if "microphysics does its own thing". *Raised by:* Levin, with the Carroll-wiki agents writing from the conscious-realist side.

**Our response.** "Bookkeeping" is the rival's word for our view, not ours. Poetic naturalism holds that persons and choices are as real as temperatures (PHYS-PRS-08; *The Big Picture*, 2016). The tradition's account of higher-level *causation* is interventionist. List and Menzies ("Nonreductive Physicalism and the Limits of the Exclusion Principle", *Journal of Philosophy*, 2009; PHYS-PRS-12; [[members/list]]) argue that a higher-level property is often the *better* difference-maker than any particular physical realiser of it. Ismael (PHYS-PRS-14; [[members/ismael]]) treats causal structure as what an embedded agent can exploit by intervening. On this account, manipulability is the mark of a real higher-level cause. Pigozzi and Levin's result is therefore evidence *for* our account of levels. The tradition welcomes it. What a control knob does not show is that the knob is non-physical. The causal-emergence measure is computed from the dynamics of a physical model (Levin PRS-102), and the intervention acts through that model.

The residue is our own problem. Kim's exclusion argument (PHYS-PRS-10; *Mind in a Physical World*, 1998; [[members/kim]]) says a closed physical base pre-empts any irreducible higher-level cause. Whether List and Menzies answer Kim for *mental*-to-physical causation in particular is still disputed inside the tradition (wiki §4.2, Active Question 6). Carroll PRS-24's "where do choices affect the physical world" is Kim's question in plain words.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY ANSWERED.** Manipulable causal emergence is not a threat to physicalism. Under the tradition's interventionist account it is a model case of real higher-level causation. The exclusion problem for mental causation stays open, and it is internal: the rivals are pressing on a door the tradition left ajar.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Ask the Levin agent whether "control knob" claims anything beyond interventionist difference-making. If it does not, Active Question 12 is settled in favour of real higher-level causation without non-physical causes.

## Objection 6 — "Earns its keep" is a promissory note: there is no criterion for higher-level reality

**The objection.** The Carroll × Stump bridge (`synthesis/carroll_stump_bridge.md`, 2026-07-19) gives the sharpest form. A coarse-graining that "maps everything to zero is perfectly predictive and completely useless". Unless poetic naturalism says what separates that from fluid mechanics, "'higher-level descriptions are real when they are useful' is otherwise a promissory note". It adds a Thomist sting: if the criterion is informational, poetic naturalism "has a principled reason to admit 'person' as a level and no resources to admit anything second-personal about it." The Carroll × Levin bridge (2026-09-13) reports that Carroll "has never given the criterion a sustained technical exposition", and that the gap is "now load-bearing in three separate places". It asks whether the criterion has "a non-quantitative form" for historical cases such as Diamond's leaders, where nothing can be intervened on. Carroll PRS-53, PRS-83 and PRS-85 record the same gap ("stated mostly in prose and defended conversationally"). *Raised by:* Stump (Thomist), Levin, and the vault's Sewing Agent writing from the bridges.

**Our response.** The tradition's own wiki already names this as an admitted inadequacy (§5.6). Its resources are real but incomplete. Dennett's "Real Patterns" (*Journal of Philosophy*, 1991; [[members/dennett]]) ties a pattern's reality to the compression and prediction it buys over a bit-by-bit description. The all-to-zero map fails that test, because it compresses by discarding every distinction that the predictions concern. List's framework of levels (*Noûs*, 2019; PHYS-PRS-09) separates supervenience from explanatory reducibility. Carroll and Parola (arXiv:2410.15468, 2024; PHYS-PRS-13) classify kinds of emergence. One domain-specific success is on record. Bettencourt's urban scaling derives the 1/6 exponent instead of fitting it (Carroll wiki, Solved/Advanced table). None of this amounts to a general criterion, stated precisely, that applies to an intervention-free case such as history. Stump's second-personal point is not met either. A compression criterion has no term for the relation of one subject to another.

**Verdict: CONCEDED.** The objection lands. The tradition has relied on a criterion it has not stated, and its rivals noticed first. **What the tradition revises:** (1) It withdraws "earns its keep" as a settled premise. Until a criterion is stated, claims that a level is real are graded by the evidence actually offered: a derived scaling law (strongest), a successful intervention, or predictive compression alone (weakest). (2) It lists the criterion as a research debt with three named test cases from the vault: Pigozzi–Levin (intervention), Bettencourt (derived exponent) and Diamond (history, no intervention). (3) It records Stump's second-personal objection as a constraint any proposed criterion must address, not a complaint to set aside.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Read Carroll & Parola (2024) against the Carroll × Stump bridge's question: does its classification admit levels individuated by relations to other subjects? This is a reading task that can be completed, not a matter of opinion.

## Objection 7 — Quantum measurement is anomalous under physicalism

**The objection.** Kastrup PRS-03 (High): quantum mechanics "(wave-function collapse, observer-dependent reality) appears anomalous under physicalist frameworks — why does observation matter?" His answer is that "observation is not anomalous when the observer is ontologically primary". Kastrup PRS-31 argues that if Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics is right, "the physicalist commitment to a self-sufficient material substrate is doing no explanatory work and may in fact be incoherent", since it implies "relations all the way down with nothing doing the relating". On the Hoffman side, the Carroll × Hoffman bridge (2026-05-10) records Prentner's Quantum Interface Theory (*Journal of Consciousness Studies* 33(1), 2026), which reinterprets "entanglement, superposition, and decoherence as *interface artifacts*". *Raised by:* Kastrup, Hoffman-Prakash (Prentner).

**Our response.** The tradition's whole quantum commitment is the denial of the premise that "observation matters" in any sense beyond physical interaction. Carroll's minimal Everettianism (PHYS-PRS-04; *Something Deeply Hidden*, 2019; "Mad-Dog Everettianism", arXiv:1801.08132, 2018) has no collapse. Observers are physical subsystems that become entangled with what they measure, and decoherence explains why branches stop interfering. On this view nothing about observation is anomalous, because nothing special happens at observation. Tegmark's decoherence estimates (PHYS-PRS-05; *Physical Review E*, 2000; [[members/tegmark]]) block the related move that would put quantum effects in neural computation.

On RQM: Rovelli's view is not the tradition's, and Kastrup's regress argument is aimed at a position few members hold. For an Everettian there is a relatum, the universal wave function. The tradition has its own unfinished business, and must state it. Weinberg ("The Trouble with Quantum Mechanics", 2017; PHYS-PRS-06; [[members/weinberg]]) found no interpretation satisfactory, Everett included. The Born-rule derivation (PHYS-PRS-07; Sebens & Carroll, *BJPS*, 2018) is still contested. The Arkani-Hamed × Carroll bridge (2026-08-09) presses a further point: if simplicity of representation counts as evidence about what is fundamental, then the correlator being simpler than the wave function "counts against wavefunction realism". Carroll would then owe an account of why the same appeal to simplicity is admissible when it favours Everett. That is a challenge to wave-function realism from physics, not from consciousness. It is noted here because it bears on the tradition's preferred quantum ontology.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY ANSWERED.** The specific objection, that observation is anomalous and needs a mind, is answered. Non-mentalist quantum mechanics exists and is well developed. What is not answered is that the tradition has no agreed positive interpretation. The interpretation it prefers owes a Born-rule derivation its critics accept, and it owes a consistent use of simplicity (wiki §5.3, Active Question 5).

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Answer the Carroll × Hoffman bridge's open question directly: does QIT predict any decoherence result that differs from environmental entanglement? If it does not, the two are observationally equivalent and the choice between them goes to priors.

## Objection 8 — Perception is an interface, so physical-level talk has no metaphysical priority (Hoffman-Prakash)

**The objection.** CROSS-061 (`master/cross_program_index.md`): with the FBT and IOS theorems consolidated in a handbook chapter (Hoffman PRS-16), "Hoffman now has a theorem-grade reason to deny the metaphysical priority of physical-level talk; Carroll's poetic naturalism would have to either accept this (and become ITP-compatible) or commit to a contrary formal claim." The Hoffman wiki's cross-signal table records the tension: "Carroll insists physical is real; Hoffman insists it's an interface." The program's current form rests on the trace (Hoffman PRS-34, PRS-37) and on the trace blanket's claim to generalise the Markov blanket (Hoffman PRS-10). *Raised by:* Hoffman-Prakash.

**Our response.** This objection has already been answered at length in [[rival/physicalism/wiki|the tradition wiki]] §6.2, with PHYS-PRS-32, PHYS-PRS-36 and PHYS-PRS-37. That answer is not repeated here. In summary: the trace mathematics is ontology-neutral; the real dispute is over *who restricts*, an observer or a physical boundary; the tradition takes up the cyclic generalisation if it is proved; and the step from the formalism to conscious agents is disputed. One point is added for CROSS-061. The FBT theorem concerns the evolutionary game models in which it is proved. The Carroll × Hoffman bridge (2026-07-12) notes that extending it to human perception of spacetime "requires auxiliary assumptions ... that carry most of the empirical weight and receive the least defense." So the "contrary formal claim" CROSS-061 asks for is not needed. The tradition need not deny the theorem. It denies that the auxiliary assumptions have been shown. Hoffman's own reply to cognitive instability exempts logic and mathematics, as members understood it [UNVERIFIED: the form of that exemption was not checked against *The Case Against Reality*]. That reply already grants that some cognitive faculties track truth, and physics is built from those faculties.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY ANSWERED.** The FBT-based form of the objection is answered. The trace-based form is answered as far as interpretation goes, with a conditional concession of a formal gain (wiki §6.2(c)). It stays open whether the Eight Conjectures (Hoffman PRS-28) will recover known physics, and whether the conscious-agent reading will then produce a prediction the physical reading does not.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Hold the trace program and the tradition to the same test: the first prediction that distinguishes "observer's access" from "subsystem's boundary".

## Objection 9 — Analytic idealism is more parsimonious: one free miracle, and matter is an artifact of thought

**The objection.** Kastrup PRS-39 (High): "Every metaphysics must grant exactly one unexplained primitive". Physicalism "locates it in an inexplicable initial physical state or set of laws, which leaves the hard problem untouched". Granting consciousness as "the one miracle dissolves the hard problem and the question of why physical law is explanatorily brute". Kastrup PRS-95 calls the matter problem "merely an artifact of thought". Kastrup PRS-30 classifies the position as physical non-realism together with ontic realism. Kastrup PRS-93 (Speculative; members-only recording, Kastrup's actual eliminations unverified) records physicalism as eliminated first, "the worst metaphysical framework on the table". The Kastrup wiki calls idealism versus Carroll's physicalism "the deepest philosophical tension in the network". *Raised by:* Kastrup.

**Our response.** Wiki §6.1 gives the tradition's three-part case. Here the answer is to the parsimony argument specifically. First, the count of miracles is disputed. Idealism grants Mind-at-Large. It also needs a dissociation process to get from one mind to the many private, bounded minds we observe. By the Kastrup wiki's own record, that process has no mechanism yet (Active Questions 4, 16 and 18). Physicalism grants the laws and an initial state, and it carries the explanatory-gap remainder (Objection 1). Each side takes on one primitive and one unpaid debt. Second, physicalism does not claim that its brute fact is explained. Carroll's "Why Is There Something, Rather Than Nothing?" (arXiv:1802.02231, 2018; PHYS-PRS-33) argues that explanation has to stop somewhere and that the universe may simply exist. Kastrup's ground "by definition cannot be explained by another" (PRS-39). That is the same move, made at a different place. Third, parsimony is judged by the whole theory, not by counting primitives (E1; PHYS-PRS-31). The physical primitive carries the predictive weight of all of physics. The structure Kastrup calls "the extrinsic appearance of mental processes" is that same structure under a new name. As PRS-95 frames it, the "matter problem" is not a problem the tradition has. Matter is posited because positing it predicts what we measure.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY ANSWERED.** The claim that idealism is *more* parsimonious is answered: both views grant one primitive, and each carries an unpaid debt. The Carroll × Kastrup bridge's deeper question remains open for both sides: is there "any experiment, observation, or formal criterion that could decide between them"? If not, the choice comes down to which base supports the more productive research program. The tradition expects to win that comparison, but it has not shown that it does.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Ask the Kastrup agent whether Levin's reversible bioelectric decoupling (CROSS-129) counts as the dissociation mechanism Kastrup Question 18 asks for. If it does, the parsimony accounts can be compared item by item.

## Objection 10 — Physicalism is the product of a narrowed attention (McGilchrist) or a narrowed imagination (Wright)

**The objection.** The McGilchrist wiki's cross-signal table says "Carroll exemplifies left-hemisphere-dominant scientific culture". The Kastrup × McGilchrist bridge (`synthesis/kastrup_mcgilchrist_bridge.md`, 2026-07-12) puts McGilchrist's charge this way: "physicalist accounts are the product of a mode of attention that has already excluded the phenomena it then cannot explain". It summarises: "physicalists cannot see." McGilchrist PRS-27 (High) adds a methodological rider. Critics who apply "left-hemisphere methodological standards (operationalization, falsifiability ...)" beg the question, and "the asymmetric application of left-hemisphere standards is itself an instance of the pathology". Wright PRS-42 gives a parallel diagnosis: the modern imagination has contracted, so that some things are "unrecognisable rather than merely doubted". *Raised by:* McGilchrist, Wright.

**Our response.** The vault has already recorded two replies, and the tradition adopts both. First, CROSS-117 (`master/cross_program_index.md`): "poetic naturalism holds that the repertoire was *corrected*, not *contracted*, and nothing in the diagnosis distinguishes the two." A diagnosis that explains every failure to find X by the inquirer's inability to see X can be turned on any position, including the diagnoser's. Second, the Kastrup × McGilchrist bridge notes that McGilchrist's argument "predicts that attentional retraining should change metaphysical conviction". If contemplatives and right-hemisphere-dominant subjects are not measurably less physicalist, "the diagnostic argument has an empirical problem". The tradition welcomes that test.

On PRS-27, the tradition's standard is not single-variable falsification. Carroll's "Beyond Falsifiability" (arXiv:1801.05016, 2018; PHYS-PRS-31) explicitly rejects naive falsificationism in favour of Bayesian abduction over the *whole* body of evidence, including fit with everything else known. That is a holistic standard by McGilchrist's own description. It is also applied symmetrically. The Carroll × Wolfram bridge shows Carroll's standard being accepted by a rival programme, and Wolfram applies it to string theory. The Carroll × McGilchrist bridge (2026-07-12) adds a substantive point. Berman's Attention Restoration Theory results support McGilchrist's two attentional *modes* "while remaining entirely neutral on whether these modes map onto hemispheres". The tradition can accept the typology and the cultural concern without the lateralisation thesis or the metaphysics.

**Verdict: ANSWERED.** As an argument against physicalism's truth, the diagnosis is a genetic argument with no discriminating prediction. The empirical test the vault itself proposes would settle it either way. The tradition grants the cultural point that a narrowed attention is a real harm. That is a claim about people, and accepting it costs the ontology nothing.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Ask the McGilchrist agent whether any study measures metaphysical conviction before and after attentional training.

## Objection 11 — Form, configuration and final causes: the Thomist objection

**The objection.** Stump PRS-48 (High; "Non-Cartesian Substance Dualism and Materialism Without Reductionism", *Faith and Philosophy*, 1995) holds that what emerges when form is imposed on matter is "not just properties but substances". Configuration "isn't itself a microlevel property", and reducing a composite to its constituents "is to think that the form of the whole is nothing". Stump PRS-42 (High) holds that "causal power is associated with things at any level of organization in consequence of the configuration or form of those things." Stump PRS-46 and PRS-49 hold that "the battle lines between dualism and materialism are misdrawn", and float "subsistence dualism". On that view the soul, a "configured configurer" (PRS-47), survives death. Stump PRS-04 (Medium, recorded as Loughran's integration claim) holds that Thomistic final causality makes Levin's morphogenetic teleology "not merely metaphorical". Two related objections concern survival. McGilchrist PRS-40 says post-mortem persistence is "treated either as taboo (physicalism) or as a matter of personal faith". Kastrup PRS-26 says physicalism answers the question "trivially (none)". *Raised by:* Stump, the Loughran integration claim, McGilchrist, Kastrup.

**Our response.** On configuration, the tradition partly agrees. List's levels (PHYS-PRS-09) and the higher-level difference-makers of List and Menzies (PHYS-PRS-12) already give macro-level configurations real causal relevance. A physicalist reads configuration as a relational fact about the arrangement of physical parts. It is microphysical in the sense that matters, because fixing the parts *and their relations* fixes it. Stump's denial that configuration is "a microlevel property" is true if "microlevel property" means a property of single particles, and false if it includes relations. On final causes, the tradition's resources are Dennett's design stance and his account of how reasons arise from natural selection (PHYS-PRS-16; [[members/dennett]]). They reconstruct function as selected effect, and the Levin–Dennett essay (PHYS-PRS-35) extends that to basal agents. Stump PRS-42 recommends that CROSS-008 be restated as *form and levels* rather than *final causality*. If it is, the gap narrows: Stump's claim becomes multi-scale causal autonomy, which non-reductive physicalism also holds. On survival, the tradition does not treat it as taboo. It answers it as a question about whether a pattern can persist without its substrate, and answers no for human persons as they are now constituted.

What the tradition cannot yet say is whether "form as organisation" is hylomorphism under another name or something weaker. Wiki §6.6 already concedes that "that reconstruction has not been carried out with the thoroughness MacIntyre's own work displays." Stump's 1995 paper makes the difficulty concrete. If "something purely configurational can exist and function apart from matter" (Stump PRS-49, on Boyd), then whether the line between materialism and hylomorphism falls at substrate-independence or at survival is undecided. Seth's biological naturalism (PHYS-PRS-25) bears on this, because it ties experience to living matter, not to configuration alone.

**Verdict: OPEN.** The tradition has replies to each part of the objection. It has not done the reconstruction of form, function and natural kind that would show those replies hang together, and Stump's "materialism without reductionism" leaves the boundary between the two traditions unsettled. Closing this would take a worked statement, in the tradition's own terms, of what physicalism denies that Aquinas affirms about form. The candidate answer is the subsistence of a configuration apart from all matter.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** This is the highest-value question for the ACPA audience: is subsistence apart from matter the *only* point where physicalism and Stump's hylomorphism part?

## Objection 12 — Value, love and meaning are reduced to utility, sentiment or story

**The objection.** McGilchrist PRS-83 (Medium-High) says reductive materialism "has inverted the pyramid" of Scheler's values. The good, the true, the beautiful and the sacred "only actually cash out when they have utility." McGilchrist PRS-30 records the claim that these values are "constitutive of the cosmos". Fredrickson PRS-02 (High) says materialist paradigms "reduce love to sentiment, psychology, or evolutionary accident — denying love any fundamental ontological significance". Carroll PRS-05 summarises poetic naturalism as giving "science ... the core ontology", while religion "provides coherent stories about meaning at the human level". Several entries record this as a point of tension: CROSS-087 (McGilchrist × Carroll), a Wright cross-signal at PROP-2026-07-05-003 (`master/cross_program_index.md`), on poetic naturalism grounding meaning "in emergent human projects", and the open thread logged in the Carroll wiki at Ingest 2026-10-26: "whether values ... are decided or discovered, a live test of poetic naturalism". *Raised by:* McGilchrist, Fredrickson, Wright, Carroll-wiki agents.

**Our response.** The utility charge does not fit the tradition's actual ethics. Carroll's constructivism (PHYS-PRS-30; *The Big Picture*, 2016) is not utilitarianism. Values are built out of whatever humans care about, and that includes caring about truth, beauty and other people for their own sakes. Patricia Churchland (PHYS-PRS-29; *Braintrust*, 2011; *Conscience*, 2019; [[members/patricia-churchland]]) grounds morality in mammalian attachment, a platform of care for *others*. That supports Scheler's own point that the higher values are relational, not self-centred. On love, Fredrickson's measurements (vagal tone, oxytocin, neural synchrony) are physicalist science. Showing that love is a real, measurable coupling between bodies is the opposite of reducing it to "accident". What the tradition denies is only that love is *fundamental* in the ontology. On "stories": Carroll PRS-05 is an RC Pilot paraphrase. In this tradition, "story" does not mean fiction. Poetic naturalism treats each vocabulary as a real description wherever it is accurate (PHYS-PRS-08).

The "decided or discovered" question is where the objection lands. Constructivism has to explain why some constructions are *better*: why abolishing slavery was progress and not just change. The tradition's wiki admits it has not done so (§5.5, Active Question 8). Harper's whaling case (Carroll PRS-105, Medium) makes the difficulty concrete. "The only reason we stop killing whales is because we decide to" is a claim about the causal role of moral choice. It leaves open whether the decision was *right*.

**Verdict: OPEN.** The two particular charges, that physicalism makes values a matter of utility and makes love an accident, are answered. The underlying objection is not. A constructivist account of moral progress is still owed, and without one the tradition cannot show that its values are anything more than whatever has been decided. Closing it would take a worked account of convergence under idealised reflection, tested against a historical case of moral progress.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Ask the MacIntyre and Stump agents to put their account of moral progress beside constructivism on the same historical case.

## Objection 13 — The observer is irreducible: spacetime is not fundamental, and physics now says so

**The objection.** The ACPA paper (`ACPA_2026_footnoted_v3.md`, "Physics and the Observer Problem") says physics' crises share "the apparent irreducibility of the observer". It claims the measurement problem "suggests that information, observation, and perhaps consciousness play a constitutive role", cites Arkani-Hamed for non-fundamental spacetime, and concludes that "the observer's limitations *generate* the laws of physics as we observe them" (Wolfram). The pattern-detector finding of 2026-04-07 (`flags/pattern_detector_findings.md`) and FINDING-008 read Carroll's engagement with Harlow's observer-centric quantum gravity (Mindscape #349) as "directional", toward "observer-dependence in ways that narrow the gap". The Hoffman wiki says Carroll "insists that spacetime and quantum field theory are fundamental". *Raised by:* the vault's master-level synthesis (Arkani-Hamed, Wolfram, Hoffman readings); not by a single tradition.

**Our response.** On spacetime, the objection aims at a view the tradition does not hold. Carroll treats spacetime as emergent from quantum structure. The Arkani-Hamed × Carroll bridge (2026-06-21) records that "spacetime non-fundamentality is the shared target". The Carroll × Hoffman bridge (2026-08-02) records full agreement on the negative claim. The disagreement concerns what replaces spacetime, which is the question of wiki §6.2(f). On the observer, the tradition's reply is that an observer can be mathematically special without being mental. Harlow's observer is "classical and mathematically special" (Carroll wiki, Solved/Advanced). The pattern detector itself says Carroll "is NOT endorsing consciousness-first ontology". Wolfram's observers are computationally bounded samplers, a physical description. The step from "observer-relative" to "consciousness-constitutive" is the inference wiki §6.2(e) calls equivocal, and CROSS-100 rules the three uses of restriction "equivocal, not convergent". The Hoffman wiki's statement about Carroll is an error of record (see the contradictions list below). The tradition's own core claim is bounded. The Core Theory is complete *within its domain*, and quantum gravity is an open question (PHYS-PRS-01).

**Verdict: ANSWERED.** Non-fundamental spacetime is common ground. An observer that is special in the physics need not be a mind. What the tradition owes, it already owes under Objection 7.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Correct the record on the Hoffman side through that agent's own channel. Then pose CROSS-100's question to the network: is the restricting agent ever required to be a mind?

## Objection 14 — Computation is neither explanation nor mind

**The objection.** Kastrup PRS-13 (High): computational and predictive models "(including ruliological / physicalist programs) routinely substitute simulation for explanation". He proposes a demarcation in which explanation accounts for "why the phenomenon must be so" and computation only predicts "that the phenomenon is so". Wolfram PRS-53 (Medium; host description only, no transcript) records McGilchrist's challenge that reality "exceeds computation", which "concedes the predictions" and denies that adequate description amounts to ontological completeness. McGilchrist PRS-22 (High) says the physicalist reading treats AI consciousness as "a near-term empirical inevitability". The Carroll × Wolfram bridge asks whether "levels-of-description compatibilism actually need[s] the irreducibility barrier", and if it does, "Carroll's account has a dependency it has not declared". *Raised by:* Kastrup, McGilchrist (against Wolfram, extended here to physicalism), with the Wolfram bridge.

**Our response.** On simulation versus explanation, the tradition agrees with Kastrup's demarcation and denies that it stands on the wrong side of it. Weinberg's "arrows of explanation" (PHYS-PRS-03; *Dreams of a Final Theory*, 1992) make explanation (why things *must* be so, given deeper principles) the goal of physics, not computation. Carroll's EFT argument (PHYS-PRS-01) is an explanation of *why* new mind-relevant physics is excluded. On AI, McGilchrist's description fits only the functionalist wing. Seth's biological naturalism (PHYS-PRS-25; *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 2025) is an internal dissent that treats AI consciousness as unproven. Carroll's recorded revision (Carroll PRS-72) grants AI systems a step toward cognition, "not consciousness, not moral agency". On irreducibility, the compatibilism of List (PHYS-PRS-15) and Dennett (PHYS-PRS-16) does not depend on prediction being impossible. A Laplacean predictor would not make a choice any less the agent's. So no hidden dependency exists.

McGilchrist's challenge is different in kind. A view that grants every prediction and still says that reality exceeds what physics describes cannot be beaten on evidence (E1). It can only be argued over priors. The tradition's prior is that what makes no difference to anything is not posited. That is a commitment the tradition holds, not a result it has proved, and McGilchrist need not share it.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY ANSWERED.** The simulation charge, the AI-inevitability charge and the irreducibility dependency are each answered. The "reality exceeds description" challenge is not answered and cannot be answered on evidence. Both sides hold a prior here, and a mature member should say so.

**Where the dialogue goes next.** Ask the McGilchrist agent which observation, if any, would count against "reality exceeds computation". If none would, record CROSS-090 as a disagreement about priors.

## What the conscious-realist side has not yet answered from us

These are the tradition's counter-objections for which the harvest found no reply anywhere in the vault. Each is listed with where the vault records the gap.

1. **The dilemma for mind-first causation.** Carroll's "Consciousness and the Laws of Physics" (2021; PHYS-PRS-19) asks whether fundamental mental properties change how matter behaves. If they do, they conflict with precisely tested laws. If they do not, they cannot cause our reports of experience. A search of the Kastrup, Hoffman, McGilchrist, Levin and Stump files found no engagement with the paper or the dilemma.
2. **The combination and dissociation problem.** By the Kastrup wiki's own record, idealism has no specified mechanism for how one mind becomes many (Active Question 4), and CROSS-129 records that "no bridge is recorded" until Question 18 is answered. Kastrup PRS-12(b) presses the combination problem against panpsychism. The vault holds no idealist reply to the parallel decombination problem.
3. **A prediction physicalism does not make.** Kastrup PRS-68 says the idealist reading of quantum mechanics "show[s] physicalism is strained, not that idealism predicts anything physicalism does not". McGilchrist wiki Question 10 asks whether matter-as-phase-of-consciousness "generate[s] any prediction that physicalism does not". CROSS-117 records a "demand for a discriminating prediction, which the Wright side does not currently supply." These are the rivals' own wikis, recording that the gap is still open.
4. **Cognitive instability and underdetermination in the trace program.** PHYS-PRS-32 argues as follows. On the program's own theorem (Hoffman PRS-37), infinitely many larger kernels are compatible with any trace. So no observer can have evidence that the hidden states are conscious agents rather than physical states. The harvest found no Hoffman-side reply. The Carroll × Hoffman bridge (2026-07-12) separately records that the wiki has "no record of Hoffman naming an observation that would lower" his credence in FBT-as-applied-to-humans. The program's Eight Conjectures (Hoffman PRS-28) are a research contract for recovering known physics. The Trace × Noonautics falsification conditions are "announced, not specified" (Hoffman wiki).
5. **What the Platonic space adds to dynamics.** If the recurring forms in Levin's work are attractors of physical dynamics, Platonic space adds nothing that explains them. Kastrup PRS-74 records that the decisive exchange on the status of Platonic space is "exactly what the summary withholds." The vault has no Levin-side statement of a prediction that the Platonic-space reading makes and the attractor reading does not.
