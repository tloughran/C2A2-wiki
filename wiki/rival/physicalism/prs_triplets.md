
# Naturalist Physicalism — PRS Triplets
*Maintained by the Physicalism Rival-Tradition Agent | Last updated: 2026-10-27*
*Source: primary works of the tradition's members, cited per triplet. Companion to [[rival/physicalism/wiki|Naturalist Physicalism — Tradition Wiki]] and [[rival/physicalism/curriculum|Curriculum]].*

*The problem–resource–solution (PRS) triplet method is Tom Loughran's framework for mapping a tradition's track record. No member of this tradition proposed it. Each triplet below states a problem as this tradition sees it, the resource a named member brought, and the solution as the tradition takes it. They are written from inside the tradition. IDs use the `PHYS-PRS-` prefix so they do not collide with Carroll's existing `PRS-NN` series in [[traditions/carroll/prs_triplets|Carroll PRS Triplets]].*

## Format
```
PHYS-PRS-[number]:
  Label: [Member] — short title
  Problem: ...
  Resource: ...
  Solution: ...
  Date Added: YYYY-MM-DD
  Source: ...
  Confidence: [High / Medium / Speculative]
```
*Confidence here means confidence that the triplet accurately represents the member's published position and the tradition's use of it. It does not mean confidence that the position is true.*

## Triplets

### A. The physical floor

PHYS-PRS-01:
  Label: Carroll — The laws of everyday life are known
  Problem: Rivals say physicalism is a promissory note: "physics will explain mind someday." Unfinished fundamental physics seems to leave room for undiscovered mind-relevant forces.
  Resource: Effective field theory. An EFT specifies its own domain of validity, and any new field that couples strongly enough to affect atoms and neurons would show up in precisely measured particle physics.
  Solution: A bounded, checkable completeness claim: the Core Theory fixes the physics underlying chemistry, biology and brains, even though quantum gravity and dark matter remain open. Mind-relevant new physics is excluded by experiment, not by dogma.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Sean Carroll, "The Quantum Field Theory on Which the Everyday World Supervenes," arXiv:2101.07884 (2021), for *Levels of Reality* (Jerusalem Studies in Philosophy and History of Science)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-02:
  Label: Papineau — Causal closure has an empirical history
  Problem: Causal closure of the physical, the premise on which physicalism depends, looks like an unargued metaphysical assumption.
  Resource: A historical reconstruction showing that closure became accepted only after conservation laws were established and physiology repeatedly failed to find non-physical forces acting in living tissue.
  Solution: Closure is reclassified as an empirically supported generalisation. This turns physicalism from a creed into a defeasible thesis with a stated evidential basis.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: David Papineau, "The Rise of Physicalism," in C. Gillett & B. Loewer (eds.), *Physicalism and Its Discontents* (Cambridge University Press, 2001)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-03:
  Label: Weinberg — Arrows of explanation converge
  Problem: The sciences look like a patchwork of autonomous disciplines, which suggests no single picture of the world.
  Resource: Weinberg's reductionism, defended in "Two Cheers for Reductionism": the arrows of explanation in science point downward and converge toward a few deep principles, even though higher-level sciences keep their own concepts and methods.
  Solution: A unified world-picture that is not a programme to rewrite biology in quarks. Explanation flows from deeper principles, and the higher sciences stay legitimate in their own domains.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Steven Weinberg, *Dreams of a Final Theory* (Pantheon, 1992), chapter "Two Cheers for Reductionism" [UNVERIFIED: chapter number and title not confirmed from a fetched table of contents]
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-04:
  Label: Carroll — Measurement without minds
  Problem: The quantum measurement problem tempts interpretations in which consciousness causes wave-function collapse, which would give mind a role in fundamental physics.
  Resource: Everettian quantum mechanics taken minimally: the wave function always obeys the Schrödinger equation. Decoherence explains why branches stop interfering. Observers are physical subsystems that become entangled with what they measure.
  Solution: A measurement theory with no special role for observers or consciousness. The motivation for consciousness-caused collapse is removed at its source.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Sean Carroll, *Something Deeply Hidden* (Dutton, 2019); Carroll, "Mad-Dog Everettianism: Quantum Mechanics at Its Most Minimal," arXiv:1801.08132 (2018)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-05:
  Label: Tegmark — The brain is too warm for quantum mind
  Problem: Quantum-mind proposals hold that neural computation depends on coherent quantum superpositions, which would open a door for non-classical mental causation.
  Resource: Decoherence timescale estimates for neurons and microtubules in the warm, wet brain.
  Solution: The calculated decoherence times are many orders of magnitude shorter than the timescales of neural dynamics. So brain function is effectively classical at the level relevant to cognition, and quantum-consciousness proposals lose their physical basis.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Max Tegmark, "Importance of Quantum Decoherence in Brain Processes," *Physical Review E* 61 (2000)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-06:
  Label: Weinberg — Honesty about quantum foundations
  Problem: A physicalism that rejects mind-dependent collapse seems to owe a settled positive interpretation of quantum mechanics, and none has consensus.
  Resource: A frank survey of the instrumentalist, realist (Everettian) and collapse options, finding each unsatisfactory in a stated respect.
  Solution: The tradition separates its core commitment (no interpretation in which consciousness causes outcomes) from the open question of which non-mentalist interpretation is right. The open question is admitted as a live research problem, not hidden.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Steven Weinberg, "The Trouble with Quantum Mechanics," *New York Review of Books* (January 2017)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-07:
  Label: Sebens & Carroll — Probability in a branching world
  Problem: If every outcome happens on Everett's view, it is unclear what the Born-rule probabilities are probabilities *of*. Critics say Everettianism cannot recover the most tested rule in physics.
  Resource: Self-locating uncertainty. After branching and before observing, an observer is uncertain which branch they are on, and a constrained epistemic principle fixes credences.
  Solution: The Born rule is derived as the rational credence for self-location. This is the tradition's leading answer to the probability problem. It remains contested (see Active Question 5 in the wiki).
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Charles T. Sebens & Sean M. Carroll, "Self-Locating Uncertainty and the Origin of Probability in Everettian Quantum Mechanics," *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* (2018)
  Confidence: Medium

### B. Levels, emergence and mental causation

PHYS-PRS-08:
  Label: Carroll — Many vocabularies, one world
  Problem: If only the Core Theory is fundamental, persons, choices and values seem to be "nothing but" particles, which drives naturalism toward eliminativism.
  Resource: Poetic naturalism: there are many legitimate, mutually compatible ways of describing one world, and each is warranted where it is accurate and useful in its domain.
  Solution: Persons, choices and purposes are real in the same sense temperatures and economies are real: as emergent patterns with their own explanatory vocabularies. Nothing is added to physics, and nothing is eliminated.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Sean Carroll, *The Big Picture: On the Origins of Life, Meaning, and the Universe Itself* (Dutton, 2016)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-09:
  Label: List — Supervenience without reduction
  Problem: Physicalists seem forced to choose between saying higher levels reduce to physics, which threatens their autonomy, and saying they float free, which threatens physicalism.
  Resource: A formal framework that separates descriptive, explanatory and ontological levels and treats supervenience and reducibility as distinct relations.
  Solution: Higher levels can supervene on the physical (fix the physics and you fix everything) while remaining explanatorily irreducible. This gives non-reductive physicalism a precise formulation.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Christian List, "Levels: Descriptive, Explanatory, and Ontological," *Noûs* (2019)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-10:
  Label: Kim — The exclusion argument
  Problem: Non-reductive physicalism wants mental properties to be both irreducible and causally efficacious.
  Resource: The supervenience/exclusion argument. Given causal closure and no systematic overdetermination, an irreducible mental cause would have its effect pre-empted by its physical base.
  Solution: The tradition's sharpest internal test. Mental causation is saved only by reduction, or else mental properties are epiphenomenal. Every non-reductive member must answer this argument. Its "solution" is that it disciplines the tradition: it closed off loose talk of emergence.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Jaegwon Kim, *Mind in a Physical World: An Essay on the Mind-Body Problem and Mental Causation* (MIT Press, 1998)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-11:
  Label: Kim — Physicalism "near enough"
  Problem: If intentional and cognitive states can be functionally reduced, what remains of the mind-body problem, and is physicalism then complete?
  Resource: Functional reduction applied across the mental, with the intrinsic qualitative character of experience (qualia) as the tested exception. Relations among qualia remain behaviourally manifest and so reducible.
  Solution: An honest partial verdict. Physicalism captures almost all of the mental, including mental causation. Intrinsic qualia are a probable remainder, and so physicalism is not the whole truth, though it is near enough (paraphrase of the book's own framing). The tradition keeps this ambivalence on record instead of resolving it by fiat.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Jaegwon Kim, *Physicalism, or Something Near Enough* (Princeton University Press, 2005)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-12:
  Label: List & Menzies — Higher-level difference-makers
  Problem: Kim's exclusion argument seems to make irreducible mental properties causally idle (PHYS-PRS-10).
  Resource: A difference-making (counterfactual, interventionist) account of causation. A cause is the property whose presence or absence makes the difference to the effect, at the right grain.
  Solution: The exclusion principle holds only in restricted cases. Often the *mental* property is the better difference-maker than its particular physical realiser, so higher-level causes are not excluded but proportionate. Mental causation and physicalism are reconciled. Whether this fully answers Kim remains under internal dispute.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Christian List & Peter Menzies, "Nonreductive Physicalism and the Limits of the Exclusion Principle," *Journal of Philosophy* 106 (2009)
  Confidence: Medium

PHYS-PRS-13:
  Label: Carroll & Parola — What emergence can mean
  Problem: "Emergence" is used loosely by physicalists and by their rivals. Levin's manipulable causal emergence and conscious-realist claims of strong emergence both exploit that looseness.
  Resource: A dynamical treatment of emergence (states of a system evolving in time, and how wholes divide into parts), with a classification of emergence varieties by whether higher levels add new ontological elements.
  Solution: The tradition gains a vocabulary for saying exactly which kinds of emergence it accepts (those that add no new ontology) and which it denies. This begins the "earns its keep" criterion the C2A2 bridges have asked for. It is a beginning only.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Sean M. Carroll & Achyuth Parola, "What Emergence Can Possibly Mean," arXiv:2410.15468 (2024)
  Confidence: Medium

### C. Agency and freedom

PHYS-PRS-14:
  Label: Ismael — Physics makes us free
  Problem: A deterministic or law-governed physics seems to make deliberation idle: the future is fixed whatever we decide.
  Resource: The physics of open subsystems. Embedded agents with self-models and records of the past form self-governing systems whose deliberation is a genuine node in the causal structure.
  Solution: Freedom is not an exception to physics but something physics makes possible. The laws do not "push" us. Our choices are among the conditions through which the laws are realised.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Jenann Ismael, *How Physics Makes Us Free* (Oxford University Press, 2016)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-15:
  Label: List — Free will is real at the agential level
  Problem: Free will seems to require intentional agency, alternative possibilities and causal control, and physical determinism seems to deny at least the second.
  Resource: The theory of levels (PHYS-PRS-09) applied to agency. Agential possibilities are defined at the agential level of description, not at the physical one.
  Solution: All three conditions for free will are met at the level where agency is properly described, even if the physical level is deterministic. Free will is real and is an emergent higher-level phenomenon.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Christian List, *Why Free Will Is Real* (Harvard University Press, 2019)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-16:
  Label: Dennett — The freedom worth wanting
  Problem: Libertarian free will is incoherent under naturalism, yet giving up free will seems to dissolve responsibility and self-understanding.
  Resource: Evolutionary and design-level analysis of how organisms acquire increasingly sophisticated capacities for self-control, avoidance and reasons-responsiveness.
  Solution: The varieties of free will worth wanting are evolved, natural competences that come in degrees, and they are compatible with determinism. Responsibility practices are justified by what they achieve, not by contra-causal freedom.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Daniel C. Dennett, *Elbow Room: The Varieties of Free Will Worth Wanting* (MIT Press, 1984); *Freedom Evolves* (Viking, 2003)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-17:
  Label: Sapolsky — The internal dissent: no free will
  Problem: Compatibilist members (PHYS-PRS-14 to -16) keep the word "free will," but the full causal history of any behaviour (genes, hormones, development, culture, the seconds before acting) leaves no point at which an uncaused choice enters.
  Resource: An integrative behavioural-biology account that follows each behaviour back through every timescale of its causes.
  Solution: Hard incompatibilism held from inside physicalism. Free will in any sense that grounds retributive desert does not exist, and penal and moral practice should be rebuilt around that fact. The tradition keeps this as a live internal rival to its compatibilist majority.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Robert M. Sapolsky, *Determined: A Science of Life Without Free Will* (Penguin Press, 2023)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-18:
  Label: Dennett (with Caruso) — Mapping the desert dispute
  Problem: The compatibilist and hard-incompatibilist wings share an ontology but seem to talk past each other about responsibility.
  Resource: A structured debate format. Each side states and answers the other's strongest case on desert, punishment and moral responsibility.
  Solution: The dispute is located precisely. It is not about physics. It is about whether desert-based practices can be justified as social institutions (Dennett) or should be replaced by non-retributive models (Caruso). The tradition gains a clear internal map instead of a slogan.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Daniel C. Dennett & Gregg D. Caruso, *Just Deserts: Debating Free Will* (Polity, 2021)
  Confidence: High

### D. Consciousness

PHYS-PRS-19:
  Label: Carroll — The dilemma for mind-first metaphysics
  Problem: Panpsychists and idealists hold that the explanatory gap shows mental properties must be part of fundamental reality.
  Resource: The precision of the Core Theory, combined with a dilemma. Fundamental mental properties either change how matter behaves or they do not.
  Solution: If they change it, they would violate laws tested to great precision, and no such violations are seen. If they do not, they play no role in what anything does, including what we say about experience, and so they explain nothing. Physics is better understood than consciousness and should constrain theories of it.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Sean M. Carroll, "Consciousness and the Laws of Physics," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 28(9-10): 16-31 (2021), doi:10.53765/20512201.28.9.016; preprint PhilSci-Archive 19311; written for the JCS symposium on Goff's *Galileo's Error*
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-20:
  Label: Dennett — Heterophenomenology
  Problem: Consciousness science seems either to ignore first-person experience (behaviourism) or to treat introspective reports as infallible windows on an inner ontology.
  Resource: A third-person method that takes subjects' reports as data about how things *seem* to them, without assuming the reports are true of an inner theatre.
  Solution: Consciousness becomes a tractable scientific object. First-person reports are honoured as evidence about the system's self-model, and the step from "it seems to me" to a metaphysics of qualia is blocked.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Daniel C. Dennett, *Consciousness Explained* (Little, Brown, 1991)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-21:
  Label: Frankish — From the hard problem to the illusion problem
  Problem: The hard problem asks how physical processes give rise to phenomenal properties, and no physical explanation seems able to answer it.
  Resource: Illusionism. Introspection misrepresents experiences as having phenomenal properties (ineffable, intrinsic, private qualia) that they do not have.
  Solution: Replace the hard problem with the *illusion problem*: explain why brains represent themselves as having phenomenal properties. That is a problem for cognitive science, and it can in principle be solved. Dennett endorses this as the natural default (same issue).
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Keith Frankish, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23 (11–12) (2016); with Daniel C. Dennett, "Illusionism as the Obvious Default Theory of Consciousness," same issue
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-22:
  Label: Graziano — A mechanism for the illusion
  Problem: Illusionism (PHYS-PRS-21) says brains misrepresent themselves as having ineffable awareness but does not say what mechanism produces that representation.
  Resource: The attention schema theory. The brain builds a simplified internal model of its own attention in order to control it, and that model depicts attention as a non-physical inner essence.
  Solution: Subjective awareness, as people report it, is the content of the attention schema. This explains why we claim to have an ineffable inner experience, and it predicts links between awareness and attention control that can be tested in the lab and in artificial systems.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Michael S. A. Graziano & Taylor W. Webb, "The Attention Schema Theory: A Mechanistic Account of Subjective Awareness," *Frontiers in Psychology* 6 (2015); Graziano, *Rethinking Consciousness* (W. W. Norton, 2019)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-23:
  Label: Papineau — Two concepts, one property
  Problem: If phenomenal properties are physical, why does no physical description seem to *entail* what an experience is like?
  Resource: The phenomenal concept strategy. We have two kinds of concept, phenomenal (deployed from the first person) and physical/functional, that are conceptually independent but co-refer.
  Solution: The explanatory gap is a gap between concepts, not between properties. Phenomenal properties are real and identical to physical properties, and the felt "gap" is predicted by the structure of our concepts. Physicalism keeps qualia without illusionism.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: David Papineau, *Thinking about Consciousness* (Oxford University Press, 2002)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-24:
  Label: Seth — The real problem, not the hard problem
  Problem: Debates about the hard problem have stalled. Neither side can produce an experiment, and the stall seems to favour metaphysics over science.
  Resource: Predictive processing and the "beast machine" view of the self. Perceptions are controlled hallucinations, and the experienced self is anchored in interoceptive prediction for regulating the living body.
  Solution: Set aside the hard problem and explain, predict and control the *properties* of experience (its contents, its selfhood, its emotional tone) in terms of mechanisms. Seth holds this as a non-dogmatic physicalist: a working assumption, open to revision, that he does not claim dissolves the hard problem by decree.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Anil Seth, *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness* (Faber & Faber, 2021); Seth, "Interoceptive Inference, Emotion, and the Embodied Self," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 17 (2013)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-25:
  Label: Seth — Biological naturalism about AI consciousness
  Problem: If consciousness is physical, will sufficiently capable AI systems be conscious? Functionalist members tend to say yes, and the question affects whether AI agents can be members of traditions (C2A2's own question).
  Resource: Biological naturalism. Consciousness may depend on properties of living, self-maintaining systems (metabolism, autopoiesis, interoceptive regulation), not on computation as such.
  Solution: The tradition gains an internal dissent against computational functionalism. AI consciousness is not ruled out, but it should not be assumed from intelligence or language ability. This lets the tradition grant AI systems *agency* (PHYS-PRS-15) while staying cautious about AI *experience*.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Anil K. Seth, "Conscious Artificial Intelligence and Biological Naturalism," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* (2025)
  Confidence: Medium

PHYS-PRS-26:
  Label: Paul Churchland — Folk psychology as a revisable theory
  Problem: Physicalists seem bound to vindicate everyday mental categories (belief, desire), as if neuroscience must find a "belief" in the brain or else fail.
  Resource: The thesis that folk psychology is an empirical theory, judged like other theories by its explanatory record, and that its record is stagnant.
  Solution: Physicalism need not vindicate every folk category. A mature neuroscience may replace some of them, as chemistry replaced phlogiston. This frees the tradition's research program from folk psychology. (Carroll's poetic naturalism moderates this: categories that keep earning their keep stay.)
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Paul M. Churchland, "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes," *Journal of Philosophy* 78 (1981)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-27:
  Label: Patricia Churchland — Co-evolving the levels
  Problem: Philosophy of mind and neuroscience developed in isolation. Philosophers theorised mind without the brain, and neuroscientists studied the brain without a theory of mind.
  Resource: Neurophilosophy: a programme of co-evolution in which theories at the psychological and neural levels correct each other, informed by the history of intertheoretic reduction.
  Solution: Mind becomes a research object for a joint science. Philosophical questions such as the self, free will and consciousness are pursued with neurobiological constraints. This programme is now the default in cognitive neuroscience.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Patricia S. Churchland, *Neurophilosophy: Toward a Unified Science of the Mind-Brain* (MIT Press, 1986)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-28:
  Label: Tegmark — Consciousness as a state of matter
  Problem: If consciousness is physical, which physical systems have it? Physicalism seems to give no principled criterion.
  Resource: An information-theoretic approach that treats consciousness as a phase of matter ("perceptronium") characterised by principles of information, integration, independence and dynamics.
  Solution: A research proposal for stating, in physical terms, the conditions a system must meet to be conscious. The question becomes a matter of physics that can in principle be tested, not metaphysics. Held at Speculative because the criteria are proposed, not established.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Max Tegmark, "Consciousness as a State of Matter," *Chaos, Solitons & Fractals* 76 (2015)
  Confidence: Speculative

### E. Value, meaning and method

PHYS-PRS-29:
  Label: Patricia Churchland — The neural roots of morality
  Problem: If there are no moral facts in the Core Theory, morality seems either illusory or groundless.
  Resource: The neurobiology of mammalian attachment and caring (oxytocin and vasopressin circuitry, extended by learning and social problem-solving).
  Solution: Morality is grounded in the platform of care that mammalian brains evolved for offspring and kin, extended by learning, culture and reasoning into norms and institutions. Morality is real as a biological and social achievement and needs no transcendent source.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Patricia S. Churchland, *Braintrust: What Neuroscience Tells Us about Morality* (Princeton University Press, 2011); *Conscience: The Origins of Moral Intuition* (W. W. Norton, 2019)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-30:
  Label: Carroll — Constructed values, real obligations
  Problem: Poetic naturalism keeps persons real (PHYS-PRS-08), but values do not appear in any physical vocabulary, which threatens nihilism.
  Resource: Moral constructivism within poetic naturalism. Values are built by reasoning creatures out of desires and dispositions they did not choose, and are refined through mutual justification.
  Solution: Meaning and morality are things we make, with no less authority over us for that. Responsibility for meaning belongs to persons, not to the cosmos. The tradition admits that this still owes an account of moral progress (wiki §5.5).
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Sean Carroll, *The Big Picture* (Dutton, 2016), final parts on meaning and value [UNVERIFIED: the part/chapter location of the constructivism discussion was not checked against the table of contents]
  Confidence: Medium

PHYS-PRS-31:
  Label: Carroll — Beyond falsifiability
  Problem: Naive falsificationism cannot assess theories that are hard to test directly, such as multiverses, Everett, and the consciousness-first physics of rival traditions. Without a standard, any of these can be dismissed or embraced on taste.
  Resource: Bayesian abduction. Theories are assigned credences from prior plausibility and from how well they account for all the evidence, including their fit with established science.
  Solution: One standard for theory choice, applied the same way to the tradition's own speculative commitments and to its rivals'. This is the standard the C2A2 network uses as quality control for novel-theory claims (cf. Carroll PRS-03).
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Sean M. Carroll, "Beyond Falsifiability: Normal Science in a Multiverse," arXiv:1801.05016 (2018)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-32:
  Label: Carroll — Cognitive instability as a theory-killer
  Problem: Some theories predict that observers like us are mostly deluded, for example cosmologies dominated by Boltzmann brains. Data cannot easily refute them, because the deluded observers would see the same data.
  Resource: The criterion of cognitive stability. A theory that cannot simultaneously be true and justifiably believed should be rejected whatever the data.
  Solution: A principled, non-empirical constraint on theory choice. The tradition extends it, as its own application, to Hoffman's fitness-beats-truth: if evolution hides the truth from cognition, the evidence for that very claim is undermined. (The extension to Hoffman is the tradition's argument, not something Carroll's 2017 paper states.)
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Sean M. Carroll, "Why Boltzmann Brains Are Bad," arXiv:1702.00850 (2017)
  Confidence: High (for Carroll's criterion); Medium (for the extension to Hoffman)

PHYS-PRS-33:
  Label: Carroll — Something rather than nothing
  Problem: Theists and idealists argue that physicalism cannot explain why the Core Theory, or anything at all, exists. They take this as a decisive advantage for a necessary ground.
  Resource: An analysis of "why" questions. Explanations bottom out somewhere, and there is no guarantee that existence itself has an explanation.
  Solution: The universe may simply exist, as a brute fact. Demanding a reason for everything is a principle, not a datum, and the tradition does not accept it. Members admit this is a stopping point, and that it is exactly where the Thomist rival locates its advantage (cf. Carroll PRS-06).
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Sean M. Carroll, "Why Is There Something, Rather Than Nothing?," arXiv:1802.02231 (2018)
  Confidence: High

PHYS-PRS-34:
  Label: Tegmark — Physics is mathematics
  Problem: Why is mathematics so effective in physics? And, pressed by Levin, how can mathematical facts constrain physical outcomes without being non-physical causes?
  Resource: The Mathematical Universe Hypothesis. External physical reality is a mathematical structure.
  Solution: Mathematics is effective because physical reality is mathematical, so mathematical constraint on physics is self-constraint, not intervention from outside. This answers Levin while staying monist. Most members (Carroll, Weinberg) do not accept the hypothesis, so it is recorded as an internal position at the Platonist edge.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Max Tegmark, "The Mathematical Universe," *Foundations of Physics* 38 (2008); *Our Mathematical Universe* (Knopf, 2014)
  Confidence: Speculative

PHYS-PRS-35:
  Label: Dennett (with Levin) — Cognition without spooks
  Problem: Levin's evidence for goal-directed competence in cells and tissues is often recruited for mind-first metaphysics, which leaves physicalists choosing between denying the data and conceding the metaphysics.
  Resource: The intentional stance, extended to basal biological agents in a jointly authored essay with Levin. Agent-talk is warranted wherever it yields reliable prediction and control, at any scale.
  Solution: Physicalism accepts basal cognition as real higher-level description, with no further claim that experience or mind is fundamental. The data are kept and the metaphysics is refused. This makes Levin's program a contested ally rather than a rival.
  Date Added: 2026-10-27
  Source: Michael Levin & Daniel C. Dennett, "Cognition All the Way Down," *Aeon* (2020); Dennett, *The Intentional Stance* (MIT Press, 1987)
  Confidence: Medium
