## Research Program Overview
**Name:** Naturalist Physicalism, in its contemporary *poetic-naturalist* form
**Centre:** Sean Carroll ([[members/carroll]])
**Core claim:** There is one world. It is made of whatever the Core Theory describes: the quantum field theory of the Standard Model, plus general relativity in the weak-gravity regime. It evolves by impersonal laws, and the laws governing everyday life are known. Persons, purposes, reasons, values and conscious experience are real as higher-level patterns in that world, not as additions to it.
**Paradigm:** Core Theory completeness within its domain · causal closure of the physical · emergence as compatible coarse-grained description · non-mentalist (for most members, Everettian) quantum mechanics · Bayesian abduction as the logic of theory choice · compatibilism about agency · constructivism about value

## 1. Self-account: what this tradition is

Naturalist physicalism is the inheritance of the scientific revolution taken as a metaphysics, not only as a method. Physics, chemistry, biology, neuroscience and the sciences of collectives are the most successful explanatory practice humans have built. Members hold that these sciences have converged on a single picture of the world, and that the picture should be taken seriously as an account of what there is. The tradition does not claim that physics tells us everything worth knowing. It claims that whatever else is true must be compatible with what physics has established, and that nothing enters the causal order from outside it.

Its present centre of gravity is what Carroll calls *poetic naturalism* (*The Big Picture*, 2016). "Naturalism" names the ontology: one world, governed by patterns of physical law, with no separate realm of spirit or mind acting on it. "Poetic" names the epistemology of levels. There are many legitimate ways of talking about that one world: particles, fluids, cells, organisms, persons, nations. Each vocabulary is warranted where it gives accurate and useful descriptions within its domain. Persons and choices are not illusions on this view. They are as real as tables and temperatures, which is to say real as emergent patterns.

The tradition sees its own history as a lineage that has repeatedly won by subtraction. Vital forces, caloric, the luminiferous aether and Cartesian interaction were each posited to fill an explanatory gap. In each case better physics, chemistry or biology closed the gap without them. Members know this is an induction, not a proof. They also know their rivals read the same history as a list of what physicalism has *not yet* explained. That disagreement is the live one, and this page tries to state it fairly from the physicalist side.

## 2. First principles

### 2.1 Ontology: the Core Theory, causal closure, and levels

**(O1) Core Theory completeness within its domain.** The Core Theory consists of quantum field theory for matter and the non-gravitational forces, plus general relativity treated as an effective field theory. It has been tested across every energy, length and time scale relevant to chemistry, biology and brains. Carroll's argument in "The Quantum Field Theory on Which the Everyday World Supervenes" (arXiv:2101.07884, 2021, for the volume *Levels of Reality*) rests on how effective field theory works. An EFT states its own domain of validity. Any new field or force strong enough to affect atoms and neurons would have to couple to ordinary particles. Such a coupling would show up in particles and processes we already measure precisely, and it does not. So the laws underlying everyday life are known. This is a bounded claim. It does not say physics is finished: dark matter, quantum gravity and the cosmological constant remain open. It says those open questions are not where brains and choices live.

**(O2) Causal closure of the physical.** Every physical effect that has a sufficient cause has a sufficient physical cause. Papineau's "The Rise of Physicalism" (2001, in Gillett & Loewer, eds., *Physicalism and Its Discontents*) traces how this premise moved from metaphysical speculation to settled background. Conservation laws, and the failure of physiology to find any non-physical force acting in living tissue, did the work. Closure is the premise that makes physicalism a thesis and not a mood.

**(O3) Emergence as compatible coarse-graining.** Higher-level entities and laws are real. They are not "strongly emergent" (new fundamental causal powers that override the micro-dynamics). Carroll and Parola give a dynamical account of emergence, built on states of a system evolving in time, and classify its varieties by whether the higher level adds new ontological elements ("What Emergence Can Possibly Mean", arXiv:2410.15468, 2024). The tradition's default is the variety that adds none. On that reading, a good higher-level theory is one whose variables evolve, to a useful approximation, without the microscopic details. *(This criterion is the tradition's working gloss, not wording verified from the paper's body text.)* List adds the main distinction ("Levels: Descriptive, Explanatory, and Ontological", *Noûs*, 2019). Supervenience of the higher on the lower does not imply that the higher can be explained in, or reduced to, the lower vocabulary.

**(O4) Non-mentalist quantum mechanics.** Quantum theory does not give observers or minds any special dynamical role. For Carroll and Tegmark the preferred reading is Everett's. The wave function obeys the Schrödinger equation always. "Collapse" is decoherence plus the branching of the observer's state (Carroll, *Something Deeply Hidden*, 2019; Carroll, "Mad-Dog Everettianism: Quantum Mechanics at Its Most Minimal", arXiv:1801.08132, 2018). Not every member is Everettian. Weinberg, in "The Trouble with Quantum Mechanics" (*New York Review of Books*, January 2017), found every available interpretation unsatisfactory, Everett included. What the tradition shares is negative: no interpretation that makes consciousness cause physical outcomes is acceptable.

### 2.2 Epistemology

**(E1) Bayesian abduction.** We hold theories with degrees of credence. We update them by how well they account for the total evidence and by their prior plausibility, which simplicity and fit with background knowledge inform. Carroll's "Beyond Falsifiability: Normal Science in a Multiverse" (arXiv:1801.05016, 2018) argues that this, not naive falsificationism, is how working science actually judges theories that are hard to test directly. The same standard, applied the same way to every rival, is the tradition's chief methodological contribution to the C2A2 network ([[traditions/carroll/wiki|Carroll wiki]], PRS-03).

**(E2) The cognitive-stability constraint.** A theory that predicts that our own beliefs about it are probably unreliable undermines itself. Carroll's "Why Boltzmann Brains Are Bad" (arXiv:1702.00850, 2017) makes this a working criterion in cosmology. Such theories, he writes, "cannot simultaneously be true and justifiably believed." The tradition uses the same principle, deliberately, against some of its rivals (see §6).

**(E3) Third-person tractability, first-person seriousness.** First-person reports are data. They are data *about* how a system models itself, gathered by the method Dennett calls heterophenomenology (*Consciousness Explained*, 1991). They are not infallible readings of an inner ontology. Most of the tradition's internal disagreement is about how far to push this principle.

### 2.3 Anthropology: persons, agency, consciousness

**(A1) Persons are organisms that model themselves.** A person is a living system of extraordinary complexity, together with the self-model it maintains. The self is a real pattern. Seth's *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness* (Faber & Faber, 2021) treats the experienced self as a controlled, predictive model anchored in the regulation of the body's interior. His earlier paper "Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self" (*Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 2013) laid the groundwork. Graziano, from a different direction, treats awareness as the brain's simplified model of its own attention (*Rethinking Consciousness*, W. W. Norton, 2019; Graziano & Webb, "The attention schema theory: a mechanistic account of subjective awareness", *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2015).

**(A2) Agency is real at the level where it is described.** Choices are real causes in the way temperatures and economies are real causes. Ismael's *How Physics Makes Us Free* (Oxford University Press, 2016) argues that the physics of open subsystems, with their records and self-models, is what makes deliberation effective rather than idle. List's *Why Free Will Is Real* (Harvard University Press, 2019) argues that intentional agency, alternative possibilities and causal control are all real at the agential level, even if the physical level is deterministic. Sapolsky's *Determined: A Science of Life Without Free Will* (Penguin Press, 2023) dissents sharply: once every cause is traced, no room is left for the kind of responsibility that would justify retribution. This is a family quarrel inside a shared ontology (§4).

**(A3) Consciousness is a natural phenomenon to be explained, not a primitive to be presupposed.** Members disagree about what the explanation will look like. They agree that it will not require new fundamental physics or a mental substrate. Carroll's "Consciousness and the Laws of Physics" (preprint, 2021, written for a *Journal of Consciousness Studies* symposium on Goff's *Galileo's Error*) gives the argument in its sharpest form. We understand physics much better than we understand consciousness. Any mental property that changed physical behaviour would have to modify laws we have tested exhaustively. Any mental property that changed nothing would explain nothing. *(Published as Carroll, "Consciousness and the Laws of Physics," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 28(9-10): 16-31, 2021.)*

### 2.4 Ethics and value under poetic naturalism

**(V1) Values are constructed, not discovered, and are no less binding for that.** On poetic naturalism there are no moral facts written into the Core Theory. Morality is something creatures like us build out of the desires and dispositions evolution and culture gave us, and refine by reasoning together. *The Big Picture* (2016) calls this constructivism. Patricia Churchland supplies the neurobiological spine. *Braintrust: What Neuroscience Tells Us about Morality* (Princeton University Press, 2011) and *Conscience: The Origins of Moral Intuition* (W. W. Norton, 2019) trace moral care to the mammalian circuitry of attachment, extended by learning and social practice.

**(V2) Meaning is made, at the level of persons.** The universe is not indifferent in any sense that should frighten us. It is simply not the kind of thing that has attitudes. Meaning belongs to the level of description where purposes live. A mature member says this without grief. It is a recognition of where the responsibility for meaning lies.

**(V3) Practical upshot.** Responsibility is retained but reformed. Dennett and Caruso's exchange in *Just Deserts: Debating Free Will* (Polity, 2021) maps the axis. Dennett keeps desert-based practices as justified social institutions. Caruso (an ally outside this roster), like Sapolsky, argues for dropping retributive desert. Both positions are physicalist. The tradition treats the question as open and politically consequential.

## 3. Carroll's place at the centre, and why

Carroll is not the tradition's founder. Its classical figures are Hobbes, La Mettrie, Laplace and Darwin, and its twentieth-century analytic shape comes from Smart, Armstrong, Lewis, Quine and the Churchlands. He sits at its centre today for four reasons.

1. **He joins the tradition's two halves.** The physicists ([[members/weinberg]], [[members/tegmark]]) supply the ontology. The philosophers ([[members/dennett]], [[members/list]], [[members/papineau]], [[members/ismael]]) supply the account of levels, agency and mind. Carroll works professionally in both. *The Big Picture* (2016) is the first book-length statement that runs from effective field theory to ethics in one argument.
2. **He states the ontological floor precisely.** The EFT argument for the completeness of everyday physics (2021) turns "physics will eventually explain everything" into a bounded, checkable claim. That claim is the tradition's best answer to the charge that physicalism is a promissory note.
3. **He is an anti-eliminativist physicalist.** Poetic naturalism keeps persons, choices and values as real. This puts Carroll between the Churchlands' revisionism and List's non-reductive physicalism. It makes him the member most able to talk across the internal quarrels of §4.
4. **He is the tradition's ambassador to its rivals.** On Mindscape he has interviewed Goff on panpsychism (episode 71 [UNVERIFIED: episode number from a third-party podcast index]) and List on free will and levels (episode 354, per the vault's [[synthesis/carroll_kastrup_bridge|Carroll × Kastrup bridge]]). The tradition's habit of hearing its rivals at length, in their own words, before answering them is largely his habit.

## 4. Internal disagreements: a tradition that argues with itself

A living tradition, MacIntyre says, is an argument extended through time about the goods that constitute it. This one has at least five standing arguments.

**4.1 Eliminate, reduce, or keep? (the Churchlands vs. List vs. Carroll).** Paul Churchland's "Eliminative Materialism and the Propositional Attitudes" (*Journal of Philosophy*, 1981) argued that folk psychology is a theory, probably a false one, and that a mature neuroscience may replace "belief" and "desire" the way chemistry replaced "phlogiston." Patricia Churchland's *Neurophilosophy* (MIT Press, 1986) set out the program of co-evolving the two levels. List and Menzies answer from the other side ("Nonreductive Physicalism and the Limits of the Exclusion Principle", *Journal of Philosophy*, 2009). Higher-level properties can be the *better* difference-makers, so intentional explanation can be both physically grounded and irreplaceable. Carroll stands between them. Higher-level vocabularies earn their keep by accuracy and usefulness. Some (phlogiston, perhaps parts of folk psychology) will fail and be dropped. Others (persons, choices) are among the most successful descriptions we have.

**4.2 The exclusion problem (Kim).** Jaegwon Kim gave the tradition its sharpest internal critique. *Mind in a Physical World* (MIT Press, 1998) argued as follows. Grant causal closure, and grant that physical effects are not systematically overdetermined. Then a mental property that is not reducible to a physical one has no causal work left to do. The non-reductive physicalist must choose between reduction and epiphenomenalism. List and Menzies (2009), and Carroll's levels-relative causation, are attempts to escape that dilemma. The tradition has not agreed on whether they succeed. Kim's own late position matters here, and members should report it accurately. In *Physicalism, or Something Near Enough* (Princeton University Press, 2005) he concluded that intentional and cognitive properties can be functionally reduced, but the intrinsic qualitative character of experience probably cannot. Physicalism is therefore not the whole truth, only "near enough." Kim died in 2019 a physicalist with a declared remainder. The tradition counts that honesty as one of its assets.

**4.3 Is phenomenal consciousness real, illusory, or a misdescribed real thing? (Frankish and Dennett vs. Seth vs. Papineau).** Frankish's "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness" (*Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 2016, target article for a special issue later published as a book by Imprint Academic) holds the following. Experiences have no phenomenal properties in the philosopher's sense of ineffable, intrinsic qualia. The real explanandum is the *illusion problem*: why the brain represents itself as having them. Dennett's reply in the same issue, "Illusionism as the Obvious Default Theory of Consciousness" (2016), endorses the position as the natural home of his *Consciousness Explained* (1991). Graziano's attention-schema theory gives the illusion a mechanism.

Papineau represents a different branch: a-posteriori identity. *Thinking about Consciousness* (Oxford University Press, 2002) argues that phenomenal properties are real and *are* physical properties. The sense of a gap comes from our having two kinds of concept, phenomenal and physical, for one referent. This is the *phenomenal concept strategy*.

Seth refuses both labels. In *Being You* (2021) he approaches consciousness explicitly as a physicalist and a scientist (paraphrase; reference summaries describe the book this way). He sets aside the hard problem in favour of the "real problem" of explaining, predicting and controlling the properties of experience by their neural mechanisms. In "Conscious artificial intelligence and biological naturalism" (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 2025) he argues that consciousness may depend on the properties of living systems, not on computation as such. That position cuts against both functionalist members and AI-consciousness optimists. Seth is therefore a non-dogmatic materialist. He works on the assumption of physicalism and does not claim to have dissolved the hard problem.

**4.4 Free will: compatibilism vs. hard incompatibilism (Dennett, List, Ismael vs. Sapolsky).** Dennett (*Elbow Room*, 1984; *Freedom Evolves*, 2003), List (2019) and Ismael (2016) hold that the freedom worth wanting is real, natural and compatible with physics. Sapolsky (*Determined*, 2023) holds that once the biological and environmental causes of behaviour are traced, the word names nothing. He holds this as a physicalist and draws humane penal conclusions from it. The disagreement is partly about whether "free will" must mean libertarian contra-causal freedom. It is also about which practices the science should reform.

**4.5 How much does mathematics own? (Tegmark vs. Carroll and Weinberg).** Tegmark's Mathematical Universe Hypothesis ("The Mathematical Universe", *Foundations of Physics*, 2008; *Our Mathematical Universe*, Knopf, 2014) holds that physical reality *is* a mathematical structure. That puts him at the Platonist edge of the tradition. Carroll and Weinberg treat mathematics as the language physics uses, not as what physics is about. This matters because Levin (see the Carroll wiki, Active Question 13) presses the tradition on whether mathematical facts constrain physics causally. Tegmark's members can say yes and remain physicalists, because for them the mathematical and the physical are identical. Carroll's cannot.

**4.6 What is the relation of mind to the many worlds? (List).** List's "The Many-Worlds Theory of Consciousness" (*Noûs*, 2023) is a deliberately heterodox proposal. It assigns each subject a first-personally centred world that realises a shared third-personal world. Some members read it as the tradition at full stretch. Others read it as a sign that the subjective cannot be fully housed in third-personal physics. It is listed here because a tradition's most interesting internal work is often done at its edges.

## 5. Inadequacies the tradition admits, and its resources for them

A tradition is rationally serious only if it can state its own unsolved problems in terms its rivals would accept. These are this tradition's.

**5.1 The explanatory gap.** No member can yet derive, from a complete physical description of a brain, why that state feels the way it does. Joseph Levine named the gap ("Materialism and Qualia: The Explanatory Gap", *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly*, 1983). Chalmers's "hard problem" made it the field's defining puzzle. Kim (2005) conceded it as the remainder. *Resources:* (a) Papineau's phenomenal-concept strategy, which locates the gap in our concepts, not in the world. (b) Frankish's and Dennett's illusionism, which redescribes the explanandum. (c) Seth's "real problem" program, which bets that as the mechanisms explaining specific properties of experience accumulate, the residual question will look as dated as "what is the vital spark?" Members admit that none of these is a proof. The honest statement is this: the gap is real as an epistemic fact, and the tradition bets that it is not an ontological one.

**5.2 The exclusion problem is not settled.** See §4.2. If List and Menzies's difference-making account fails, the tradition must choose between reductionism and epiphenomenalism. That choice is costly for its anthropology. *Resources:* the interventionist theory of causation, Carroll's levels-relative causal talk, and Ismael's account of the self-governing subsystem.

**5.3 Quantum foundations.** The tradition rejects mind-dependent collapse, but it has not agreed on a positive interpretation. Weinberg (2017) found none satisfactory. Everettians still owe a widely accepted derivation of the Born rule. Sebens and Carroll's self-locating-uncertainty derivation ("Self-Locating Uncertainty and the Origin of Probability in Everettian Quantum Mechanics", *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science*, 2018) is the tradition's leading attempt, and it remains contested.

**5.4 Where the Core Theory comes from.** Physicalism explains things *by* the laws. It does not explain the laws. Carroll's "Why Is There Something, Rather Than Nothing?" (arXiv:1802.02231, 2018) argues that the question may have no answer and need not have one: the universe may simply be. The tradition admits that this is a stopping point, not a solution. This is exactly where Thomists and idealists locate their advantage ([[traditions/carroll/prs_triplets|Carroll PRS-06]]).

**5.5 The status of value.** Constructivism has to explain why some constructions are *better*. It has to say why abolishing slavery was progress and not just change. Patricia Churchland's neurobiology explains where caring comes from. It does not by itself say which extensions of care are right. Members disagree about how much work reflective equilibrium and convergent human interests can do here.

**5.6 The criterion for "earns its keep."** The [[synthesis/carroll_levin_bridge|Carroll × Levin bridge]] notes that Carroll has never given a full technical statement of when a coarse-grained description counts as real. Carroll and Parola (2024) is the beginning of one. Until it is finished, Levin's interventionist criterion and Diamond's historical cases are pressing on an unfinished wall.

## 6. Rivals, named, and how this tradition reads them

The tradition reads its rivals with one standard, applied the same way to each (E1). It also holds that most of what it disagrees with is a serious response to a real problem: usually the explanatory gap, sometimes the exclusion problem.

**6.1 Analytic idealism (Kastrup; [[traditions/kastrup/wiki|Kastrup wiki]]).** *How we read it:* Kastrup sees the hard problem as fatal to materialism and inverts the ontology. Mind-at-large is the ground, and matter is how mental processes look from outside. *Our case:* (a) Idealism trades the hard problem for a *combination/decombination* problem: how one universal mind divides into the private, bounded minds we observe. Its answer, dissociation modelled on dissociative identity disorder, borrows a clinical category that this tradition reads neurobiologically (a contested reading; the Kastrup tradition does not grant it). The Kastrup wiki's open question 4 concedes that the mechanism of dissociation is not yet specified; questions 16 and 18 bear on that mechanism without conceding it. (b) Idealism predicts nothing that physics plus neuroscience does not already predict. The Kastrup tradition disputes this: it claims loose neural correlates as a prediction idealism satisfies (Kastrup PRS-71) and records the psychedelic case, richer experience with reduced cortical activity, as its strongest empirical bet (Kastrup PRS-57). Our reply to both is in [[rival/physicalism/responses|responses]], Objection 2; it holds as a candidate, not as settled. The structure Kastrup calls "the extrinsic appearance of mental processes" is exactly the structure physics describes, re-labelled. By E1, a re-labelling with no distinguishing prediction cannot win on evidence, so it must win on priors, and adding a universal subject does not lower the prior. (c) The [[synthesis/carroll_kastrup_bridge|Carroll × Kastrup bridge]] calls the two positions formally dual. Members accept the observation and draw the opposite moral. If they are empirically equivalent, choose the one that integrates with the rest of science and with a working research program in neuroscience. *What we concede:* idealism takes the reality of experience seriously, and illusionist members should learn from the force of that.

**6.2 Conscious realism and trace logic (Hoffman and Prakash; [[traditions/hoffman/wiki|Hoffman wiki]]).** *Whom we are answering.* The program is now co-led by Donald Hoffman and Chetan Prakash through the TRACE Institute, and the vault reads it as the Hoffman-Prakash tradition (Hoffman wiki, PRS-19, PRS-20). Its earlier stage was the fitness-beats-truth (FBT) argument of *The Case Against Reality* (W. W. Norton, 2019): perception tuned to fitness outcompetes perception tuned to truth, so spacetime and objects are an interface. Members pressed cognitive instability (E2) against FBT, and Hoffman's reply, as members understood it, exempted logic and mathematics from the selection argument [UNVERIFIED: the exact form of that exemption was not checked against the book]. That exchange is now historical. The current program does not rest on evolutionary premises, and a mature member answers the current program.

*How we read it now.* Three formal pieces carry the current program. Each is stated here as recorded in the Hoffman tradition wiki.
- **The trace.** Take a Markov kernel Q and a subset A of its states, with U the complement of A. The Trace Chain Theorem gives a unique Markov kernel on A, Q_A = I_A Q [Σ_k (I_U Q)^k I_A] (Hoffman, Prakash & Chattopadhyay, *Traces of Consciousness*; Hoffman PRS-34). Information about the unobserved states is lost irreversibly, and infinitely many larger kernels share any given trace (PRS-37). The vault leaves the paper's date unresolved. The whitepaper's bibliography gives 2024 (preprint deposited October 2024), and the Trace Institute publications page gives 2025 (Hoffman wiki, Question 17).
- **Trace logic.** "Is a trace of" defines a partial order on Markov chains. The order is globally non-Boolean and locally Boolean, and some pairs of kernels are strictly incomparable, so there is no universal order (PRS-07, PRS-35, PRS-36). The talk source is Hoffman & Prakash, "A Multiscale Logic of Collective Intelligence," Thoughtforms Life (Michael Levin, host), April 2026.
- **The trace blanket.** This is the boundary structure the trace order induces on interacting agent systems. The same talk claims it strictly generalises Friston's Markov blanket from acyclic graphs to cyclic Markov chains, with the free-energy principle as a special case (PRS-10). The 2026 whitepaper extends the claim from boundaries to surprise-minimising dynamics (PRS-31).
Conscious realism is the interpretation laid over all three. A Markov chain is an observer, and the subset A is what that observer can access.

*Our case.*
(a) **The mathematics is ontology-neutral, and part of it is already ours.** Tracing a chain onto a subset of its states integrates out the time the chain spends in the hidden states. That is a form of marginalisation, a close cousin of the coarse-graining the tradition uses to explain emergence (O3; Carroll & Parola 2024). "Cousin" is the honest word, because tracing eliminates states rather than lumping them together. The construction also has roots outside the program: the Trace Institute whitepaper credits Revuz (1984) alongside Hoffman et al. when it restates the theorem (Hoffman PRS-34, evidence line). Markov blankets are already in physicalist hands. The tradition reads Friston's framework as physical theory (§6.5), and Seth's interoceptive-inference account draws on it (§2.3, A1). Nothing in a kernel, a trace or a partial order says what the states are states *of*. A population of neurons, the conformations of a molecule or a market can be traced exactly as a "conscious agent" can.
(b) **So the dispute is about who restricts.** In trace logic the restricting agent is the observer by definition, since A *is* the observer's accessible states (Hoffman wiki, Question 19). For the physicalist a blanket is a physical boundary. Conditional independence in a physical system picks it out, and it exists whether or not anything is observing. The set-theoretic move is the same; the account of what privileges the subset differs. The formalism cannot settle that difference, because it is complete before either interpretation is added. A mature member therefore does not attack the mathematics. The member asks what licenses reading A as "an observer's access" rather than "a subsystem's boundary." The member also points out that the trace order's own results (uniqueness, irreversible loss, incomparability) hold under both readings.
(c) **What we must concede.** If trace blankets really do strictly generalise Markov blankets from acyclic to cyclic dynamics, that is a genuine formal gain, and the tradition should take it up. Physical systems are full of cycles. Organisms are cyclic throughout, and physicalist modelling of them needs a boundary concept that handles that. Two cautions travel with the concession. First, the vault records the generalisation claim from a talk and holds no written proof of it (PRS-10 is sourced to the talk, as PRS-34 notes). Second, the physicalist will ask whether "acyclic" describes Friston's own blankets or only the Bayesian-network blankets they descend from. The Friston tradition wiki records Friston's recent starting point as a "universal partition of random dynamical systems" (Friston wiki, Ingestion Log, on FINDING-062) [UNVERIFIED: whether Friston's random-dynamical-systems blankets are already defined for cyclic coupling was not checked against a primary source]. The tradition should also register a further claim. A Trace Institute working paper, "Trace Logic and Theory of Computation," argues that hidden Markov models and probabilistic automata cannot express trace logic's internal logic of many observer-relative views of one computation (Hoffman PRS-56). The vault records that paper as undated, without a named author and unreviewed, and holds the claim at Medium. If it holds, "it is only marginalisation" understates the formalism. It still would not show that the views are the views of conscious observers.
(d) **What we dispute.** We dispute the step from the formalism to the ontology. A formal gain in a boundary concept is a gain for whoever uses it, and physicalists can use it. The same holds for the claim that FEP is a special case of trace logic. If that claim is true, it makes FEP a special case of a piece of mathematics, not a special case of consciousness. On spacetime, the program's own framing is conjecture. "Fusions of Consciousness" (Hoffman, Prakash & Prentner, *Entropy* 25(1): 129, 2023) presents its link between spacetime physics and the fusion of conscious agents as a conjecture. The 2026 whitepaper lists eight Conjectures, among them Minkowski space as a limit of Markov n-cycles and the Born rule, and, as the Hoffman wiki quotes it, says "Proving these conjectures is a prime focus of the scientific program" (Hoffman PRS-28). We welcome a published research contract. Recovering known physics would still be a consistency check, though, not evidence for the conscious-agent reading (E1). That reading needs a prediction that the physical reading of the same kernels does not make (cf. [[synthesis/carroll_hoffman_bridge|Carroll × Hoffman bridge]]).
(e) **Where the dialogue can go.** The vault has noticed one pattern in three programs: restriction to a slice generates apparent structure. It appears in Arkani-Hamed's boundary-condition results, in Wolfram's bounded observers and in the trace kernel (Hoffman wiki, Question 19; master index CROSS-100). Trace logic is the only one of the three that names the restricting agent. The master index currently rules the three uses equivocal, not convergent, partly because the direction of epistemic access runs opposite in Arkani-Hamed and Hoffman. The physicalist has something to add here. The Arkani-Hamed case already shows a restriction whose "agent" is a physical boundary condition with no observer in it. Whether that, rather than the observer, is the right identification for the trace is an open question for the network. It is not a result, and no convergence is claimed.
(f) Both programs agree that spacetime is not fundamental. They still part over what replaces it. Physicalists replace it with quantum structure that exists independently of observers. Hoffman and Prakash replace it with observers.

**6.3 Basal cognition and "mind everywhere" (Levin; [[traditions/levin/wiki|Levin wiki]]).** *How we read it:* Of the three, this is the rival closest to an ally. Dennett co-wrote "Cognition All the Way Down" with Levin (*Aeon*, 2020). Bioelectric pattern memory, xenobots and anthrobots are spectacular natural science, and nothing in them violates the Core Theory. *Our case:* (a) "Cognition," "goal" and "agent" applied to tissues are, on poetic naturalism, legitimate higher-level vocabularies when they earn predictive and interventional keep. Levin's lab demonstrates that they often do. (b) What we reject is the step from *goal-directedness* to *mind* in the conscious-realist sense, and from *mathematical constraint* to *non-physical causation*. Levin's claim that physicalism "has been dead for a long time" (recorded in the Carroll wiki, Active Question 13 [UNVERIFIED: quotation taken from the vault's own record; primary source not re-checked]) rests on running together constraint and cause. (c) The Pigozzi–Levin result, that causal emergence can be manipulated, is the strongest test of our account of levels, and we accept it as a test (Carroll wiki, Active Question 12). *What we concede:* our criterion for when a higher-level variable is real has to be stated precisely enough that Levin's experiments could count against it.

**6.4 Panpsychism (Goff, and the Russellian monists).** *How we read it:* the most physics-respecting of the consciousness-first views, because it leaves the dynamics untouched and places experience in the intrinsic nature of what physics describes only structurally. *Our case:* Carroll (2021) states the dilemma directly. If the mental properties change how particles behave, they collide with laws tested to great precision. If they do not, they make no difference to what anything does, including what we say about our experience. Then the panpsychist's own reports of experience are not caused by the experience. The view also inherits the combination problem.

**6.5 The free-energy principle (Friston), as a contested middle.** Seth's interoceptive-inference work and Friston's active inference share a mathematics. Nothing in the free-energy principle requires consciousness to be fundamental. Members read FEP as a powerful *physicalist* framework that the conscious-realist cluster has taken over, and they expect to claim it back. That expectation is a forecast, not a finding.

**6.6 Thomistic hylomorphism and MacIntyrean Aristotelianism (Stump; the vault's editorial tradition).** *How we read it:* this is the rival members should take most seriously as a *tradition*, because it has what physicalism is often accused of lacking: an account of natural kinds, of teleology, and of the goods of practices. *Our case:* Members argue that each of these can be reconstructed at the level poetic naturalism already allows. Function can be reconstructed as selected effect, practice-goods as constructed and evolved social realities, and form as the organisation of matter. The reconstruction loses only the claim that these are metaphysically fundamental. *What we concede:* that reconstruction has not been carried out with the thoroughness MacIntyre's own work displays. Whether "form as organisation" is hylomorphism under a new name or something weaker is a genuinely open question between the two traditions, and the most productive one for an ACPA audience.

## 7. Active Research Questions
*(Questions this tradition is currently generating, from the inside)*

1. Can the criterion for when a coarse-grained description "earns its keep" be stated precisely enough to be tested against Levin's manipulable causal emergence and against historical cases with no intervention available?
2. Does the phenomenal-concept strategy survive the objection that phenomenal concepts themselves need a physicalist explanation, or does it move the gap into concepts?
3. Is illusionism consistent with taking pain and suffering seriously as moral data, which the tradition's ethics (V1–V3) requires?
4. Does Seth's biological naturalism imply that no current AI system is conscious? If so, what would change that verdict? This bears directly on C2A2's question about AI members of traditions.
5. Can Everettian quantum mechanics give a Born-rule derivation that its critics accept? If not, which non-mentalist interpretation should the tradition prefer?
6. Does List and Menzies's difference-making answer to Kim's exclusion argument hold up under interventionist scrutiny for mental-to-physical causation specifically?
7. What would count, by the tradition's own Bayesian standard, as evidence *against* physicalism? A tradition that cannot name its defeaters has stopped inquiring. Candidate answers: a reproducible violation of conservation laws in neural tissue; a reliable correlation between experience and physical outcomes that the Core Theory does not predict.
8. How should constructivism about value account for moral progress?

## 9. Paradigm Assessment (self-assessment)

The tradition is in good health by its own standards. Its ontological core has had decades of experimental success. Its account of levels is under active, technically serious development (List 2019; Carroll & Parola 2024). Its anthropology has two working neuroscientific research programs behind it (Seth's predictive self, Graziano's attention schema). It argues openly with itself, and it has a member (Kim) whose final position is an exemplary admission of remainder. Its weaknesses are as follows. It cannot close the explanatory gap. It has not settled quantum foundations. It has an unfinished criterion for higher-level reality. It has not fully worked out an account of moral progress. Its rivals see each of these clearly. The tradition's wager is that these are the problems of a research program still producing results, not the anomalies of a degenerating one. The C2A2 network is a good place to test that wager, because there the rivals can answer back.
