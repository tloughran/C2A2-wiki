## Research Program Overview
**Thinker:** Eleonore Stump (Saint Louis University)
**Core claim:** Aquinas offers a hylomorphic psychology that maps onto cognitive science; Thomism is a living, generative tradition capable of organizing inquiry across disciplines; suffering is permitted as a condition for deep personal union
**Paradigm:** Analytic Thomism, hylomorphism, final causality, MacIntyre's tradition-constituted rationality

**Summary (from RC Pilot document):**

Eleonore Stump is the tradition's analytic philosopher, the figure who brings Aquinas and MacIntyre into direct contact with the natural sciences gathered around the project. Her central contribution is the recovery of Thomistic hylomorphism — the doctrine that form and matter are co-constitutive of substance — as a framework able to accommodate the cognitive turn in biology and neuroscience without reducing it away. Where modern philosophy abandoned final causality (or domesticated it as Mayr's "teleonomy"), Stump rehabilitates it in analytic terms: organisms act toward forms not yet realized, and this is metaphysics recoverable inside a naturalistic research program, not mysticism.

Her second contribution is to show that Thomism meets MacIntyre's own criterion for a living tradition — it has engaged analytic philosophy, cognitive science, and evolutionary biology on their own terms without dissolving into them. This is what lets the project use Stump as the bridge between its theological and scientific wings. Two couplings do most of the work: the link between Thomistic final causality and Levin's morphogenetic teleology (bodies pursuing forms they have not yet reached), and the link between Thomistic *beatitudo* — union as the highest good — and Fredrickson's positivity resonance, love understood as biophysiological coupling between embodied persons.

The questions her program is generating press on exactly these joints: can Thomistic final causality ground biology's teleonomic explanations without circularity; does hylomorphic psychology map onto computational models of cognition such as active inference and hierarchical temporal memory; and what would a Thomistic account of *AI* agents attaining second-first-language status within a tradition actually look like. Her recorded track record is correspondingly strong on the tradition-level claims — that traditions can engage rationally across paradigm gaps, and that Thomism is a live tradition by contemporary standards — and more provisional on the biological bridge, where final causality is offered as the framework *for* Levin's findings rather than a finished reduction of them.

**A provenance note carried over from the vault:** the PRS / Synergistic-Coil *method* is Loughran's, not Stump's. Two triplets originally filed here (PRS-01, PRS-03) were re-homed to Loughran on 2026-06-11 as methodology triplets; the brief therefore credits Stump with the analytic-Thomist content (PRS-02 "Thomism and the cognitive turn," PRS-04 "final causality and modern biology") and not with the coil apparatus that frames it. Within the architecture she sits at the hinge of the Aquinas/Summa material, MacIntyre's account of tradition-rationality, and the empirical programs that the project is trying to read through a recovered teleology.
