PRS-34:
  Problem: Stump's books have been received as separable contributions — the Routledge *Aquinas*, *Wandering in Darkness*, the atonement book, *The Image of God* — with the unity among them supplied by commentators rather than by her. Is there a single thesis, and if so is it one she owns?
  Resource: Her own retrospective statement of it, offered in the one venue whose genre licenses that kind of first-person summation, together with the narrated causal chain among the books: each was written because the previous one had grown too large to hold the next part of the problem of suffering.
  Solution: The corpus is one argument, and Stump states it herself: the thesis of all these books is "the whole Christian worldview in all its complexity, the view that at the ultimate, irreducible foundation of all reality there is love in powerful, welcoming personal relationship." This makes love an **ontological** claim about what is foundational, not an ethical claim about what agents should do — which is why PRS-33's reading of the title as an ethics-of-love thesis was the wrong register. The wiki's Research Program Overview already asserts that Stump "treats love as an ontological and epistemic category, not merely an ethical one"; this is the first time the tradition holds that claim **in her own voice** rather than as the agent's synthesis.
  Date Added: 2026-08-10
  Source: 2023 Central Division Dewey Lecture: Dilige et quod vis fac (full text, Saint Louis University); PROP-2026-08-06-001
  Confidence: High

PRS-35:
  Problem: PRS-18 records that defeated suffering still merits mourning — a claim about the *sufferer*. It leaves the harder case untouched: does a successful theodicy leave God with something to mourn? If it does, the problem of evil has not been answered but relocated.
  Resource: Aquinas's distinction between God's antecedent will (what God would have willed had everything been up to God alone) and God's consequent will (what God actually wills given what human beings will), pressed against the theodicy it is normally used to support.
  Solution: The increment over PRS-18 is that the mourning problem runs **both ways**. If the antecedent/consequent distinction holds, it appears to imply that God settles for plan B in creation and so has reason to mourn what creation turned out to be. This is why a fourth book was needed after the atonement book, and it names the residue that the network's other theodicy material (PRS-05) does not address.

PRS-36:
  Problem: Granting PRS-35, what could possibly *answer* a mourning that survives a successful theodicy? Consolation by compensation is exactly what the problem rules out — the sufferer has already been granted the outweighing benefit and still has something to mourn.
  Resource: The Psalmist's "In the presence of the angels, I will sing your praises," read against medieval angelology, on which angels are metaphysically greater than human beings but do not suffer.
  Solution: Suffering love is a good that the metaphysically greater being cannot have. Because angels do not suffer, they cannot mirror the love of God manifest in Christ's passion in their own love of God; so the sufferer's life-as-song is "more glorious than any song the angels sing," and the angels are the audience rather than the standard. The structure of the answer is worth naming precisely: it does not compensate the suffering, and it does not explain it. It identifies a specific good — a mode of loving available only to a sufferer — that is not reachable by any route around the suffering. This is a sharper claim than the network's usual "suffering permitted for the sake of union" (PRS-05), because it makes the *ranking* invert.

PRS-38:
  Problem: C2A2's operational test of tradition vitality is MacIntyre's — does the tradition engage rivals on their own terms without dissolving. That test says nothing about the mechanism by which a *discipline* forecloses such engagement, which is what the accelerator is built to counteract. Naming the mechanism is prior to claiming the architecture addresses it.
  Resource: Stump's account of boundary-policing in analytic philosophy: the dismissal of medieval philosophy as based on dogma and authority; the challenge "Is it possible to do metaphysics after Kant?"; the senior scholar's objection that Aquinas's corpus contains no Latin word for "meta-ethics"; and above all the question "Yes, but is it philosophy?" deployed against feminist philosophy and anything else deviating from "the narrowly focused pattern-processing favored by a small group of established philosophers talking largely only to each other."
  Solution: Name the mechanism as **taxonomy** — foreclosure achieved not by refuting a position but by classifying it out of the discipline, which requires no argument and admits of no reply on the merits. Stump's stated counter-moves are correspondingly non-taxonomic: "Ab esse ad posse valet consequentia" (I just did metaphysics, so it must be possible) and "Well, I'm a philosopher, and as a philosopher I'm interested in it; so it is philosophy" — both refuse the classificatory frame rather than argue inside it. The diagnosis is self-implicating and therefore credible: she notes the same disputes persist in newer form over whether "analytical Thomism" is true to Aquinas or a travesty, i.e. over her own work.

  Source: 2023 Central Division Dewey Lecture: Dilige et quod vis fac (full text, Saint Louis University); PROP-2026-08-06-001
  Confidence: High
  Evidence: Verbatim: "In philosophy, taxonomy was (and still is) one of the weapons enforcing orthodoxy in the profession. Often enough when there was some topic that seemed interesting, it was dismissed with condescending authority by the question, 'Yes, but is it philosophy?'" and "a gate-keeping that impoverished the discipline." **Register caveat:** this is autobiographical testimony about a discipline, not a study of it.
