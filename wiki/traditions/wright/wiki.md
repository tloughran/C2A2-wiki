## Research Program Overview
**Thinker:** N.T. Wright (University of St Andrews, emeritus)
**Core claim:** The biblical narrative is a five-act drama (creation, fall, Israel, Jesus, church) in which the present community is called to improvise faithfully within the unfinished final act; critical realism provides an epistemology that avoids both naive positivism and postmodern relativism; the resurrection of Jesus is a historical event with cosmological implications
**Paradigm:** Critical realism, narrative theology, new perspective on Paul, inaugurated eschatology, faithful improvisation

**Summary (from RC Pilot document):**

N.T. Wright is the tradition's narrative theologian and historian — the thinker who gives the project a model of how a community inhabits and extends a story it did not originate. His critical realism stakes out a precise epistemological position between Carroll's poetic naturalism and Kastrup's idealism: reality exists independently of our knowing it, but all knowing is perspectival, tradition-embedded, and revisable. That places him close to MacIntyre's tradition-constituted rationality while adding an explicitly historical and narrative dimension that MacIntyre's more formal account leaves implicit.

His most distinctive contribution is the five-act hermeneutic. If the biblical story is an unfinished drama — creation, fall, Israel, Jesus, church, with the final act still open — then the task of the present community is not to repeat the past but to *improvise faithfully*: to act consistently with the story's trajectory while responding to circumstances the original authors could not have anticipated. This maps with unusual directness onto the project's own method. Each agent inherits a thinker's research program and must extend it into new territory — cross-program connections, novel proposals — while remaining faithful to that program's internal logic. "Faithful improvisation" is, in effect, the job description of every tradition agent in the system.

Wright's work on Paul and his concept of inaugurated eschatology — the decisive event has already occurred, but its full implications are still unfolding — gives the project a temporal model for how research programs mature. The founding insight may already be present in the early texts, but its significance becomes visible only through patient elaboration and cross-traditional encounter, which is precisely the timescale the detector is meant to compress.

The questions his program raises are methodological and testable: does the five-act hermeneutic actually provide a workable model for how AI agents should extend the programs they inherit; is critical realism a near-relation of Friston's active inference, where inference inside a generative model is a formal version of perspectival-but-real knowing; can inaugurated eschatology serve as a model for program development; and where exactly does his account of resurrection as a historical event with cosmological significance challenge — or merely sit athwart — Carroll's physicalism. His recorded advances are the epistemological ones: that history and theology can be methodologically integrated, and that critical realism offers a genuine middle way between positivism and relativism. He connects to MacIntyre (his five-act hermeneutic as a narrative application of tradition-constituted rationality), Rohr (already/not-yet alongside order-disorder-reorder), Carroll (a direct, evidence-serious disagreement about whether the evidence permits claims exceeding naturalism), Stump (historically grounded theology engaging analytic philosophy on its own terms), and Loughran (the project's faithful-improvisation methodology as a direct application of his hermeneutic).

## Active Research Questions
*(Questions currently being generated by this program)*

1. Does Wright's five-act hermeneutic provide a workable model for how AI agents should extend research programs they inherit?
2. How does critical realism relate to Friston's active inference — is inference within a generative model a formal version of critical realist knowing?
3. Can inaugurated eschatology serve as a model for research program development — the core insight is present but its implications are still unfolding?
4. What is the relationship between Wright's faithful improvisation and MacIntyre's tradition-constituted inquiry — are they the same methodology in different registers?
5. Does Wright's account of resurrection as a historical event with cosmological significance challenge or complement Carroll's physicalism?
6. How does Wright's narrative epistemology relate to McGilchrist's account of the right hemisphere as the hemisphere of narrative, context, and embodied meaning?
7. Does the corporate-person reading of 2 John's "elect lady" (PRS-40) strengthen the church-as-one-thing thesis, or complicate it? A community that can be *addressed* as a person is a stronger claim than one that can be *described* as a body — and it may be stronger than Wright himself wants.
8. Can critical realism hold Rohr's claim that revelation is the self-disclosure of a Person rather than a deposit of propositions (Rohr PRS-40), without losing the text's referential control?
9. Is "tradition adjudicated by narrative" (PRS-41) a general method or a move available only from inside the tradition whose narrative is doing the adjudicating? An Orthodox interlocutor could accept the method and reach the opposite verdict.
10. Does the narrowed-imagination diagnosis (PRS-42) survive Carroll's rejoinder that the modern repertoire was *corrected* rather than contracted? And if a reader's repertoire governs what can register at all, how much work is PRS-01's independent constraint still doing? Wright would distinguish uptake from truth, but that distinction is asserted here, not argued from any captured source.
11. Is corporate faith (PRS-45) the claim that a community believes as one subject, or only that individual faith is formed and sustained communally? The two differ sharply and the captured source does not decide between them.
12. Does the intermediate state require a bearer? Wright specifies the interval relationally and names none (PRS-50); Stump's hylomorphism cannot proceed without one.
13. Is the four-Gospel material (PRS-47) four independent lines of evidence, or four illustrations of a thesis already held? The answer decides whether the homecoming thesis has an evidential base or only an exposition.
14. Does Wright's historical method yield normative guidance in applied ethics, or only description? Two 2026 episodes force the question - divorce and remarriage (PROP-2026-08-12-047) and the defence industry (PROP-2026-08-25-055) - and neither answer was retrieved.
15. How strong is the Homer claim (PRS-53) - resonance, which is uncontroversial, or deliberate literary imitation, which is MacDonald's contested position? The triplet is substantive on the second reading and near-trivial on the first.
16. Does the credal-vocabulary question (PRS-56) extend past "Trinity" to other terms coined to protect a concept the narrative revises - and what stops the method from dissolving the vocabulary it is revising?
17. Why is "Do not be afraid" the standing preface to angelic appearances - a datum about the phenomenology of the encounter, or a literary convention? (PROP-2026-08-12-044; question verbatim in the description, no answer retrieved.)
18. Could Satan have prevented Jesus from completing his mission, and in what sense is a temptation real if the outcome was never open? (PROP-2026-08-12-043; no answer retrieved.)
19. Does the Gospel stand to Greek epic as it stands to Israel's scriptures - a story reaching its goal - or in some other relation? (PROP-2026-08-12-048; the *praeparatio evangelica* question, still unanswered for Greek culture in this wiki.)
20. How does Jesus reveal the glory of God - is "glory" a property displayed, or something else? (PROP-2026-08-25-054; question named, answer not retrieved.)
21. Where did Pilate try Jesus, how public were such proceedings, and does the answer shrink the transmission problem for the passion narrative? (PROP-2026-08-25-055; answer not retrieved.)
