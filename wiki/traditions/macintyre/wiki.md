## Research Program Overview
**Thinker:** Alasdair MacIntyre (University of Notre Dame, emeritus)
**Core claim:** Rational inquiry is always tradition-constituted; moral reasoning requires narrative coherence within a living tradition; the Enlightenment project of grounding morality in tradition-independent reason has failed, and the choice is between Aristotelian-Thomistic virtue ethics and Nietzschean emotivism
**Paradigm:** Tradition-constituted rationality, narrative identity, practice-virtue-institution triad, epistemological crisis and recovery

**Summary (from RC Pilot document):**

Alasdair MacIntyre is the project's philosopher of rationality itself — the thinker who supplies the epistemological frame within which the whole C2A2 architecture makes sense. His central argument, developed across *After Virtue*, *Whose Justice? Which Rationality?*, and *Three Rival Versions of Moral Enquiry*, is that all rational inquiry is conducted from within a tradition, and that traditions can nonetheless be rationally evaluated — by their capacity to solve their own internal problems, to survive epistemological crises, and to render rival traditions intelligible on their own terms. The Enlightenment project of a tradition-independent reason has failed; the honest choice, he argues, lies between an Aristotelian-Thomistic account of inquiry and a Nietzschean emotivism that abandons the idea of rational adjudication altogether.

This is the methodological foundation for the project's multi-tradition design. Each agent operates inside a tradition — Levin's basal cognition, Friston's active inference, Hoffman's conscious realism, Stump's Thomism — and the value of the system lies not in collapsing them into one super-theory but in staging precisely the encounters MacIntyre describes: moments where one tradition's resources solve a problem the other could not solve on its own terms. The detector half of the "accelerator-detector" is, in MacIntyrean language, an instrument for watching tradition-constituted rationality do its work at speed.

His practice-virtue-institution triad supplies the project's sociological grammar: practices generate internal goods, virtues sustain practices, and institutions sustain both while always risking their corruption. Read this way, Loughran's Synergistic Coil is a *practice* whose internal goods include cross-traditional insight and whose characteristic virtues include intellectual honesty, patience with incommensurability, and willingness to revise premises under rational pressure.

The open questions are unusually self-referential, which is appropriate for the tradition that theorizes the project's own form: does the C2A2 multi-agent architecture itself constitute a MacIntyrean practice, and if so what are its internal goods; can his criteria for tradition vitality be formalized as metrics for evaluating agent programs; can Friston's active inference model an epistemological crisis as a free-energy spike followed by model revision; and — the question with the most weight for an AI-mediated system — can an agent be a *genuine participant* in a tradition or only a simulator of one. His recorded advances are the load-bearing ones for the whole enterprise: that traditions can be rationally compared (by one solving what another cannot), and that a non-relativist alternative to Enlightenment universalism exists. He connects outward to Stump (Thomism as his exemplary living tradition), Friston (crisis as inference under constraint), McGilchrist (narrative rationality and right-hemisphere understanding), Rohr (order-disorder-reorder as the contemplative face of epistemological crisis), and Loughran (the project as a MacIntyrean practice made into architecture).
