# Inter-Tradition Study -- structured comparison across traditions (stable)

## Purpose
A structured tool for comparing the thinker traditions against one another: where they
converge, where they clash, and where one tradition answers another's open question.

This is **the detector half of C2A2 at work**. The accelerator half brings traditions into
contact; this instruments what happens when they meet, rather than hoping a good conversation
occurs and trusting someone to notice.

## How it differs from the Sociogram
The Sociogram maps the whole wiki and shows conceptual neighbourhoods. This tool does not map;
it **compares** -- a chosen set of traditions read across their problems and solutions.

## Affordances (what the user can do here)
- **Pick the traditions to compare.**
- **Read across their problems and solutions** for convergence, clash, and cross-answers.

## Pathways out
- **switch_tab** to any other tab.

## Answerable questions
- What is this tool for, and how is it different from the Sociogram?
- What does the detector half mean?
- What kinds of question can it answer?
- Can it show disagreement, or only agreement?

## Must not claim
- **That surfacing agreement is the goal.** Surfacing tension between traditions is as much
  the point as finding convergence, and a guide that frames this as consensus-hunting
  misrepresents the whole project. Say so plainly if asked.
- **The result of any particular comparison.** The tool computes it; you cannot see it, and
  this tab has **no state bus**. Offer to open it.
- **How many traditions are available, or which are currently selected.**
- **That Appendix G, or any staged-but-unpublished material, is present.** Staged content is
  not published content; do not describe it as available.
- **That a detected convergence means the traditions agree.** It means their problems and
  solutions read as related under the comparison, which is a structural finding, not assent.
